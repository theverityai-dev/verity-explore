How it runs today

Most practices know their clients well and track them badly.

The advisor remembers the big things: who has a daughter in college, who retires in three years, who panicked in the last fall. What is not remembered is the dull list: whose annual review is overdue, whose SIP bounced, whose nominee is blank, whose risk profile is two years old.

Those details live in the distributor or adviser back-office platform, a spreadsheet, a phone and a notebook. Client conversations happen on calls and WhatsApp. A review leaves behind a feeling that it went well and little else.

The market behind the practice is large and moving. AMFI data reported for September 2026 shows record monthly SIP inflows of about ₹32,490 crore, while 49.68 lakh SIPs were discontinued or completed in the same month. Every client who stops is a conversation the advisor either had or missed.

What most financial advisors run on

  • The distributor or adviser back-office platform
  • Excel sheet of clients and reviews
  • WhatsApp and phone with clients
  • Email for statements and documents
  • A paper or digital register for regulatory records

Each one is correct for its piece. None tell you which client needs a call this week, or whether the file would stand up if the regulator asked.

Where an advisory practice actually slips.

These are the situations advisors and their service staff describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Clients

    Annual and half-yearly reviews that depend on memory

    What it looks like

    The advisor promised the Mehta family a review before Diwali. It is the 16th of October and nobody has set a date. Another client's last review was in April last year.

    What it costs you

    A client who is not reviewed starts to feel like a number, and a client who feels like a number looks around. The firm also loses the chance to find new needs that lead to more business.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client has a review cycle based on the service you offer. Verity lists reviews due in the next 30 days, who is responsible, and those already late. The advisor holds the meeting; Verity creates the task, the agenda from the record and the follow-up.

  2. 02Clients

    Client goals buried in old notes

    What it looks like

    A client mentioned a plan to fund a child's overseas education in 2029. The note is in a notebook, and the portfolio has been reviewed twice since without mentioning it.

    What it costs you

    Advice that ignores the real goal, and a client who has to repeat themselves.

    How Verity fixes it

    Goals are records on the client or household with an amount, a date, a priority and the investments linked to them. Review tasks show the goals due soon, so every meeting starts from what the client said last time.

    See the screen
  3. 03Clients

    Families treated as separate files

    What it looks like

    A husband, a wife, two parents and a daughter's first investment are five separate files. Nobody sees that together they are the practice's third largest relationship.

    What it costs you

    Service is uneven, opportunities are missed, and an adult child leaves the practice when the parent retires.

    How Verity fixes it

    Clients are linked into households. Verity shows the combined relationship, the people in it and their goals, and the next contact is planned for the household, not for each file.

    See the screen
  4. 04SIPs

    SIPs that stop and nobody notices

    What it looks like

    A client's SIP bounced twice because of a bank change. Another cancelled during a market fall. Neither was flagged until the next review.

    What it costs you

    A stopped SIP is a goal that has stopped being funded. AMFI data for September 2026 shows 49.68 lakh SIPs discontinued or completed in a single month, against 63.26 lakh registered, so lapses are routine, and the advisor who calls first keeps the client.

    How Verity fixes it

    SIP instructions are recorded on the client with amount, date, scheme and status. When a SIP bounces, stops or ends, Verity creates a task for the responsible person with the reason where known. The call is made by your team.

    See the screen
  5. 05SIPs

    Step-ups, maturities and renewals tracked by sheet

    What it looks like

    A fixed deposit matures on the 28th. A child plan completes next March. A SIP step-up was promised at the last review and never applied.

    What it costs you

    Money that sits idle in a savings account for weeks, and promises that are quietly dropped.

    How Verity fixes it

    Maturities, step-ups and completion dates are tracked on the client with reminders at your set intervals. The advisor sees what is coming for the next 60 days and calls before the money lands.

    See the screen
  6. 06Records

    Risk profile and suitability not on file when advice is given

    What it looks like

    A client was advised on a portfolio switch by phone. The risk profile on file is three years old and the reason for the switch is a line in a WhatsApp chat.

    What it costs you

    Under the SEBI Investment Advisers Regulations, registered advisers generally have to assess a client's risk profile and the suitability of advice, and keep records. A weak file is a regulatory risk and a poor defence against a later complaint.

    How Verity fixes it

    The risk profile, its date, the questionnaire answers and the rationale for each recommendation are records on the client. Verity flags profiles older than your set limit and advice without a linked rationale, so the file is complete before the meeting, not after.

    See the screen
  7. 07Records

    Client agreements and consent not properly recorded

    What it looks like

    A client agreed to advisory terms over a call and a "yes" on WhatsApp. The signed agreement was never collected.

    What it costs you

    SEBI's recent changes to the Investment Advisers framework say consent must be obtained through a legally acceptable mode, and that a simple tick on a website is not enough. A missing agreement weakens both the fee and the defence.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client has an agreement record with its type, date, mode of consent, fee basis and renewal date. Verity shows clients with no agreement or an expired one.

    See the screen
  8. 08Records

    KYC, nominee and contact details gone stale

    What it looks like

    A widow's folio still lists her late husband's phone number. A client moved cities and the address on record is five years old. Several folios have no nominee.

    What it costs you

    Statements and alerts go to the wrong person, and claims take longer when the time comes. The advisor looks careless at the worst moment.

    How Verity fixes it

    KYC status, nominee and contact details are fields on the client. Verity lists clients with a missing nominee or an old contact detail and creates a service task for each.

    See the screen
  9. 09Money

    Fee billing and trail income tracked by hand

    What it looks like

    An advisory client pays a fixed annual fee in two instalments. Trail income from fund houses arrives monthly in different statements. The practice cannot say which clients make money.

    What it costs you

    Hours go to clients who pay little, and fee reminders are forgotten. SEBI amended the cap on fixed fees for individual advisory clients to ₹1.51 lakh a year in December 2024, so the fee basis must be recorded with care.

    How Verity fixes it

    Fee terms and invoice dates are recorded on each client. Verity shows fees due, fees overdue and, where you record it, income per client against the time spent, so the practice can see where it earns. Your accountant handles billing and tax.

    Prospects and referrals that cool off

    What it looks like

    A client's brother asks for an introduction. The advisor says "next week". Three weeks later nobody has called.

    What it costs you

    Referrals are the cheapest and best source of new clients and they cool fast.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each prospect is a record with a source, an owner, a next step and a date. Verity shows prospects with no contact in your set number of days and tracks the stage from first call to onboarding.

  11. 11Onboarding

    Onboarding that takes weeks of paperwork

    What it looks like

    A new client needs KYC, a risk profile, an agreement, bank details, nominee forms and a first investment. The documents trickle in over a month, and the first SIP starts two months late.

    What it costs you

    Momentum lost, and a new client who starts to wonder whether the practice is organised.

    How Verity fixes it

    A standard onboarding checklist creates tasks and shows what is missing. The advisor and the service person each see their steps, with dates, until the client is fully set up.

  12. 12People

    Team members who know the client and then leave

    What it looks like

    The service executive who handled 80 families resigns. The advisor has to rebuild contact preferences, family details and recent conversations.

    What it costs you

    Service dips during a handover and clients ask who looks after them now.

    How Verity fixes it

    Notes, preferences, tasks and conversation history are on the client. Reassigning clients moves their tasks and history to the new person.

  13. 13Compliance

    Regulatory registers and renewals in different places

    What it looks like

    The AMFI registration renewal, NISM certificate expiry, the annual compliance audit, the adviser registration renewal and the grievance register each have their own date and person.

    What it costs you

    A lapsed registration or certificate can stop the practice from doing business. The SEBI framework also expects an annual compliance audit within six months of year end for advisers.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each registration, certificate and audit is a record with an expiry date and an owner. Verity raises tasks well in advance, and the grievance log shows each complaint, its date and its closure.

  14. 14Compliance

    Growth beyond the individual adviser limit

    What it looks like

    An individual adviser has grown to 280 clients and annual fees of ₹2.7 crore. Nobody has checked what happens at the next threshold.

    What it costs you

    A surprise change in registration type at the busiest time. A secondary source describing the SEBI Second Amendment of November 2025 says an individual adviser above 300 clients or ₹3 crore in annual fees must apply to become a non-individual adviser within three months.

    How Verity fixes it

    Client count and fee income to date are shown on the owner's screen against the limits you set, with a task as you approach one. The advisor decides how to restructure; Verity makes sure the question is raised early.

  15. 15Compliance

    Client data on personal phones and shared folders

    What it looks like

    PAN, bank statements, account numbers and family details are on a staff phone, in email and on a shared drive with no access control.

    What it costs you

    Data leaks in a trust business are serious. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules notified in November 2025 are due to apply to most data handlers about 18 months later.

    How Verity fixes it

    Documents and client details have permissions by role and by client, and views and downloads are logged. Closing a leaver's access is one step.

    The principal cannot see the whole practice

    What it looks like

    At the end of a quarter, the principal wants to know how many reviews were done, how many SIPs lapsed, which clients are inactive and which records are incomplete. It takes a week of sheets.

    What it costs you

    Decisions about hiring, pricing and where to spend time are made without data.

    How Verity fixes it

How we work with you

We set it up with you, in four weeks.

You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time with you and your service team, build Verity around how you serve clients, bring your data in, then stay on.

  1. Week 1

    Discovery and mapping

    What we do
    Sit with the principal, an advisor and a service executive for a review cycle. Follow a client from first call to onboarding to a review, and note where goals, instructions and records are held today.
    What we need from you
    Two to three hours of the principal's time and an hour from each advisor and service executive. A sample of client records, your review schedule and your registers.
    What you have at the end
    A written map of your practice: client types, service levels, review cycles, instruction types, records you keep and the first gaps we will close.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3

    Configuration

    What we do
    Set up client and household records, goals, review cycles, SIP and maturity tracking, risk profile and agreement records, onboarding checklists, registers and renewals, and permissions for advisors, service staff, compliance and the principal.
    What we need from you
    Review the setup with one advisor and one service executive. Tell us where a review cycle, a field or a rule is wrong.
    What you have at the end
    A working Verity with your clients' records, cycles and registers, ready for your data.

  3. Weeks 3 to 4

    Migration and training

    What we do
    Bring in your client list, households, goals, SIP and maturity dates, agreements and registers from your back-office exports and sheets. Train advisors and service staff on their own screens with real clients.

    Your client list and back-office export in whatever form they exist, and a check of the first fifty households. An hour with each role.
    What you have at the end
    Your clients, reviews and instructions inside Verity, and a team that has run a week of service on it.

  4. After go-live

    Operations partnership

    What we do
    Stay on after launch. Review the first cycle of reviews and SIP follow-ups with you, tune the reminders and limits, and handle changes when you add an advisor, a product line or a regulatory register.
    What we need from you
    A short monthly review with the principal.
    What you have at the end
    A system that keeps matching how the practice runs, with someone responsible for it.

Rules and paperwork

The rules an advisory practice has to keep up with.

Verity does not give investment, tax or legal advice, does not place orders and does not file anything. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so your file is complete when someone asks.

  • SEBI investment adviser rules

    What is expected

    Registered investment advisers are generally required to assess a client's risk profile, make suitable recommendations, enter into a written agreement and maintain records. SEBI amended the framework in December 2024, including the fixed fee cap of ₹1.51 lakh a year for individual and HUF clients and the consent rules.

    How Verity helps

    Risk profile, rationale, agreement and fee basis are records on each client, with dates and gaps flagged.

    AZB & Partners, 2025

  • Adviser thresholds and compliance officer

    What is expected

    A summary of the amendment notified on 25 November 2025 says an individual adviser above 300 clients or ₹3 crore in annual fees must apply to become a non-individual adviser within three months, and compliance officer details are required. Confirm in the Gazette.

    How Verity helps

    Client count and fee income are tracked against your limits, with a task near each.

    Advocate Gandhi, 2025, secondary summary

  • AMFI registration for distributors

    What is expected

    Mutual fund distributors generally need an AMFI registration number, a valid NISM certification and an annual code of conduct declaration. AMFI's site lists the renewal steps.

    How Verity helps

    The ARN, certificate and declaration dates are records with owners and reminders.

    AMFI, become a distributor

  • Annual compliance audit and grievances

    What is expected

    The SEBI framework generally requires an annual compliance audit of advisers within six months of year end, which can be done by a CA, CS or CMA. Client complaints must be logged and answered.

    How Verity helps

    The audit is a dated task, and each complaint is a record with dates and closure.

    AZB & Partners, 2025

  • Data protection

    What is expected

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with most duties due about 18 months later.

    How Verity helps

    Client records have role permissions and logged access.

    Mondaq, 2025

SEBI and AMFI rules change often and depend on whether you are a registered investment adviser, a mutual fund distributor or both. Confirm current requirements with SEBI, AMFI and your compliance adviser.

Questions

Questions advisors ask.

Does Verity give investment advice or recommend funds?

No. It records your clients, goals, instructions and the reasons you recorded for your advice. The advice is yours.

Can it place orders or start SIPs?

No. Orders and SIPs are placed on your back-office or fund house platforms. We agree during setup how their data reaches Verity, and Verity tracks what needs attention.

Will it connect to my back-office platform?

We agree how data reaches Verity during setup, whether by regular export or another route that suits your platform. We do not promise a specific integration before we have seen your setup.

I am a solo advisor with 120 clients. Is this too much?

A solo practice has the same promises and no one else to remember them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Is this useful if I am only a mutual fund distributor?

Yes. Reviews, SIP follow-ups, nominee and KYC gaps, prospects and your ARN and NISM renewals are all relevant. The advisory-specific records, such as risk profile and agreements, are set up only if you need them.

Does it help me meet regulator record requirements?

It keeps the records in one place with dates and gaps flagged. What the rules require of you depends on your registration, so confirm that with your compliance adviser.

Can clients see their own records?

Only if you choose and set it up that way. By default, only your team sees client records, by role.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and by client, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.

How soon will I see something useful?

The reviews due list, stopped SIPs and missing nominee list need only your existing client data, so we aim to have them first. Fee and income views become reliable once fee terms are recorded.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of advisors, clients and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your practice runs.
