How it runs today

Most studios track bugs well and everything around them loosely.

The game has a design document, a task board and a build server. The business around it, meaning publisher milestones, vendor art, live event calendar, support and spend, sits in chats and spreadsheets.

The market is crowded. India has approximately 1,888 gaming companies employing more than 130,000 people, and most studios build for mobile stores that take a 30 percent commission, where being found without a paid budget takes luck.

Players expect more than a launch. Mobile audiences expect complex live-ops systems, a production load many indie studios cannot yet sustain, so a calendar of events and fixes matters as much as the first build.

What most game studios run on

  • Game engine and build server
  • Bug tracker or task board
  • Spreadsheet of milestones and vendor payments
  • Chat groups for art and QA
  • Analytics dashboards for each title

Each tool is good at one job. None shows whether the next publisher payment is at risk, which vendor is late or whether the live event can ship.

14 problems, one by one

Where a game studio loses time, money and players.

These are the situations studio founders and producers describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Publishers

    Publisher milestones and payments drift

    What it looks like

    A publisher pays on milestones. The definition of "done" for milestone three is in an email from March. The build is delivered, the publisher asks for changes and payment waits.

    What it costs you

    Cash stuck for weeks, and a studio that pays salaries before the milestone money arrives.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each milestone records its deliverables, acceptance criteria, due date, the publisher's feedback and the payment tied to it. Verity shows which milestones are at risk and how much money depends on them.

    See the screen
  2. 02Production

    Scope grows and the design document is out of date

    What it looks like

    Features are added in meetings and chats. The design document describes a different game from the one in the build.

    What it costs you

    A schedule that slips with no clear cause and a team that argues about what was agreed.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each feature is a record with its description, owner, size and status. Changes need a recorded decision, and the milestone shows what has been added and removed.

    See the screen
  3. 03QA

    Builds and test passes are tracked in chats

    What it looks like

    Three builds went out this week. Testers do not know which one contains which fix, and a regression test is run on an old build.

    What it costs you

    Bugs reported twice, a bad build sent to a publisher and release days lost.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each build is a record with its version, contents, test results and who approved it. Testers see which fixes to verify, and a build cannot be marked ready without passing the checks you set.

    See the screen
  4. 04QA

    The bug list has no priority that people agree on

    What it looks like

    Two thousand open bugs. Nobody can say which five block the release or which can wait.

    What it costs you

    Engineers fixing minor issues while a crash that hits many players waits.

    How Verity fixes it

    Bugs carry severity, how many players or testers hit them and a release target. Verity shows release blockers and bugs by area and age.

    See the screen
  5. 05Vendors

    Art and audio vendors deliver late and in the wrong format

    What it looks like

    An outsourcing studio delivers 3D characters. Some miss the polygon budget and the lead artist asks for rework. Payment was promised on delivery.

    What it costs you

    Schedule slips, rework disputes and bills that do not match what was accepted.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each asset request has a brief, spec, due date and status through review. Verity lists deliveries late or rejected and compares bills with accepted work.

    See the screen
  6. 06Live ops

    Live events and content drops are missed

    What it looks like

    A weekend event needs new content, a store item and a notice. The item is not ready on Friday and the event goes out thin.

    What it costs you

    Players who see a poor event stop coming back. Mobile audiences expect complex live-ops systems, and a missed event is visible at once.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each event is a record with a date, its content, store items, tests and a sign-off. Verity lists events with open tasks and counts down to the day.

    See the screen
  7. 07Live ops

    Player support and bug reports are scattered

    What it looks like

    Players report issues in reviews, forums and support emails. A refund request and a crash report sit in the same inbox.

    What it costs you

    Slow replies, repeated reports of the same issue and no link between players' words and the bugs engineers fix.

    How Verity fixes it

    Player issues become tickets linked to known bugs. Verity shows how many reports each bug has and which players were promised a fix or a refund, and the team sends the replies.

    See the screen
  8. 08Money

    Monetisation numbers are read late

    What it looks like

    Revenue per paying user, retention and store fees sit in different dashboards. A pricing change is judged weeks later.

    What it costs you

    Decisions made on gut. With both big stores taking 30 percent and average revenue per paying user in non-real-money games at about USD 3, small changes matter.

    How Verity fixes it

    Key numbers for each title are recorded weekly beside releases and events, so a change can be read against what shipped. Detailed analytics stay in your tools.

    See the screen
  9. 09People

    Crunch is invisible until people leave

    What it looks like

    Before a milestone the team works late for three weeks. Nobody records the hours, and two people resign after launch.

    What it costs you

    Lost people in a market short of specialist skills, and a studio that repeats the pattern each milestone.

    How Verity fixes it

    Hours worked and load per person are recorded against tasks. Verity shows who is over capacity for how many weeks, so a producer can cut scope or move work before people burn out.

    See the screen
  10. 10Vendors

    Outsourced work and freelancers are paid from memory

    What it looks like

    Freelance artists and sound designers are paid per task. Some tasks were approved, some reworked, and the tracker disagrees with the chat.

    What it costs you

    Overpayment, delayed payment and unhappy freelancers.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each paid task shows its approval and the amount due. Verity lists approved work not yet paid and rework outstanding.

    See the screen
  11. 11Compliance

    Store rules, ratings and children's data

    What it looks like

    A store update asks for a new data disclosure. The game collects age and device data, and some players are children. Nobody owns the checklist.

    What it costs you

    A rejected build, a delayed release or a complaint about children's data. India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, and most duties start to apply over the following 12 to 18 months.

    How Verity fixes it

    Store requirements, age ratings and data disclosures are a checklist with owners and dates for each title. Verity shows what is open before a submission.

    See the screen
  12. 12Publishers

    Publisher recoupment and revenue share are hard to follow

    What it looks like

    A publisher advances money against future revenue. Revenue statements arrive monthly and nobody checks that deductions are right.

    What it costs you

    Money lost in deductions you never questioned, and surprise when recoupment is slower than expected.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each publisher deal records the advance, share, deductions allowed and statements received. Verity compares statements with the terms and lists items to query.

    See the screen
  13. 13Money

    User acquisition spend is not tied to results

    What it looks like

    A campaign spends ₹3 lakh in a week. Installs come in, but nobody joins the spend to the players who stayed.

    What it costs you

    Budgets burnt on channels that bring players who never return. Without paid acquisition, discovery on the stores is hard, so spend matters.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each campaign records spend, dates and the result figures you enter. Verity shows cost per install and per retained player by channel and title.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    The founders carry every title in their heads

    What it looks like

    With two live games and one in development, the founders remember which release is blocked, which milestone is due and which vendor is slow.

    What it costs you

    A studio that cannot add a third title or a second team without losing context.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each morning Verity shows what needs a decision across titles, each item with an owner. A second team or studio becomes a location in the same system.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a gaming studio has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules and your publishers' contracts depend on in order.

  • Online money games and what is permitted

    What is expected

    The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 prohibits online money games, whether of skill or chance, and sets no licensing route. Online social games without monetary stakes and qualifying e-sports are permitted. The date it took effect was reported differently by different sources, so confirm it.

    How Verity helps

    Each title records its game type and monetisation, so you can show that real-money features are not offered.

    Mondaq

  • Store rules and commission

    What is expected

    Both major app stores take a 30 percent commission and set their own rules on data disclosure, age ratings and content. A build can be rejected for missing items.

    How Verity helps

    Store requirements are a checklist per title with owners and dates, and submissions record what was declared.

    Business Today

  • Players' personal data, including children

    What is expected

    India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, and most duties start to apply over the following 12 to 18 months. Games with child players may need extra care and parental consent.

    How Verity helps

    Data collected by each title, notices and consent records are listed with owners, and access to player data is by role.

    AZB Partners

  • Publisher, vendor and freelancer contracts

    What is expected

    Publisher deals set milestones, advances and revenue shares, and vendor contracts set specifications and acceptance. Rights to the work depend on what the contract says.

    How Verity helps

    Terms sit on each deal and vendor record and are compared with deliveries, statements and payments.

Rules and store requirements change often and differ by title and market. Confirm current requirements with your lawyer and the store's own documents.

Questions

Questions game studio owners ask.

We already use a bug tracker and a task board. Why add this?

Keep them. Verity does not replace them. It joins them to the business around the game: milestones and payments, vendors, live events, spend and compliance. We agree during setup what stays in your tools.

Will artists and engineers use it?

It only works if recording takes seconds, so we set it up around the boards they already use. They get a clear view of their week, the feature or asset spec and what the milestone depends on.

Can it track bugs well enough to replace my tracker?

It holds bugs, severity and status and links them to builds and player reports. If you prefer a specialist tracker, we agree how status reaches Verity so everything is in one view.

Does it handle live ops?

Yes. Events are records with content, store items, tests and sign-off, and a calendar. Verity does not run the live game or send notices to players.

Can it help with publisher payments?

Yes. Milestones, acceptance, payments and revenue statements are on one view, with deductions compared with the agreed terms. It does not make or receive payments.

We outsource a lot. Can it manage vendors?

Yes. Each request has a spec, due date, review and payment due, and late or rejected items are listed.

Who can see what?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and by title, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How long before I see results?

The release blockers list, milestone risk view and vendor delivery list need only your existing records, so they are the first things we aim to have working. Spend and live ops trends need a few weeks of use.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of users and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your studio runs.
