How it runs today

Most chemical plants run well on the floor and loosely on paper.

The process itself is usually tightly controlled by an experienced chemist or plant head. What is loosely held is the record trail around it: a batch sheet on a clipboard, a lab result on a separate sheet, lot numbers of raw material in a stores register, and a dispatch note written at the gate.

When a customer queries a drum six weeks later, someone has to dig through three registers to find which raw material lot and which batch it came from. When an inspector asks for the last quarter's waste records, the same hunt starts again.

Compliance adds its own lists. A consent to operate, a hazardous waste authorisation, annual returns, safety data sheets for each product, labels and transport papers. Each has a date and an owner that lives in someone's head.

What most chemical manufacturers run on

  • Batch sheets and lab notebooks
  • Stores and weighbridge registers
  • Excel for stock, costing and dues
  • Tally or similar for accounts
  • WhatsApp with customers, transporters and the consultant

Each tool is fine for its own job. Nothing connects a batch to the lot it used, the customer it went to, or the paper that had to travel with it.

15 problems, one by one

Where a chemical plant actually loses money and sleep.

These are the situations plant owners and heads describe again and again. For each: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Production

    Yield drifts and nobody sees it until the month end

    What it looks like

    A batch that used to give 94 kg per 100 kg of input now gives 90. It happens over three weeks, across two shifts, with a new operator on one and a worn agitator on the other. The monthly report shows it after the material is already gone.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a product with ₹120 per kg of input and 4 percent yield loss on 40 tonnes a month loses about ₹1.9 lakh a month.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each batch records inputs and output against the standard recipe. Verity shows yield by product, shift and reactor, flags a batch that falls outside your range on the day it closes, and lists the batches behind the drift.

  2. 02Traceability

    Nobody can say which raw material lot went into which batch

    What it looks like

    Three suppliers send the same solvent. Drums are issued from stores by whichever is nearest. A customer reports a colour issue and the plant cannot say whether it came from the March lot or the April one.

    What it costs you

    Without lot-to-batch links, one complaint can mean holding every batch made in that period. The real cost is the stock you stop selling while you search.

    How Verity fixes it

    Raw material lots are received with supplier, date and test result. Each batch records which lots were issued to it. Verity shows, both ways, every batch a lot went into and every lot a batch used.

    See the screen
  3. 03Quality

    Certificates of analysis are late, retyped or wrong

    What it looks like

    The lab result is on a sheet. The dispatch clerk retypes it into a certificate at the last minute while the truck waits. A decimal goes wrong, or a result from the previous batch is reused.

    What it costs you

    A wrong certificate is a returned consignment, a credit note and a customer who now double checks everything you send.

    How Verity fixes it

    Lab results are recorded against the batch and drawn into the certificate from that record, not retyped. Dispatch is held until the batch is released by the person who has the right to release it.

    See the screen
  4. 04Quality

    A complaint arrives and the retained sample is nowhere

    What it looks like

    A buyer says the last drum failed their incoming test. The retained sample for that batch should be on a shelf, but the shelf has no labels. The batch sheet is in a box from last year.

    What it costs you

    A complaint you cannot investigate is one you have to accept. It also costs the regular order that follows.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each complaint is a record linked to the batch, the customer, the dispatch and the retained sample location. The investigation, the finding and any credit are recorded on it, and the open ones are listed by age.

    See the screen
  5. 05Safety

    The safety data sheet is out of date, or did not go with the truck

    What it looks like

    The product formulation changed in July. The safety data sheet on file is from last year. A new customer asks for the current one and a driver arrives at a plant without it.

    What it costs you

    The Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals Rules treat safety data sheets as part of hazard communication. A missing or stale sheet is a compliance gap and a customer trust problem.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each product holds its current safety data sheet as a record with a version and review date. Verity lists sheets due for review, and a dispatch cannot be closed until the current sheet is recorded as sent with it.

    See the screen
  6. 06Dispatch

    Hazardous goods dispatch papers are done in a hurry

    What it looks like

    The tanker is loaded, the label is on, but the transport emergency card, the driver's trained status and the vehicle papers are checked by whoever remembers. A dispatch goes out with one missing.

    What it costs you

    A stopped vehicle on the road, a delayed delivery and in the worst case an unprepared response to a leak.

    How Verity fixes it

    A dispatch checklist per product class: label, emergency card, driver and vehicle details, quantity against order. The gate supervisor signs it off, and Verity records who and when against the dispatch.

    See the screen
  7. 07Stores

    Drums of different products share the same godown

    What it looks like

    Stock has grown in the past quarter. Oxidisers sit near solvents because that is where space was. Nobody has written down which products are held where, or the limit for each area.

    What it costs you

    A storage mistake is a safety risk first and a licence problem next. It also means stock is hard to find when an order comes in.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each storage location is a record with the products allowed and quantity limits you set. Verity shows what is stored where, warns when a receipt would break your own limit, and lists the oldest stock per location.

    See the screen
  8. 08Compliance

    Effluent and hazardous waste records are filled in before the audit

    What it looks like

    Effluent readings, waste generated, waste sent out and manifests live on different sheets. The annual return is due and the person who knows the numbers is on leave.

    What it costs you

    Waste rules in India require records and an annual return to the State Pollution Control Board, and a gap can lead to notices or consent trouble.

    How Verity fixes it

    Daily effluent readings, waste generated and each manifest are recorded as they happen. Verity totals them by month and year so the annual return figures are already on screen, and flags a missing entry the same day.

    See the screen
  9. 09Compliance

    Consent, authorisation and licence dates sit with one person

    What it looks like

    The consent to operate, hazardous waste authorisation, factory licence, boiler certificate and fire clearance each expire on a different date. The consultant knows them. If the consultant changes, nobody does.

    What it costs you

    A lapsed consent can stop production. The cost is the days of lost output and the fee to regularise.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each licence, consent and certificate is a record with number, authority, expiry and owner. Renewal tasks open weeks ahead and carry the documents needed.

    See the screen
  10. 10Equipment

    The reactor stops because a gasket was never replaced

    What it looks like

    A reactor seal leaks during a batch. The spare was not in stores. The vendor is two days away. Planned maintenance is on a whiteboard that was wiped last month.

    What it costs you

    A stopped batch loses material in process and the delivery date with it. Unplanned stops cost more than planned ones.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each reactor, pump, boiler and pressure vessel is an asset record with service history, spares and next due date. Maintenance becomes a scheduled task with a person, and Verity flags overdue ones.

    See the screen
  11. 11Costing

    Raw material prices move and the quote does not

    What it looks like

    A solvent rate goes up 8 percent. Sales quotes are still on last quarter's price list. The cost of a batch includes losses, recovered solvent and fuel that nobody has worked out.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a ₹95 per kg product with ₹70 of material, ₹12 of conversion and a 6 percent hidden loss earns about ₹9 per kg, not the ₹13 the price list suggests.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every purchase records the rate. Verity costs each batch from actual lot rates, yield and conversion, and shows the margin per product and per customer, so a price rise shows up as a changed margin the same week.

    See the screen
  12. 12Money

    Customers pay late and the plant finds out when cash is short

    What it looks like

    Four customers owe more than 90 days. The accounts person chases when asked. Meanwhile the plant buys raw material on shorter terms.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹60 lakh outstanding beyond 60 days, financed at 12 percent, costs about ₹60,000 a month in interest alone. Where you are the seller to a small buyer, the MSME payment rules also matter to them.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each invoice is tracked against the dispatch, due date and customer's usual pay days. Verity lists overdue amounts by customer and age, and creates a follow-up task for the person who owns the relationship. Staff make the call.

    See the screen
  13. 13Orders

    Sales promises a date the plant cannot meet

    What it looks like

    A customer asks for 8 tonnes by Friday. Sales says yes. The plant has one reactor booked for another product until Thursday and the raw material has not arrived.

    What it costs you

    A missed date is a lost customer. Over-promising also forces expensive air freight or overtime to catch up.

    How Verity fixes it

    Orders show required quantity, raw material available, reactor slots and expected finish. The planner sees conflicts before a date is confirmed.

    See the screen
  14. 14People

    Operator training, PPE and permits are not recorded

    What it looks like

    A new operator is moved to a hazardous step on his second week. Nobody remembers if his induction and PPE issue were completed. A hot work permit is a paper slip.

    What it costs you

    A person without the right training in a chemical process is a safety risk and, after an incident, a record you cannot produce.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each person's training, induction, PPE issue and station clearance are records with dates. Permits for hot work and entry are tasks with sign-off, and shifts are set from who is cleared.

    See the screen
  15. 15Owner

    The owner reads the plant through phone calls

    What it looks like

    The owner asks what was made today, what is pending dispatch and who has not paid. Each answer is a different person on a different call. By evening the picture is already old.

    What it costs you

    Decisions run on memory and calls. Small gaps in yield, stock and dues compound before they are seen.

    How Verity fixes it

    One morning screen with production, yield flags, stock below reorder, compliance dates due, dispatches and overdue dues, each item with an owner.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a chemical plant has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Hazardous chemicals rules and safety data sheets

    What is expected

    The Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals Rules, 1989, made under the Environment (Protection) Act, generally cover the hazardous chemicals you make, store or import, including hazard communication through safety data sheets and labels. Importers also have notification and record duties. Check which of your products are covered.

    How Verity helps

    Each product holds its current safety data sheet, version and review date. Storage areas, labels and dispatch checks are records tied to the product.

    Maven Regulatory, MSIHC Rules guide

  • Hazardous waste authorisation, manifests and annual return

    What is expected

    The Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016, generally require authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board, a manifest for waste moved out, records of waste handled, and an annual return on Form 4 by 30 June for the previous year.

    How Verity helps

    Waste generated and each manifest are recorded as they happen and totalled by month and year, so the return figures are on screen.

    Mynd Solution

  • Consent to establish and operate, and effluent conditions

    What is expected

    A chemical unit generally needs consent from the State Pollution Control Board, is placed in a pollution category, and must meet the conditions written in its consent, such as effluent limits. Consents are for a fixed term and are renewed.

    How Verity helps

    The consent is a record with its conditions, expiry and owner. Daily effluent readings and equipment checks sit beside it, and renewal becomes a task weeks ahead.

  • BIS quality control orders

    What is expected

    Some chemicals and polymers had mandatory BIS certification under Quality Control Orders, and 14 such orders were revoked by notifications of 12 November 2025, with further changes reported. Whether your product is covered depends on the current list.

    How Verity helps

    Each product records whether a BIS licence applies, its number and its renewal date, and the list is reviewed when notifications change.

    Chemradar

  • Payment terms for micro and small suppliers

    What is expected

    From 1 April 2024, a buyer that owes a micro or small enterprise generally must pay within the agreed period, capped at 45 days, or the amount is not deductible until paid. This matters when you buy from small suppliers, and when your own customers are small.

    How Verity helps

    Supplier bills show a due date against the 45 day limit, so payments are planned before they become a tax cost.

    SetIndiaBiz

Rules change and differ by state, product and plant size. Confirm current requirements with your consultant or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions plant owners ask.

My operators are not computer people. Will they really record lots and batches?

Only if it takes seconds at the point of work, so we set up entry at the stores window, the reactor and the gate, with a few fields each. We train during a real batch and start with the records that matter most.

Can Verity trace a raw material lot to the customers who received it?

Yes, if lots are recorded at receipt and issued to batches, and batches are linked to dispatches. Verity then shows the chain both ways. It is only as complete as the records made.

Does it replace my accounts software?

No. Your accounts stay where they are. We agree during setup how invoices and payments reach Verity, which then handles lots, batches, dispatches, compliance dates and dues.

Does it file my returns and manifests?

No. It keeps the figures and dates in order and tells the right person what is due. Your consultant or your team still files with the authority.

I make many small products in a few reactors. Will it cope?

Yes. Each product has its own recipe and range, and each batch records the reactor used. Planning shows reactor slots against orders.

Can it hold safety data sheets for every product?

It holds each sheet as a record with version and review date, and records that it was sent with a dispatch. It does not write the sheet for you.

I have two plants. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each is a location in one system, with yield, stock, compliance dates and dues side by side, and customers are the same across both.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so operators, the lab, sales and accounts each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How quickly will I see something useful?

The licence calendar, overdue dues and stock by lot need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. Yield and margin patterns become reliable after a few weeks of recorded batches.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of plants, people and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your plant runs.
