How it runs today

Most gift shops know their festivals well and their numbers badly.

The shop is easy to see. The rakhi rush, the Diwali boxes, the last-minute Valentine’s hamper, the stack of personalised mugs for a wedding. What is hard to see is what sits behind it: which items sold through and which are left, what each hamper really costs to make, which custom order is due tomorrow and which corporate quote is still waiting.

That information exists in pieces. The billing screen knows the sale. Wholesale purchases are on slips and a few are cash. Custom orders are on a pad or a chat. Corporate enquiries sit in an inbox. The owner holds the rest in memory.

The market is modest and seasonal. One 2025 estimate puts India’s gifting market at about USD 816 million, with corporate gifting about 36 percent of it, and local shops the largest physical channel at 28 percent. A shop that knows its season and its buyers has an edge over a website.

What most gift shops run on

  • Billing screen at the counter
  • Wholesale purchase slips and a few cash buys
  • Order pad or WhatsApp for custom and corporate orders
  • Excel or notebook for stock
  • The owner’s memory of what sold last Diwali

Each one does its own job well enough. None of them talk to each other, and nobody has time to join them.

Where a gift shop actually loses money.

These are the situations gift shop owners and managers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Stock

    Festival stock bought on a hunch

    What it looks like

    Raksha Bandhan, Ganesh Chaturthi, Diwali and the wedding season come one after another. You buy rakhis, idols, diyas and gift boxes in the weeks before each. Last year’s best seller is on your mind, but the numbers are not written down.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹2 lakh of festival stock with a quarter unsold is ₹50,000 tied up in items that will sit until next year or sell at a loss.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each item’s sales by week for each festival are recorded. Verity shows last year’s sell-through next to this year’s opening stock and suggests how much to order for each peak, with the supplier’s lead time.

    See the screen
  2. 02Stock

    What is left after the festival

    What it looks like

    The day after Diwali, a corner of the shop holds diyas, string lights and boxes nobody will buy for another year. Someone says they will mark them down. Nobody does, and the stock slowly fades and gets damaged.

    What it costs you

    Seasonal stock loses its value on the day the season ends. Each week you wait costs you more.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity tracks stock remaining after the season by item and value held. It suggests a markdown date, a bundle or a carry-over, and shows what you actually did last year, so the leftover is a decision and not a pile.

    See the screen
  3. 03Stock

    Breakage and shrink in fragile items

    What it looks like

    Glass photo frames, ceramic mugs and decorative items arrive cracked or break on the shelf. A shop assistant moves a box and something rattles. Nobody records which items break and where.

    What it costs you

    Fragile stock bought and never sold, and a pattern nobody sees: the same supplier, the same type of packing, the same shelf.

    How Verity fixes it

    Breakage is logged with a reason in two taps, with the item, supplier and shelf. Verity totals it by supplier and item so a bad pack or a bad shelf shows up, and a credit claim to the supplier has the evidence.

    See the screen
  4. 04Custom orders

    A personalised gift with a deadline and a typo

    What it looks like

    A customer orders a mug with a name and a date, a photo cushion for an anniversary, or a name plate for a house. The details are on a pad. The name is spelled differently on the proof. The order is due the next morning.

    What it costs you

    A wrong or late custom item cannot be resold, and the customer is angry on the one day they needed it. The cost is the item, the redo and the customer.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each custom order is a record with the customer, the item, the exact text, the photo, the proof approved, the due date and the status. Staff see what is waiting for approval, what is in production and what is due today.

    See the screen
  5. 05Custom orders

    Gifts that have to arrive on a day

    What it looks like

    A birthday cake topper and gift must reach a house at 6 pm. A bouquet and a hamper go to an office at 10 am. A delivery boy has three drops and no list, and the customer calls to ask where it is.

    What it costs you

    A missed delivery on an occasion day is a lost customer, and often a refund. Phone calls to find out where things are take up the same hour you need for packing.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each delivery is a task with the address, the time window, the person carrying it and the status. The counter sees what has left, what has arrived and what is late, and the customer’s call is answered from the record.

    See the screen
  6. 06Corporate

    Corporate gifting enquiries answered late

    What it looks like

    An office manager wants fifty Diwali boxes by the 20th. She emails three shops and picks the one that replies first with a clear quote. Yours was in the inbox and you saw it at night.

    What it costs you

    Corporate gifting is about a third of the gifting market in one estimate, and corporate orders are the largest tickets a gift shop sees. They are lost to slow replies and unclear quotes far more often than to price.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each enquiry is a record with an owner, quantity, budget, items, the quote and the next step. Verity lists the ones with no reply and tells the person responsible. Staff still send the quote, and the outcome is recorded.

    See the screen
  7. 07Corporate

    Sample approvals and changes that go round and round

    What it looks like

    A company asks for a sample box, changes the chocolate, adds a logo card, then changes the quantity. Each change is a message in a different chat. By the third round nobody is sure what was agreed.

    What it costs you

    The wrong box goes out, or the right one goes late, or you quietly absorb the cost of changes nobody priced.

    How Verity fixes it

    The order record holds the approved sample, the changes with dates, the final quantity and price, and who approved it. Changes after approval are flagged and priced, so the final bill matches what was agreed.

    See the screen
  8. 08Corporate

    Corporate payments that take months

    What it looks like

    A firm takes the delivery in October and pays after sixty days. The invoice is in the accounts person’s inbox, someone is on leave, and the follow-up is a call you keep postponing.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹3 lakh of corporate invoices outstanding for 60 days is cash you needed to buy next season’s stock.

    How Verity fixes it

    Corporate buyers are accounts with invoices, due dates and balances. Verity shows what is owed and for how long, and gives the owner a task to chase on the schedule you set, with every promise to pay recorded.

    See the screen
  9. 09Hampers

    A hamper whose cost nobody has added up

    What it looks like

    A Diwali hamper holds a box, dry fruits, a candle, a diya set, chocolates and a card. Each was bought at a different rate from a different supplier. The price on the shelf was set by gut feel and a look at the competitor.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: if a hamper sells at ₹1,499 and the parts cost ₹1,050, packing ₹120 and a ₹90 card and ribbon, the hamper earns about ₹240, not the ₹450 you thought.

    How Verity fixes it

    A hamper is a record built from its parts, each at its latest purchase cost. Verity shows the cost, the margin at the price you set, and what changes if the dry fruit rate moves, and takes parts out of stock when a hamper is packed.

    See the screen
  10. 10Hampers

    Price tags, bargaining and discounts nobody approved

    What it looks like

    A customer asks for 20 percent off a set. A friend of the owner gets a special price. A salesperson matches a nearby shop. Each is a small human decision, and none is written down.

    What it costs you

    Margin lost and a door open to misuse, with no way to see patterns by person, item or week.

    How Verity fixes it

    Discounts above a limit need a one-tap approval with a reason. Verity totals them by person, item and week, and shows which items are being sold below the margin you set.

    See the screen
  11. 11Money

    Different tax rates on items that sit in one bill

    What it looks like

    Handicrafts and paper gift boxes moved to a lower GST rate from 22 September 2025, while other items such as many decorative goods may sit at a different rate. A hamper holds items at more than one rate, and staff are unsure how to show it.

    What it costs you

    A wrong rate on a bill, confused customers and hours of correction by your accountant. Rates and classifications can change again, and sources disagree on some items.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each item carries its tax category, and a mixed bill is split by line as it is made. A hamper records its parts, so your accountant can see what was in it. A rate change is one update to an item’s setup.

    See the screen
  12. 12Customers

    The birthday and anniversary customers forget until it is too late

    What it looks like

    A man buys an anniversary gift every October for his wife. A woman sends a hamper to her brother each Rakhi. You know them by face. The staff member who knew the occasions has left, and nobody calls before the date.

    What it costs you

    Occasion buyers are the surest repeat sales in a gift shop. When no one reminds them, they buy from whichever shop or website comes first.

    How Verity fixes it

    A customer record holds the occasions they shop for, the usual budget and what they bought before. Verity lists customers with an occasion coming in the next two weeks, and a task goes to the person who knows them.

    See the screen
  13. 13Customers

    Wedding and event orders in bulk, with a long tail of details

    What it looks like

    A family orders return gifts for two hundred guests, a welcome hamper for sixty and a few special items for the couple. The details come over several weeks and several chats.

    What it costs you

    Large orders go wrong in the details: the wrong count, the wrong date, a part missing. The family remembers.

    How Verity fixes it

    The order is a record with phases, quantities by item, the delivery date, the advance and the balance. Staff see what is packed, what is waiting on a supplier and what is due, and the family’s message is answered from the record.

    See the screen
  14. 14Money

    Day-end takings do not tie to the bills

    What it looks like

    At closing, cash, card and UPI are counted against the billing report and are off by a few hundred rupees. Nobody can say whether a bill was missed, a refund was given or an advance was recorded in the wrong place.

    What it costs you

    Small daily gaps compound, and the owner stops trusting the numbers on the screen. Advances on custom orders make it worse.

    How Verity fixes it

    The day’s sales and advances by payment mode are compared with what was collected. A mismatch is flagged with the shift and amount, and the person closing records the reason.

    See the screen
  15. 15Owner

    Seasonal staff, a second counter and an owner who is the system

    What it looks like

    You hire extra hands for the festival rush and train them in an afternoon. A second counter in a mall or a stall at an exhibition keeps its own sheet. You know what is low, what is due and who owes you. You are also packing.

    What it costs you

    The business cannot grow past what one person can hold in their head, and stock sits idle at one counter while another runs out.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each morning Verity shows a short list of what needs a decision, each with an owner. Counters and stalls are locations in one system with stock, sales and orders side by side, and seasonal staff open only what their role needs.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a gift shop has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so your accountant is not rebuilding them from a bill book.

  • GST on gift items

    What is expected

    From 22 September 2025, handicrafts and paper gift boxes generally moved from 12 to 5 percent, and some other gift items are at 18 percent. Sources disagree on a few items such as toys, candles and hampers, so check the tariff line for each.

    How Verity helps

    Each item carries its tax category and a mixed bill is split by line, so the split your accountant needs is already clean. A hamper records its parts.

    taxo.online, 2025

  • Selling at or below MRP

    What is expected

    For packed goods, the Legal Metrology rules generally bar a dealer from selling above the printed retail price, with narrow exceptions for a valid tax revision.

    How Verity helps

    Each item records its MRP and the price charged, and a bill above it is flagged before it closes.

    IndiaLaw, 2025

  • Customer data and consent

    What is expected

    Phone numbers, addresses and the occasions people shop for are personal data. India’s data protection rules were notified on 13 November 2025, and most duties start in phases, with the main ones about 18 months later.

    How Verity helps

    Customer data sits in one permissioned place, with who viewed or changed it recorded, instead of in personal phones and notebooks.

    AZB Partners, 2025

  • Food items inside hampers

    What is expected

    If your hampers include packed food such as dry fruits or chocolates, the food safety rules for sellers of food may apply to you. Details depend on your turnover and category, so check with your adviser.

    How Verity helps

    Any registration or licence is stored as a record with its number and renewal date, and food parts in a hamper are tracked by batch expiry.

  • Shops and establishments registration and staff records

    What is expected

    Most states require a shops and establishments registration and records of working hours, weekly offs and leave. Details differ by state, so check your own.

    How Verity helps

    The registration is stored with its renewal date and owner, and shifts, attendance and leave are recorded against each person, including seasonal staff.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions gift shop owners ask.

My business is mostly a few festival weeks. Is it worth setting up?

The peak weeks are where the leaks cost the most, and the quiet months are when you plan for them. We set it up in four weeks so it is working before your next peak, and the season history it builds is what makes next year’s buying easier.

Does Verity replace my billing system?

No. Your billing screen keeps taking bills and payments. We agree during setup how those bills reach Verity, which then does the stock, supplier, order and customer work around them.

Can it handle personalised orders?

Yes. Each custom order is a record with the exact text, the photo, the proof, the due time and the status, so nothing depends on a pad or a chat.

Can it cost a hamper?

Yes. A hamper is built from its parts at their latest purchase cost, so a rate rise on one part shows up as a changed margin on the hamper.

Can it take online orders?

No. If you sell through a marketplace or a social page today, that keeps working. Verity makes sure every order and enquiry has an owner and a next step.

I have a shop and a mall kiosk. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each is a location in one system, with stock, sales and orders side by side. Stock can move between them against a recorded transfer.

I hire extra hands for the festival. Can they use it?

Yes. A seasonal person gets a role that opens only what they need, and you switch it off when the season ends.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so counter staff, packers, managers and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of counters, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your shop runs.
