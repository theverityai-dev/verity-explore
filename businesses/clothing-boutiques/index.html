How it runs today

A boutique runs on trust, memory and a very good tailor.

Customers arrive with a picture, a date and a budget. The owner chooses fabric, notes measurements, takes an advance and promises a day. That one conversation holds the whole order, and most of it is written on a slip, a notebook page or a chat message.

Then the order is handed on: fabric to a tailor, embroidery to a karigar, a trial date to the customer. Each step is a promise made over the phone. When the tailor has a backlog, the boutique finds out on the delivery day, in front of the customer.

The ethnicwear market is still mostly unorganised: industry experts put the unorganised share at 80 to 85% in 2025. That means many boutiques compete with each other and with large chains on the same festivals and weddings, and the boutique's edge is service that does not slip.

What most boutiques run on

  • Order slips and a notebook
  • Photos and chats on WhatsApp and Instagram
  • A tailor's register
  • A cash drawer and UPI
  • The owner's memory

Each one works while the shop is quiet. In a wedding fortnight the owner is the only thing holding them together.

14 problems, one by one

Where a clothing boutique actually loses money.

These are the situations boutique owners describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Orders

    Order details live in chats and a notebook

    What it looks like

    A customer wants a blouse with a deep back, specific sleeve length and a hook at the side. The owner writes half of it on a slip, sends the photo to the tailor, and remembers the rest. A week later the tailor asks about the neckline.

    What it costs you

    Re-work, wasted fabric and a customer who thinks you did not listen. Example arithmetic: three remakes a month at ₹1,500 of labour and fabric is ₹54,000 a year.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each order is a record with the client, the piece, the fabric, the design notes, photos, the measurements and the promised date. The tailor sees the same record, and a change is a dated note, not a new chat.

    See the screen
  2. 02Money

    The advance was taken, the balance was not

    What it looks like

    A customer pays ₹5,000 on a ₹14,000 outfit. The piece is ready, the customer collects it on her way to a function and says she will pay the rest tomorrow. Tomorrow comes late.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ten such cases a month averaging ₹4,000 is ₹40,000 outstanding at any time, and some of it is never collected.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every order shows price, advance, balance and a status. Verity lists orders ready for pickup with the balance due and tells the person at the counter to collect it before the piece leaves.

    See the screen
  3. 03Orders

    Delivery dates are promised before the tailor is asked

    What it looks like

    The owner promises Friday because the customer needs it for Saturday. The tailor already has nine pieces due that week. Nobody checks, and on Friday evening the piece is half done.

    What it costs you

    A broken promise to a customer who tells her friends, and a tailor who works through the night and makes mistakes.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each tailor has a load of pieces due by day. Verity shows capacity before the date is promised, flags orders at risk two days out, and assigns a task to tell the customer early.

    See the screen
  4. 04Fabric

    Fabric goes to tailors and nobody knows what came back

    What it looks like

    Six metres of silk goes to a tailor for a lehenga. The piece comes back with 40 centimetres left over, or none, or a different length of lining. There is no record of what was given, what was used, and what the tailor should return.

    What it costs you

    Fabric is the biggest cost in a custom piece. Example arithmetic: 30 centimetres unaccounted on 40 orders a month, at ₹500 a metre, is ₹6,000 a month.

    How Verity fixes it

    Fabric given out is a record against the order and the tailor, with length and rate. When the piece returns, the leftover is entered and the difference is shown. Verity totals fabric held by each tailor.

    See the screen
  5. 05Fabric

    Tailor and karigar payments are argued at the end of the month

    What it looks like

    A tailor says he stitched 22 pieces and the boutique's book says 19. A karigar's embroidery job was redone and nobody agrees who pays. Rates were agreed on the phone.

    What it costs you

    Hours of arguing and a skilled tailor who may leave for the next boutique. Losing a good tailor is one of the biggest risks in the trade.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job has a rate, a quantity and a status, and rework is recorded with a reason. Verity builds each tailor's statement from completed jobs, so the month-end conversation uses the same list.

    See the screen
  6. 06Stock

    A one-off piece is sold twice

    What it looks like

    A single sample piece is posted on Instagram. A customer asks for it in the shop on Sunday, and another reserves it by message on Monday. Both are told it is available.

    What it costs you

    One disappointed customer and a rushed remake that costs more than the piece earned.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each ready piece is a record with a status: on display, reserved, sold. A reservation holds it with the client's name and an expiry. Verity shows staff the status before they promise it.

    See the screen
  7. 07Sales

    Instagram and WhatsApp enquiries go unanswered

    What it looks like

    A customer sends a photo from a story asking for a price and a delivery date. The reply comes six hours later or never. Details of who asked for what are on two phones.

    What it costs you

    These are often the highest-intent leads a boutique gets. A slow reply sends the buyer to another seller.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each enquiry is a record with an owner, the piece asked about, a quote and a next step. Verity lists enquiries with no reply and assigns them. Staff still reply to the customer, and the outcome is recorded.

    See the screen
  8. 08Clients

    Measurements are lost, and clients change size

    What it looks like

    A bride ordered her sister's blouse two years ago. The old measurements are on a slip that has gone. Another client has lost weight and the notebook's numbers no longer fit.

    What it costs you

    A fitting that could have been avoided, a remake, and a repeat client who starts to feel like a stranger.

    How Verity fixes it

    Measurements are stored on the client record with the date taken and who took them. Verity flags measurements older than a limit you set and shows the last piece made for each client.

    See the screen
  9. 09Stock

    Ready pieces age while the owner is busy with orders

    What it looks like

    Ready-to-wear kurtas hang on the rail through three festivals. The owner is busy with custom orders and nobody marks them down until the season is over.

    What it costs you

    Vector Consulting Group's 2026 study found about 24% of on-shelf apparel and footwear stock past its optimal selling window. A boutique has less room to carry it than a chain.

    How Verity fixes it

    Days on rail, price and views are tracked for each piece. Verity proposes a markdown for pieces past your limit and shows the margin after each step, with a one-tap approval for the owner.

    See the screen
  10. 10Money

    No one knows what a custom piece really costs

    What it looks like

    A heavy embroidered lehenga is priced from what the customer says she can pay. Fabric, embroidery, stitching, lining, packing and two trial visits are not added up. The piece looked profitable.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹38,000 sale, ₹22,000 fabric and embroidery, ₹7,000 stitching and lining, ₹2,500 rework and packing leaves ₹6,500 before rent and the owner's time.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each order carries its fabric, labour and rework costs as they are recorded. Verity shows the margin on each order and on each type of piece, so the next quote starts from real cost.

    See the screen
  11. 11Peaks

    Wedding and festival weeks overwhelm a small team

    What it looks like

    Four weeks before a wedding season, orders pile in. Trials, final fittings and deliveries all land in the same ten days. Hours go to chasing, not to selling.

    What it costs you

    Peaks are where a boutique makes much of its year. A missed date in these weeks is a customer lost for the next season.

    How Verity fixes it

    Last year's orders by week are laid against this year's tailor capacity. Verity shows the week the load goes over capacity, lists orders to move up, and assigns each order a next step.

    See the screen
  12. 12Clients

    Fitting visits clash and customers do not turn up

    What it looks like

    Three trial visits are fixed for the same hour. A customer comes without calling, and the tailor is out. Another does not arrive, and the slot is wasted.

    What it costs you

    Staff time spent waiting, and rushed fittings that lead to alterations.

    How Verity fixes it

    Trial dates are on the order and on the shop's schedule, with the tailor and the room marked. Verity flags clashes, assigns a reminder task to staff, and records if a customer did not come.

    See the screen
  13. 13People

    The business depends on one tailor or one designer

    What it looks like

    The head tailor knows every client's preferences and the shop's finishing standard. When he is on leave or resigns, quality drifts and delivery slips.

    What it costs you

    Losing a master tailor can stop custom orders for weeks and cost the clients who came for his finishing.

    How Verity fixes it

    Finishing standards, client preferences and past pieces sit on records, not in one person's head. Verity shows the load by tailor and the pieces only one tailor has worked on, so you can cross-train before it hurts.

    See the screen
  14. 14Pricing

    GST on occasion wear changes at ₹2,500 per piece

    What it looks like

    Many occasion pieces are priced well above ₹2,500, and some ready pieces sit just below. A small discount, or a price set at a round figure, can change the rate. The invoice is raised without anyone checking.

    What it costs you

    Since 22 September 2025, apparel is generally taxed at a lower rate up to ₹2,500 per piece and at 18% above. A wrong rate repeated on many bills becomes a correction and a notice.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity shows each piece's price band against the ₹2,500 mark and flags any discount or price that moves a piece across it, so the owner and the accountant can agree the right treatment first. It does not file returns.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a boutique has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • GST rate by price per piece

    What is expected

    From 22 September 2025, apparel is generally taxed at 5% up to ₹2,500 per piece and 18% above, tested on the sale value and not automatically on the printed price. Many occasion pieces sit above the line. Your accountant decides how a discount or a custom piece should be treated.

    How Verity helps

    Each piece records its price, discount and final value, and Verity flags those near or across the line so the rate can be agreed before billing. Verity does not file returns.

    Outlook Business, PTI, September 2025: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/garments-priced-above-25k-to-be-costlier-will-hurt-middle-class-affordability-industry-bodies; TaxAJ commentary

  • Paying small makers within the allowed days

    What is expected

    If you buy fabric or garments from micro or small manufacturers, Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act generally makes the expense deductible in the year only if paid within the 15 day default or the agreed term up to 45 days. Traders are generally outside the rule. Confirm with your accountant.

    How Verity helps

    Each supplier record carries its type and terms, and Verity lists bills near their deadline.

    ClearTax on Section 43B(h)

  • Advances and written terms for custom work

    What is expected

    Custom orders involve advances and promised dates. A written order slip with the price, advance and delivery terms helps if a dispute reaches a consumer forum. This is general practice, not a rule we have checked against the Consumer Protection Act.

    How Verity helps

    The order record holds the price, the advance, the promised date and any change agreed, with dates, so there is one version of the terms.

  • Local trade and shop registrations

    What is expected

    A boutique generally needs its municipal trade licence and Shops and Establishments registration, which differ by city and state and have their own renewal dates.

    How Verity helps

    Each registration is stored with its renewal date and owner, so a renewal becomes a task weeks before it lapses.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions boutique owners ask.

I have two staff and three tailors. Is this too much?

The leaks are the same in a small boutique and there is less time to catch them. We set up only the order stages, tailors and screens you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Will my tailor really use it?

He does not have to. The tailor's job is marked complete at your counter when the piece comes back, and he sees his own load and statement on a simple screen if you choose to give it to him.

Can it keep my clients' measurements?

Yes. Measurements are stored on the client record with the date and who took them, and a flag appears when they are older than the limit you set.

Does Verity take orders from Instagram?

No. Staff reply to the customer. Verity records the enquiry, assigns it, tracks the quote and the outcome, and makes sure someone owes the next step.

How does it help with fabric?

Fabric given to a tailor is recorded against the order, with length and rate. When the piece returns, the leftover is entered and the difference is shown, so you know what each tailor holds.

Can it tell me what a custom piece really cost?

Yes, once fabric, labour and rework are recorded against the order. Each order shows its margin, and the system groups it by type of piece so your next quote starts from actual cost.

Does it replace my billing?

No. Your billing or invoice book keeps working. We agree during setup how orders, advances and payments reach Verity.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so counter staff, the head tailor and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of people and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your boutique runs.
