Verity for clinics

A clinic lives on repeat visits. Most are lost between one appointment and the next.

A clinic is a doctor, a room, a front desk and a register of patients who should come back. The leaks are quiet: the follow-up nobody called, the empty slot after a no-show, the injection that expired in the cupboard, the old report nobody could find. Verity connects the patient record, the schedule, the stock and the desk so these show up while they can still be fixed.

How it runs today

Most clinics are run by the doctor, one receptionist and a lot of memory.

The doctor sees patients. The receptionist books slots on a diary or a phone, writes up bills, finds old files and answers the phone. Medicines and consumables are counted when someone notices. Follow-ups are told to the patient in the room and remembered by nobody.

That works at ten patients a day. At forty, with a second doctor or a second branch, things slip: files are not found, a patient due for review in two weeks never comes, a dressing pack runs out mid-clinic, and the owner learns the month's numbers when the accountant asks.

Digital records are spreading. As of July 2026, official figures say over 93 crore ABHA numbers have been created and 2.72 lakh health facilities have adopted ABDM-enabled software, which means patients increasingly expect their records to be orderly.

What most clinics run on

  • Paper files or a simple EMR
  • Appointment diary or phone calendar
  • Billing slips or a small billing app
  • Excel or a notebook for stock
  • WhatsApp with patients and suppliers

Each works on its own. None tells you who has not come back or what will run out before Saturday.

14 problems, one by one

Where a clinic loses patients, time and money.

These are the situations clinic doctors, managers and receptionists describe. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Patients

    Follow-ups are told but never tracked

    What it looks like

    The doctor says, come back in two weeks. The patient nods. Nobody writes a date anywhere but the case sheet, and the case sheet is in a cabinet. Three weeks later the patient has not returned and nobody knows.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: if 400 patients a month are asked to return and one in four does not, at ₹600 a visit that is about ₹60,000 of revisit income a month, plus patients whose treatment was never completed.

    How Verity fixes it

    A follow-up is a record with a date, a patient and a reason, created at the visit. Verity lists follow-ups due and overdue each day, and the desk calls. The outcome is recorded: booked, declined or unreachable.

    See the screen
  2. 02Schedule

    No-shows leave empty slots

    What it looks like

    Six of twenty-eight booked patients do not turn up on Thursday. The doctor sits idle at 11 and rushes at 5. Nobody called to confirm the day before.

    What it costs you

    Published studies of outpatient clinics report no-show rates from about 12 percent to 42 percent, mostly outside India. At even 15 percent, an empty slot is a fee you do not earn.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each slot has a status. Verity lists tomorrow's appointments for confirmation calls, marks no-shows with a reason, and shows no-show share by day, doctor and booking source, so the pattern can be addressed.

    See the screen
  3. 03Schedule

    The walk-in queue and the booked queue clash

    What it looks like

    Booked patients wait behind walk-ins, and walk-ins leave when they see the crowd. The receptionist cannot say who is next, or how long the wait is.

    What it costs you

    Patients who wait too long often do not come back, and a doctor with uneven rush hours earns less per hour.

    How Verity fixes it

    Walk-ins and bookings sit on the same day sheet with a time, a status and a waiting time. The desk sees the real queue, and the doctor's day is balanced across the hours.

    See the screen
  4. 04Records

    Old records cannot be found

    What it looks like

    A patient returns after eight months and the file cannot be found. Or the file is found and the last prescription is unreadable. The doctor starts again from the patient's memory.

    What it costs you

    Time lost in the room, repeated tests, and a poorer visit. Records also matter when a patient or a regulator asks for them.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each patient has one record with visits, reports, prescriptions and attachments in date order. The desk searches by name or number. Verity organises what is recorded; the doctor's notes remain the doctor's.

    See the screen
  5. 05Records

    Duplicate patients and wrong numbers

    What it looks like

    The same patient is registered twice with different spellings. A phone number is wrong, so the reminder goes to a stranger.

    What it costs you

    History splits, bills go to the wrong account, and a follow-up call reaches nobody.

    How Verity fixes it

    Registration flags a possible duplicate for a person to merge or keep, and contact details are confirmed at each visit. One record, one history.

    See the screen
  6. 06Compliance

    Privacy: anyone at the desk can open anything

    What it looks like

    The receptionist, the billing clerk and a part-time intern all open the same file. A relative asks the desk about a patient's results, and the desk is not sure what it may say.

    What it costs you

    Health data is high risk. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 14 November 2025, and most duties apply 18 months later. Loose access is a legal and trust risk.

    How Verity fixes it

    Access is set by role. The desk sees appointments and dues; only the doctor sees clinical notes. Every view and change leaves a trail of who and when, and consent given by the patient is recorded on the record.

    See the screen
  7. 07Records

    Teleconsultation records are on WhatsApp

    What it looks like

    A follow-up is done over a call. The prescription goes by WhatsApp. The consent, the call time and the advice are in no file.

    What it costs you

    The 2020 Telemedicine Practice Guidelines expect consent to be recorded in the patient's record and logs, prescriptions and records to be kept. Scattered chats make that hard to show.

    How Verity fixes it

    A teleconsultation is a visit on the same patient record, with the mode, the time, the consent and the attached prescription. The doctor writes the note; Verity files it.

    See the screen
  8. 08Stock

    Consumables run out in the middle of clinic

    What it looks like

    Dressing packs, syringes, gloves, vaccines and test strips are counted by eye. The clinic runs out of a vaccine on a Saturday. Another vial expires in the fridge unnoticed.

    What it costs you

    A procedure postponed is a patient who may go elsewhere. Expired stock is bought and thrown away.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each consumable has a reorder point and a batch with expiry. Use is deducted when a procedure is recorded, and Verity lists what runs short before the supplier's next delivery and what expires in 30 and 60 days.

    See the screen
  9. 09Suppliers

    Supplier bills and rates are not checked

    What it looks like

    A pharmacy supplier delivers 20 boxes and bills 24. A vaccine distributor changes a rate. The invoice is paid because the doctor is busy.

    What it costs you

    Overpayment is invisible and compounds across months.

    How Verity fixes it

    Receipts are recorded against the order, compared with the invoice and rates are shown over time. A mismatch is flagged before the bill is paid.

    See the screen
  10. 10Money

    Bills, dues and package balances slip

    What it looks like

    A patient on a physiotherapy or dental package of six visits pays for four. A consultation fee is waived at the door. At the end of the day the till does not match the register.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ten unrecorded waivers of ₹400 in a month is ₹4,000, and unpaid package balances add up quietly.

    How Verity fixes it

    Packages show visits used and paid. Waivers need a reason and a name. The day's collections by mode are compared with the visits recorded, and a gap is flagged with the shift.

    See the screen
  11. 11Money

    Doctor shares and visiting doctors

    What it looks like

    A visiting specialist is paid a share of each consultation. At month end the share is calculated by hand from slips, and the doctor disputes it.

    What it costs you

    Disputes strain relationships with the doctors who bring patients.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each visit is tagged to a doctor and a fee, so the share comes from the same records. The statement is a view, not a spreadsheet to build. Verity does not run payroll.

    See the screen
  12. 12Equipment

    Room and equipment time sits idle

    What it looks like

    A procedure room is used two hours a day. An ECG machine or autoclave is serviced when it fails. The vendor's number is on a sticker.

    What it costs you

    Idle rooms are rent paid for nothing, and an unplanned failure cancels a day's procedures.

    How Verity fixes it

    Rooms are on the schedule, so use shows by hour. Each device has a service date, vendor and history, with a task before it is due.

    See the screen
  13. 13People

    Staff cover and leave

    What it looks like

    The nurse is on leave and nobody told the desk. The receptionist is sick on a Monday. Training dates for fire or hygiene are not recorded.

    What it costs you

    A clinic with the wrong people on the wrong day runs short or pays extra.

    How Verity fixes it

    A roster shows who is on each day and who covers. Training and certificate dates are records with owners. Verity does not run payroll.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    A second branch, and no comparison

    What it looks like

    The owner opens a second branch. One has long waits and the other has empty rooms. Numbers come in on WhatsApp at night in different formats.

    What it costs you

    Without a fair comparison nothing can be improved, and the owner is the integrator.

    How Verity fixes it

    Branches are locations in one system. Visits, no-shows, follow-ups returned, stock and dues sit side by side, with the items that need a decision listed under them.

How we work with you

We set it up with you, in four weeks.

You do not get a login and a manual. We sit at your front desk for a morning, build Verity around how your clinic really runs, bring your data in, then stay to make sure it keeps working.

  3. Weeks 3 to 4

    What we do

    What you have at the end

    What we do

    What you have at the end
    A system that keeps matching how the clinic runs, with someone responsible for it.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a clinic has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or medical advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Doctor registration and clinic registration

    What is expected

    Doctors must hold a valid registration with a medical council. Clinics generally need registration under the Clinical Establishments Act where a state has adopted it, or the state's own law, plus local trade permits. Rules differ by state.

    How Verity helps

    Each registration is a record with a number, dates and an owner, with a task before a renewal.

  • Patient records

    What is expected

    Doctors are expected to keep patient records, and retention periods are set by medical council rules and state law. A June 2022 NMC draft proposed three years from the last contact for both outpatients and inpatients; check the current final rule.

    How Verity helps

    Records stay in date order on the patient record, with the retention period set for your type of record.

    Medical Dialogues on the NMC draft

  • Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 2020

    What is expected

    Teleconsultations expect consent to be recorded in the patient record, with logs and prescription records kept.

    How Verity helps

    A teleconsultation is a visit with mode, time, consent and attached prescription.

    PSA Legal

  • Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025

    What is expected

    Clinics handle health data, which is high risk. The Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 and most duties apply 18 months later. They include security safeguards and keeping logs for at least a year.

    How Verity helps

    Role-based access and an audit trail on every view and change.

    SCC Online

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your legal adviser, your medical council and the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions clinic owners ask.

I have one doctor and one receptionist. Is this too much?

A small clinic has the same leaks and less time to see them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Does Verity give medical advice or store diagnoses?

No. Verity records and organises: visits, documents, follow-ups, stock and tasks. The doctor's clinical notes and judgement stay with the doctor.

Who can see patient data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so the desk, the nurse and the doctor each see only what they need, and every view and change is recorded with who and when.

Does it replace my billing software?

No. Your billing keeps taking payments. We agree during setup how visits and fees reach Verity, which then connects follow-ups, stock and the schedule around them.

Can patients book online through Verity?

No. Verity does not take online bookings. If you use another booking tool, it keeps working. Verity makes sure every appointment and follow-up has an owner.

Can it send reminders to patients?

Not on its own. Verity lists who must be called and records the outcome. Your staff call or message the patient.

I have two branches. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each branch is a location in one system, with visits, no-shows, stock and dues side by side. A patient can be one record across both.

How quickly will I see something useful?

The follow-up list, stock warnings and dues need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. No-show and room-use patterns become reliable after a few weeks.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of doctors, branches and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your clinic runs.
