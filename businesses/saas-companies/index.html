How it runs today

Most SaaS teams can tell you their sign-ups. Few can tell you which customers are about to leave.

Each tool sees one slice. Billing knows who paid, support knows who complained, the product knows who logged in and sales knows what was promised. Nobody sees the same customer at once.

Customer success is often the founders and one or two people. The renewal date sits in a spreadsheet, the contract in an email and the open request in a chat.

Industry benchmarks show how much the customer mix matters. One vendor reports average annual churn of 4.9 percent for B2B SaaS, but 30 to 58 percent for SMB SaaS, so a company selling to small businesses has to watch its accounts far more closely.

What most SaaS companies run on

  • Product analytics
  • Support inbox or helpdesk
  • Billing or subscription tool
  • Spreadsheet of renewals
  • Founders' memory and chat groups

Each tool is good at its job. None of them says which accounts are at risk, what they were promised or what is waiting for them.

14 problems, one by one

Where a SaaS company loses customers and time.

These are the situations SaaS founders and customer success leads describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Customers

    At-risk accounts are invisible until they cancel

    What it looks like

    A customer's logins have halved, two tickets are open and the champion left the company. Each fact sits in a different tool, and nobody puts them together.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: with 200 customers at ₹60,000 a year, losing 10 more than planned is ₹6 lakh of yearly recurring revenue, usually found at the renewal.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each account has a record that collects its plan, usage notes, open tickets, requests and contacts. You mark accounts at risk with a reason, and Verity lists them by revenue and renewal date.

    See the screen
  2. 02Renewals

    Renewals are chased late or not at all

    What it looks like

    A contract renews in three weeks. Nobody has talked to the customer since onboarding, and the price uplift was never discussed.

    What it costs you

    Surprise cancellations, discounts given in a rush and renewals at the old price when a rise was due.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each contract has a renewal date, a value and an owner. Verity lists renewals in the next 90 days with a task at 60 and 30 days, and records the outcome.

    See the screen
  3. 03Onboarding

    Onboarding stalls and the customer never goes live

    What it looks like

    A customer signs, attends one kickoff and does not set up the integration. A month later they are paying and not using it.

    What it costs you

    A customer who sees no value and cancels at the first renewal, however good the product is.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each new account has an onboarding checklist with an owner and dates. Verity flags accounts that have not reached each step on time and records why.

    See the screen
  4. 04Support

    Support tickets breach the promise and nobody notices

    What it looks like

    A customer on a premium plan has waited 30 hours for a reply to a problem. The helpdesk shows the ticket, but nobody has the plan or the promised response time next to it.

    What it costs you

    An unhappy paying customer and a support team that works the loudest tickets rather than the most important ones.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each ticket is linked to its account and plan, and the promised response time is on it. Verity warns before a breach and lists tickets over the limit by account value.

    See the screen
  5. 05Support

    The same bug is reported again and again

    What it looks like

    Six customers report the same export failure on different days. Each is answered one by one and nobody connects them to a single fix.

    What it costs you

    Support hours repeated, an engineer interrupted six times and customers who feel ignored.

    How Verity fixes it

    Tickets that describe the same problem are linked to one issue. Verity shows how many customers and how much revenue an issue touches, so it is prioritised by impact.

    See the screen
  6. 06Product

    Feature requests are scattered and unweighted

    What it looks like

    Requests arrive in calls, emails and chats. The loudest customer wins, and the roadmap follows the last conversation.

    What it costs you

    Features built for one account and a product that drifts, while a request from ten customers waits.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each request is a record linked to the accounts that asked and their revenue. Verity ranks requests by number of accounts and value, and records the decision and the reason.

    See the screen
  7. 07Product

    Sales promises outrun the roadmap

    What it looks like

    A salesperson tells a prospect a feature "is coming next quarter". It is in nobody's plan. After signing, the customer asks for it.

    What it costs you

    A customer who feels misled, a founder in an awkward call and an unplanned scramble for an engineer.

    How Verity fixes it

    Promises made in a deal are recorded against the account and linked to a roadmap item or marked as not planned. Verity lists promises due and promises without a plan.

    See the screen
  8. 08Support

    Incidents are fixed but customers are not told or credited

    What it looks like

    An outage lasts three hours. Engineers fix it and move on. Some customers are told, some are not, and credits are promised on a call and forgotten.

    What it costs you

    Lost trust and disputes over invoices months later.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each incident is a record with the affected accounts, the updates sent, the cause and any credit promised. Verity lists credits owed and accounts not yet informed.

    See the screen
  9. 09Money

    Failed payments and unpaid invoices are chased by hand

    What it looks like

    A card fails, a UPI mandate expires or a company pays late. The billing tool records the failure and nobody in customer success knows.

    What it costs you

    Revenue that is owed but not collected, and customers who lapse by accident rather than by choice.

    How Verity fixes it

    Overdue invoices and failed payments are tasks on the account with an owner and a history of contacts. Verity lists them by amount and age. Billing stays in your billing tool.

    See the screen
  10. 10Money

    Payments from foreign customers need their own tracking

    What it looks like

    Much of the revenue comes from overseas customers paying in dollars. Invoices, payment dates and bank credits have to be matched by someone.

    What it costs you

    A missed payment that is noticed late, and paperwork the bank asks for months afterwards. Nasscom reported that about 75 percent of India's SaaS revenue came from global sales in FY2020, so this is the norm for many Indian SaaS companies.

    How Verity fixes it

    Foreign-currency invoices are recorded against the account with their dates. Verity shows what is outstanding, what has been realised and the days since the invoice, so the finance owner can follow up and keep bank records straight.

    See the screen
  11. 11Compliance

    Security questionnaires and compliance evidence take weeks

    What it looks like

    A large prospect sends a 200-question security questionnaire. The answers live in different people's heads, and evidence for policies is in a folder nobody has opened for a year.

    What it costs you

    A deal that stalls for weeks while engineers and founders hunt for answers.

    How Verity fixes it

    Policies, certificates, past answers and evidence are records with owners and dates. Verity lists what is due for review and keeps past questionnaire answers ready to reuse.

    See the screen
  12. 12Customers

    Expansion is left to chance

    What it looks like

    A customer hits the seat limit and keeps using the product with extra people. Nobody notices until the next renewal.

    What it costs you

    Revenue that could have been earned a year earlier, and a customer who might have been happy to pay more.

    How Verity fixes it

    Usage notes and plan limits sit on the account. Verity lists accounts near their limits and gives the account owner a task to talk about the next plan.

    See the screen
  13. 13Owner

    Business numbers are assembled by hand each month

    What it looks like

    At month end someone exports billing data, adds a spreadsheet of cancellations and works out recurring revenue, churn and net retention. The numbers differ depending on who did it.

    What it costs you

    Hours of founder time and decisions based on a number that nobody fully trusts. Benchmarks for churn differ widely by customer size, so your own number matters more than an industry one.

    How Verity fixes it

    Accounts, plans, contracts and cancellations are records, so recurring revenue, churn and expansion are calculated from them the same way each time.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    The founders are the system

    What it looks like

    One founder remembers which customers are fragile, which promises are pending and which renewals matter. When they travel, nothing moves.

    What it costs you

    The company cannot add customer success staff, or a second product line, without losing context.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each morning Verity shows what needs a decision, each item with an owner. A second product or region becomes a location in the same system.

    See the screen

Rules and paperwork

The rules a SaaS company has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules and your customers' requirements depend on in order.

  • Customer personal data

    What is expected

    Your product may hold personal data about your customers' users. India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, and most duties start to apply over the following 12 to 18 months.

    How Verity helps

    Customer and contact records sit in one permissioned place, with who viewed or changed them recorded.

    AZB Partners

  • Foreign payments and bank records

    What is expected

    Export proceeds generally have to be received within a period set by the RBI. A November 2025 amendment raised it from nine to fifteen months. Whether and how it applies to a particular software service depends on the transaction, so confirm with your bank.

    How Verity helps

    Foreign-currency invoices carry their dates and what has been received, so the finance owner has the facts ready.

    Taxguru on the RBI amendment

  • Security commitments in contracts

    What is expected

    Enterprise customers often require security policies, access reviews and evidence of testing, and may audit you against what you promised.

    How Verity helps

    Policies, reviews and test reports are records with owners and dates, so evidence is ready when a customer asks.

  • Contract terms, credits and service levels

    What is expected

    Contracts may promise response times, uptime and credits for outages. Missing them can lead to disputes.

    How Verity helps

    Promised terms sit on the account and are compared with tickets and incidents, so credits owed are visible.

Rules and customer requirements differ by market and contract, and they change. Confirm current requirements with your lawyer, your bank and your accountant.

Questions

Questions SaaS founders ask.

I already have a helpdesk, a CRM and a billing tool. Why another system?

Verity does not replace them. It holds the account record that joins what they each know: plan, tickets, requests, promises, renewals and compliance, so someone can see the whole customer.

Does it connect to my billing tool?

We agree during setup how renewal dates, plans and overdue invoices reach Verity. Billing stays in your billing tool, and Verity does not take payments or send invoices.

Can it tell me which customers will churn?

It does not predict. It shows the signals you decide matter, such as open tickets, no onboarding or a missing contact, and lists accounts by revenue and renewal date so people can act.

Can it send emails to my customers?

Not by itself. Verity assigns the follow-up to a named person and records the outcome. Your team writes and sends the message.

We sell to customers abroad. Does it help with that?

Yes. Foreign-currency invoices are recorded against the account with their dates, and what is outstanding is on the finance owner's list. Verity does not move money or file anything.

Can I use it to answer security questionnaires?

Yes. Policies, certificates and past answers are kept as records with owners, so you can reuse answers and see what is open. It does not certify you against any standard.

Who can see customer information?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How long before I see results?

The renewals list, at-risk accounts and tickets over limit need only your existing records, so they are the first things we aim to have working. Request and promise trends become reliable after a few weeks of use.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of users and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your company runs.
