Verity for medical distributors

A distributor earns a few points on every strip. A batch that expires or a chemist who never pays can erase a month of them.

A medical distributor buys from manufacturers, stores by batch, sells to hundreds of chemists on credit and takes back what expires or fails. The margin is thin and fixed by others, so the money is in what you do not lose: stock that expires before it is sold, dues that run past their date, returns nobody claimed. Verity connects the batch, the chemist, the van and the return so you see each leak while it can be fixed.

How it runs today

Most distributors run big numbers on thin margins and manual memory.

The billing software knows invoices. The godown knows what is physically there. The salesmen know their chemists. The accountant knows the ledger. The owner knows who is slow to pay. None of these agree on the same day.

Margins in the trade are thin and largely set by others. In 2022 the chemists' and druggists' body asked that a minimum trade margin of 10 percent for wholesalers be recognised and said margins had not been restructured since 1995. When the percentage is small, a single unclaimed return or an unpaid invoice matters.

Every day, a distributor also deals with batch-level quality action. In July 2026, 280 samples were reported not of standard quality or spurious by the drug regulator's monthly alert, and such failures are batch-specific. A distributor has to know which chemist holds which batch.

What most medical distributors run on

  • Billing software and stock reports
  • Ledger and Excel for outstanding
  • Paper or Excel for returns and expiry claims
  • WhatsApp with salesmen, chemists and drivers
  • Temperature sheets or a data logger report

Each does its piece. Nothing tells you which chemist holds a recalled batch or which return claim is about to lapse.

14 problems, one by one

Where a medical distributor loses margin.

These are the situations distributors, stockists and their accounts and godown teams describe. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Stock

    Stock expires in the godown

    What it looks like

    A slow scheme item bought in bulk sits in a rack. Nobody checks expiry until a quarterly physical count, when a lot has three weeks left and the manufacturer's return window has closed.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹6 lakh of short-dated stock written off at 1.5 percent margin takes the margin on about ₹4 crore of sales to recover.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every receipt records batch and expiry. Verity lists stock by months to expiry and value, shows which items can still be returned to the manufacturer, pushed on a scheme to chemists or moved between godowns.

  2. 02Stock

    Wrong batch is picked, and FEFO is only a hope

    What it looks like

    The picker takes the nearest box, not the earliest-expiring batch. The chemist's copy of the invoice shows one batch, the carton holds another.

    What it costs you

    The older batch expires on your shelf, and a chemist's batch query turns into an argument you cannot settle.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each sale line is linked to the batch and the pick list shows the earliest-expiring batch first. A batch mismatch at dispatch is flagged before the van leaves.

    See the screen
  3. 03Compliance

    A quality alert, and you cannot say who holds the batch

    What it looks like

    The regulator's monthly alert names a batch as not of standard quality. You need to know within hours how much remains, which chemists were sold it and what has already been consumed.

    What it costs you

    Selling or leaving a flagged batch in circulation is a compliance and reputation risk. Tracing by hand through invoices takes days.

    How Verity fixes it

    Because every sale line carries a batch, Verity answers who holds the batch, how much is in the godown and which invoices carried it, and creates a task per salesman to recover it. The source of the alert is checked by a person.

    See the screen
  4. 04Credit

    Chemists pay late and nobody sees the pattern

    What it looks like

    Sixty chemists owe money. Some pay in a week, some in sixty days. A salesman keeps taking new orders from one who has two invoices overdue. The owner learns the total at month end.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹1.2 crore of dues running 20 days late on a distributor with a 1.5 percent margin costs more in interest than the margin on those sales.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every chemist has a credit limit, terms and an ageing view. Verity shows outstanding by age and by salesman, and holds a new order for approval when dues are over limit or days.

    See the screen
  5. 05Credit

    Collections depend on the salesman's memory

    What it looks like

    The collection round is whoever remembers. A cheque is received and posted late. A post-dated cheque bounces and the entry is not reversed.

    What it costs you

    Unrecorded or late cheques hide the real dues and delay follow-up.

    How Verity fixes it

    Collections and cheques are recorded against invoices with dates, including post-dated cheques and returned cheques. Daily collection targets by salesman come from the ageing list.

    See the screen
  6. 06Returns

    Returns and expiry claims lapse

    What it looks like

    A chemist returns expired and damaged strips. They sit in a bag for weeks. By the time the claim goes to the manufacturer the window has closed, or the debit note does not match.

    What it costs you

    Unclaimed expiry is money you refund to the chemist and never recover from the principal.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each return is a record with chemist, batch, quantity, reason and the manufacturer's claim window. Verity tracks claim status through raised, accepted, credited or rejected, and flags claims close to their cut-off date.

    See the screen
  7. 07Suppliers

    Credit notes and scheme claims are not reconciled

    What it looks like

    The manufacturer's scheme promised a free box on ten. The credit note arrives short. Nobody compared it to the sales that earned the scheme.

    What it costs you

    Scheme benefits you earned and did not receive are quiet loss.

    How Verity fixes it

    Scheme terms are recorded per manufacturer, sales earning them are tallied, and the credit due is compared with the credit note received.

    See the screen
  8. 08Cold chain

    Cold chain items have no temperature trail

    What it looks like

    Insulin and vaccines go into a van in the afternoon. The ice pack was changed or not. A chemist's fridge reading is never asked for. The godown fridge alarm goes off at night.

    What it costs you

    A study of vaccine transport in India found 9 to 35 percent of some transfers exposed to sub-zero temperatures. A spoiled lot is a total loss and a patient-safety concern.

    How Verity fixes it

    Cold-chain stock is flagged. Fridge checks and van packing are tasks signed off with temperature readings. An excursion is recorded against the batches affected, with the decision taken by a named person.

    See the screen
  9. 09Dispatch

    Wrong or short deliveries come back as disputes

    What it looks like

    The chemist says eight strips of one item were missing. The driver says he delivered all. The invoice was signed on a phone photo.

    What it costs you

    Disputes mean credit notes, re-delivery trips and strained relationships.

    How Verity fixes it

    Orders are picked, packed and loaded against the invoice, and delivery is confirmed with quantities received. Differences are recorded at the door, with the reason.

    See the screen
  10. 10Dispatch

    Vans, drivers and routes are planned by habit

    What it looks like

    Three vans cover overlapping areas. A driver waits for an invoice that is not printed. A chemist who ordered at noon gets goods the next day.

    What it costs you

    Fuel, driver time and late deliveries cost more than the margin on small orders.

    How Verity fixes it

    Orders are grouped by route and day, each van has its load and sequence, and late orders appear on the dispatch screen. Staff plan the route; Verity shows what is waiting.

    See the screen
  11. 11Buying

    Purchases are bought on instinct

    What it looks like

    A scheme tempts a bulk buy. Another item sold out last week and its purchase order was missed. Salesmen's orders and the godown's counts do not meet.

    What it costs you

    Bulk buying slow items ties up cash. Missing a fast item loses a chemist's loyalty.

    How Verity fixes it

    Sales by item and chemist, current stock and incoming orders are shown together with reorder points. A purchase draft is created for review, and a scheme purchase shows how many months of sales it represents.

    See the screen
  12. 12People

    Salesmen's targets and chemist coverage

    What it looks like

    Salesmen visit the same easy chemists. A chemist who used to order weekly has gone quiet. Targets are given on paper and reviewed at month end.

    What it costs you

    A lost chemist is a recurring order that quietly goes to a competitor.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each chemist has a usual order pattern. Verity lists chemists who have gone quiet, with last order and value, and assigns a visit task to the salesman covering them.

    See the screen
  13. 13Compliance

    Licences, registers and inspections

    What it looks like

    The wholesale licence retention fee date is in a drawer. The competent person's records are on a certificate nobody has scanned. An inspector asks for purchase records and the Schedule H1 register is incomplete.

    What it costs you

    A notice, penalty or licence action. Licences are generally valid indefinitely if the retention fee is paid within each five-year period, with a late fee that rises monthly.

    How Verity fixes it

    Licences, retention-fee dates, competent person details and registers are records with owners and dates. Sales line records give the purchase and sale trail by batch when asked.

  14. 14Owner

    The owner sees the day after

    What it looks like

    With two godowns and several salesmen, the owner asks for sales, dues and stock and gets three different sheets.

    What it costs you

    Decisions about credit, buying and routes wait on whoever builds the sheet.

    How Verity fixes it

    Godowns are locations in one system, with stock, dues, returns and routes side by side. Each morning Verity lists what needs a decision, with an owner.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a medical distributor has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when an inspector or an auditor asks.

  • Wholesale drug licence

    What is expected

    Wholesale of drugs generally needs a licence from the state drug authority: Form 20B for general drugs, and Form 21B for drugs in Schedules C and C(1). Since 27 October 2017 the licence is generally valid indefinitely if a retention fee is paid in each five-year period. A competent person is generally required, not necessarily a registered pharmacist.

    How Verity helps

    The licence, retention-fee dates and competent person details are records with owners and dates, and the fee is a task before it lapses.

    CalcGuru

  • Purchase, sale and register records

    What is expected

    A wholesaler generally keeps purchase and sale records, with batch details, and registers for certain drug schedules. Retention periods differ by record; a secondary source gives two years for purchase records and three for the Schedule H1 register, which should be confirmed.

    How Verity helps

    Every sale line carries item, batch, quantity, buyer licence and invoice, so the trail exists by batch when asked.

  • Quality alerts and withdrawals

    What is expected

    The regulator publishes monthly alerts on samples not of standard quality or spurious. Failures are batch-specific.

    How Verity helps

    Verity finds stock and sold lines by batch and creates recovery tasks. A person checks the official notice.

    ETV Bharat

  • Storage and cold chain

    What is expected

    Drugs that need controlled temperatures must be stored accordingly. Exact conditions depend on the product label and the licence conditions.

    How Verity helps

    Fridge readings and van packing checks are signed off with the date and a name, and excursions are recorded against the batches.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your state drug controller, your accountant and your legal adviser.

Questions

Questions medical distributors ask.

Does Verity replace my billing software?

No. Your billing software keeps issuing invoices. We agree during setup how invoices, batches and receipts reach Verity, which then does the batch, dues, returns and cold-chain work around them.

Can it track every batch from purchase to chemist?

Yes, once receipts and sale lines carry the batch. That is the basis for expiry lists and for finding who holds a batch when an alert arrives.

Will it keep me from missing return claim dates?

It records each manufacturer's claim window on the return and flags those near their cut-off. Your team still raises the claim with the manufacturer.

Can it handle cold chain?

It records fridge readings, van packing checks and excursions against the batches affected. It does not provide hardware or sensors; we agree during setup how readings reach Verity.

Can chemists order through Verity?

No. Verity does not take online orders. Your salesmen and phone orders continue, and Verity makes sure each order and each due has an owner.

I have two godowns. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each godown is a location in one system, with stock, dues, returns and routes side by side, and transfers are recorded.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so a salesman sees only his chemists and your accountant sees dues and credit notes. Every change is recorded with who and when.

How quickly will I see something useful?

The expiry list and dues ageing need only your billing exports, so we aim to have them first. Return claim and quiet-chemist patterns become reliable after a few weeks.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of godowns, users and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your business runs.
