Verity for photographers

You sell a day of photographs. The money is made or lost in the months of editing, approvals and chasing after it.

A photographer's business has two halves. The shoot, which is a date that cannot move, and everything after: culling thousands of frames, editing, picking album spreads, getting the family's approval and collecting the balance. Most of the losses are in the second half. How it runs today

Most photographers know the next shoot date and have lost track of the last ten jobs.

The shoot is the visible part. The call time, the venue, the kit bag. What gets lost is behind it: which weddings are still being edited, which albums have been sent for approval and not returned, whose balance is outstanding and which second shooter is owed for last month.

The information is in pieces. A calendar for dates. A WhatsApp chat per client for requests and approvals. A drive full of folders named by date. A notebook for payments. The photographer, who is often also the editor, holds the rest in memory.

Photography is a small but steady slice of an Indian wedding budget, generally 5 to 10 percent according to a fund house blog on the 2025 season, in a peak where about 100,000 weddings a day are projected for about 45 days. Many studios shoot several weddings a week then spend months behind.

What most photographers run on

  • Calendar and WhatsApp
  • Drive folders and hard disks
  • Editing software and online galleries
  • Notebook or Excel for payments
  • Album designer tools

Each tool is good at its own job. None tells you that a wedding shot in March is still not delivered in October.

14 problems, one by one

Where a photography business actually loses money.

These are the situations photographers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Bookings

    Enquiries are answered late or not at all

    What it looks like

    A couple writes on Instagram while you are shooting a three-day wedding. You reply on Thursday. They have already booked someone who replied on Monday.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a wedding package of ₹1.5 lakh lost every month is ₹18 lakh a year, simply because the reply came a few days late.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each enquiry is a record with date, event type, city, budget, source and owner. Verity lists enquiries with no reply and tasks the person responsible. Staff reply; the outcome is recorded.

  2. 02Bookings

    Two bookings for the same date, or one with no second shooter

    What it looks like

    A pre-wedding shoot is promised on a Saturday that already has a wedding. Another wedding needs a second shooter and an assistant, and the one you counted on has another job.

    What it costs you

    A refunded advance, an angry client or a sub-par team on a once-only day.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every booking sits on a schedule with the people and kit assigned. Verity warns of overlap, shows who is free and lists bookings that still have no second shooter or assistant.

  3. 03Money

    Advances are taken in cash and the date is held without proof

    What it looks like

    A client pays ₹20,000 in cash to hold a date and it is written on a slip. Later the client says they paid ₹30,000. A studio listing on WedMeGood says it asks for a 50 percent advance to secure a date; many small studios have no standard rule at all.

    What it costs you

    Disputes over the advance, and a date held without a commitment from the client.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each booking has a payment schedule with the advance, the amount, the payment mode and the receipt. Verity shows the balance and lists bookings where the advance has not been received.

    See the screen
  4. 04Money

    Balances go unpaid after the wedding

    What it looks like

    The wedding is over, the family is happy, and the last ₹50,000 is "next week". Three months later you still do not like to ask. The client then says the album was late.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ten weddings a year with ₹50,000 left unpaid is ₹5 lakh that you did the work for.

    How Verity fixes it

    Balances and due dates are on the booking, with a task to the right person to ask. Verity lists balances overdue by age, and links them to what is still undelivered so the conversation is clear.

    See the screen
  5. 05Shoots

    The shoot day has no checklist and details are missed

    What it looks like

    The family said they wanted a particular photo with the grandparents and the first look at 4 pm. The details were in a call. On the day the second shooter did not know.

    What it costs you

    The one photograph the family cared about missing is remembered more than a thousand good ones.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each shoot has a brief with the shot list, the key people, timings, venue contacts and kit list. The team sees the same brief on their phones and tasks to confirm the key moments.

    See the screen
  6. 06Shoots

    Memory cards and hard disks are not accounted for

    What it looks like

    Five cards from a wedding, three from the second shooter, two from the drone. Back-up copies are on two disks in two bags. One card is unaccounted for until Tuesday.

    What it costs you

    Lost files from a day that cannot be repeated, and a very hard conversation with the client.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each shoot records cards, who has them, when they were copied, to which disks, and when they were checked. Verity shows shoots with unconfirmed back-ups the same day.

  7. 07Editing

    The editing queue is a pile and deadlines are guessed

    What it looks like

    Eleven weddings are waiting. Two editors each hold a different list. A client asks "when will my photos be ready?" and the honest answer is "I will check".

    What it costs you

    Late deliveries lead to constant follow-up messages, according to a photo delivery vendor, and one late wedding can push back the next ones. Clients who wait too long stop recommending you.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job in editing has a stage, an editor, a promised date and the count of photos done. Verity shows the queue by editor and deadline, and flags jobs likely to miss their date given the current load.

  8. 08Team

    Outsourced editors and second shooters are paid from memory

    What it looks like

    A freelance editor does eight albums in a month at different rates. A second shooter worked on four weddings. At month end the owner adds it up from chats.

    What it costs you

    Hours of owner time and quiet disputes with the people who deliver your work.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each shoot, edit and album job is recorded against the person with the rate rule you agreed. Verity totals what each person is owed. You pay them, or your accountant does; Verity does not run payroll.

  9. 09Albums

    Album drafts go to the family and approvals never come back

    What it looks like

    The album draft was sent in July. The bride's mother wanted changes, the groom's side wanted others, and the final approval has not come in October. The printer's slot has gone.

    What it costs you

    Cash and storage tied up in unfinished jobs, and the final payment held hostage by the approval.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each album is a job with the draft, version, who must approve, the date sent and a follow-up task. Verity lists albums waiting for approval by age and records each round of changes.

    See the screen
  10. 10Albums

    Changes are requested in chat and versions get mixed up

    What it looks like

    The family sends twelve small change requests across WhatsApp, voice notes and phone calls. The designer builds version 4 from an older chat. The printer gets the wrong file.

    What it costs you

    Reprints, a damaged relationship and the cost of a mistake that cannot be fixed after printing.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each change request is attached to the album with who asked and the version it applies to. Verity shows which request is in which version and only marks "approved" when the family's final yes is recorded.

    See the screen
  11. 11Compliance

    Rights, usage and consent are unwritten

    What it looks like

    A client posts your pictures without credit. A hotel wants to use a wedding shoot in advertising. Another client wants the raw files. The booking terms never said.

    What it costs you

    Disputes over who may use the images, and lost chances to charge for commercial use. Under Section 17 of the Copyright Act, a photograph taken at a person's instance is generally owned first by that person unless the agreement says otherwise, so the written terms matter.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each booking has a signed agreement with its usage and delivery terms, attached to the record. Verity reminds the team to attach it before the shoot and shows bookings with no terms on file.

    See the screen
  12. 12Delivery

    Delivery means files on drives that clients cannot find

    What it looks like

    The final gallery link was sent once. Two years later a client asks for the files again. The folder was moved when the disk filled.

    What it costs you

    Repeated requests, goodwill redos and a studio that cannot prove what it delivered when.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each delivery is recorded with what was sent, when, the link or disk and the client's acknowledgement. Verity keeps the archive location of the originals and the retention date you set.

    See the screen
  13. 13Equipment

    Gear, lenses and insurance are not tracked

    What it looks like

    A lens needs repair and nobody knows the warranty date. A second body is lent to an assistant and not returned. Insurance for the kit lapsed in September.

    What it costs you

    A repair bill that should have been warranty, a missing camera on a shoot day, or an uninsured loss.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each camera, lens, flash and drone is an asset record with serial number, who holds it, service history, warranty and insurance dates. Verity tasks the owner before a date lapses.

  14. 14Owner

    Studio income is seasonal and the owner cannot see where it comes from

    What it looks like

    You shoot twenty weddings between November and February and four in the rest of the year. Corporate shoots, product shoots and pre-wedding shoots run on different sheets. You cannot say which type pays best.

    What it costs you

    You take work that pays poorly in peak season and sit idle off season.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every job is tagged by type, source and the hours in shooting and editing. Verity shows income, cost and time per job type, so you can decide what to promote and what to price up.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a photography business has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • GST on photography services

    What is expected

    Photography and related services generally attract 18 percent GST, and registration is generally mandatory above ₹20 lakh of yearly turnover (a lower limit applies in some states). Your accountant should confirm how it applies to prints, albums and drone work.

    How Verity helps

    Each package, instalment and album sale is a record with the amount and date, so returns can be prepared from clean data. Verity does not file returns.

    ClearTax on GST for photography

  • Who owns the photographs

    What is expected

    Under Section 17 of the Copyright Act, 1957, where a photograph is taken at someone's instance, that person is generally treated as the first owner unless there is an agreement to the contrary. This is why your written terms on usage, delivery and portfolio use matter. Confirm wording with a lawyer.

    How Verity helps

    A signed agreement with its usage terms is attached to every booking. Bookings without terms are flagged before the shoot.

    Khurana & Khurana on first owner of copyright

  • Clients' personal data and images

    What is expected

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. Notice, consent and safeguard duties for businesses holding personal data are generally due from 13 May 2027. Client lists and photographs of people are personal data.

    How Verity helps

    Client records, galleries and archive locations sit with access by role, and every change is logged.

    AZB & Partners on the DPDP phases

  • Venue shoot permissions

    What is expected

    Some venues, monuments and public places require permission or a fee for commercial photography, and drones have their own rules. Requirements vary by place.

    How Verity helps

    Each permission is a task with an owner and a date, and the approval is attached to the booking.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant, a lawyer or the relevant authority.

I am a solo photographer. Is this too much?

A solo studio has the same stages and less time. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Do you store my photographs?

No. Your files stay on your disks, drive and gallery tools. Verity records where the originals and the delivered gallery live, the back-up checks and the delivery date.

Can clients see their job's status?

Verity is for your team. If you want to share a status with a client, your team does it as they do now, and the update is recorded.

Will it help me stop missing album approvals?

Yes. Each album records the version, who must approve, the date sent and a follow-up task, and lists albums waiting by age.

Can it handle outsourced editors?

Yes. A freelance editor or second shooter is a person with a rate rule, and each job is recorded against them. Verity totals what they are owed; you or your accountant pay them.

Does it tell me when to ask for the balance?

It keeps the balance and the due date on each booking and lists overdue ones by age with a task for the right person. Your team speaks to the client and records the answer.

Can it take enquiries or bookings from my website?

No. Where enquiries come from your website or Instagram, your team creates the record in Verity, and Verity makes sure someone replies.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so editors, second shooters and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How quickly will I see something useful?

The balance list and the album approval list need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. Editing load and job type profit become reliable after a few weeks of recorded jobs.

What does it cost?

It depends on the size of your team and the number of jobs a year. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your studio works.
