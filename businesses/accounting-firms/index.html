How it runs today

Most accounting practices are efficient inside the books and chaotic around them.

The real work of a bookkeeping or accounting practice is predictable: collect inputs, post entries, reconcile, prepare monthly statements, support statutory returns and answer the owner's questions. What breaks the rhythm is that inputs come from clients who are running their own businesses.

The documents arrive in WhatsApp, email, a courier bag and a shared drive. Someone has to download, rename and sort them, and then remember which client is still missing which month. An article on the ICAI's own AI platform describes exactly this: hours lost chasing documents, a crowded WhatsApp channel and no record of what was sent, when and by whom.

Work is tracked on a whiteboard or an Excel sheet. Hours are not tracked at all, so the firm cannot say which clients are profitable. Fees are invoiced late and followed up when someone remembers.

What most accounting firms run on

  • Tally or other accounting software
  • Excel sheet of clients and monthly status
  • WhatsApp groups per client
  • Email and shared drive for documents
  • A whiteboard or sheet for who is doing what

The accounting software is good at the books. Nothing connects the books to the people, the documents and the money owed to you.

    Documents arrive late, partial and in the wrong place

    What it costs you

    Books cannot be closed, and the staff who were meant to start on the 5th sit waiting, then work at night to catch up. A vendor guide on CA firms puts the cost at 10 to 15 days of overtime a month, a vendor claim worth testing with your own numbers.

    Nobody knows which clients are stuck, and where

    What it looks like

    The partner asks, "Where are we on the March close?" Three accountants give three answers. One client is waiting for a query reply, another for a review, a third for a bank statement.

    What it costs you

    A month-end that slips for one client slips for others because the same people are pulled across. Surprises reach the partner when the client is already upset.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client's month is a set of stages: documents in, posted, reconciled, reviewed, delivered. Verity shows the board by stage and by person, with the oldest items first, so the partner sees the real position without asking.

  3. 03Clients

    Queries sit in WhatsApp and are answered once

    What it looks like

    An accountant asks a client what a ₹1.2 lakh transfer was for. The client replies two days later in a voice note. The answer is not written next to the entry, and the same query comes up next month.

    What it costs you

    Rework each month, mistakes in classification and no record to show a reviewer or an auditor why an entry was made.

    How Verity fixes it

    Queries are records against the client and the period, with the entry, the question, the answer and who answered. Open queries age on the screen and the answer stays with the record.

    What it looks like

    What it costs you

    Reconciliation is the first thing to go wrong when staff change, and old unmatched items grow into year-end clean-up work.

    How Verity fixes it

    What it looks like

    Overtime, errors in the overloaded and boredom in the underloaded, until the overloaded person leaves and takes her clients' knowledge with her.

    How Verity fixes it

    Hours are not tracked, so profitability is a guess

    What it looks like

    A small client on a fixed monthly fee of ₹6,000 sends 40 messages a month and needs three corrections. A larger client on ₹25,000 hardly calls. The firm thinks it earns more on the first.

    What it costs you

    Fixed fees that look fine on paper can lose money in hours. Example arithmetic: 30 hours a month at an internal cost of ₹400 an hour is ₹12,000 against a ₹6,000 fee.

How we work with you

We set it up with you, in four weeks.

You do not get a login and a manual. We sit with your team during a real month-end, build Verity around how your clients and work flow, bring your data in, then stay on.

    Discovery and mapping

    What we do
    Watch a month-end close from the 1st to the 10th, or sit with the team through its last few days. Follow a document from the client's phone to the finished statements, and note where it waits.
    What we need from you
    Two to three hours of a partner's time and an hour from each senior accountant. Your client list, engagement terms and a sample month of tasks.
    What you have at the end
    A written map of your practice: client types, monthly stages, filing calendar, fee terms, team and the first delays we will remove.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3

    Configuration

    What we do
    Set up client types, monthly checklists, stages, filing calendars by registration, time codes, engagement scopes, invoice schedules and permissions for partners, seniors, juniors and clients.
    What we need from you
    Review the setup with one manager and two accountants. Tell us where a checklist or a stage is wrong.
    What you have at the end
    A working Verity with your clients' checklists, calendars and fee terms, ready for data.

  3. Weeks 3 to 4

    Migration and training

    What we do
    Bring in your client list, registrations, engagement terms, open tasks and unpaid fees from your sheets. Train partners and accountants at their desks with real clients.
    What we need from you
    Your client sheet in whatever form it exists, and an hour with each role.
    What you have at the end
    Your clients, calendars and fees inside Verity, and a team that has run part of a close on it.

    What we do

    What we need from you
    A short monthly review with a partner.
    What you have at the end
    A system that keeps matching how the practice works, with someone responsible for it.

Rules and paperwork

The rules an accounting firm has to keep up with.

Verity does not give tax or accounting advice and does not file returns. It keeps the calendar and the records these rules depend on in order, so your team prepares and files from a clear position.

  • GST return calendar

    What is expected

    Monthly filers generally file GSTR-1 by the 11th and GSTR-3B by the 20th of the next month, with late fees and interest for delay. Quarterly filers have different dates.

    How Verity helps

    Each client's filing frequency is on the record, and due dates and status are shown for every client in one view.

    TaxConcept, 2026

  • E-invoicing

    What is expected

    E-invoicing generally applies where aggregate turnover exceeded ₹5 crore in any year since FY 2017-18, and the portal rejects late reporting for turnover of ₹10 crore and above. Exemptions exist.

    How Verity helps

    Turnover to date is shown on the client record with a review task near the limit.

    TaxGuru, 2026

  • Income tax calendar

    What is expected

    Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, while income earned in FY 2025-26 is generally still assessed under the 1961 Act. Return dates differ by taxpayer type.

    How Verity helps

    Client tasks follow the filing category on the client record, and your accountant confirms the dates.

    Patron Accounting, 2026

  • Data protection

    What is expected

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with most duties due about 18 months later. How they apply to your practice and its clients is for your adviser.

    How Verity helps

    Client documents have role and client permissions, and views and downloads are logged.

    Mondaq, 2025

Dates, thresholds and fees change by notification and differ by taxpayer type. Confirm current requirements on the official portals or with your chartered accountant.

Questions

Questions accounting firms ask.

Does Verity replace Tally or my accounting software?

No. Your team keeps posting in the software they use. Verity runs the work around it: documents, stages, queries, hours, filing calendar and fees.

Will it chase my clients for documents?

Your staff still send the reminder. Verity shows who is missing what, assigns the task to the right person and records that it was done and what the client said.

Can my clients upload documents themselves?

We agree with you during setup how documents reach Verity, whether through staff, a shared folder or a client view. Each client sees only their own checklist.

I have 25 clients and four staff. Is this too much?

A small practice has the same chase with fewer people. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Can it tell me which clients are unprofitable?

Yes, once staff log time. Verity compares each client's fee with hours at your internal cost, so the clients that lose money show up and the fee or scope can be changed.

Does it file my GST and TDS returns?

No. Your team prepares and files on the official portals. Verity keeps the calendar, the owner and the reviewer for every client's return.

How do I stop staff taking client data when they leave?
