How it runs today

Most AI companies are strong on the model and loose on everything around it.

The work itself is visible to the people doing it. A researcher knows which run was good. A founder knows which client is hot. What is hard to see is the join: which run produced the model that went to the pilot, which dataset was in it, what that compute cost, and whether the client has signed off on anything in writing.

Research is kept in notebooks, experiment trackers, cloud billing pages, a shared drive of datasets and a chat channel per client. Each is fine alone. Nobody owns the picture across them, so the founder asks the same questions each week by message.

Surveys give a sense of the gap. An MSL report in February 2026 found only 15% of Indian organisations had deployed generative AI workloads into production, and just 8% could fully measure and allocate AI costs. A Salesforce study reported in July 2026 found 38% of workers in India said their organisation had an unsuccessful AI pilot in the past year.

What most AI companies run on

  • Notebooks and an experiment tracker
  • Cloud billing consoles and GPU provider invoices
  • A shared drive of datasets
  • Slack or WhatsApp channel per client
  • Spreadsheet of pilots and grants

The engineers keep what works for research. The business side runs on memory, so the cost and the promise are never in the same place as the model.

14 problems, one by one

Where an AI company actually loses time and money.

These are the situations founders, heads of research and delivery leads describe. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary week, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Experiments

    A good run that nobody can repeat

    What it looks like

    Priya's team gets 91% on the held-out set in March. In September the client asks for the same model with a new label. The notebook was edited, the data folder was renamed and the engineer who ran it has moved teams. Two weeks go on trying to reproduce a number already shown to the client.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: two engineers for two weeks at a loaded ₹2.5 lakh a month each is about ₹2.3 lakh, spent to get back to where you were, with the client waiting.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each run is a record linked to the project, the dataset version, the code reference, the owner and the result. Verity shows which run each shipped model came from, so a rerun starts from a record and not from memory.

  2. 02Compute

    The GPU bill arrives after the run is finished

    What it looks like

    A fine-tune ran for six days on rented GPUs. The provider invoice lands in the next month, and the finance lead cannot say which project, client or experiment it belongs to. The founder finds out the pilot lost money when the invoice is paid.

    What it costs you

    Only 8% of Indian enterprises can fully measure and allocate AI costs, per an MSL report of February 2026. Example arithmetic: ₹6 lakh of compute in a quarter, unassigned, makes every project look cheaper than it was.

    How Verity fixes it

    Compute spend is entered or imported against the project and run it served. Verity shows spend to date against each project's compute budget, and flags a project close to its limit while there is still time to stop.

    See the screen
  3. 03Compute

    Reserved capacity that sits idle

    What it looks like

    You committed to a block of GPUs for a year because capacity was hard to find. Two projects have since finished. The nodes sit at low use on weekends and between training runs, and the commitment is still billed in full.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a commitment of ₹4 lakh a month used at 45% leaves about ₹2.2 lakh a month paid for nothing.

    How Verity fixes it

    Compute commitments are records with start, end, monthly cost and the projects that draw on them. Verity shows booked hours against the commitment by week, so idle capacity is visible and can be offered to the next project.

    See the screen
  4. 04Data

    Datasets with no clear origin or licence

    What it looks like

    A scraped corpus, a client's exported tickets, a purchased set and an open dataset are all in one folder. A prospect's legal team asks where the training data came from and whether it carries personal information. The answer takes a week to assemble.

    What it costs you

    A deal stalls or dies in due diligence. Delhi High Court's July 2026 interim ruling in ANI v OpenAI refused an injunction, but the suit is still going to trial, so the legal position on training data is not settled.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every dataset is a record with source, licence or contract, whether it holds personal data, who approved its use and which models were trained on it. Verity answers "which models used this dataset" in one search.

    See the screen
  5. 05Data

    Labelling vendors, rework and quality drift

    What it looks like

    A labelling team delivers 20,000 images. A spot check finds one class labelled inconsistently. The vendor says the guideline was unclear. The next batch is already in progress under the same guideline.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 20,000 items at ₹6 each is ₹1.2 lakh. If 15% need redoing, ₹18,000 is paid twice, plus weeks of model results built on noisy labels.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each labelling batch is a record with vendor, guideline version, sample check result and accepted count. A failed check holds the batch from training and creates a rework task for the vendor contact.

    See the screen
  6. 06Evaluation

    Evaluations scattered, so nobody says "ready"

    What it looks like

    Accuracy is in one notebook, a bias check in a slide, red-team notes in a document and latency in a dashboard. The release call is made by whoever speaks loudest in the stand-up.

    What it costs you

    A model ships with a gap nobody tracked, or a good model waits because nobody can show it is good.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each model version has an evaluation checklist: accuracy, safety checks, latency, cost per call, and a named approver for each. Verity shows which checks pass and which are open, and records who signed the release.

    See the screen
  7. 07Pilots

    Pilots begin with no written test of success

    What it looks like

    A bank branch network agrees to a six-week pilot of a document-reading model. The aim is "see how it goes". Week six arrives, nobody agrees what a good result is, and the client asks for another month at no charge.

    What it costs you

    Free extensions eat engineering weeks. Example arithmetic: a four-person pilot team for an extra month at ₹10 lakh a month of combined cost is ₹10 lakh of unpaid work.

    How Verity fixes it

    A pilot is a record with the success measure, the target, the start and end dates, what is in and out of scope, and the client approver. Verity tracks the measure through the pilot and flags a pilot that has passed its end date without a decision.

  8. 08Pilots

    Pilot works, production never happens

    What it looks like

    The pilot hits its target. Then the client's security team asks for a review, the data team needs six weeks to open a feed, and the sponsor changes jobs. The pilot sits in "approved" for four months.

    What it costs you

    Only 15% of organisations had deployed generative AI workloads into production, per MSL in February 2026. A stalled conversion is revenue that was promised and never started.

    How Verity fixes it

    After a successful pilot, Verity creates a conversion checklist: security review, data access, contract, support owner. Each item has an owner on both sides and a date. A stuck item shows against the deal.

    See the screen
  9. 09Pilots

    Outputs that lack the client's business context

    What it looks like

    The model gives answers that are correct in general and wrong for this client's products and terms. Their staff stop trusting it after a week and go back to the old way.

    What it costs you

    The Salesforce study reported in July 2026 found 34% of workers whose AI pilot failed said outputs lacked business context. A pilot that people stop using is a pilot you cannot convert.

    How Verity fixes it

    Client feedback is logged on the pilot as records with a category such as wrong context, too generic or slow. Verity groups them by type and by week, so the team fixes the largest category first and the client sees the fix.

    See the screen
  10. 10Delivery

    Deliverables and versions do not match what was promised

    What it looks like

    The contract lists a model, an evaluation report, an API handover note and two training sessions. The client holds version 2 of the report and version 3 of the model. Nobody checks until the final payment is due.

    What it costs you

    A delivery dispute holds up the final instalment. Example arithmetic: 20% of a ₹60 lakh contract held back is ₹12 lakh.

    How Verity fixes it

    Contract deliverables are listed on the project with owner, due date, version and client acceptance. Verity shows what has been delivered, what is accepted and what is still open, and links each to the payment milestone your finance lead tracks.

    See the screen
  11. 11Compliance

    Client data handled without a clear record

    What it looks like

    A client shares sample customer records for the pilot. Copies end up on three laptops and a cloud bucket. At the end of the pilot, nobody can say who deleted what.

    What it costs you

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025 and phase in over 18 months, with breach intimation and data retention duties generally from 13 May 2027. Poor records of who held client data become a problem once these apply.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client data share is a record: what was received, for what purpose, who has access, where it is held and when it must be deleted. Deletion is a task with a due date, and the confirmation is stored on the record.

    See the screen
  12. 12People

    Researchers stretched across too many projects

    What it looks like

    Rahul is the only person who can tune the speech model. He is half on the bank pilot, half on a grant project, and an urgent request from a third client takes the rest. Nobody sees the overload until he leaves.

    What it costs you

    Over-allocated people ship late and quit. A single departure takes the knowledge of a model with them.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each person is allocated to projects by percentage of time. Verity shows allocation per person and per week, flags anyone above 100%, and shows the single points of failure: models with one person who understands them.

    See the screen
  13. 13Money

    Grants, compute subsidies and paperwork on someone's laptop

    What it looks like

    The company applied for subsidised GPU access and a research grant. Utilisation reports, usage letters and invoices are due on different dates. The person who applied is now busy with a pilot.

    What it costs you

    The IndiaAI Mission offers a 100% compute subsidy for core foundational-model work and 40% for other GPU-intensive work, per press reports of June 2025. Missed reporting can put the benefit at risk.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each grant or subsidy is a record with terms, reporting dates, supporting documents and an owner. Verity creates reporting tasks ahead of each date and keeps the usage evidence attached.

  14. 14Owner

    The founder cannot see which projects earn

    What it looks like

    Revenue looks healthy. Underneath, one pilot is profitable, one consumes three engineers for little money, and a grant project is quietly paying for the rest. The picture is built by hand on the last weekend of the month.

    What it costs you

    Decisions about hiring and which pilots to chase are made on feel. A loss-making client is kept because nobody can prove it loses.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each project has revenue, compute spend and people time on one line. Verity shows margin by project and client, and lists the projects that have crossed a limit you set.

Rules and paperwork

The rules an AI company has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when a client or regulator asks.

  • Digital Personal Data Protection Act and Rules

    What is expected

    The Rules were notified on 13 November 2025 and phase in. Some parts apply from notification, consent manager rules generally from 13 November 2026, and most remaining duties, including breach intimation and data retention, generally from 13 May 2027. Whether you are a data fiduciary or processor depends on your contracts.

    How Verity helps

    Each client data share and personal data dataset is a record with purpose, access, location and deletion date, so the facts the Rules ask about are on file.

    Hogan Lovells

  • India AI Governance Guidelines

    What is expected

    MeitY released principle-based guidelines in November 2025. They generally rely on existing law and do not create a separate AI statute.

    How Verity helps

    Evaluation checklists, release approvals and a log of client feedback give you a record of the checks you ran and who signed them.

    AZB & Partners summary

  • Copyright and training data

    What is expected

    In ANI v OpenAI, the Delhi High Court on 24 July 2026 refused an interim injunction. The findings are interim and the suit continues to trial, so the position on training data is not final.

    How Verity helps

    Every dataset records its source and licence or contract, and which models used it, so a question about training data can be answered from the record.

    iPleaders blog

  • IndiaAI compute subsidy terms

    What is expected

    Compute subsidies under the IndiaAI Mission were reported at 100% for core foundational-model work and 40% for other GPU-intensive work. Terms and reporting duties depend on your approval.

    How Verity helps

    Each subsidy is a record with terms, reporting dates and usage evidence, and each report is a task with an owner.

    Outlook Business

Rules and court positions change. Confirm current requirements with your lawyer or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions AI company founders ask.

Our engineers hate process. Will they actually log runs?

Only if it takes seconds, so we agree how run results reach Verity during setup rather than asking for typed entries. What people add by hand is short: the project, the dataset version and a line on why.

Does Verity replace our experiment tracker?

No. Your tracker stays where researchers work. Verity holds the business side: which project and client a run serves, what it cost, which dataset it used and who approved release.

Can it track GPU spend across several providers?

Yes. Each provider's invoice is allocated to projects as a record, whether the numbers come in by import or by hand. We agree the route for each during setup.
