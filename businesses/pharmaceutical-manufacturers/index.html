How it runs today

Most pharma plants have a quality system on paper and a lot of people holding it together.

Batch records are issued from a master, filled in by hand on the floor, reviewed by production and then by quality. Between the floor and the file, pages get stuck, signatures are missing and corrections are made late.

Quality control has its own world: sample registers, instrument logs, results, out-of-specification investigations and stability studies. These connect to the batch only when someone writes the batch number on a result.

Deviations, change controls and complaints each have a register. A deviation raised in March may still be open in September because nobody owns the due date.

Revised Schedule M raised the documentation bar. For smaller units the effort is often carried by a few quality and production people who also do their normal jobs.

What most pharmaceutical manufacturers run on

  • Master and batch records on paper
  • QC registers and instrument logs
  • Excel for stock, deviations and training
  • Accounts software
  • WhatsApp and email with customers and suppliers

Each is needed. What is missing is one view that links a batch to its material, tests, deviations and release.

14 problems, one by one

Where a pharma plant actually loses time, stock and standing.

These are the situations plant heads, quality heads and owners describe again and again. For each: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Batch records

    Batch records come back incomplete

    What it looks like

    A batch record returns to quality with two missing signatures, one overwritten entry and a missing weighing slip. Production has moved on to the next batch.

    What it costs you

    A batch that cannot be released waits. Records that look altered also draw questions. Reporting on one Supreme Court matter notes that failures in manufacturing and testing records can amount to an offence under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each batch record is a set of steps with a person, a time and a check. A step cannot be closed without the entry, and a missing step is listed for the shift in charge before the batch moves on.

  2. 02Stores

    Raw materials get used before they are approved

    What it looks like

    A new lot of an excipient arrives in a hurry. The sample goes to QC but the line needs it today. The lot is issued with an under test label that nobody checks.

    What it costs you

    A batch made from an unapproved lot may have to be quarantined or destroyed. The cost is the batch and the trust of the customer.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each material lot has a status: quarantine, approved or rejected. Issuing is blocked unless the lot is approved. QC result entry changes the status, with who and when.

    See the screen
  3. 03Quality control

    The QC result is in one place and the batch in another

    What it looks like

    The assay result for batch 24-118 is on an instrument printout. The batch record has the result written, but not the sample ID. A reviewer cannot tell which sample it was.

    What it costs you

    Review stretches to days. In an audit, a result you cannot trace to a sample is a finding.

    How Verity fixes it

    Samples are recorded against batch and test, with the analyst, the instrument and the result. The batch record shows all results with their sample IDs, and the release screen shows which tests are pending.

    See the screen
  4. 04Quality control

    Out-of-specification results are handled in a hurry

    What it looks like

    A dissolution result comes back low. The analyst repeats the test and records only the repeat. The investigation is a short note.

    What it costs you

    A repeat without a documented reason is a data integrity risk and a likely audit finding.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each out-of-specification result opens an investigation record with its phases: lab check, review, decision. Every retest is linked to the original result and cannot hide it, and open ones are listed by age.

    See the screen
  5. 05Deviations

    Deviations sit open for months

    What it looks like

    A temperature excursion in the packing area was logged in March. The root cause was never agreed, so the corrective action is blank.

    What it costs you

    Open deviations block batch release and show up at audits as a lack of control.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each deviation is a record with batch, area, owner, classification, root cause, action and due date. Verity lists open deviations by age and owner, and links each to the batches it affects.

    See the screen
  6. 06Change control

    Changes are made without a trail

    What it looks like

    A supplier changes, a mixing time is shortened, a new packing line is used. The change is agreed on a call and put into practice the next week.

    What it costs you

    A change nobody assessed can alter product quality and leaves no justification at inspection.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each change is a request with an assessment, approvals by the right roles, and the batches made under it. The master record only changes after approval, and the history stays.

  7. 07Compliance

    Revised Schedule M asks for more than the old paperwork

    What it looks like

    A unit that met the old rules now needs a Pharmaceutical Quality System, quality risk management, qualification and validation, complaint and recall procedures, and change control, as the updated Schedule M expects. The MSME timeline was set to 31 December 2025 for units that filed an upgrade plan.

    What it costs you

    Units that cannot show the system face audits, penalties and possibly closure, per reports on the upgrade timeline.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each requirement is a task with an owner, a due date and evidence. Verity shows readiness by area, the open gaps and the documents each gap needs, so the quality head can show where the plant stands.

    See the screen
  8. 08Equipment

    Equipment calibration and validation lapse quietly

    What it looks like

    A balance or a tablet press was due for calibration last month. The sticker still shows an old date. A batch is weighed on it.

    What it costs you

    Results from an uncalibrated instrument can invalidate a batch. The cost is the batch and an investigation.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each instrument and machine is an asset record with calibration, qualification and maintenance dates. Verity blocks use in the batch record when the date has passed, and opens tasks weeks ahead.

    See the screen
  9. 09People

    Training records do not match who is doing the job

    What it looks like

    A new operator is placed on granulation in his first week. His training on the SOP is dated after the batch. The training matrix is a spreadsheet updated before audits.

    What it costs you

    Work by an untrained person is a finding and, if it affects a batch, a deviation.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each person's SOP training, with version and sign-off, is a record. A batch step asks for someone cleared for that SOP, and Verity lists people whose training is overdue or whose SOP version has changed.

    See the screen
  10. 10Complaints and recall

    A complaint or recall drill is slow

    What it looks like

    A distributor reports a defect in one batch. The team must find the batch, the customers, the remaining stock and the retained samples. It takes two days of calls.

    What it costs you

    A slow response increases exposure to the customer and the regulator. Regulators publish alerts of batches that fail testing, and failures are batch-specific.

    How Verity fixes it

    A complaint is a record linked to batch, customer, dispatch and retained sample. Verity lists the affected batches, stock held, quantity shipped and customers, with tasks for each call.

  11. 11Quality control

    Retained samples and stability studies are hard to track

    What it looks like

    Retained samples are on a shelf in a cold room with handwritten labels. A stability pull is due this week; the person who knows is on leave.

    What it costs you

    A missing retained sample cannot be tested when a complaint arrives. A missed stability pull leaves a gap in the data.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each retained sample and stability pull is a record with batch, storage location and due date. Verity raises tasks at each pull and shows where each sample is.

  12. 12Customers

    Contract and loan-licence customers need documents on time

    What it looks like

    A contract customer wants the batch record summary and certificate for a lot by Friday. Quality is in the middle of an audit.

    What it costs you

    A missed document date delays the customer's release, and the next order goes elsewhere.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each order lists the documents required for release, with status and owner. Verity flags missing ones and shows delivery risk before the date.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a pharma plant has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or regulatory advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Revised Schedule M (GMP)

    What is expected

    Revised Schedule M was notified on 28 December 2023. It expects a pharmaceutical quality system, quality risk management, qualification and validation, complaint and recall procedures and change control, among others. Larger manufacturers had until 28 June 2024, and eligible MSME units until 31 December 2025 after filing an upgrade plan. Check your own category and status with CDSCO.

    How Verity helps

    Each requirement is a task with an owner, a date and evidence, and Verity shows readiness by area.

    India Briefing

  • Manufacturing records under Rule 74 and Schedules M and U

    What is expected

    The Drugs Rules, 1945 and their schedules set documentation duties for manufacturers, including batch and testing records. A reported Supreme Court matter treated non-maintenance and manipulation of such records as a breach under section 18(a)(vi) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, punishable under section 27(d). Check the retention periods in the current rules.

    How Verity helps

    Batch steps, results and sign-offs are recorded with person and time, and corrections are kept as history.

    Raw Law

  • Testing, alerts and recalls

    What is expected

    CDSCO and state laboratories test samples from the market and publish monthly alerts of batches not of standard quality, and these findings are batch-specific. Recall and complaint procedures are part of the GMP expectations.

    How Verity helps

    A complaint or alert is a record linked to the batch, customers and stock, so the response is traceable.

    ETV Bharat

  • Manufacturing licences and renewals

    What is expected

    A pharma unit holds manufacturing licences for its product categories, with renewals and conditions set by the state and central licensing authorities. Confirm your licence terms.

    How Verity helps

    Each licence is a record with number, category, conditions and renewal date, and renewals are tasks with an owner.

  • Payment terms for micro and small suppliers

    What is expected

    From 1 April 2024, a buyer that owes a micro or small seller generally must pay within the agreed period, capped at 45 days, or the amount is not deductible until paid.

    How Verity helps

    Supplier bills show due dates against that limit.

    SetIndiaBiz

Rules change and differ by licence and product type. Confirm current requirements with your regulatory adviser or the licensing authority.

Questions

Questions pharma owners and quality heads ask.

We already have a quality system. Does Verity replace it?

No. Your procedures stay yours. Verity keeps their records connected, so a batch, its materials, results, deviations and sign-offs can be seen together, with dates and owners.

Can it hold electronic batch records?

It records the steps, entries, people and times for a batch, and keeps corrections as history. How that fits your own validation approach is for your quality team; we agree it during setup.

Will operators on the floor really fill it in?

Only if it takes seconds at the point of work, so entry is where the work happens, in steps they already follow. We train during a real batch and start with the records that matter most.

Does it help with revised Schedule M?

It gives you a tracker with an owner, date and evidence for each requirement, and links the records to it. It does not decide whether you comply, and it does not replace your adviser.

Can it block use of an unapproved material lot?

Yes. A lot has a status, and issuing it to a batch is blocked until QC approves it. The change of status records who and when.

Does it replace my accounts software?

No. Your accounts stay where they are. We agree during setup how invoices and payments reach Verity, which then handles batches, quality, documents and dues.

Can I see more than one unit?

Yes. Each is a location in one system, with batches, holds, deviations and dues side by side.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so production, QC, QA, sales and accounts each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How quickly will I see something useful?

The deviation list, calibration and training dates and overdue dues need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. Batch and QC patterns become reliable after a few weeks of recorded work.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of units, people and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your plant runs.
