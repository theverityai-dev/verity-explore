How it runs today

Most firms are good at the law and loose about everything around it.

A matter has many moving parts. A next hearing date, a limitation period, a filing deadline, a client who wants an update, a junior who drafted the reply, a senior who has to settle it, and a bill that has to be raised. In most firms these live in different places.

The court diary is paper or a personal calendar. The matter list is an Excel sheet. Time is remembered at the end of the month. Client money is in the accountant's books and the advocate's head. Drafts move between lawyers on WhatsApp and email with names like "final_v3_revised".

The courts are the reason dates matter so much. Government figures show over 4.76 crore cases pending in district and subordinate courts alone at the end of 2025, so a lawyer's roll is long, listings move often and a single missed date can turn into an adverse order.

What most law firms run on

  • A paper or phone diary for hearing dates
  • Excel sheet of matters and clients
  • WhatsApp groups with clients and juniors
  • Word drafts and email attachments
  • The accountant's ledger for fees and client money

Each tool holds one piece of the matter. Nothing joins them, so the partner has to ask three people to find out where a case stands.

16 problems, one by one

Where a law firm actually loses control of its work.

These are the situations partners, associates and practice managers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Matters

    A next hearing date lives in one lawyer's diary

    What it looks like

    The senior who handles a commercial suit noted the next date in his own diary after the last hearing. He is travelling on that day. The junior only learns of it when the court master calls the matter.

    What it costs you

    A missed or poorly prepared listing can mean an adjournment at cost, an ex parte order or a dismissal for default. The client's trust goes first.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every matter has a record with its forum, case number, stage and next date, set at the end of each hearing. Verity lists the week's dates by lawyer, shows who is appearing and who is preparing, and creates a prep task ahead of each date.

  2. 02Matters

    Limitation and filing deadlines tracked by memory

    What it looks like

    A client brings a cheque dishonour, a money claim and an appeal against an order in the same month. Each has a different clock, running from a different event. The dates are in the file note, in the associate's head and in an email.

    What it costs you

    Limitation is generally strict. The Supreme Court held in 2022 (F. Liansanga v. Union of India) that the power to condone delay under Section 5 of the Limitation Act does not apply to suits, so a late suit may not be rescued.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each matter records the event the clock runs from, the period the lawyer has chosen and the last date to file. Verity counts down from it, shows the nearest dates on the partner's screen and sends tasks at the intervals you set. The lawyer decides the period; Verity makes sure it is not forgotten.

    See the screen
  3. 03Matters

    Supplementary lists and listings that change overnight

    What it looks like

    A matter shows as not listed. At 6 pm a supplementary cause list is posted, and it is now on tomorrow's board. The advocate on record is already at dinner.

    What it costs you

    A late-night listing is a scramble, a junior sent without a brief, or an application nobody prepared.

    How Verity fixes it

    Staff record the listing as soon as it is seen, and Verity reassigns the date, tells the lawyer who is on the record and opens a prep task with the last order, the pending applications and the file attached.

    See the screen
  4. 04Time and billing

    Calls, drafts and research that never become billable time

    What it looks like

    A partner takes a forty-minute client call after court, an associate spends a Sunday on a reply, and a clerk spends the morning at the registry. The timesheet is filled on the last day of the month from memory.

    What it costs you

    Time that is not recorded is not billed. Example arithmetic: 20 hours a month per lawyer left out, across 8 lawyers at an average of ₹2,500 an hour, is ₹4 lakh a month of work given away.

    How Verity fixes it

    Time is logged against the matter in a few taps, at the end of a hearing or a call, with a note. Verity shows each lawyer's hours against what has been billed, and which matters have time that no invoice has covered yet.

    See the screen
  5. 05Time and billing

    Fee disputes because the bill cannot be explained

    What it looks like

    The client questions an invoice that has a lump sum and the words "professional services". Nobody can say how many hours went into the arbitration application, and the client pays late or negotiates down.

    What it costs you

    Partners say justifying hours is a recurring difficulty in Indian practice. A vague bill is paid later, and often for less.

    How Verity fixes it

    Invoices are built from the time entries and expenses recorded on the matter, grouped by task. Verity shows what has been agreed in the engagement, such as a fixed fee, a stage-wise fee or an hourly rate, and compares it with actual hours as they build up.

    See the screen
  6. 06Money

    Fees and disbursements that are billed late or never chased

    What it looks like

    Court fees, stamp duty and travel are paid from the firm's pocket for months. A final bill goes out after the judgment. By then the client's urgency has gone and so has the willingness to pay.

    What it costs you

    Out-of-pocket expenses and old invoices tie up cash. Example arithmetic: ₹18 lakh outstanding across 40 clients for over 90 days is a month's payroll that you are lending out.

    How Verity fixes it

    Expenses are recorded against the matter on the day they are paid and flagged for recovery. Verity ages unpaid invoices by client, lists the ones past due, and gives the partner a follow-up task for each, with the last reminder noted.

    See the screen
  7. 07Client money

    Client money without a clean ledger

    What it looks like

    A client deposits ₹5 lakh for a settlement and another pays an advance on fees. The amounts sit in the firm's account, are partly used for expenses and are matched to matters only when the accountant asks.

    What it costs you

    Advocates are generally expected under the Bar Council of India Rules to keep accounts of money entrusted by clients. Mixing client funds with the firm's own and being unable to show a balance is a professional conduct risk, not just an accounting one.

    How Verity fixes it

    Money received for a client is a separate ledger record against the matter, with the purpose, the date and the person who recorded it. Each use is recorded with the matter and a reason, and the balance for each client is always visible to the partner and the accountant.

    See the screen
  8. 08Intake

    Conflicts found after the engagement letter is signed

    What it looks like

    A new client wants the firm to act against a company that another partner advised two years ago. The only record is a closed file in a cabinet, and the intake lawyer does not know that partner's clients.

    What it costs you

    Acting for conflicting interests breaches professional rules, can force the firm to withdraw mid-matter and may cost both clients.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every client, opposite party, co-party and related person is recorded on the matter. At intake Verity searches names across all current and past matters and shows any match before the engagement is accepted, for a partner to decide.

    See the screen
  9. 09Intake

    Enquiries that wait on a phone for days

    What it looks like

    A founder messages a partner about a notice at 9 pm. The partner replies two days later. The founder has already spoken to another firm.

    What it costs you

    A new matter from a good client is the most valuable thing the firm gets, and it is lost to slow replies, not to price.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each enquiry is a record with its source, the nature of the matter, who is handling it and the next step. Verity shows the ones with no reply, tells the person responsible, and records whether the firm was engaged. Staff and partners still speak to the client.

    See the screen
  10. 10Documents

    Drafts in four versions and a bundle that is never complete

    What it looks like

    The petition is in the associate's laptop as v3, in the partner's email as "final", and the filed copy is on the clerk's pen drive. Annexures are renumbered at the last minute.

    What it costs you

    An old version filed by mistake, annexures that do not match the index, or a document that cannot be found when the court asks for it.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every document is a record with a version history, an owner and a state: draft, settled, filed. Verity keeps the filed copy with the date, links annexures to the matter, and shows who changed what and when.

    See the screen
  11. 11Documents

    Files that cannot be found when a lawyer leaves

    What it looks like

    A senior associate resigns. Her matters were run from her personal drive and a phone. The partner takes a week to rebuild what is pending for each client.

    What it costs you

    Handover delays, client questions nobody can answer, and the real risk that something falls through the gap between two lawyers.

    How Verity fixes it

    Matters, notes, dates and documents sit in the firm's record, not in a person's device. Reassigning a matter moves its dates, tasks and history to the new lawyer, and the access of the person leaving is closed in one step.

  12. 12People

    Associate workload is guessed

    What it looks like

    One associate has 40 matters and a hearing every day, another has 15. The partner assigns the next brief to whoever answers first.

    What it costs you

    Burnout in one place, idle time in another, and quality that depends on who happened to be free.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity shows each lawyer's open matters, hearings this week, pending drafts and logged hours side by side. Partners assign new work and rebalance from that view.

    See the screen
  13. 13Clients

    Client updates that rely on the partner's memory

    What it looks like

    A client calls to ask where the matter stands. The partner is in court and nobody else can say what happened at the last hearing or what is next.

    What it costs you

    A client who has to chase for news feels ignored, even when the work is going well.

    How Verity fixes it

    After each hearing the lawyer records the order and the next step on the matter. Anyone in the firm can see it, and a task prompts someone to update the client after every date. Staff send the update; Verity makes sure it is sent.

    See the screen
  14. 14Compliance

    Client data and the new data protection law

    What it looks like

    Client IDs, financial statements and medical records are in email attachments, on personal phones and in shared folders that anyone in the firm can open.

    What it costs you

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with most duties for organisations that handle personal data due to start about 18 months later. A firm that cannot say who has seen a client's papers will find it hard to meet them.

    How Verity fixes it

    Documents and client details carry permissions by role and matter, and every view and change is logged. The record of who accessed what exists without any extra work.

  15. 15Matters

    Opposite party, witness and expert details scattered

    What it looks like

    The expert's number, the opposing counsel's, the witness's address and the court clerk's contact are in different phones.

    What it costs you

    Time lost on every date, and a gap when the lawyer who knows these people is away.

    How Verity fixes it

    People linked to a matter, such as opposing counsel, experts, witnesses and registry contacts, are records on that matter with role and contact. Anyone covering the matter finds them in one place.

    The partner cannot see the whole firm at a glance

    What it looks like

    At month end the managing partner asks for the matter count, the billed amount and the dues. Three people spend a day producing three different answers.

    What it costs you

    Decisions about hiring, fees and which practice areas to grow are made on feeling.

    How Verity fixes it

    The owner's screen shows live dates due, hours logged against billed, receivables, client balances and workload by lawyer, from the same records the team already updates.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a law firm has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not searching when someone asks.

  • Limitation periods

    What is expected

    The Limitation Act, 1963 sets time limits for suits, appeals and applications, and courts generally dismiss late filings. The Supreme Court held in 2022 that Section 5 condonation does not apply to suits. The lawyer decides the period for each matter.

    How Verity helps

    Each matter records the event the clock runs from and the last date to file, with reminders at set intervals.

    The Indian Lawyer, 2022

  • Bar Council of India Rules on client money and conflicts

    What is expected

    The Rules framed under the Advocates Act, 1961 are generally understood to require advocates to account for client money, to disclose connections with other parties and not to act for the opposite party in the same matter. Questions

Questions lawyers ask.

Will Verity decide the limitation period for a matter?

No. The lawyer decides it. Verity records the event, the period you chose and the last date, and reminds the right people so the date is not forgotten.

Does it replace the court cause list or e-courts?

No. You keep using them. Staff record a listing in Verity as soon as it is seen, and Verity turns it into a task and a date on the right lawyer's screen.

I am a small firm with three lawyers. Is this too much?

A small practice has the same dates and the same bills, and fewer people to remember them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Can it keep client money separate from the firm's?

Yes, as records. Each client has a ledger with deposits, uses and a balance, and an overdrawn or low balance is flagged. Verity does not hold money or replace your bank and accountant.

How does the conflict check work?

Every client, opposite party and related person is a record on a matter. At intake, Verity searches current and past matters for the same or similar names and shows the match. A partner makes the decision.

Will my associates log their time?

Only if it takes seconds, so we set it up as a few taps at the end of a hearing or a call, with a note. We start with the time codes that matter, not a long form.

Can Verity prepare my invoices and GST returns?

It builds the invoice lines from time and expenses on the matter, and your accountant prepares returns from the figures. It does not file returns.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and matter, so a lawyer outside the team cannot open a file, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.

Can I move to Verity in the middle of a busy season?

Yes. We bring live matters in first, with their next dates, and check each date with the lawyer. Older closed matters can follow in stages.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of lawyers, offices and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your firm runs.
