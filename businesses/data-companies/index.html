How it runs today

Most data firms track volume well and quality loosely.

The work is counted in units: images, documents, minutes or records. Volumes are in a tool, and instructions are in a document that changes. Quality is checked by sampling, and feedback arrives by email.

The people are many and the turnover is high. One 2026 report on annotators described pay of ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month, strict targets and software that monitors speed and accuracy, so performance data matters, and so does how it is used.

The commercial pressure is real. A 2026 industry page says high volume commitments compress quality review cycles, and that hundreds of vendors compete with few shared quality frameworks.

What most data companies run on

  • Annotation or work tools from each client
  • Instruction documents and chat groups
  • Spreadsheets for output and quality
  • Attendance and payroll sheets
  • Email for client feedback

Each tool shows one slice. None of them shows whether a project makes money, which team is slipping on quality or which client data is still on a machine it should not be.

14 problems, one by one

Where a data company loses margin, quality and trust.

These are the situations data firm owners and delivery heads describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Projects

    Guidelines change and the team works from the old version

    What it looks like

    A client revises the labelling rules on day six. The update goes to a chat group. Half the team keeps working to the old version for a day.

    What it costs you

    A batch with inconsistent labels, rework at your cost and a quality complaint that is hard to explain.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each project holds its guidelines as versions with the date they took effect. A change creates a task for team leads to brief their teams and confirm, and work is tagged to the version it followed.

    See the screen
  2. 02Quality

    Throughput targets squeeze quality checks

    What it looks like

    The client wants 40,000 items by Friday. Review is the first thing cut so the target is met.

    What it costs you

    High-volume commitments compress review cycles, and the errors appear when the client audits. The cost of rework and lost trust is higher than the review time saved.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each batch shows items done, items reviewed and error rate. Verity warns when review coverage falls below the level you set before a batch is sent.

    See the screen
  3. 03People

    Annotator performance is measured, but not fairly or usefully

    What it looks like

    Speed is tracked and accuracy is sampled. A new hire is compared with a veteran and a slow week is read as a poor worker.

    What it costs you

    Good people leave. In one 2026 report annotators described strict targets and monitoring by software, with pay of ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month.

    How Verity fixes it

    Output and accuracy are shown per person by task type and by week, with experience and project difficulty alongside. Leads see trends and coaching needs, not only a red cell.

    See the screen
  4. 04Projects

    Work queues and deadlines are juggled by hand

    What it looks like

    Five projects share one floor. A team lead moves people between queues by feel. A deadline is missed because a queue was drained too slowly.

    What it costs you

    Late deliveries, overtime and idle people on a different project.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each project has a queue of work, a deadline and an allocation of people. Verity shows queue size, daily output and the date the batch will finish at the current pace.

    See the screen
  5. 05Quality

    Error types are not categorised, so the same mistakes repeat

    What it looks like

    Reviewers mark items wrong. The reasons are in comments and nobody counts them.

    What it costs you

    The same error is made again by many people, and coaching is generic.

    How Verity fixes it

    Reviewers choose an error type when they mark an item. Verity shows common errors by project, person and guideline section, so fixes aim at the cause.

    See the screen
  6. 06Money

    Projects are priced per unit and nobody knows the real unit cost

    What it looks like

    A project is sold at ₹4 per item. Rework, review, tool time and idle hours are not added up.

    What it costs you

    Projects that look busy and lose money. Throughput pressure and many competing vendors make price cuts tempting.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each project tracks units delivered, hours spent by role, rework and tool cost. Verity shows cost per accepted unit against the price.

    See the screen
  7. 07Delivery

    Client acceptance is slow and disputed

    What it looks like

    A batch is delivered. The client takes three weeks to review and rejects 6 percent with a spreadsheet of comments. The invoice waits.

    What it costs you

    Cash held up by acceptance and disputes with no record of what was delivered and when.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each delivery records items, the version, the date and the client's acceptance or rejection with reasons. Rejected items become rework tasks and invoices follow accepted units.

    See the screen
  8. 08Security

    Client data spreads onto machines and chats

    What it looks like

    A client shares sensitive documents. Annotators view them on shared machines and a team lead forwards a sample to ask a question.

    What it costs you

    A leak ends a client relationship and may breach the contract. Data privacy concerns in sectors like banking and healthcare can require on-shore delivery.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each project records what data it holds, who has access, where it may be processed and when it must be deleted. Access is removed when a person leaves a project, and a log shows who opened what.

    See the screen
  9. 09People

    Domain experts and language reviewers are scarce

    What it looks like

    A medical imaging project needs reviewers with a clinical background. Only two are available and both are booked on another project.

    What it costs you

    Delays or reviews done by people who lack the skill, and quality that suffers for the sensitive part of the work.

    How Verity fixes it

    People have skills, languages and certifications recorded. Verity shows who can review a type of work and their load, so scarce reviewers are planned.

    See the screen
  10. 10Projects

    Tools and licences are tracked per client

    What it looks like

    Each client asks for a different annotation tool. Licences and seats are bought, and some stay unused after a project ends.

    What it costs you

    Money spent on seats nobody uses and delays when a person has no access on day one.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each tool and its seats are records linked to projects and people. Verity lists unused seats and projects that need access.

    See the screen
  11. 11Delivery

    Recurring data deliveries and refresh cycles are scheduled in calendars

    What it looks like

    A client buys a dataset refreshed monthly. A delivery date slips because the source update was late and nobody told the client.

    What it costs you

    A client who counts missed refreshes at renewal and credits that are argued.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each recurring delivery has a schedule, an owner and checks before it ships. A late source creates a task, and a renewal list shows what each client has received.

    See the screen
  12. 12Money

    Invoices follow memory, not accepted work

    What it looks like

    Invoices are raised from a spreadsheet of units. Some include items later rejected, some leave out accepted items, and a foreign client pays late.

    What it costs you

    Credit notes, delayed cash and an accounts team that reconciles by hand.

    How Verity fixes it

    Billable units come from accepted deliveries. Verity lists work accepted and not invoiced, and invoices overdue by client, with a task for the account owner. Billing stays in your accounts software.

    See the screen
  13. 13Compliance

    Personal data duties are written, not tracked

    What it looks like

    Contracts say the firm will delete data at the end and report any breach. Nobody can show that it did.

    What it costs you

    A client audit that fails, or a dispute after an incident. India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, with most duties applying over the next 12 to 18 months.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each project has deletion dates, breach contacts and the steps to follow. Verity lists projects whose data is due for deletion and records that it was done.

    See the screen
  14. 14People

    Hiring and training come in waves

    What it looks like

    A large new project needs 80 people in two weeks. Hiring, training and quality ramp-up are tracked in separate sheets.

    What it costs you

    New people on live work before they are ready, and early errors that hurt a new client's first impression.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each batch of new people has a training plan, assessments and a probation period. A person starts live work only after the assessment, and early quality is tracked separately.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a data company has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules and your clients' contracts depend on in order.

  • Personal data you process for clients

    What is expected

    India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, and most duties start to apply over the following 12 to 18 months. The client who decides why data is processed remains responsible for what a processor does on its behalf, so clients will ask what you do.

    How Verity helps

    Each project records what data it holds, who has access, where it is processed and when it is deleted.

    AZB Partners

  • Client contracts and confidentiality

    What is expected

    Each contract sets the guideline version, quality level, security terms and deletion duties. A breach can mean credits or termination.

    How Verity helps

    Terms sit on the project and are compared with delivery and access records.

  • On-shore or in-country delivery

    What is expected

    Data privacy concerns in sectors such as banking and healthcare may require work to be done in specific locations.

    How Verity helps

    Each project records where data may be processed, and people and machines are assigned only to the allowed places.

    Nasscom

  • Working hours and staff records

    What is expected

    A 2026 report described long screen hours, strict targets and few paid leaves at one firm. State shop and establishment rules and wage laws set limits, and records are what show you comply.

    How Verity helps

    Attendance, shifts and leave are recorded per person.

    ThePrint

Rules and client requirements differ by sector and contract, and they change. Confirm current requirements with your lawyer and your clients.

Questions

Questions data company owners ask.

Does Verity replace my annotation tool?

No. Your annotation tools stay as they are. We agree during setup how output and quality results reach Verity, so you see projects, people, quality and delivery in one place.

Will team leads and reviewers use it?

It works only if recording takes seconds, so we set it up around the sheets and checks they already use. They get a clear view of their queue and their team's quality.

Can Verity measure my annotators?

It shows output and accuracy per person with experience and task type alongside. How you use it is your call, and the system supports coaching as well as targets.

Does it hold client data?

No. It records which data each project holds, who has access and when it must be deleted. The data itself stays in your secure systems.

Can it help with invoicing?

Yes. It lists work accepted and not invoiced and invoices overdue. Billing stays in your accounts software.

I run two sites and a remote team. Can I see all of it?

Yes. Each site is a location in one system, and projects, people and quality can be compared side by side.

Can it send reports to my clients?

Not by itself. It tracks delivery, acceptance and rework, and your team sends the reports and records the date.

Who can see what?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and by project, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of users and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your firm runs.
