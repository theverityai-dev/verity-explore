How it runs today

The agency's most valuable asset is in people's phones.

A PR agency of ten to fifty people serves a dozen or more clients, each with announcements, spokespeople and a monthly report to show. Media relationships are held by individuals. A senior consultant knows which reporter covers funding, who prefers a call and who was burned by a late embargo. When she leaves, the knowledge leaves.

The working records are in pieces. Media lists are Excel sheets that differ by person. Pitches go out from personal email and WhatsApp. Coverage is saved as screenshots and links. The retainer is a monthly fee that has not changed since the day it was signed, while the work has grown.

So the agency learns late. Two team members pitch the same reporter on the same day. A client is told coverage is "coming" with no record of who was pitched. A monthly report is built in a night from folders. And a retainer sold for 20 hours takes 45. A trade survey of Indian PR professionals in 2025 found two in three say securing editorial coverage has become harder, and 66% find outdated client expectations a major challenge.

What most PR agencies run on

  • Excel media lists per team
  • Personal email and WhatsApp for pitches
  • Shared folders of clippings
  • A media monitoring service
  • Timesheets or none

Each one does its job. Nothing joins the journalist, the pitch, the coverage and the retainer.

13 problems, one by one

Where a PR agency actually loses money.

These are the situations PR founders and account heads describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Media lists

    Media lists differ by person

    What it looks like

    Four consultants each keep their own list of tech reporters. One has a new number for a reporter, another still uses the old one. Nobody knows who left which publication.

    What it costs you

    Pitches go to the wrong person or a dead email, and a good story gets no coverage because the contact was out of date.

    How Verity fixes it

    Journalists are records with beat, outlet, contact details, preferred way to be reached, and last verified date. Everyone works from the same list, and a record not checked for six months is flagged.

  2. 02Media lists

    The same reporter is pitched twice in a day

    What it looks like

    A business reporter gets a funding story from one consultant at 10 am and an unrelated product pitch from another at 4 pm. She replies to neither and marks both as noise.

    What it costs you

    A relationship built over years is spent on careless contact. Reporters talk to each other about agencies that spam.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every pitch is logged on the journalist's record with the client and the result. Before a pitch is sent, the consultant sees contact in the last 14 days and who made it.

    See the screen
  3. 03Pitches

    Pitch follow-ups depend on memory

    What it looks like

    A pitch went out on Tuesday. The plan was to call on Thursday. Thursday brings a client crisis and the follow-up is never made.

    What it costs you

    A story that needed one nudge lapses. The client asks why nothing has run and the honest answer is that nobody called.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each pitch has a follow-up date and an owner. Verity lists pitches awaiting a response, due today, and marks the outcome: covered, declined, no response.

    See the screen
  4. 04Pitches

    Embargoes and announcement dates collide

    What it looks like

    Two clients plan announcements for the same Tuesday. A third client's embargoed news has to break at 6 am. Briefings are being given by the same person.

    What it costs you

    A clash dilutes both stories. A broken embargo damages trust with reporters and the client.

    How Verity fixes it

    Announcements are on a shared calendar with date, time, embargo state, spokesperson and the consultant responsible. Clashes show as the plan is made, and a story with an embargo is marked until release.

    See the screen
  5. 05Approvals

    Spokespeople and approvals

    What it looks like

    A quote for a founder goes to the client by chat. The founder edits it twice by voice note. The version sent to the reporter is the first one.

    What it costs you

    A misquote or an unapproved line printed under the client's name is hard to take back.

    How Verity fixes it

    Statements and releases have versions and a named client approver. Only the approved version can be marked as sent, and the approval date stays on the record.

    See the screen
  6. 06Coverage

    Coverage is collected by hand on the 30th

    What it looks like

    On the last day of the month, an executive searches each outlet for the client's name, takes screenshots and pastes links into a sheet. Some pieces are missed. Others are old.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 15 clients at 5 hours each is 75 hours a month, nearly two weeks of one person, on a task that proves value but builds none.

    How Verity fixes it

    Coverage is logged as it appears, against the client, the pitch it came from and the journalist. The monthly view builds from those records, so the report starts on day 1 and not day 30.

    See the screen
  7. 07Coverage

    Nobody can show what the coverage was worth

    What it looks like

    A client's CMO asks, "We got 40 mentions. So what?" The agency shows a pile of links. Only 46% of corporate communication respondents in a PRCAI study said they have a separate measurement budget, and 92% said the industry needs a clear, standard measurement system.

    What it costs you

    Without agreed measures, the client judges PR by vibes and the retainer is the first cost cut in a bad quarter.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client's goals are set on the record at the start: key messages, target outlets and spokespeople. Every piece of coverage is tagged against them, so the monthly view shows what moved against what was agreed.

    See the screen
  8. 08Retainers

    The retainer that never rises while scope does

    What it looks like

    A retainer was agreed at ₹80,000 a month for media relations. It now includes social posts, event support, a founder's LinkedIn ghostwriting and an investor note.

    What it costs you

    One PR founder's published opinion piece puts it plainly: the scope keeps growing while the retainer stays the same. Example arithmetic: 55 hours against 30 sold, at ₹900 an hour of cost, is ₹22,500 a month absorbed.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each retainer lists its inclusions and sold hours. Time is logged against the client as work is done, and Verity shows hours used against hours sold and any extra work waiting for an approval before it begins.

    See the screen
  9. 09Retainers

    Clients with outdated expectations

    What it looks like

    A client expects a front-page story every month because that is what PR meant ten years ago. The agency is delivering relevant coverage in the right outlets but cannot show why that is better.

    What it costs you

    In a 2025 India survey, 66% of PR professionals found managing outdated client expectations a major challenge. The mismatch ends in a lost client.

    How Verity fixes it

    The goal and agreed outlets are on the client record from the first week. Reviews are scheduled and recorded, with notes on what was agreed, so expectations are reset against a written record.

    See the screen
  10. 10Crisis

    A crisis starts on a Saturday night

    What it looks like

    A negative post about a client goes viral at 10 pm. The only person with the client's crisis contacts is on a flight. The statement draft is in her laptop.

    What it costs you

    The first few hours decide the story. Delay and confusion make it larger.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client has a crisis record with named contacts, approvers, a holding statement and an escalation order. When an issue is raised, Verity creates tasks for each role and keeps one log of what was said and approved.

    See the screen
  11. 11Compliance

    Influencer and sponsored content without the right label

    What it looks like

    A creator campaign for a client goes out with the disclosure buried in a hashtag block.

    What it costs you

    ASCI's scorecard found 69 of the top 100 influencers failed its disclosure guidelines, so the risk is common. A complaint names the brand and the agency that set it up.

    How Verity fixes it

    Creator and sponsored posts have a checklist for disclosure and claims, a reviewer and the live link recorded. The task closes only after the check.

    See the screen
  12. 12Compliance

    Journalists' personal details and client data

    What it looks like

    A media list with personal phone numbers is forwarded by email to a freelancer. A client's investor list is saved on three laptops.

    What it costs you

    The DPDP Rules, notified in November 2025, set duties on personal data, with most starting about 18 months after publication. Loose copies of lists become a risk for you and your clients.

    How Verity fixes it

    Lists are records with permissions and an audit trail. Retention dates are set, and a task appears to remove access for a freelancer when a project closes.

    See the screen
  13. 13Growth

    Pitching for new business while serving old

    What it looks like

    The founder is pitching a new client and also handling a crisis for an old one. The proposal is late, and the team's hours for the pitch are not counted anywhere.

    What it costs you

    The agency's new business depends on the same few seniors who also hold the key relationships. Overload loses both.

    How Verity fixes it

    New business is a pipeline with stage, value, owner and next step. Load by person is visible next to live work, so the pitch is staffed knowingly.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a PR agency has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • ASCI and CCPA rules on sponsored and influencer content

    What is expected

    ASCI's influencer guidelines and the CCPA guidelines generally require prominent disclosure of any material connection, such as a paid or gifted post. ASCI found 69 of the top 100 influencers failed in its 2025 scorecard.

    How Verity helps

    Creator and sponsored posts have a checklist with a reviewer and the live link recorded.

    exchange4media on ASCI study

  • Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025

    What is expected

    Notified on 14 November 2025. Most duties, including notice, security safeguards, breach reporting and retention, generally apply 18 months after publication. Media lists and investor lists hold personal data.

    How Verity helps

    Lists are records with permissions and an audit trail, with retention dates and removal tasks.

    Scconline on DPDP Rules

  • Client contract, confidentiality and embargo terms

    What is expected

    Your contract generally decides what the retainer includes, confidentiality and how embargoed news is handled. Verity does not draft contracts.

    How Verity helps

    Embargo dates and terms are on each announcement record, and the contract date and inclusions are on the retainer.

  • GST and TDS on professional fees

    What is expected

    PR fees generally attract GST, and clients may deduct TDS on professional fees. Your accountant decides and files.

    How Verity helps

    Each retainer and project record shows fee, billing date and payment state for your accountant.

Rules change and differ by situation. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or lawyer.

Questions

Questions PR agency owners ask.

My consultants guard their media contacts. Will they put them in a shared list?

Contacts are visible to the agency, but each journalist shows its relationship owner, so credit and responsibility stay clear. It also protects the agency when someone leaves. We agree the rules with you during setup.

Does Verity send pitches or press notes?

No. Your team sends them from your own email or phone. Verity records the pitch, sets the follow-up and shows the result.

Does it track coverage by itself?

No. You keep your monitoring service. We agree how coverage reaches Verity, and your team tags it against the client's goals.

Can it measure PR value?

It shows coverage against the goals and outlets you agreed with the client, by message, outlet and spokesperson. Money values and scores depend on measures you choose, and we set up only those.

How does it help with scope creep on retainers?

Each retainer lists what is included. Hours are logged against the client, and extra work needs an approval step before it starts.

Can freelancers use it?

Yes, with access to only their own tasks and the files they need. Access is removed when the project closes.

We are a team of eight. Is this too much?

A small team has the same duplicate pitches and growing retainers, with fewer people to notice. We set up only what you need in the same four weeks.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and client, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of people, clients and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your agency runs.
