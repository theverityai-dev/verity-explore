How it runs today

Most contractors know their sites well and their cash badly.

The site is the visible part. The shuttering, the bar-bending, the concrete pour, the finishing gang. What is hard to see is the money behind it: which measurement has been certified, which running bill is with the client's accounts, which retention is held for how long, and what each site really cost against what it will bill.

That information is spread out. The supervisor has a site diary and a photo of the measurement. The engineer has a measurement sheet. The accountant has a bill folder. Material goes out on a slip. The owner has the memory of who owes what.

Delays in client payments are a theme in the news. In September 2026, the Karnataka State Contractors' Association said about Rs 38,000 crore of bills were pending across eight state departments. That is one state and an association's claim, but it describes the pressure many contractors work under.

What most contractors run on

  • Site diary or supervisor's notebook
  • Measurement sheets in Excel
  • Bill folder and the accountant's ledger
  • WhatsApp groups with crews and suppliers
  • Material issue slips

Each one works. None of them tells you that site B has been measured but not billed for three weeks.

16 problems, one by one

Where a contracting business actually loses money.

These are the situations owners, site engineers and accountants describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary week, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Measurements

    Work done is not measured on time

    What it looks like

    The slab was cast on the 4th. The client's engineer is busy and the joint measurement happens on the 19th. By then the shuttering is stripped and the reinforcement cannot be re-checked. The numbers are argued over.

    What it costs you

    Every day between work and measurement is a day of delay in the bill, and measurements argued late tend to go against the contractor.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each item of work has a measurement task with the quantity done, the date, the photo and the client engineer who must sign. Verity lists measurements pending for more than a set number of days, by site.

  2. 02Measurements

    Measurements are taken but never become a bill

    What it looks like

    The site engineer has a signed measurement sheet in a bag. The accountant is waiting for it. The next running bill is due on the 25th and half the work is still not in it.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹18 lakh of work measured and not billed for a month, on borrowed money at 18 percent a year, costs about ₹27,000 in interest, before any dispute.

    How Verity fixes it

    A signed measurement turns into a bill line on the site's next running bill. Verity shows work measured, work billed and the difference for each site, so a gap is seen the week it opens.

    See the screen
  3. 03Bills

    Running bills sit with the client for months

    What it looks like

    Bill 7 for the commercial block was submitted on 5 August. It is with the client's engineer, then with accounts, then with a director. Nobody on your side knows where it is.

    What it costs you

    A 2026 report on about 1.1 lakh Indian MSMEs found invoices were settled in about 73 days on average. For a contractor on a state or large private job, the wait can be longer.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every bill has a stage: submitted, checked, certified, passed, paid, with the date it moved and the person you spoke to. Verity lists bills stuck longer than your limit and creates a follow-up task for the person who handles that client.

    See the screen
  4. 04Retention

    Retention money is held and nobody tracks the release

    What it looks like

    Five percent of every running bill has been held across three projects for two years. The defects period ended on one of them in March. Nobody applied for the release.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 5 percent of a ₹4 crore job is ₹20 lakh, locked until the defects period is over and the client certifies it. Contract terms differ, for example some tenders release half at virtual completion and the rest after the defects period.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each contract records the retention terms: percentage, when half is due, the defects period end and the release condition. Verity shows retention held by project and date released is due, and creates a task to apply before the date.

    See the screen
  5. 05Variations

    Variations and extra work are done on a phone call

    What it looks like

    The client's site engineer asks for an extra wall and a changed toilet layout on a call. Your crew does it that evening. The written instruction never comes. Three months later the client says it was in scope.

    What it costs you

    Extra work that is not instructed in writing is hard to bill. It consumes crews, materials and days, and is one of the most common sources of disputes.

    How Verity fixes it

    A variation is a record with the instruction, the person who gave it, a photo, an estimate and a status of instructed or not. Work on an uninstructed variation is flagged to the project manager, and the approved value flows into the next bill.

    See the screen
  6. 06Materials

    Material is issued to site without a record

    What it looks like

    Cement goes from the store to site three on a loose slip. Steel leaves for site one but is used on site two. At the end of the job the bill of quantities and the stock disagree.

    What it costs you

    Material cost is typically the largest cost on a job, and loss shows only at the end when it can no longer be recovered.

    How Verity fixes it

    The store records each issue against a site and a work item, and each return. Verity compares material issued with the quantity the measured work should have used, and shows the gap by site and by item.

    See the screen
  7. 07Materials

    Supplier prices rise in the middle of a fixed-price job

    What it looks like

    The contract was priced in March. Steel and cement rise through the year. As of March 2022, cement and steel costs had risen by over 20 per cent every year according to a Colliers estimate. The client will not pay a revision.

    What it costs you

    A fixed contract price with a moving material cost shrinks the margin with every purchase, and the loss is clear only at the final account.

    How Verity fixes it

    Purchases record the rate by supplier and item against the contract's estimated rate. Verity flags when the rate paid goes above what the estimate assumed, with the quantity still to buy, so the owner sees the effect while a price escalation claim can still be made.

    See the screen
  8. 08Crews

    Crews, attendance and wages are on paper

    What it looks like

    The supervisor's notebook shows 38 workers on Monday. The labour contractor's claim shows 44. Wages are paid on Saturday, and arguments follow.

    What it costs you

    Overpaying labour by even three or four heads a day, across sites, is a quiet and steady loss.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each site has an attendance record by crew and trade, taken once a day on the supervisor's phone or sheet. Verity compares days worked with the labour contractor's claim and flags differences before wages are paid.

    See the screen
  9. 09Crews

    Sub-contractors are paid without checking the work

    What it looks like

    The plastering sub-contractor asks for ₹2.4 lakh as the next payment. The supervisor thinks he has done about 70 percent. No joint measurement was taken.

    What it costs you

    Paying more than the work done leaves nothing to hold when quality goes wrong or the sub-contractor leaves.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each sub-contractor has a work order with a rate and quantity. Payments are made against measured work, with a retention you set. Verity shows work done, amount paid and the balance for each sub-contractor.

    See the screen
  10. 10Sites

    Several sites and no single view of progress

    What it looks like

    The owner runs five sites. One is ahead, one is stuck waiting for drawings, one is waiting for a pour. Each supervisor calls in the evening and the owner builds the picture in their head.

    What it costs you

    Problems that were small on Monday are large on Friday. A crew stands idle on one site while another site is short.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each site is a location with its tasks, progress against plan, issues raised and next milestones. The owner sees which sites are on track and which have blockers, with the person responsible.

    See the screen
  11. 11Sites

    Drawings and instructions change and the crew builds the old one

    What it looks like

    The architect revised the staircase. The supervisor has the old drawing in his drawer. The crew builds it.

    What it costs you

    Demolition and rework, a client dispute and a day of crews wasted.

    How Verity fixes it

    The latest drawing per item is a record on the site. The supervisor sees what is current, and when a new revision arrives, a task asks the supervisor to confirm the older one is withdrawn.

    See the screen
  12. 12Equipment

    Machines and tools are not tracked

    What it looks like

    A concrete mixer, a vibrator and scaffolding sets move between sites. The mixer breaks and nobody knows which site had it last or when it was serviced.

    What it costs you

    Hire costs for replacements, idle crews and equipment lost between sites.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each machine is an asset record with the site it is on, hours used, service dates and the person responsible. A service due date becomes a task, and moves between sites are recorded.

  13. 13Compliance

    Statutory labour paperwork piles up

    What it looks like

    A project with ten or more workers needs registrations and welfare cess handled, and the labour paperwork differs by state. The accountant is asking for it on the day of an inspection.

    What it costs you

    A cess payable on the cost of construction, penalties for missed filings and an inspection that finds no records.

    How Verity fixes it

    Registrations, cess payments and returns are records with dates and owners. Each project has the tasks that apply to it, such as advance cess and the final assessment, with the proof attached.

  14. 14New work

    Tender and enquiry follow-ups are missed

    What it looks like

    A tender closes on Thursday. The earnest money, the experience certificates and the price sheet are in three places. Another enquiry from a developer has had no reply in ten days.

    What it costs you

    A tender submitted late or incomplete is lost, and a developer who gets no reply goes elsewhere.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each tender and enquiry is a record with a closing date, documents needed, an owner and a next step. Verity lists items with missing documents and enquiries with no reply, and records the result.

  15. 15Money

    Cash gets tight and the owner learns too late

    What it looks like

    Wages are due Saturday, a steel supplier wants payment, and the next client payment is promised for Monday. The owner arranges a short loan.

    What it costs you

    Short loans at high interest, and sometimes work stopped on site because of a cash gap that was visible two weeks earlier.

    How Verity fixes it

    Expected receipts from bills in each stage and expected payments for wages, suppliers and sub-contractors are shown by week. The owner sees the week the cash dips and which bill to chase.

  16. 16Money

    Every site's profit is known only at the end

    What it looks like

    The accounts show total purchases and wages. Nobody can say if the school building job is making or losing money until the final bill.

    What it costs you

    A loss is found when it can no longer be fixed, and the next tender is priced without lessons.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each site shows billed, received, cost to date for material, labour and sub-contractors, and the estimated cost to finish. The owner sees margin by site while there is still time to act.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a contracting business has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Construction welfare cess

    What is expected

    Under the Code on Social Security, 2020, a cess of 1 percent of the cost of construction is levied, fixed by a notification dated 8 May 2026. It is generally paid in advance on a self-assessment certified by a chartered engineer, with a final assessment within 60 days of completing each project. Land cost is generally excluded.

    How Verity helps

    Each project has the cess steps as tasks with dates and the proof of payment attached.

    Taxmann on BOCW cess

  • Worker registration and welfare boards

    What is expected

    Establishments doing construction work with ten or more workers generally need to register with the state building workers welfare board, and each state has its own board and procedures.

    How Verity helps

    Registration numbers and renewal dates are stored on the site record, with tasks before they lapse.

  • GST on works contracts

    What is expected

    Works contract services generally attract GST at 18 percent, with concessional rates for specified government works, and clients that are government departments may deduct tax at source. The rules are detailed, so confirm the rate for each contract with your accountant.

    How Verity helps

    Each contract and bill records the GST treatment your accountant sets, and tax deducted by clients is recorded against the bill so it is not missed.

    GST Council, works contract services note

  • Contract retention and security deposit terms

    What is expected

    Retention, security deposits and defects periods are set in each contract, not by one law. Examples in public tender documents deduct a fixed share of each running bill and release it in parts after completion and after the defects period.

    How Verity helps

    Each contract stores its own terms, and release dates become tasks.

Rules change and differ by state and by contract. Confirm current requirements with your accountant, your lawyer and the relevant authority.

Questions contractors ask.

My supervisors are not computer people. Will they use this?

The daily record is short: attendance, work done and material issued. We train on the site, in the local language where needed, and we start with the three or four records that matter most.

Does it replace my accounting software?

No. Your accountant keeps the books. We agree during setup how bills, payments and tax deducted reach Verity, which then does the site, measurement, material and follow-up work around them.

Can it prepare my running bills?

It turns signed measurements into lines on the next bill and shows work measured but not billed. Your engineer or accountant finalises and submits the bill in your or your client's format.

Can it track retention across many contracts?

Yes. Each contract has its own percentage, release stages and dates. Verity shows the total held and what is due to be claimed.

Do you handle sub-contractors and labour contractors?

Yes. Each has a work order, measured work and payments. Attendance from your supervisor is shown beside the labour contractor's claim.

I work on state government contracts. Is this different?

The records are the same, with longer bill stages and more documents. We set up the stages and checklists for your department's process during week 1.

Does it plan my schedule like a project planning tool?

It tracks tasks, milestones and progress against plan by site. If you use a dedicated planning tool, you keep it, and we agree how its dates reach Verity.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. A supervisor sees their site, the store sees materials and the owner and accountant see money. Every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How quickly will I see something useful?

Measured-but-not-billed and bills stuck with clients need only your existing bill register and measurement sheets, so they come first. Material and crew patterns become reliable after a few weeks of recorded days.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of sites, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your business runs.
