How it runs today

Most facility management companies are good at showing up and weak at proving it.

The work is visible to the client: clean washrooms, a guard at the gate, a lift that runs. What is hard to see, for both you and the client, is the proof: who was on the post at 2 am, which task was done at which floor, how many tickets were closed inside the SLA and what each site actually cost against its monthly charge.

That proof lives in many places. Attendance is on paper registers or a supervisor's phone. Consumables are issued from a store and noted in a book. Complaints arrive as calls, emails and messages. Invoices are made at head office from sheets that arrive on the 3rd of the month, if they arrive.

Estimates of the size of India's facility management market vary widely by scope, with one 2026 research estimate at about USD 19 billion in 2025. In a market that large and that fragmented, the companies that can prove delivery site by site keep their contracts.

What most facility management companies run on

  • Paper attendance registers or a supervisor's app
  • Excel at head office for rosters and billing
  • WhatsApp groups per site
  • Complaint calls and emails to a help desk
  • Accounts and payroll run by an accountant or a vendor

Each one works inside its own limits. None of them tells you that site 17 had two vacant posts last week and the client's SLA clause says a penalty applies.

16 problems, one by one

Where a facility management business actually loses money.

These are the situations owners, operations heads and site supervisors describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary week, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Staffing

    A post is vacant and the client notices before you do

    What it looks like

    A guard does not turn up for the night shift at the bank branch. The supervisor is eleven kilometres away. By the time a replacement arrives, the branch manager has already phoned your head office.

    What it costs you

    Most contracts treat a vacant post as a deduction or a penalty, and a pattern of vacancies is the main reason clients change vendors.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each post on each site is a record with a shift, a required headcount and a person. When a person is not marked present by shift start, the post shows vacant and a task goes to the nearest relief person and the supervisor.

  2. 02Staffing

    Rosters are made in Excel and change by phone call

    What it looks like

    A housekeeping lady is on leave for her daughter's wedding. The supervisor calls three people before one agrees. The Excel roster at head office does not change, and payroll is based on it.

    What it costs you

    Wrong wages, arguments at month end and a client invoice that shows people who were not there.

    How Verity fixes it

    One roster per site and shift, with changes recorded as who replaced whom and why. The month's attendance and wage inputs come from the same record that the client invoice is built from.

    See the screen
  3. 03Staffing

    Attendance is claimed but cannot be proved

    What it looks like

    A client's audit team asks for the guard attendance for August. The register has overwriting on four days and one supervisor's initials are missing.

    What it costs you

    An invoice with a disputed attendance count is the largest single source of delay in payment and a standard excuse to hold part of it.

    How Verity fixes it

    Attendance is taken daily by post, by the supervisor, with the time and the person who recorded it. Corrections need a reason and an approver, so the register shown to the client is the one with a history.

    See the screen
  4. 04Contracts

    SLA terms are in the contract and nowhere else

    What it looks like

    The contract says toilets cleaned every two hours, a complaint answered in 30 minutes and closed in four hours, a fire drill each quarter. The supervisor has never seen the contract. Only the operations head knows the clauses.

    What it costs you

    A published guide on FM contracts says targets like "prompt response" cannot be measured, so they cannot be enforced. Where they can be measured, the client does the measuring and you find out at invoicing.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each contract is a record with its clauses turned into measurable SLAs: response time, closing time, task frequency, headcount. Each SLA shows the live result for the month and the site where it is slipping.

    See the screen
  5. 05Contracts

    SLA penalties are deducted and nobody checks them

    What it looks like

    The client's accounts pay 94 percent of the invoice with a note about SLA deductions. Nobody can match the deduction to a ticket or a vacant post.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a ₹6 lakh monthly invoice with 6 percent deducted is ₹36,000 a month, or about ₹4.3 lakh a year, on one site. Part of it may be wrongly applied, and you cannot argue without the record.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each SLA miss is recorded with the date, the cause and the evidence. When a deduction arrives, Verity lines it up against the misses, so you accept the valid ones and challenge the rest with the ticket history.

    See the screen
  6. 06Contracts

    Contract renewals and rate revisions sneak up

    What it looks like

    A three-year contract ends in November. Minimum wages have gone up twice since it was signed. The renewal discussion starts the week before expiry.

    What it costs you

    Working on old rates while wages rise cuts the margin. A lapsed contract with no renewal in writing is a risk to payment.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each contract has its start, end, notice and review dates, and the wage rates it assumed. Tasks open 90 days before expiry with the current cost per post against the contract rate, so the renewal talk starts with numbers.

    See the screen
  7. 07Tickets

    Complaints arrive in five places and close in none

    What it looks like

    A tenant calls about a water leak, a manager emails about the lobby, and a client's facilities head sends a message about the AC. The help desk writes each on a different paper.

    What it costs you

    A complaint that is not logged cannot be closed inside the SLA, and the client counts only the ones they had to ask about twice.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every complaint becomes a ticket with the site, location, category, time received, owner and due time. Tickets near their SLA time are flagged, and closing needs a note and, where needed, a photo.

    See the screen
  8. 08Tickets

    Repeat problems are fixed again and again

    What it looks like

    The same washroom drain chokes every 10 days. Each time a ticket is closed. Nobody asks why.

    What it costs you

    Labour and consumables are spent on the same fix, and the client sees a vendor that only reacts.

    How Verity fixes it

    Tickets are grouped by site, location and category. Repeats are shown, and a repeat opens a root cause task for the supervisor or a technician with a due date.

    See the screen
  9. 09Maintenance

    Preventive maintenance of client equipment slips

    What it looks like

    The contract includes pumps, DG sets, lifts and HVAC. The service schedule is on a calendar at head office. A DG set fails during a power cut because its service was a month late.

    What it costs you

    A failure during peak use is blamed on the vendor, and emergency call-outs cost more than planned visits.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each piece of equipment is an asset record with its site, service schedule and history. A service due date becomes a task for the technician or the vendor, and completion needs the checklist and a signature.

    See the screen
  10. 10Consumables

    Consumables run out at one site and pile up at another

    What it looks like

    Phenyl, hand wash, tissue rolls and garbage bags are issued from a central store. Site 9 is out by the 20th. Site 22 has four months of stock.

    What it costs you

    A client's washrooms without soap is a complaint within hours, and over-issue at some sites quietly eats the margin.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each site has a monthly allowance by item based on its headcount and footfall. Issues are recorded against the site, and Verity compares use against allowance, flags sites that are over or under and drafts a restock.

    See the screen
  11. 11Consumables

    Machines and tools are lost between sites

    What it looks like

    Scrubber-driers, vacuum cleaners and walkie-talkies move between sites. When a scrubber breaks nobody remembers where it was last.

    What it costs you

    Replacement purchases of items that were only misplaced, and contract-required equipment missing at a site.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each machine is an asset with its current site and holder, service dates and issues. A transfer between sites is recorded when it happens, and the contract's equipment list is checked against what is actually there.

    See the screen
  12. 12Compliance

    Wages, PF and ESI inputs depend on rosters nobody trusts

    What it looks like

    Wages for 300 people depend on attendance sheets that arrive late from 41 sites. PF and ESI contributions are based on the same sheets. A principal employer's auditor asks for proof of contributions.

    What it costs you

    Under the labour codes in force since 21 November 2025, contract labour is restricted for core activities and clients are asked to confirm vendors' compliance. A vendor that cannot show records loses contracts or has payments held.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verified attendance by person and site is the single input for the month's wage run, which your accountant or payroll provider does. Registrations, contribution proofs and licence dates are records with owners, and a client's request for proof is a task with the documents attached.

    See the screen
  13. 13Compliance

    Guard licences, training and police verification expire quietly

    What it looks like

    A private security licence is valid for years, then needs renewing with the agency's training tie-up and records. A guard's police verification is one month from expiring and nobody has noticed.

    What it costs you

    A lapsed licence or an untrained guard can end a security contract, and in some states the licence runs for only one year.

    How Verity fixes it

    The agency licence, each guard's training and verification dates are records with owners. A date 60 days away becomes a task, and a post cannot be shown as fully compliant while a guard's paperwork is expired.

  14. 14Money

    Invoices are built late and from memory

    What it looks like

    Invoices are prepared at head office after the supervisors' sheets arrive, usually after the 5th. Extra work, such as a deep clean before a client event, is not recorded and not billed.

    What it costs you

    A late invoice is a late payment. Unbilled extra work is margin given away, and each client invoice gets a different treatment.

    How Verity fixes it

    Billable items for the month, including headcount, extra jobs and consumable charges that the contract allows, accumulate on the contract record. A draft billing summary is ready on the last day, and your accountant raises the invoice.

  15. 15Money

    A site's real margin is not known until the contract ends

    What it looks like

    The company bills ₹6.5 lakh a month for a campus. Wages, statutory costs, consumables, supervisor travel and machines are spread across other accounts. Nobody can say if that campus pays.

    What it costs you

    A contract can sit at a loss for two years. By the time the loss is found, it has to be repriced or exited.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each contract shows billed against cost to date for people, consumables, machines and travel, by month. A contract whose margin falls below your line is flagged to the owner.

    What it looks like

    The owner depends on supervisor calls in the evening. A supervisor may cover four sites. Problems are known to the person who happens to be there.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a facility management company has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice, does not run payroll and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Labour codes and contract labour

    What is expected

    Key provisions of the four Labour Codes took effect on 21 November 2025. Contract labour engagement is restricted for core activities of a business, subject to exceptions, and principal employers generally expect vendors to prove compliance with wages, PF and ESI. State rules differ.

    How Verity helps

    Verified attendance by person and site is the single input for wages, and registrations and contribution proofs are records with owners that can be shared when a client asks.

    BDO India alert on the Labour Codes

  • Minimum wages

    What is expected

    Minimum wages for housekeeping and security staff are set by state governments and are revised from time to time, so a long contract can fall behind current rates.

    How Verity helps

    Each contract stores the wage rates it assumed, and Verity compares the cost per post with the contract rate so the gap shows before renewal.

  • Private security agency licence

    What is expected

    Agencies supplying guards generally need a licence under the Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act, usually valid for five years, with renewal applied for well before expiry and tied to continued guard training. Some states differ.

    How Verity helps

    The licence, each guard's training and police verification are records with dates, and tasks open well before they lapse.

    SetIndiaBiz, PSARA renewal

  • Contract terms and SLA penalties

    What is expected

    SLA penalties are a matter of the contract, and under the Indian Contract Act a stated sum is not always recoverable in full, so evidence of each miss matters. Ask your lawyer about your own clauses.

    How Verity helps

    Every SLA miss and its cause is recorded with evidence, so a deduction can be accepted or challenged on facts.

    Vidhikarya on SLA breach

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your adviser and the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions facility management companies ask.

My supervisors work from a phone at client sites. Will it work for them?

The daily record is short: who is present, tickets opened and closed, items issued. We train supervisors on their own sites, and we start with attendance and tickets only.

Does Verity run payroll for my 300 staff?

No. Your accountant or payroll provider does that. Verity keeps the verified attendance by person and site, and your payroll provider uses it as the input.

Can the client see tickets and SLAs?

Only if you decide so. Access is by role, and a client view of tickets and SLA results for their own site can be agreed during setup.

How does it handle different SLAs for different clients?

Each contract has its own SLAs, due times and allowances. A bank branch and a hospital wing can have very different targets in the same system.

Can it work out whether a contract is making money?

Yes, once billing and costs for people, consumables and machines are recorded. Each contract shows billed against cost to date, so a drop in margin shows early.

We have guards, housekeeping and technicians. Is that one system?

Yes. Posts and shifts are set up for each service line, and the same screens cover rosters, tickets and stock.

Does it track vehicles or guard location?

No. It records posts, shifts and attendance. It does not track vehicles or locations by GPS.

How do you bring in current rosters and contracts?

From your sheets and registers. We load posts, people and contract terms for each site, then check one site with the supervisor before go-live.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. A supervisor sees their sites, the help desk sees tickets and the owner and accountant see money. Every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of sites, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your business runs.
