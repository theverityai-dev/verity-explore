How it runs today

The delivery is tracked and the business around it is not.

Engineers work in repositories and project boards. The project manager runs the sprint. That part is organised. What sits outside is the business view: who is free next month, which contract promises a four-hour response, which milestone is done but not signed off, and what each project actually earns after the team's cost.

The information lives in a resource sheet kept by one person, a ticket tool per client, contracts in a folder, timesheets in another tool and the invoice schedule in the finance team's sheet. The delivery head and the finance head hold the connection in their heads, and they disagree on dates.

So the company finds out late. A bench of ten that was visible two months ago. A premium SLA breached on a Sunday because no one watched the clock. A milestone completed in March and invoiced in May. And a small vendor paid late, which now has a tax effect. Nasscom's figures show industry revenue growing 6.1% in FY26 while headcount grew 2.3%, so the pressure to use each person well is real.

What most IT services companies run on

  • Resource allocation sheet
  • Ticketing tool per client
  • Timesheet tool
  • Contracts folder
  • Invoice schedule spreadsheet

Each tool is fine for its job. Nothing connects the contract, the people, the tickets and the invoice.

Where an IT services company actually loses money.

  1. 01Resources

    The bench is invisible until it is expensive

    What it looks like

    A project ends on the 15th. The people from it have no next assignment. The resource sheet is updated on the 28th, when the delivery head finds eleven people unassigned.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: eleven engineers at ₹1.2 lakh a month of cost sitting idle for a month is about ₹13 lakh. Reports on large Indian IT firms say bench has shrunk from over 20% to 8 to 15%, so an idle bench is one of the first things a client asks about.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every person has an allocation by project and date. Verity shows who rolls off in the next 30, 60 and 90 days, who is unassigned now and for how long, and what open demand could use them.

  2. 02Resources

    One engineer, three projects, at 100% each

    What it looks like

    A senior developer is allocated 100% to a client project and also named at 50% on two others. Each project manager believes they have her.

    What it costs you

    Over-allocation breaks deadlines on all three jobs and burns out the person everyone depends on.

    How Verity fixes it

    Allocation is entered as a percentage by project, and a person above 100% is flagged on the delivery head's screen with the clashing projects named.

  3. 03Resources

    Who can do this job?

    What it looks like

    A client needs a cloud migration engineer in two weeks. The delivery head recalls two names but not whether they are free, or who has done it before.

    What it costs you

    Hiring from outside for a skill you already have costs more and takes longer. Losing the deal because you could not staff it costs more still.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each person's skills, certifications and past projects are on their record. A request for a skill and a start date lists who matches, when they are free and who would have to be moved.

  4. 04Resources

    Notice period with no knowledge transfer

    What it looks like

    A lead engineer resigns with 60 days of notice. His handover is a few meetings and a document no one reads. A client asks a question about a module only he understood.

    What it costs you

    Delays, errors and sometimes a client who loses trust in the team.

    How Verity fixes it

    A resignation creates a handover plan: projects owned, systems and credentials held, documents to write and the person taking over each. The plan is tracked to completion before the last day.

  5. 05Service desk

    Tickets live in a shared inbox

    What it looks like

    A client's support mailbox gets 60 mails a day. Priorities are judged by whoever reads first. A "system down" mail sits behind a password reset for two hours.

    What it costs you

    Slow response on a serious ticket costs trust and, under many contracts, a service credit.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every request becomes a ticket with a client, priority, owner and a clock set by the contract's SLA. The queue is sorted by time left, not by arrival order.

  6. 06Service desk

    SLA breaches found at the monthly review

    What it looks like

    A contract promises a four-hour response and a one-day fix for critical incidents. Weekend tickets are watched by whoever remembers. The client's review lists three breaches.

    What it costs you

    Service credits, difficult renewals, and a reputation for being slow when it matters.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each ticket shows time to first response and time to resolution against the contract. Tickets nearing a breach are flagged to the owner and the service manager, and the monthly SLA view is built from the records.

    See the screen
  7. 07Service desk

    The same incident, again and again

    What it looks like

    A client's batch job fails every month-end. Each time a different engineer restarts it and closes the ticket. No one writes down the cause.

    What it costs you

    The same hours are spent every month on a problem that should have been fixed once.

    How Verity fixes it

    Tickets link to a problem record with the cause and the fix. A ticket that matches an open problem is tagged, and the count of repeat tickets by cause is shown to the service manager.

    See the screen
  8. 08Milestones

    A finished milestone that nobody invoiced

    What it looks like

    Phase 2 of a fixed-price project was delivered in March. The client's sign-off was verbal. The invoice for the milestone was raised in May.

    What it costs you

    Cash arrives two months later than it could have. At ₹25 lakh per milestone, that is real working capital the company has to find elsewhere.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each contract has milestones with a delivery date, acceptance state and invoice state. A milestone marked delivered with no acceptance after a set number of days is chased, and an accepted milestone with no invoice raises a task for finance.

  9. 09Milestones

    Acceptance never recorded

    What it looks like

    The client's manager says the module is fine on a call. Later, a new manager disputes that it was ever accepted and holds the payment.

    What it costs you

    A payment held on a technicality can be delayed for months.

    How Verity fixes it

    Acceptance is a recorded step with the approver's name, date and the document or message attached. The milestone cannot move to invoicing without it.

    See the screen
  10. 10Milestones

    Time-and-material hours that do not match the invoice

    What it looks like

    Timesheets are filled on Fridays from memory. The invoice is based on a spreadsheet summary that the client's team questions line by line.

    What it costs you

    Disputed hours are written off and the next invoice takes longer to approve.

    How Verity fixes it

    Hours are logged against the project and task as work happens, with a manager approval step. The invoice view sums approved hours by person and rate, so the finance team prepares the bill from agreed numbers.

    See the screen
  11. 11Vendors

    Small vendors and freelancers paid late

    What it looks like

    A specialist subcontractor is paid when the client pays you. Their bill is 70 days old. They are a registered micro enterprise.

    What it costs you

    Under Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, amounts owed to micro and small suppliers beyond the allowed period are generally disallowed as a deduction until paid. The statutory limit is 45 days from acceptance. It can also lead to disputes with the vendor.

    How Verity fixes it

    Vendor bills are records with the date of acceptance, the vendor's registration status and the due date. Bills approaching the limit are listed for the finance head with the vendor and amount.

    See the screen
  12. 12Contracts

    Contracts, AMC and licences lapse quietly

    What it looks like

    A support contract ended on the 31st. The client's team kept raising tickets. For six weeks the company worked free while a renewal sat in a manager's inbox.

    What it costs you

    Unpaid support, and a weak position when renewing because the client has got used to free service.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every contract and AMC is a record with start, end, scope, SLA terms and a renewal owner. Renewal tasks appear 90, 60 and 30 days before expiry, and expired contracts are flagged on any new ticket.

    See the screen
  13. 13Projects

    Scope on a fixed-price project drifts

    What it looks like

    A client adds three small features by chat. The project manager says yes each time. The fixed fee has not moved and the team has worked 400 hours over the plan.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 400 extra hours at ₹1,000 a hour of cost is ₹4 lakh of margin gone on one project.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each project has a scope list and a change request step. Hours against the estimate are visible to the delivery head weekly, and a change either becomes a priced change request or is declined on the record.

    See the screen
  14. 14Compliance

    Overtime and weekend support with no record

    What it looks like

    A support engineer works extra hours on a go-live weekend. The overtime is agreed on a call and noted nowhere. A month later HR and the engineer disagree.

    What it costs you

    The Labour Codes took effect on 21 November 2025 and generally require consent for overtime, paid at twice the regular wage, though the central and state rules were still being finalised in 2026. Undocumented overtime is a dispute waiting to happen.

    How Verity fixes it

    Extra hours are logged against the person and the project with a manager approval and the reason. HR sees the month's overtime and the consent record before payroll is prepared in your own payroll process.

    See the screen
  15. 15Compliance

    Security incidents and client data

    What it looks like

    A server at a client site shows unusual logins at night. No one knows who should report it, to whom, or within what time. The access logs are kept for a week.

    What it costs you

    CERT-In's directions, effective 27 June 2022, generally expect covered entities to report listed incidents within six hours of noticing and to keep ICT logs for 180 days. The DPDP Rules add data duties from about 18 months after November 2025.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client system has an owner, a named incident contact and a log retention date on the record. A reported incident creates a log with time noticed, who was told and when, so the six-hour clock is visible.

Rules and paperwork

The rules an IT services company has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • MSME payment terms and Section 43B(h)

    What is expected

    From AY 2024-25, amounts owed to micro and small suppliers beyond the allowed period, which cannot exceed 45 days from acceptance of goods or services, are generally disallowed as a deduction until paid. Medium enterprises are excluded.

    How Verity helps

    Vendor bills carry the acceptance date, the vendor's registration status and a due date, with bills near the limit listed for finance.

    TaxGuru

  • CERT-In directions, 2022

    What is expected

    Issued on 28 April 2022 and effective 27 June 2022, they generally expect covered entities to report listed cyber incidents within six hours of noticing them and to keep ICT logs for 180 days within India.

    How Verity helps

    Each client system has an owner, an incident contact and a log retention date. A reported incident records the time noticed and who was told.

    PSA Legal

  • Labour Codes and overtime

    What is expected

    The four Labour Codes took effect on 21 November 2025. Overtime generally needs the worker's consent and is paid at twice the regular wage. Central and some state rules were still being finalised in 2026.

    How Verity helps

    Extra hours are logged with the reason, approver and consent. Verity does not run payroll.

    DLA Piper

  • Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025

    What is expected

    Notified on 14 November 2025. Most duties apply 18 months after publication and cover data fiduciaries and processors. An IT services company that handles client personal data may be a processor.

    How Verity helps

    Client data access is by role with an audit trail, and retention dates are set per client.

    Scconline

  • GST and export of services

    What is expected

    Invoices to clients outside India may be zero-rated under conditions, and rules on receiving foreign payments are set by the RBI and have been changing. Your accountant and bank decide and file.

    How Verity helps

    Each invoice record carries the client, country, amount, invoice date and payment state.

Rules change and differ by situation. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or lawyer.

Questions

Questions IT company owners ask.

We already use a ticketing tool. Why Verity?

Your ticketing tool knows tickets. It does not know the contract, who is free to be assigned, or the milestone that is waiting. We agree during setup how tickets reach Verity so SLA clocks and repeat problems appear next to the rest of the business.

Does Verity replace our project boards?

No. Engineers keep their boards. Verity holds the business view: contracts, allocations, hours against estimate, milestones and invoices.

Can it handle fixed-price and time-and-material together?

Yes. Fixed-price work has milestones and hours against estimate; time-and-material work has approved hours by person and rate. Each is set up in your terms.

How does it show the bench?

From allocations. Each person is allocated to projects by percentage and date, so the unassigned or rolling-off list is a result, not a separate sheet.

Will it send SLA alerts to clients?

No. It alerts your team, who talk to the client. Verity records the response and the outcome.

Can it show profit by project?

Yes, once hours, rates and contract values are in. Verity shows revenue, hours, team cost and margin per project. Team cost uses the rates you set.

We are 40 people. Is that large enough?

A 40-person company has the same bench, SLA and milestone gaps with less slack to absorb them. We set up what you need in the same four weeks.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and by client, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of people, clients and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your company runs.
