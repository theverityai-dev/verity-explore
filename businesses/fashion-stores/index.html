How it runs today

A fashion store sells a season it bought months ago.

The buying happens before anyone knows what will sell. The shop then spends the season correcting for it: moving stock between outlets, bringing forward the sale, giving a staff discount on the dead stock and asking the supplier for something fresh. All of that is decided on feel because the numbers are in a billing report that lists bills, not styles.

A 2026 study by Vector Consulting Group found that about 24% of on-shelf apparel and footwear stock was past its optimal selling window, and that only 9% of surveyed retailers use shelf throughput as a key metric for buying and replenishment. In a business where a style has a short life, those are the figures that decide the margin.

Meanwhile the paperwork moves on its own schedule. Supplier bills, challans and debit notes sit in files. The GST rate depends on the price of each piece, and since 22 September 2025 that sits close to the ₹2,500 mark for many styles. Staff incentives are worked out in a notebook.

What most fashion stores run on

  • Billing report by bill and item
  • Supplier challans and bills
  • Excel stock sheets by outlet
  • WhatsApp groups with staff and suppliers
  • The owner's memory of what sold last year

Each one is useful. None of them can tell you which size of which style is about to run out in which outlet.

15 problems, one by one

Where a fashion store actually loses money.

These are the situations fashion store owners describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Buying

    Buying is done on total pieces, not on what sells

    What it looks like

    Two hundred pieces of a style are ordered because it looked good and the supplier gave a rate. Another style sold out in a week and the reorder takes a month. The sizes that arrive follow the supplier's pack, not your customers.

    What it costs you

    Vector Consulting Group's 2026 study found retailers tolerate long lead times and buy opportunistically in bulk, which raises inventory. Money sits in the wrong styles while the right ones run out.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each style is tracked from the first delivery with its sales by week, by size and by outlet. Verity shows the sell-through curve against your own past seasons, so the next order follows what sold rather than what looked good.

    See the screen
  2. 02Stock

    The size curve breaks and the leftovers are the sizes nobody wants

    What it looks like

    By week three the shirt is gone in M and L. What is left is XS, XXL and a pile of 44. Staff tell the customer it is out of stock when it is in the other outlet.

    What it costs you

    Broken runs are what get marked down first, and every missing size is a customer who leaves without buying.

    How Verity fixes it

    Stock is held by style, size and colour in each outlet. Verity shows which styles have lost their core sizes, how many complete runs remain, and which outlet holds the size the customer wants.

    See the screen
  3. 03Stock

    Stock is stuck in one outlet while another sells out

    What it looks like

    The Koramangala outlet has sixty kurtas of a style that sold out in Indiranagar in a week. The transfer is done when someone remembers, by courier, with a handwritten slip.

    What it costs you

    A sale lost in one place for stock that is idle in another, and transfers that go missing in between with nobody responsible.

    How Verity fixes it

    Outlets are locations on one record. Verity lists styles that are stuck in one outlet and short in another, drafts the transfer, and holds it open until the receiving outlet signs the count.

    See the screen
  4. 04Markdowns

    Stock ages quietly and then needs a big discount

    What it looks like

    A style that did not sell in the first month is moved to the back rail. Three months later it is in the sale at 50 percent off, with the same margin gone that the early 20 percent would have kept.

    What it costs you

    Vector Consulting Group found about 24% of on-shelf apparel and footwear stock past its optimal selling window. Each extra week takes a deeper cut to clear.

    How Verity fixes it

    Days on floor, sell-through and cost value are shown for every style. Verity proposes a markdown ladder for ageing styles, shows the margin after each step, and routes each approval to the owner before the price changes.

    See the screen
  5. 05Markdowns

    Discounts stack up without anyone adding them

    What it looks like

    A festival offer, a loyalty coupon and a manager's request for a regular customer all apply to the same shirt. The cashier layers all three because the system lets her.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: if a ₹1,800 shirt that costs ₹900 ends up sold at ₹1,100 after stacking, margin falls from ₹900 to ₹200 on that piece.

    How Verity fixes it

    Discount rules are set per type, with a ceiling on the total. Anything above the ceiling waits for a one-tap approval with a reason, and Verity totals discounts by person, outlet and offer.

    See the screen
  6. 06Pricing

    A piece priced at ₹2,500 and one at ₹2,501 are taxed at different rates

    What it looks like

    Since 22 September 2025 apparel generally attracts 5% GST up to ₹2,500 per piece and 18% above. A shirt marked down from ₹2,700 to ₹2,400 changes rate. Staff do not know, and the billing master is set by item, not by final sale value.

    What it costs you

    A wrong rate on a few hundred bills a month becomes a notice and a customer who sees a different tax on the invoice. Your accountant corrects it later.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity shows price bands against the ₹2,500 mark for each style and flags any price or discount that moves a piece across it, so your team and your accountant can agree the right treatment before the bill. It does not file returns.

    See the screen
  7. 07Shrinkage

    Pieces walk out between the rail and the till

    What it looks like

    A fitting room has two shirts under the bench. A tag is swapped from a cheap piece to an expensive one. A staff member sets aside a piece for a friend. Nobody saw it, and it shows only at the yearly count.

    What it costs you

    Trent reported shrinkage of 0.41% of sales in FY24 and V-Mart 0.5%. A retail association said losses rise during festive seasons and cricket tournaments, mostly through employees.

    How Verity fixes it

    Cycle counts of one rack or outlet at a time are compared with the book. Verity lists each gap with the style, the value, the shift and the staff on duty, and ranks outlets and shifts by loss.

    See the screen
  8. 08Suppliers

    Deliveries do not match the challan, and rejects are argued

    What it looks like

    The challan says 120 pieces and the carton has 112. Twelve have stitching faults found a week later. The supplier says they were fine on dispatch.

    What it costs you

    You pay for missing pieces and carry faulty ones. Example arithmetic: ten faulty or missing pieces a delivery at ₹600 is ₹6,000 a delivery.

    How Verity fixes it

    Goods are received against the order with the counted quantity and any faults noted with a reason. Verity compares order, receipt and bill, and raises a debit note task for the difference before the bill is paid.

    See the screen
  9. 09Suppliers

    Small suppliers are paid late and the tax treatment changes

    What it looks like

    Your tailoring units and small garment makers are paid when cash allows. Nobody tracks which of them are registered as micro or small enterprises, or how long each bill has been open.

    What it costs you

    Under Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, an amount owed to a micro or small supplier that is not paid within the allowed days is generally deductible only in the year it is paid. Traders are generally excluded. Your accountant confirms who counts.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each supplier record carries its type and terms. Verity lists bills nearing their deadline and the suppliers who need prompt payment, and assigns the payment task to the accounts person.

    See the screen
  10. 10Customers

    Exchanges and returns are given on goodwill

    What it looks like

    A customer returns a dress after a wedding with the tag tucked in. A staff member says yes to avoid an argument. The piece goes back on the rail with a stain.

    What it costs you

    A sale reversed and a piece that can no longer be sold at full price. For stores that also send pieces by courier to Instagram or WhatsApp customers, a vendor benchmark puts fashion COD return rates at 25 to 40%.

    How Verity fixes it

    Returns and exchanges are records tied to the original bill, with a reason, the item's condition and where it goes next. Verity totals returns by style and reason, so a fit problem is visible before the next order.

    See the screen
  11. 11Customers

    Regulars and wedding shoppers are not remembered

    What it looks like

    A customer who bought for her sister's wedding last year and for Diwali before that has not been in this season. The staff who served her left in March.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 300 repeat customers spending ₹6,000 a visit, three visits a year, is ₹54 lakh. A fifth lost quietly is ₹10.8 lakh.

    How Verity fixes it

    Bills are tied to the customer by phone number. Verity shows each person's past styles, sizes and occasions, lists those past their usual gap, and assigns a call to a staff member.

    See the screen
  12. 12People

    Staff incentives are worked out on paper and attrition is high

    What it looks like

    The manager keeps a notebook of who was on which day and who sold how much. At month end two salespeople disagree. A good seller who knew regulars by name leaves.

    What it costs you

    Hours of manager time each month, a salesperson who feels cheated, and the loss of customer knowledge when someone leaves.

    How Verity fixes it

    Sales, attendance and returns are recorded against each person. Verity builds the incentive sheet from the rule you set, shows the same figures to the staff member, and holds customer notes on the customer record, not in one person's head.

    See the screen
  13. 13Customers

    Alterations and custom orders slip past the date

    What it looks like

    A customer is promised a hem on Thursday. The tailor has a backlog and nobody told the shop. The customer arrives and the piece is not ready.

    What it costs you

    A promise broken to a customer who may not come back, and a tailor who is blamed for a problem of tracking.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each alteration is a job with the customer, the piece, the tailor and the promised date. Verity shows jobs due in the next two days and flags the ones at risk, with a task for the shop to tell the customer.

    See the screen
  14. 14Sales

    Instagram and WhatsApp enquiries are lost in chats

    What it looks like

    A customer messages a photo of a dress asking for size and price. The reply comes six hours later or never. The details of who asked for what are in three phones.

    What it costs you

    These enquiries are often the highest intent a fashion store receives. A slow reply sends the buyer to another seller.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each enquiry is a record with an owner, the style asked for, the size, a quote and a next step. Verity lists the ones without a reply and assigns them. Staff still reply to the customer, and the outcome is recorded.

    See the screen
  15. 15Owner

    The owner sees outlets only after the month closes

    What it looks like

    One outlet is strong, another struggles, and the figures arrive in different formats. Rent is fixed, so the weak outlet's problem is in the stock, the staff or the location, and no one can tell which.

    What it costs you

    A month of a weak outlet before the issue is noticed, and decisions about reordering made on the strongest outlet's sales.

    How Verity fixes it

    Outlets are locations on one system. Each morning Verity shows sales, discounts, ageing stock and shortfalls by outlet side by side, with the items needing a decision listed and owned.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a fashion store has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • GST rate by price per piece

    What is expected

    From 22 September 2025, apparel is generally taxed at 5% up to ₹2,500 per piece and 18% above, tested on the sale value and not automatically on the printed price. A small price change near ₹2,500 can move the rate. The GST Council and your accountant decide how discounts are treated, so confirm before relying on any single reading.

    How Verity helps

    Each style records its price, discount and final sale value, and Verity flags those that cross the line, so the correct rate can be agreed before billing. Verity does not file returns.

    Outlook Business, PTI, September 2025: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/garments-priced-above-25k-to-be-costlier-will-hurt-middle-class-affordability-industry-bodies; TaxAJ commentary

  • Paying small suppliers within the allowed days

    What is expected

    Under Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, an amount owed to a micro or small enterprise supplier is generally deductible in the same year only if paid within the 15 day default, or the agreed term up to 45 days. Wholesale and retail traders are generally outside this rule. Confirm the details with your accountant.

    How Verity helps

    Each supplier record carries its type and terms, and Verity lists bills near their deadline.

    ClearTax on Section 43B(h)

  • Seller liability for product defects

    What is expected

    The Consumer Protection Act 2019 extends product liability to product sellers, with the relevant sections in force from 20 July 2020.

    How Verity helps

    Each complaint and return is a record linked to the bill, the style and the supplier, so a defect has a history and a supplier to pass it to.

    S.S. Rana on the Act

  • Local trade and shop registrations

    What is expected

Questions

Questions fashion store owners ask.

My billing system already shows sales by item. What does Verity add?

Billing shows what sold. Verity adds what is left by size, how old it is, where it should move, what each markdown step leaves in margin, and who bought it. It reads from your billing, so staff do not enter sales twice.

Does Verity replace my billing system?

No. Your billing system keeps taking payments and raising bills. We agree during setup how bills, returns and discounts reach Verity.

How does it handle sizes and colours?

Each style has a size scale and colours you define, and stock is held by size and colour in each outlet. That is what lets Verity tell you a run has broken before the customers do.

We sell some pieces over Instagram and courier them. Can it track that?

It records each enquiry, the quote, the order and the return, and who owns it. It does not take orders or payments online and does not message customers on its own. Staff reply and Verity tracks the follow-up.

Can it help with the ₹2,500 GST line?

It shows which prices and discounts cross the line so your team and your accountant can agree the treatment. It does not decide the rate and does not file returns.

I have two outlets and a small team. Is this too much?

The leaks are the same and there is less time to see them. We set up only what you need in the same four weeks and leave out what you do not.

Will my staff record exchanges and counts?

Only if it takes seconds, so we keep the steps short and train at the shop during a real shift. We start with the few reasons that matter, not a long form.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so salespeople, managers and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of outlets, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your store runs.
