How it runs today

A shoe store is a size problem dressed up as a stock problem.

A customer wants a pair in a particular size. If it is in the stockroom, the sale is made. If it is in another outlet, or the supplier's godown, the sale is usually lost. So the real question is never how many pairs you have, but how many in the sizes that sell.

That question is hard to answer from a billing report that lists bills. The shelf is checked by eye, and the stockroom is a wall of boxes with a label. Pairs get mixed up between boxes, left and right pieces are separated, display samples are sold, and a style that stopped selling in July sits with the new arrivals.

On top of the stock sits paperwork that is specific to footwear. Since 1 August 2024 many footwear types need to carry the BIS mark, with a window for selling old stock. Since 22 September 2025 GST on footwear turns on whether a pair sells for up to ₹2,500 or above. Brands owe you credit for defects, and the claims pile up.

What most shoe stores run on

  • Billing report by bill and item
  • Brand invoices and challans
  • Excel sheet or register of boxes
  • WhatsApp with brand reps and distributors
  • The owner's memory of what sold last year

Each one is correct for its purpose. None of them can say which size of which style has been on the shelf for ninety days.

14 problems, one by one

Where a shoe store actually loses money.

These are the situations shoe store owners describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Stock

    The popular sizes sell out and the odd sizes stay

    What it looks like

    A new sports shoe arrives in a full run, sizes 5 to 12. By the third week 7, 8 and 9 are gone. What is left is 5, 11 and 12, in a style that is still on the front shelf.

    What it costs you

    Every missing size is a walk-out at full price. The leftovers are what get marked down first. Example arithmetic: ten missed sales a week at ₹2,200 is over ₹11 lakh a year.

    How Verity fixes it

    Stock is held by style, size and colour. Verity shows sell-through by size for each style, lists styles whose core sizes have run out, and drafts a reorder or a transfer for the sizes that are missing.

    See the screen
  2. 02Stock

    Pairs age on the shelf and need a deep discount

    What it looks like

    A style that did not sell at launch moves to the back wall. Months later it joins the clearance sale at half price. Bata cut its gross inventory by 15% to ₹815 crore in the year to March 2025, showing how large the stake is for a chain.

    What it costs you

    Vector Consulting Group's 2026 study found about 24% of on-shelf apparel and footwear stock past its optimal selling window. Each extra month takes a deeper cut to clear.

    How Verity fixes it

    Days on shelf, sell-through and cost value are shown for every style and size. Verity proposes a markdown ladder for ageing styles, shows the margin after each step, and routes each approval to the owner.

    See the screen
  3. 03Stock

    The box says one thing and holds another

    What it looks like

    A customer asks for the black loafer in 8. The box says 8, the pair is a 9, the other shoe is in a different box. Staff spend ten minutes looking and the customer leaves.

    What it costs you

    Lost sales from pairs that are in the store but cannot be found, and returns when a mixed pair goes home.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each box is a record with its style, size and shelf location. When a pair is shown, sold or returned, its status changes. Verity lists boxes not checked in a while, and gaps found during a count are tied to a shelf and a date.

    See the screen
  4. 04Shrinkage

    Single shoes, swapped boxes and display pairs go missing

    What it looks like

    A customer tries a shoe and walks off with one. A cheaper pair is put into an expensive pair's box. A display pair on the front shelf is sold and nobody records it. At the yearly count the numbers do not agree.

    What it costs you

    Trent reported shrinkage of 0.41% of sales in FY24 and V-Mart 0.5%, both up on the year before. A retail association said chain losses are mostly through employees and rise at festive times.

    How Verity fixes it

    Display pairs are separate records. Cycle counts by shelf are compared with the book, and each gap is listed with the style, size, value, date range and the staff on shift. Verity ranks shelves and shifts by loss.

    See the screen
  5. 05Compliance

    The BIS mark on stock is not recorded anywhere

    What it looks like

    Since 1 August 2024, many leather, rubber and polymeric footwear types generally need the BIS mark. Old stock without it could be sold until 31 July 2026, and a June 2026 amendment is reported to extend that to 31 July 2027 for stock declared to BIS. Nobody on the shelf knows which boxes are which.

    What it costs you

    Selling non-compliant footwear risks penalties and seizure, and a store that cannot show which stock is old and declared has a hard conversation with an inspector.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each style records whether it carries the BIS mark, whether it is declared old stock, and the date by which old stock must be sold. Verity lists old stock by days to the cut-off and flags new purchases that arrive without the mark.

    See the screen
  6. 06Pricing

    A pair at ₹2,500 and a pair at ₹2,501 are taxed at different rates

    What it looks like

    Since 22 September 2025, footwear up to ₹2,500 a pair generally attracts 5% GST and above that 18%. A pair marked down from ₹2,800 to ₹2,450 changes rate, and so does a pair that is priced just above because a brand raised its MRP.

    What it costs you

    A wrong rate repeated across bills becomes a correction and a notice, and a customer sees an unexpected tax on the invoice.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity shows each style's price band against the ₹2,500 line and flags any price or discount that moves a pair across it, so your team and your accountant agree the right treatment before billing. It does not file returns.

    See the screen
  7. 07Brands

    Brand schemes, margins and dues are tracked in different places

    What it looks like

    A brand offers an extra 5 percent if you buy 200 pairs this quarter. Another gives a year-end rebate on target. A distributor extends 45 days of credit. The rep's promises are on WhatsApp.

    What it costs you

    Rebates not claimed, schemes missed by a few pairs, and payments made late or early without a plan.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each brand is a record with its terms, targets, schemes and payment dates. Verity shows progress against each target and what is due this week, so a scheme is not missed by a few pairs.

    See the screen
  8. 08Brands

    Defective pairs wait months for a credit

    What it looks like

    Soles come away after a month. A customer returns the pair, and the store replaces it from stock. The defective pair goes in a box labelled "to return". The brand's credit note arrives late or never.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 12 defective pairs a month at ₹1,400 each is about ₹2 lakh a year of stock that has been paid for and replaced but not credited.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each defect return is a record with the pair, the reason, the brand and a claim status. Verity lists claims waiting more than a limit you set, and assigns a task to chase the rep.

    See the screen
  9. 09Peaks

    School season and festival rushes meet the wrong stock

    What it looks like

    School shoes sell in a three-week window in June. Monsoon footwear sells out in July. Festive weeks need sandals and party shoes in kids' and women's sizes. Orders are placed on last year's memory.

    What it costs you

    A peak week is a big share of the year, and a shortage cannot be recovered afterwards. Unsold school shoes in July are a month of money in the wrong place.

    How Verity fixes it

    Last year's sales by week and size are laid against this year's stock and open orders. Verity lists the sizes that will run out before a peak, and assigns a reorder task with the brand's lead time.

    See the screen
  10. 10Customers

    Parents of growing children are not brought back

    What it looks like

    A child's shoe size changes every six to nine months. A parent who bought school shoes in June does not return for the festive pair, though the old pair is now too small. Nobody wrote down the child's size or the date.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 400 families buying two pairs a year at ₹1,800 is ₹14.4 lakh. Losing a quarter of them to a nearby shop is ₹3.6 lakh.

    How Verity fixes it

    Purchases are tied to the customer with the child's name and size. Verity lists families past their usual gap with the last size bought, and assigns a follow-up to the staff member who served them. Staff send the message, and the outcome is recorded.

    See the screen
  11. 11Customers

    Exchanges and returns are handled on goodwill

    What it looks like

    A customer returns a worn pair after a wedding. A staff member says yes. The pair goes back in its box and onto the shelf.

    What it costs you

    A sale reversed and a pair that cannot be sold at full price, with no reason recorded.

    How Verity fixes it

    Returns and exchanges are linked to the original bill with a reason, the condition and where the pair goes next. Verity totals returns by style and size so a fit problem is visible before the next order.

    See the screen
  12. 12People

    Sales staff are paid on feel and leave often

    What it looks like

    A good salesperson knows which brand suits which foot. The incentive is worked out in a notebook. When he leaves for the shop next door, his regulars follow.

    What it costs you

    Hours of manager time each month, a salesperson who feels cheated, and customers who move with the person who knew them.

    How Verity fixes it

    Sales, returns and attendance are recorded against each person. Verity builds the incentive sheet from your rule, shows the same figures to the staff member, and keeps customer notes on the customer record, not in one head.

    See the screen
  13. 13Stock

    One outlet sells out while another holds the sizes

    What it looks like

    Your second shop has the size 9 that customers in the first shop are asking for. The transfer is by hand on a bike, on a slip, when someone remembers.

    What it costs you

    Sales lost in one place for stock sitting idle in another, and transfers that go missing in between.

    How Verity fixes it

    Outlets are locations on one record. Verity lists sizes short in one outlet and held in another, drafts the transfer, and keeps it open until the receiving outlet signs the count.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    The owner knows sales but not margin by brand

    What it looks like

    Billing shows daily sales. Which brand earns the best margin after discount, returns and defects is a feeling. A brand with a big name takes the best shelf and gives the least back.

    What it costs you

    The best shelf goes to the brand that earns the least, and reorders follow volume rather than margin.

    How Verity fixes it

    Sales, discount, returns, defect claims and margin are shown by brand and by outlet. Each morning Verity lists what needs a decision, with an owner for each.

How we work with you

We set it up with you, in four weeks.

You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time in your shop and stockroom, build Verity around how you stock and sell, bring your data in, and stay on after go-live.

    What you have at the end

  3. Weeks 3 to 4

    What you have at the end
    Your styles, stock, brands and claims inside Verity, and a team that has used it through a weekend.

  4. After go-live

    Operations partnership

    What we do

    What we need from you
    A short monthly review of what the numbers say.
    What you have at the end
    A system that keeps matching how the shop works, with someone responsible for it.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a shoe store has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • BIS mark on footwear

    What is expected

    Quality control orders for leather and other footwear and for rubber and polymeric footwear came into force on 1 August 2024. Covered footwear generally needs to carry the BIS mark. Old stock made before that date and reported to BIS could be sold until 31 July 2026, and a June 2026 amendment is reported to move that to 31 July 2027. A retailer generally does not hold the licence, but should check that the manufacturer does. Confirm the exact scope and dates with BIS.

    How Verity helps

    Each style records its mark status, declared old stock and the date by which it must be sold, and new deliveries without a recorded mark are flagged.

    UL Solutions, March 2025: https://www.ul.com/news/textiles-apparel-and-footwear-india-issues-new-quality-control-order-footwear; Corpseed, June 2026 amendment summary

  • GST rate by price per pair

    What is expected

    From 22 September 2025, footwear is generally taxed at 5% up to ₹2,500 a pair and 18% above, tested on the sale value and not automatically on the printed price. Your accountant decides how discounts are treated.

    How Verity helps

    Each style records price, discount and final sale value, and Verity flags those near or across the line. Verity does not file returns.

    Outlook Business, PTI, September 2025: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/garments-priced-above-25k-to-be-costlier-will-hurt-middle-class-affordability-industry-bodies; TaxAJ commentary

  • Seller liability for product defects

    What is expected

    The Consumer Protection Act 2019 extends product liability to product sellers, with the relevant sections in force from 20 July 2020.

    How Verity helps

    Each defect return is a record linked to the bill, the pair and the brand, so a claim has a history and a brand to pass it to.

    S.S. Rana on the Act

  • Local trade and shop registrations

    What is expected

    A shop generally needs its municipal trade licence and Shops and Establishments registration, which differ by city and state and have their own renewal dates.

    How Verity helps

    Each registration is stored with its renewal date and owner, so a renewal becomes a task weeks before it lapses.

Rules change and differ by product type and state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions shoe store owners ask.

I have hundreds of styles in ten sizes. Will staff enter every pair?

No. Stock comes in from your deliveries and goes out with the bill, so sizes update as part of work already done. We set the shelf locations once and count in small cycles.

Does Verity replace my billing system?

No. Your billing system keeps taking payments and raising bills. We agree during setup how bills, returns and discounts reach Verity.

Can it track every box and every pair?

It records styles, sizes and shelf locations as stock records, and counts compare the book with the shelf. We do not set up barcode or hardware tracking as part of the standard engagement, so box-level tracking depends on what you can record at the counter.

How does it help with the BIS mark?

It records each style's mark status, whether it is declared old stock and the date by which it must be sold, and flags new deliveries without a recorded mark. It does not certify anything and does not give legal advice.

Can it help with the ₹2,500 GST line?

It shows which prices and discounts cross the line so your team and your accountant can agree the treatment. It does not decide the rate and does not file returns.

We have two shops. Can we see them together?

Yes. Each outlet is a location in one system, with sales, stock and counts side by side, and stock can be moved between them against a recorded transfer.

Will my staff really use it?

Only if it saves them time, so we set up the short lists they need: sizes to move, returns to log, counts to do. We train at the shop during a real shift.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so salespeople, the stock keeper and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of outlets, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your shop runs.
