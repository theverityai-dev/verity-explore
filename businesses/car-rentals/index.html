How it runs today

Most rental operators know how many cars they own and not how many were earning yesterday.

The counter is simple. A car goes out, a car comes back. What is hard to see is the rest: which cars have been idle for six days, which are due for service, what each trip cost in fuel, damage and fines, and which documents expire this month.

That information exists in pieces. A booking sheet or portal. A WhatsApp group for drivers. Photos of handover on someone's phone. A folder of insurance and permit copies. A notebook of service dates. The owner holds the rest in memory, often while also handling bookings.

The market is growing from a small base. One research firm estimates India's self-drive rental market at about USD 141 million in 2025, growing around 14 percent a year to 2031, in a business where unlicensed private cars compete with licensed fleets and regulators have begun to act.

What most car rental businesses run on

  • Booking sheet or portal
  • WhatsApp with drivers and renters
  • Phone photos at handover
  • Folder of insurance, permit and fitness copies
  • Excel for service dates and expenses

Each tool does its own job. None of them tells you that the car going out today has a permit that expires on Friday.

15 problems, one by one

Where a car rental business actually loses money.

These are the situations rental operators describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Fleet

    Cars sit idle between bookings and nobody sees it

    What it looks like

    A hatchback has been parked since Monday. A sedan was double-promised for the weekend. The booking sheet shows both as "available" because nobody updated them after the last return.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a car that earns ₹2,500 a day and is idle for six days a month leaves ₹15,000 a month on the table, per car.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every car has a calendar of bookings, trips, service and repair. Verity shows availability by car and date, lists cars idle for more than a set number of days and warns about double bookings.

  2. 02Fleet

    A car is promised that is not ready

    What it looks like

    The customer arrives at 9 am. The car is in the garage for a brake job that the workshop said would be done yesterday. The substitute has a different fuel type and a different price.

    What it costs you

    A refused booking, a discount, or a customer who never returns.

    How Verity fixes it

    Repairs and service are tasks on the car's calendar with an expected return date. Verity blocks the car for new bookings until the repair is signed off and suggests an equal car that is free.

    See the screen
  3. 03Bookings

    Bookings come from many channels and are entered by hand

    What it looks like

    A walk-in, a call, an aggregator listing, a corporate email and a WhatsApp message for the same weekend. Each is written in a different place and some are entered late.

    What it costs you

    Double bookings, a car sent to the wrong customer, and a pricing mistake on a long trip.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each booking is a record with renter, car, dates, price, deposit and source. Verity shows all bookings on one calendar and flags clashes. Where bookings come from a portal, we agree during setup how they reach Verity.

    See the screen
  4. 04Trips

    Handover has no condition record, so damage is argued

    What it looks like

    A car returns with a scratch on the left door. The renter says it was there before. The pickup photos are on an employee's phone, who left last month.

    What it costs you

    A repair bill that falls on you, or a dispute with a customer, over damage that no one can place in time.

    How Verity fixes it

    Pickup and return have a condition checklist with photos, fuel level, odometer and the renter's acknowledgement, attached to the trip. Verity compares the two and shows what changed.

    See the screen
  5. 05Incidents

    Damage claims and insurance are a slow, paper chase

    What it looks like

    A car is returned with a dent and a cracked headlight. The claim needs forms, photos, a police report if there was an accident, workshop estimates and the policy. The papers are in four places and the claim deadline is near.

    What it costs you

    A guide to Indian self-drive rentals notes that own-damage cover usually comes with a substantial excess, often ₹5,000 to ₹15,000, and tyres are usually not covered. What the policy does not cover, the operator absorbs unless it is recovered from the renter.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each incident is a record with photos, estimates, claim status, the policy used, the excess and the amount recovered from the renter. Verity lists claims by age and the amount outstanding.

    See the screen
  6. 06Challans

    Challans arrive weeks later and the renter has gone

    What it looks like

    A traffic notice for a camera violation comes in the owner's name a month after the trip. The trip sheet shows three renters that week. Finding the right one takes a day.

    What it costs you

    A challan is generated against a vehicle registration number, not a person, and in 2024 roughly three quarters of the fines issued across India were still unpaid, according to a used-car portal citing an industry report. A fine you cannot pass on is yours.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each challan is logged with the car, date and time. Verity matches it to the trip that covers that moment, tasks the right person to recover it under the rental terms, and records the outcome.

    See the screen
  7. 07Maintenance

    Service intervals are tracked by memory and odometer guesses

    What it looks like

    A car has done 11,000 kilometres since its last service, because it has been out on long trips. The next customer takes it to a hill station.

    What it costs you

    A breakdown on a trip costs a tow, a replacement car and a bad review, many times the cost of the service.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each car has an odometer record updated at every handover and return, and service rules by distance and date. Verity raises a service task before the limit and blocks long trips for cars close to it.

    See the screen
  8. 08Compliance

    Insurance, permit, fitness and pollution papers lapse

    What it looks like

    The insurance on two cars expired on Sunday. A permit renewal is in a driver's glove box. A fitness certificate is due for an older vehicle. The renter is stopped at a checkpoint without valid papers.

    What it costs you

    Fines, impounding and, if there is an accident without valid insurance, a claim you cannot make. In Goa a private vehicle rented out without a valid permit can see a ₹10,000 fine and suspension of its registration, according to a 2025 report.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each car's insurance, permit, fitness, pollution certificate and tax is a record with an expiry date and owner. Verity lists papers expiring in 45 days and blocks booking a car whose papers have lapsed.

    See the screen
  9. 09Compliance

    The operating licence and vehicle category are not clear

    What it looks like

    A partner suggests adding five private cars from friends to meet demand. Nobody is sure if they may be used for rental, and in which plates.

    What it costs you

    In August 2025 Bengaluru's transport department seized 21 private cars rented through a self-drive app, and owners faced fines of ₹30,000 to ₹35,000 each as reported. Enforcement is real.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each car record shows its registration category, plate type, permit and licence it operates under. Verity flags cars added without the right category, so the question is asked before the car goes on the road.

  10. 10Drivers

    Drivers' duty hours and trips are managed from a chat

    What it looks like

    A chauffeur-driven booking runs from Bengaluru to Mysuru and back. The driver was on a night duty the day before. The next day another booking needs him at 6 am. Overtime and allowances are worked out at month end.

    What it costs you

    A tired driver, a disputed allowance and a trip run late.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each driver's duty is a schedule with start and end time, trips, allowances and rest. Verity warns when a new trip breaks rest hours you set and totals allowances per trip.

    See the screen
  11. 11Drivers

    Driver behaviour and fuel use are not tied to trips

    What it looks like

    Two drivers take the same route and one uses more fuel. A car comes back with an unexplained extra 40 kilometres on the odometer.

    What it costs you

    Fuel and wear that nobody can trace, and no basis to reward the careful driver.

    How Verity fixes it

    Fuel, distance and trip are recorded together, so Verity shows cost per kilometre by car and by driver, and flags trips whose distance does not match the route.

    See the screen
  12. 12Money

    Deposits, advances and extra charges are settled at the counter

    What it looks like

    A renter returns a day late, with a low fuel tank and a toll receipt for the operator's account. The security deposit is returned in cash with a rough deduction.

    What it costs you

    Undercharged extras and deposits returned wrongly, with no record either way.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each trip records deposit, advance, extra days, fuel, tolls and fines, with the amount charged and the person who approved any waiver. Verity totals what is owed or returned and flags waivers above your limit.

  13. 13Customers

    Corporate and repeat customers are not recognised

    What it looks like

    A company books five cars every month. A returning customer always asks for the same SUV. The counter staff treat both like first-time renters and ask for papers again.

    What it costs you

    A repeat customer who feels unknown goes to a competitor, and a corporate account without a contact owner drifts.

    How Verity fixes it

    Renters and companies are records with every trip, preferences, documents on file and payment history. Verity lists regulars and accounts who have gone quiet and tasks the person who looks after them.

    See the screen
  14. 14Compliance

    Renters' licences and identity papers are copied and kept in photos

    What it looks like

    A scan of a driving licence and an ID card sits in a WhatsApp chat. A year later nobody can say how long it should be kept or who has seen it.

    What it costs you

    A data leak, a complaint and a poor answer to a regulator. Renters' licence validity is also not checked in time.

    How Verity fixes it

    Licence number, validity and ID verification are recorded on the renter with who checked them and when. Documents are kept with access by role and a retention date, and a booking is flagged when a licence has expired.

    Several branches, airport counters and franchise cars with no single view

    Idle cars on one side and refused bookings on the other, and trust lost with partners who cannot see their own numbers.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a car rental business has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Rent-a-Cab licence and commercial plates

    What is expected

    Self-drive rental in India generally operates under the Rent a Cab Scheme, 1989 and the Motor Vehicles Act, with state transport authorities issuing licences and cars needing commercial registration and permits. One report says the scheme sets a minimum fleet of 50 vehicles; state rules vary, so check yours. Using private white-board cars for rental has led to fines, seizures and licence action.

    How Verity helps

    Each car records its registration category, plate type, permit and the licence it operates under. A car added without the right category is flagged before it goes out.

    The Goan on Goa enforcement, 2025: https://thegoan.net/goa-news/govt-to-crack-down-on-private-rented-vehicles/126764.html; News Karnataka, 2025

  • Vehicle papers and roadworthiness

    What is expected

    Insurance, permit, fitness, pollution certificate and road tax generally need to be valid for a commercial vehicle to operate, and penalties for lapses vary by state and by item. Check current amounts with the transport authority.

    How Verity helps

    Each paper has an expiry date and an owner, and a car with a lapsed paper is blocked from bookings.

  • Traffic fines and your rental terms

    What is expected

    A challan is generated against the vehicle registration number, while liability for the violation itself falls on the driver. Recovery from a renter generally depends on your signed rental terms, such as a clause for fines in the rental period and an administrative fee. Some operators publish such terms. Confirm yours with a lawyer.

    How Verity helps

    Each challan is matched to the trip and renter, and the recovery or write-off is recorded.

    Vahan Bazaar on challans, 2026: https://vahanbazaar.in/news/india-challan-crisis-9000-crore-unpaid-used-car-buyers-2026; an operator's published policy, IndusGo, 2026

  • GST on rentals

    What is expected

Questions

Questions car rental owners ask.

I have fifteen cars and one counter person. Is this too much?

A small fleet has the same papers and fines and fewer hands. We set up only what you need in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Does it track GPS or connect to my devices?

No. We do not integrate with named devices. During setup we agree how the odometer or trip data you already collect reaches Verity, and the trip record holds it.

Can it recover challans from the renter?

It matches each challan to the trip that covers the date and time and tasks the right person to recover it under your rental terms. Your staff speak to the renter and the outcome is recorded. Verity does not message the renter itself.

Can it take online bookings?

No. If bookings come from a portal or website today, that keeps working. Verity records each booking and the handover around it.

Will my counter really record condition photos?

Only if it takes seconds, so the checklist is short and the photos are taken on a phone at the car. We train at the counter during real handovers.

We have partners who own cars in our fleet. Can they see their car?

Yes, if you choose. A partner's car shows earning, cost and trips, and you decide what the partner can see.

We run chauffeur-driven trips too. Does it handle drivers?

Yes. Each driver has trips, start and end times, rest and allowances, and Verity warns when a trip breaks the rest hours you set. It does not run payroll.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so counter staff, drivers, the workshop and accounts each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How quickly will I see something useful?

The papers expiring list and the idle car list need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. Challan and damage patterns become reliable after a few weeks of recorded trips.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of cars, branches and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your fleet runs.
