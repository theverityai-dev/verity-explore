How it runs today

Most dance academies run on a founder's energy and a notebook.

The floor is easy to see: a batch of eight children in a mirror room on a Saturday, an instructor who knows every step. What is hard to see is everything behind it: how many of a batch's students paid for this term, which instructor is covering which class, who has a costume and who owes one back.

Costs sit outside the monthly fee. One Chennai guide for parents advises asking about costume fees, exam or grading fees and whether annual day performances carry an additional charge, and cites Bharatanatyam costume sets of about ₹1,500 to ₹5,000. These extras are where parents and academies argue.

Dance carries a public programme as well. The Ministry of Culture's scholarship scheme paid for 313 classical dance scholarships in 2025-26 and about ₹150.61 crore in grants to cultural bodies for school-level classical dance, according to a parliamentary reply. Classical forms have a long tradition of guru and disciple, but the business of running a class still needs records.

What most dance academies run on

  • Notebook or sheet for batches and attendance
  • Excel or UPI screenshots for fees
  • WhatsApp groups per batch
  • Cupboards and a list for costumes
  • Chat messages for annual day planning

Each one works. None of them tells you that Saturday's 4 pm batch has no instructor.

14 problems, one by one

Where a dance academy actually loses students and money.

These are the situations owners, instructors and front-desk staff describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Students

    Students stop coming after the first term

    What it looks like

    A child joins a Bollywood batch in April, enjoys the first month, then misses two weeks for exams. Nobody calls. When the new term starts, her seat is empty and the fee never comes.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 150 students at ₹2,500 a month is ₹3.75 lakh a month. Losing one in eight without noticing is about ₹47,000 a month, over ₹5.6 lakh a year.

    How Verity fixes it

    Attendance is recorded per class. Verity lists students who miss more than your limit, with their batch and fee status, and assigns a follow-up task to the instructor or front desk. What the parent said is recorded.

    See the screen
  2. 02Students

    Trial classes and enquiries are lost in chats

    What it looks like

    Instagram brings in thirty enquiries a month about hip hop and Kathak. Replies depend on who has the phone. Trial classes are promised for Saturday and nobody tells the instructor.

    What it costs you

    A trial is the best sale an academy has. An unfollowed trial is a lost year of fees.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each enquiry and trial is a record with style, age, preferred timing and next step. Verity lists trials with no follow-up and assigns each to a named person. Staff make the call; the outcome is recorded.

    See the screen
  3. 03Batches

    Batches are too thin or too full

    What it looks like

    The Kathak batch has four students, and the hip hop batch has twenty-six in a room built for eighteen. The owner finds out when the instructor complains.

    What it costs you

    A thin batch loses money every class, and an overfull one loses students who feel unseen or unsafe on a crowded floor.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each batch has a minimum, a cap and a floor capacity you set. Verity shows batches by students enrolled and flags those under the minimum or over capacity, with the room and instructor.

    See the screen
  4. 04Instructors

    Instructors are paid per class and the count is disputed

    What it looks like

    A Bharatanatyam guru is paid per session. At month end she counts 31 classes and the academy counts 28. Some were rescheduled, some were covered by a senior student, and some were group workshops.

    What it costs you

    A dispute over three classes sours a relationship with someone who may take her students elsewhere.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each class is recorded with the instructor, duration, type and whether it was taken, covered or cancelled. Verity totals classes by instructor for the month so the accountant works from a record. The academy still runs its own payroll.

    See the screen
  5. 05Instructors

    A star instructor leaves and the batch follows

    What it looks like

    The best contemporary instructor opens her own studio. Her students follow, because the academy has no record of each student's level or progress and no second instructor who knows them.

    What it costs you

    The loss is not one instructor but her whole batch list.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student has a record with style, level, items learned and notes. A second instructor is named for each batch. Verity shows batches without a backup.

    See the screen
  6. 06Students

    Missed classes and make-ups are on WhatsApp

    What it looks like

    A batch is cancelled for a public holiday. A student is told she can join another batch, another is told to wait. By the next term parents say they have lost classes they paid for.

    What it costs you

    Parents who feel they lost paid classes do not renew, and unrecorded make-ups distort attendance.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each cancelled class is recorded with the reason and creates a make-up owed to each student. Verity lists make-ups owed and expiring, and records each when taken.

    See the screen
  7. 07Fees

    Fee renewals lapse and the extras are argued

    What it looks like

    Fees are paid monthly or per quarter. Some families pay for costumes, some for exams and some for the annual day. A family disputes a charge because the academy never wrote down what was included.

    What it costs you

    Overdue fees are a direct cash gap. Disputes about costume and event charges are the commonest cause of a family leaving on bad terms.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student has a plan with due dates, discounts such as sibling, and what is included: costume, exam fee, annual day. Verity lists renewals due and dues by age, with a task for a named person. Payments come through your existing channels.

    See the screen
  8. 08Costumes

    Costumes: who has what, and what is owed back

    What it looks like

    The academy owns forty Bharatanatyam sets, thirty folk costumes and a rack of hip hop jackets. For the annual day, costumes are assigned from memory. After the show, six sets are not returned and two are damaged.

    What it costs you

    Costume sets are a real purchase, with Bharatanatyam sets quoted at roughly ₹1,500 to ₹5,000 in one parents' guide. Lost sets are bought again.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each costume is an asset record with size, condition, who holds it and when it is due back. Verity lists costumes not returned, those needing repair and sizes short for the next event.

    See the screen
  9. 09Costumes

    Costume sizes and orders are worked out the week before

    What it looks like

    Sixty children need costumes for the annual day. Sizes are collected on a paper, a tailor is found late and a rush charge is paid.

    What it costs you

    Rush orders cost more, and a child with the wrong size on stage is remembered by parents.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student's measurements and costume needs are recorded per item in an event. Verity shows items and sizes to buy or stitch, by vendor and due date, and the cost against what was collected.

    See the screen
  10. 10Events

    Annual day and recitals run on chat groups

    What it looks like

    The annual day has 14 items, 3 rehearsals, a hall, a sound team, a photographer and a programme to print. Parents ask what time their child performs. The answers are in three chats and two sheets.

    What it costs you

    A badly run annual day is what parents remember, and last-minute changes leave students unprepared. One setup guide advises deciding early whether showcases are a cost or an income line.

    How Verity fixes it

    An event is a record with items, performers, rehearsals, venue, vendors and tasks with owners. Verity shows who is not yet in the line-up, which items lack a rehearsal and what the event costs against what has been collected.

    See the screen
  11. 11Compliance

    Music licences for events and recorded tracks

    What it looks like

    Dances at a show are performed to recorded film songs and some live music. Nobody has checked whether a licence is needed, or who to ask.

    What it costs you

    In India, IPRS generally licenses live performance of music and PPL generally licenses recorded music used outside a private setting. Whether a given event is exempt is not always clear, so the academy should confirm it.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each event record holds a checklist: the music used, whether recordings are used, the licence status and who decided. Verity raises a task for the owner before the date. The academy confirms with the rights bodies or an adviser.

    See the screen
  12. 12Compliance

    Child safety, floors and photos

    What it looks like

    A parent raises a concern about an instructor. There is no written policy, no record of the instructor's references and no named person. Elsewhere, a student sprains an ankle on a slippery floor and photos of children go on the academy's page without a record of consent.

    What it costs you

    Section 19 of the POCSO Act generally obliges any person who knows or has reason to believe an offence has been committed to report it. Children's photos and data also need care under the DPDP Act.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each instructor has a record with identity checks, references and training. The child safety policy and named contact are kept as documents. Concerns and incidents are logged with who acted, with access limited by role. Photo consents are recorded on the student with their purpose.

    See the screen
  13. 13Exams

    Exams, gradings and scholarships

    What it looks like

    Classical students may sit gradings or apply for cultural scholarships. The Ministry of Culture's Cultural Talent Search Scholarship pays ₹3,600 a year plus up to ₹9,000 a year to the guru for tuition. Documents and dates are in the guru's cupboard.

    What it costs you

    A missed entry date or an incomplete form costs a student a year, and parents blame the academy.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student's exam or scholarship is a record with the body, date, fee and documents needed. Verity lists students who should apply and those whose papers are incomplete. Staff and gurus apply on the official channel.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    More than one branch or rented studio hour

    What it looks like

    The academy rents a hall on weekdays and has a second branch in a school. Rent, rooms and instructors move between them. The owner cannot say which branch earns more.

    What it costs you

    Rent paid for empty hours, and no basis for deciding where to add a batch.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each branch and room is a location. Verity shows batches, students, collections and room use side by side, with empty rented hours listed.

How we work with you

We set it up with you, in four weeks.

You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time in your studio during classes, build Verity around how your batches, instructors and shows really run, bring your data in, then stay to make sure it keeps working.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a dance academy has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Child protection and POCSO reporting

    What is expected

    Section 19 of the POCSO Act generally places a duty on any person who has knowledge of an offence to report it. Institutions working with children are generally advised to keep a written child protection policy, a reporting protocol and background checks for staff.

    How Verity helps

    Instructor identity and reference checks, the policy and the named contact are records, and concerns are logged with limited access.

    Sansa Legal

  • Music licences for performances and recordings

    What is expected

    In India, IPRS generally licenses live performance of music and PPL licenses recorded music used outside a private setting. Whether an academy show is exempt is not clear from the sources found, so confirm it.

    How Verity helps

    Each event has a licence checklist and an owner, and the decision is recorded.

    S.S. Rana & Co., 2019

  • Premises, fire and local registrations

    What is expected

    Studios generally need local trade, building and fire approvals, and the rules differ by place.

    How Verity helps

    Each approval is a record with its renewal date and owner, and a task is raised before it lapses.

    Raysolute

  • Tax

    What is expected

    GST treatment of dance classes depends on the nature of the course and whether it leads to a recognised qualification. One non-official guide lists hobby classes at 18%, so ask your accountant.

    How Verity helps

    Fees received are recorded by plan and your accountant prepares returns.

  • Children's data and photos under the DPDP Act

    What is expected

    Students under 18 generally need verifiable parental consent for their data, including photos used in promotion.

    How Verity helps

    Consent is recorded on the student with its purpose, and access is by role.

Rules differ by state and by the kind of events you hold, and they change. Confirm current requirements with your adviser.

Questions

Questions dance academy owners ask.

I am the lead instructor. Will I have time to use this?

It is built so the record is a by-product of the work. An instructor marks a class taken or missed in a few seconds. You see what needs attention in the morning rather than searching for it.

We teach several styles. Does one setup cover them?

Yes. Each style has its own levels, batches and fees, set up in the first weeks. The records and screens are the same for all of them.

Can it track costumes by size and by child?

Yes. Each costume is a record with size, condition and holder, and each event lists items and sizes needed. Verity does not manage a tailor's work; it records the order and due date.

Does it run the annual day?

It keeps the line-up, rehearsals, vendors, costs and tasks in one place. Your team still makes the programme and runs the show.

Can it handle workshops and guest instructors?

Yes. A workshop is a batch with its own dates and fee, and a guest instructor is a person with their own rate and classes.

Do parents see their child's records?
