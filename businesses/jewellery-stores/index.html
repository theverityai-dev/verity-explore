How it runs today

Most jewellery shops can say what the gold rate is. Fewer can say where every gram is right now.

The showcase is easy to see. The ring at the front, the wedding set in the vault, the necklace on trial. What is hard to see is everything behind it: how many grams sit with karigars, what the old gold bought this month really assayed at, which designs have not moved in a year, and which scheme customers are due this week.

That information exists in pieces. The billing software knows what was sold. The stock register is a book or a sheet. Karigar issue slips are on paper. Scheme instalments are in a ledger. The owner, who is often also the buyer and the closer, holds the rest in memory.

Gold prices have been at record highs. The World Gold Council reported India's jewellery demand at 430.5 tonnes in 2025, down 24% from the year before. When fewer grams sell at a higher price, each gram of loss, each stuck design and each unpaid instalment matters more.

What most jewellery stores run on

  • Billing software at the counter
  • A stock register or Excel sheet
  • Paper issue and receipt slips for karigars
  • A scheme ledger or card
  • WhatsApp groups with customers and karigars

Each one does its own job. None of them agree on how many grams you actually hold.

14 problems, one by one

Where a jewellery shop actually loses money.

These are the situations jewellers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Pricing

    Quotes at yesterday's gold rate

    What it looks like

    The rate changed at 11 am. A customer was quoted at 10:30, comes back at 4 pm for a necklace and expects the old number. A new salesperson works from a rate that was updated twice on the counter board and once by phone.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: on a 40 gram chain, a rate gap of ₹300 a gram is ₹12,000 given away or a sale lost. Over a month of small mismatches, that is real money with no name on it.

    How Verity fixes it

    The day's rate is one record, changed by one person with the time stamped. Estimates take the rate and the making charge from it, and an estimate older than a limit you set is marked stale before it is turned into a sale.

    See the screen
  2. 02Pricing

    Making charges discounted at the counter with no trail

    What it looks like

    A regular asks for less on making. The salesperson says yes to keep the sale. Another customer, on the same design, gets a different number the next day.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: on a ₹3 lakh sale with making at 12%, a two point discount is ₹6,000. Ten such sales a month is ₹60,000 that nobody approved.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each estimate carries the making charge, the wastage and the discount asked. Above your limit it goes to the owner or manager for one tap approval with a reason, and the month's discounts are totalled by person and by design.

    See the screen
  3. 03Old gold

    Old gold exchange, with a purity dispute at the counter

    What it looks like

    A customer brings a chain and says it is 22 carat. The test says 20. Another brings broken pieces and wants a higher rate than the day's. The deduction for impurity and melting is argued out in the shop, and each salesperson settles it differently.

    What it costs you

    Old gold exchange is a growing share of how jewellers get gold, so each wrong purity call becomes a direct loss or an angry customer. Example arithmetic: 50 grams valued at 22 carat that tests at 20 carat is a gap of about 4 grams of fine gold, which at ₹8,000 a gram is ₹32,000.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every exchange is a record: weight, test reading, agreed purity, rate used, deduction and who approved it. The customer's name and ID details sit on the record and the value is carried to the new bill as a credit.

    See the screen
  4. 04Old gold

    Old gold sits in a drawer and nobody can say what it is worth

    What it looks like

    Exchanged pieces collect in a tray. Some are melted, some are sent to a refiner, some are sold on. At month end the book says 600 grams of old gold, the tray says 540.

    What it costs you

    Gold with no record is gold that can leave unnoticed. Example arithmetic: 60 grams short at ₹8,000 a gram is ₹4.8 lakh.

    How Verity fixes it

    Old gold lots are records with a status: received, held, sent to refiner, returned as fine gold. Verity compares what was received with what came back from the refiner and flags the shortfall by lot.

    See the screen
  5. 05Stock

    The tag stock never matches the book

    What it looks like

    Close of day. The sheet says 1,240 tagged pieces, the trays hold 1,236. Two are with a customer on approval and nobody noted it. One is a ring that was sold, billed, and still shows on the shelf list.

    What it costs you

    A piece missing is a loss of lakhs, and a daily reconcile that takes an hour from your best salesperson is an hour not spent selling.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every piece is a record with its tag number, gross and net weight, and a state: in showcase, in vault, on approval, sold. Taking a piece out on approval is a two tap entry with the customer's name and a return time. The close-of-day count compares trays to the record and lists only the pieces that differ.

    See the screen
  6. 06Compliance

    Hallmark and HUID records that do not match the piece

    What it looks like

    A bangle is billed as 22 carat but the HUID on the piece belongs to another item. A batch from the supplier arrives without the hallmark details written down. A customer checks the HUID on their phone and finds a mismatch.

    What it costs you

    Mandatory hallmarking has applied in 380 districts since 2 March 2026, and the Bureau of Indian Standards runs the HUID check for buyers. A mismatch is a complaint, a fine risk and a customer who tells others.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each hallmarked piece stores its HUID, purity and tag. Verity flags pieces that show in stock without a HUID where one is required, and checks the HUID on the bill against the piece before the sale is closed.

    See the screen
  7. 07Stock

    Designs that sit for a year while the gold in them could be working

    What it looks like

    A heavy antique set was bought two years ago and still sits in the vault. A light daily wear range sold out in a month and you reordered late. Buying follows what the owner liked, not what sold.

    What it costs you

    Gold stuck in slow designs is cash that is not available and exposed to the rate. Example arithmetic: ₹25 lakh in designs that have not sold in 12 months, at an 8% yearly cost of money, is ₹2 lakh a year.

    How Verity fixes it

    Sell-through by design, weight band and age is calculated from the tag records. The slow list shows what to melt, rework or discount, and the fast list shows what to restock before it runs out.

    See the screen
  8. 08Karigars

    Gold goes to the karigar and comes back lighter

    What it looks like

    120 grams issued for a necklace. 112 grams of finished work returns, plus 4 grams of scrap. The slip says 3 grams allowed as wastage. The difference is argued from memory a month later.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a gap of 1 gram a job across 40 jobs a month is 40 grams, about ₹3.2 lakh at ₹8,000 a gram. Gold held by karigars that is not clearly on your books is also a problem when records are checked.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job records gold issued, finished weight, scrap returned and wastage allowed. Verity shows gold held by each karigar at any time and flags a job whose loss goes beyond the agreed wastage.

    See the screen
  9. 09Karigars

    Custom orders that miss the date

    What it looks like

    A bride's family orders a set for a wedding on 14 November. The design is approved on WhatsApp, the advance is in the book, the karigar is busy with another order. Nobody checks until the week before.

    What it costs you

    A missed wedding date is a lost customer and a story told to every relative. Advances taken with no tracking are also liabilities that are easy to forget.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every order is a record with the design reference, weight estimate, advance, due date and the karigar. Orders at risk show on the board as the date nears, and the customer's fitting or pick up is a scheduled task.

    See the screen
  10. 10Schemes

    Savings scheme instalments on paper

    What it looks like

    A customer pays ₹5,000 a month for eleven months in a scheme ledger. One card is lost. Another customer says she paid in June. The bonus month is promised in different words by different staff.

    What it costs you

    A scheme is a promise and a liability. Disputes are costly in goodwill, and in the past, regulators have looked at such schemes. The rules for companies in this area were tightened as far back as 2014.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each scheme member is a record with every instalment, the due date, the bonus rule and the maturity date. Verity lists members who missed an instalment, those who mature this month, and the total held against the scheme.

    See the screen
  11. 11Customers

    Birthdays, anniversaries and regulars that nobody remembers

    What it looks like

    A family bought a mangalsutra five years ago. Their daughter is getting married. Nobody at the shop knows, and they go to the newer showroom across town.

    What it costs you

    Jewellery buying is tied to occasions and families. A shop that remembers wins the next purchase, and one that does not loses it quietly.

    How Verity fixes it

    Customers carry family members, past purchases, important dates and the salesperson who knows them. Verity lists upcoming occasions and customers who have not visited past their usual gap, and gives the task to the right person. Staff make the call.

    See the screen
  12. 12Compliance

    Large cash sales and ID records scattered

    What it looks like

    A customer pays a large amount in cash for a set. The PAN is on a photocopy in one file, the Aadhaar number on a slip in another, and the exchange value in the billing software only.

    What it costs you

    Large cash dealings come with record and reporting duties, and the records are asked for when an officer or auditor visits. Hunting for them costs days.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each large sale or exchange is linked to the customer record with the ID details and the payment mode. Verity flags sales above the limit you set so the right details are captured while the customer is still at the counter.

    See the screen
  13. 13Money

    Money locked in stock, loans and metal loans

    What it looks like

    Gold is up and the vault is worth more than last year, but the cash account is thin. A gold loan from the bank has an interest date. Some stock was bought on credit from a wholesaler with a due date.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹2 crore of stock funded at a 10% yearly cost is ₹20 lakh a year, whether or not the stock sells.

    How Verity fixes it

    Stock value by design age, supplier payments due and loan dates sit on one screen. The owner sees how much money is tied up in slow stock against what is due in the next 30 days.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    More than one showroom and no common picture

    What it looks like

    The second showroom reports by phone at night. A piece moved between showrooms is on paper at one end and nowhere at the other. Salespeople at one showroom sell a design the other has more of.

    What it costs you

    Pieces in transit are the easiest to lose. Without side by side numbers, you cannot say which showroom earns more per gram of stock.

    How Verity fixes it

    Showrooms are locations in one system. A transfer is a record that must be received at the other end. Sales, stock age, exchange and scheme numbers show side by side, and every morning a short list shows what needs a decision.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a jewellery shop has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • BIS hallmarking and HUID

    What is expected

    Hallmarking of gold jewellery is generally mandatory in notified districts, now 380, for 14, 18, 20, 22, 23 and 24 karat. Each hallmarked piece carries a HUID, which buyers can check on the BIS CARE app. Confirm whether your district is covered.

    How Verity helps

    Each piece stores its HUID, purity and tag. Pieces without a HUID where one is required are flagged, and the HUID on a bill is matched to the piece.

    All India Radio News

  • Silver hallmarking

    What is expected

    Silver hallmarking is voluntary as of June 2026, but hallmarked silver carries a HUID from September 2025. The Bureau of Indian Standards is studying a mandatory regime and has set no date. One trade report suggested October 2026 and it is unconfirmed.

    How Verity helps

    Silver pieces carry the same fields as gold, so a change in the rule is a setting, not a rebuild.

    ETV Bharat, citing PTI

  • Cash sales, PAN and reporting

    What is expected

    Dealers in precious metals and stones generally have record and reporting duties for large cash dealings with a customer, and PAN or ID is generally needed above set sale values. The thresholds and procedures have changed over time, so confirm the current ones with your accountant.

    How Verity helps

    Each large sale or exchange is linked to the customer record with ID details and payment mode, and sales above the limit you set are flagged at the counter.

  • Savings schemes

    What is expected

    Schemes that collect instalments from customers have been looked at by regulators for years, and rules for companies on accepting deposits have applied since 2014. Whether and how a scheme is permitted depends on how your business is set up, so take advice before you run or change one.

    How Verity helps

    Each scheme has its terms, members, instalments and maturity as records, so what you owe members is always clear.

    Moneylife, 2014

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant, your Bureau of Indian Standards licence contact or the relevant authority.

Who sees what

Questions

Questions jewellers ask.

Will my salespeople actually enter exchange and approval details at a busy counter?

Only if it takes seconds. We set exchange as a short form with a few fields and approval as one tap, and we train at the counter during real hours. We start with what matters most and add fields later.

Does Verity replace my billing software?

No. Your billing keeps making bills. We agree during setup how bills and payments reach Verity, which then does the stock, karigar, exchange, scheme and customer work around them.

Can it tell me the exact gold rate?

No. You or your manager enter the day's rate, with the time. Verity uses that one rate in every estimate and shows when it has gone stale.

Can it handle HUID and hallmark records?

Yes, as fields on each piece, matched to the bill. Verity does not check the BIS system itself. Your team verifies the HUID the way they do today.

I use karigars outside the shop. Can it track gold I give them?

Yes. Each job records issue, return, scrap and wastage, so you see the gold each karigar holds and how a job compares with the allowed loss.

Can it run my savings scheme?

It records members, instalments, bonus terms and maturity, and lists who is due or has missed. It does not collect money and does not advise whether your scheme is permitted. Take that advice from your adviser.

I have two showrooms and a small workshop. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each is a location in one system, with stock, exchange and scheme numbers side by side. A transfer is a record that must be received at the other end.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so a salesperson can see the price but not your cost, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of showrooms, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your shop runs.
