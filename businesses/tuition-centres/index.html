How it runs today

The classroom works. The notebook behind it is doing too much.

A good tuition teacher knows every child by name, which chapter they are stuck on and how their parents like to be spoken to. What the teacher often does not know is who has not paid for October, which batch has an empty seat, or how many students will leave when the school year ends.

The records are in a fee notebook, an attendance register, a phone full of parent numbers and a few WhatsApp groups. They are accurate on a good week. In exam season, when the centre is busiest, the notebook is the first thing to fall behind.

Nothing here is dramatic. It is a month of fees collected two weeks late, a parent who stops replying, a student who quietly joins another centre, and the owner who never gets an evening off.

What most tuition centres run on

  • Fee notebook or receipt book
  • Attendance register
  • Parent WhatsApp groups
  • Test marks in a diary
  • Phone contacts

Each is simple and fast. Together they cannot say who owes, who is slipping or who is about to leave.

13 problems, one by one

Where a tuition centre loses money and students.

These are the situations tuition teachers describe. For each: what it looks like in a normal month, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Fees

    Monthly fees are collected by memory, and asking feels awkward

    What it looks like

    Fifty-eight students, fees due on the 5th. By the 20th, twelve have paid late and nine have not paid at all. You remember some of them. You are embarrassed to ask the others in front of the class.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 20 students unpaid for two weeks at ₹1,500 a month is ₹30,000 not in hand. If six of them are quietly skipped for a month, that is ₹9,000 you never collect.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student has a monthly fee line. Anyone unpaid after the due date is on a short daily list with the amount and who has been told, so the call is a task and the awkwardness is taken out of it.

    See the screen
  2. 02Fees

    Mid-month joins, leave and fee changes confuse the books

    What it looks like

    A student joins on the 17th and is charged half. Another is away for two weeks for a wedding. A sibling gets a discount that you agreed over the phone. At month end nobody agrees on what each should pay.

    What it costs you

    Arguments with parents, under-charged months, and a standing worry that someone has been charged wrongly.

    How Verity fixes it

    Joining date, leave, siblings and discounts are recorded on the student with a reason. The monthly amount is worked out from those records, and the parent sees the same figure.

    See the screen
  3. 03Fees

    Cash and receipts do not match at the end of the week

    What it looks like

    Some pay by cash, some by UPI to your phone, some give money to the child to hand over. The receipt book is behind. You are not sure whether that ₹1,200 was Divya’s fee or Aarav’s.

    What it costs you

    Small mismatches that cannot be traced, and fees that were received but never marked as paid, so you chase a parent who has already paid.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every payment is recorded against a student, the month it covers and the mode, and a weekly page shows receipts against what was marked paid. An unmatched payment is flagged with the date.

    See the screen
  4. 04Admissions

    The April and May rush brings in enquiries that are never followed up

    What it looks like

    After results, phones ring all week. You note a few numbers on a scrap of paper between classes. By June half of them have found a centre that called them back.

    What it costs you

    Admission season decides the year. An enquiry that goes cold in week one is a student you will not see until the next year, if at all.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every enquiry is a record with the child’s class, school, subjects and the parent’s preferred time. Those not called back appear on your list, and the outcome of each call is written down.

    See the screen
  5. 05Admissions

    Students leave at the end of each school year and no one asks why

    What it looks like

    Class 10 finishes and the batch thins out. Some continue to Class 11, some go to a bigger centre, some are done. You never find out which, or whether it could have been different.

    What it costs you

    Each lost student is a year of fees. If you keep half of the leavers by a simple call in March, the next year starts stronger.

    How Verity fixes it

    Students due to move up are listed 60 days before the year ends, with their fee history and results. Each gets a call task, and the reason for leaving is recorded as a plain choice that adds up over the year.

    See the screen
  6. 06Students

    Parents ask "how is my child doing" and you answer from memory

    What it looks like

    A parent texts at 9 pm. You remember the last test but not the one before, nor how many classes were missed. You answer well enough, but not with numbers.

    What it costs you

    Parents who do not see progress question the fee. Trust is the product, and it is built on being able to show something.

    How Verity fixes it

    Attendance, test marks by subject and your notes for each child sit on one page. A monthly progress summary comes straight from them, and you send it.

    See the screen
  7. 07Students

    Test marks and homework are scattered, so a slipping child goes unnoticed

    What it looks like

    Marks are in a diary, homework in your head. A Class 8 student has done no homework for two weeks and scored 14 out of 30 in maths. You see it only when the parent calls.

    What it costs you

    The child falls behind and the parent reads it as your failure.

    How Verity fixes it

    Marks and homework status are recorded per child. Three poor scores or repeated missed homework put the child on your list, with the weakest chapter shown, so you speak to the parent first.

    See the screen
  8. 08Students

    Absences are not followed up the same day

    What it looks like

    A student is absent for four days. You assume illness. It turns out the child has stopped attending because a friend moved to another centre.

    What it costs you

    A lost student that a phone call on day two might have saved, and a fee month at risk.

    How Verity fixes it

    Attendance is taken per batch, and a student absent twice in a row is flagged for a call that day. The reason given is noted on the child’s page.

    See the screen
  9. 09Batches

    Batch timings clash with school timings, exams and holidays

    What it looks like

    The Class 9 science batch runs at 4 pm. The school changes its timetable in September and six students cannot come. A festival week and a school exam week leave you rescheduling by phone.

    What it costs you

    Missed classes, make-up sessions that nobody records, and parents who feel the centre is disorganised.

    How Verity fixes it

    Batches have a day, time and room. A change or holiday updates the batch calendar and lists the students affected, so make-up classes are scheduled and recorded.

    See the screen
  10. 10Batches

    A single room with many batches leaves seats empty and rooms overfull

    What it looks like

    Class 10 maths has 18 students in a room for 12. Class 6 English has 5. You know it, but moving anyone means a conversation with each parent.

    What it costs you

    Poor teaching conditions in one batch and idle capacity in another, with no number to show which one to fix first.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each batch has a capacity, a roster and a room slot. The batches page shows fill levels together, and a move updates the roster, the fee line and the calendar in one step.

    See the screen
  11. 11Teachers

    A part-time teacher leaves in the middle of the term

    What it looks like

    The college student who took Class 7 and 8 science gets an internship and stops coming. Her students, her marks and her lesson plans were in her own notebook.

    What it costs you

    Classes cancelled or covered badly, and parents who ask why the teacher changed.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each batch has a named teacher and a backup. Attendance, marks and lesson notes are kept in the centre’s records, so a new teacher can start from where the last one stopped.

    See the screen
  12. 12Teachers

    Teacher pay by hours is worked out from memory

    What it looks like

    Three part-time teachers are paid per class. At month end each says how many they took. You have to remember who covered whom.

    What it costs you

    Disagreements, and either overpaying or losing a good teacher who feels under-counted.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each class taken is marked against the teacher, with swaps and cover recorded. A monthly statement is built from those marks. You still make the payment yourself.

    See the screen
  13. 13Owner

    You are the whole office

    What it looks like

    You teach five hours, then answer calls, chase fees, set test papers and update the register. When you are ill, the centre stops.

    What it costs you

    The centre can only be as big as one person’s evenings, and the owner has no day off in exam season.

    How Verity fixes it

    The Today page lists who to call, which fees are late, which batches change tomorrow and which tests are due. A helper can run the list when you are away.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a tuition centre should know about.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps your records in order so your accountant is not rebuilding them.

  • GST on tuition and coaching fees

    What is expected

    Fees from private tuition and coaching are generally taxable, unlike regular school education, which is exempt. Registration is generally needed only above an annual turnover limit, commonly ₹20 lakh, or ₹10 lakh in some special category states. Check the current rules and your own position with a CA.

    How Verity helps

    Fees received are recorded by month and student, so your accountant can see the turnover without going through a notebook.

  • Local registration and fee rules

    What is expected

    Some states regulate coaching and tuition centres through their own laws, covering registration, fees and safety. The Ministry of Education also issued guidelines on coaching centres in January 2024. Whether these apply to a small tuition centre depends on your state and the way it defines coaching.

    How Verity helps

    Your registration number, renewal date and fee receipts are stored with the centre, with a reminder before renewal.

    Careers360, January 2024

  • Children’s personal data

    What is expected

    Names, phone numbers, school and marks of children are personal data. India’s data protection rules notified in November 2025 generally expect a parent’s consent for children’s data and start to apply in stages, so check the dates with your adviser.

    How Verity helps

    Student data sits in one permissioned place with a record of who viewed it, in place of personal phones and photos.

  • Safety of children in your care

    What is expected

    A centre that teaches children is expected to take care over who teaches them and who collects them. Schools and many centres check teacher backgrounds and keep a record of authorised pick-ups.

    How Verity helps

    Each teacher’s papers and each child’s authorised pick-up names are kept as records on the centre’s system.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant, adviser or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions tuition teachers ask.

I have only 60 students. Is this too much for me?

No. A centre of 60 has the same fee chasing, absentees and parent questions as a big one, with fewer hours to cope. We set up only what you need, and the four weeks are the same.

I am not good with computers. Will I manage?

The daily use is attendance, a fee tick and a mark, which is about what you do on paper now. We train you in your first live week, with your own students.

Can Verity send fee reminders to parents?

Not by itself. It lists who is late and what they promised, and you or your helper call or message. The outcome is then recorded.

Does it work for home tuition and small groups?

Yes. A batch can be a group of three or a single student, with the day, time and place of each lesson, and the fee line works the same way.

Can parents see their child’s marks?

You decide what to share. Verity builds a short summary of attendance, marks and a note, and you send it to the parent. Parents do not log in.

We teach different school boards. Does that matter?

It is set up as a label on each class, so you can see marks and coverage by board. We agree the labels with you in week 1.

Who can see my students’ details?

Only the people you allow. Teachers see their own batches, a helper sees fees and calls, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.

How long before I see a benefit?

The late-fee list and the uncalled-enquiry list appear as soon as your students and fees are loaded, in week 3 or 4. Marks and attendance trends become useful after a few weeks.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of students, batches and teachers. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your centre runs.
