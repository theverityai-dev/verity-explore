How it runs today

Tailoring shops run on memory. A good master remembers every customer. A busy week breaks that.

A customer's measurements are on a slip, in a register or in the master's head. A returning customer is measured again because the old slip cannot be found.

The shop works in bursts. In the weeks before festivals and weddings, orders pile up faster than the karigars can finish them, and promised dates are given by hope.

Work is shared across cutting, stitching, finishing and sometimes outside embroidery. Payment to karigars is by piece, tracked in a notebook, and disputes are settled by memory.

What most tailoring shops run on

  • Order slip book
  • Measurement register
  • WhatsApp with customers and karigars
  • Cash book
  • Fabric and trim cupboard

Each tells you one thing. Nothing tells you which orders are at risk this week or what each karigar is owed.

14 problems, one by one

Where a tailoring shop loses time, money and customers.

These are the situations tailors and boutique owners describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Orders

    Measurements are lost or out of date

    What it looks like

    A regular customer returns after a year. The old slip cannot be found, so she is measured again, and the fit is slightly different from last time.

    What it costs you

    Time at the counter, a garment that does not fit like her favourite one and a customer who starts looking elsewhere.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each customer has a record with her measurements, date taken and notes on posture and preferences. A returning customer starts from the last record and changes are marked.

    See the screen
  2. 02Orders

    Order details are said, not written

    What it looks like

    The customer asks for a deeper neck, a longer sleeve and piping on the border. The counter says "noted". The karigar gets a slip that says "blouse, red".

    What it costs you

    A garment that is wrong, a redo at the shop's cost and a customer who will not accept the explanation.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each garment order records style, fabric, lining, trims and every instruction, with a photo of the reference if available. The karigar sees the same record the counter wrote.

    See the screen
  3. 03Capacity

    The festival and wedding rush overbooks the shop

    What it looks like

    Three weeks before a festival, the counter accepts every order and promises "by the 28th". By the 25th, thirty garments are unfinished.

    What it costs you

    Missed deadlines, disappointed customers and overtime pay. Tailoring is seasonal, with work clustered around festivals and wedding months, so the same problem returns every year.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each day has a capacity set by karigars and garment type. Verity shows how booked each date is, and a new order checks the date against it before a promise is made.

    See the screen
  4. 04Work

    Nobody can say where a garment is

    What it looks like

    A blouse is with the cutter, then the karigar, then waiting for hooks. A customer comes at 5 pm and nobody can find it.

    What it costs you

    Hours spent searching, a late delivery and a customer who feels ignored.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each garment moves through cutting, stitching, trial, finishing and ready. Everyone sees its stage and who has it, and the counter answers from the record.

    See the screen
  5. 05People

    Karigar pay is counted by hand and argued about

    What it looks like

    Karigars are paid per piece. At the end of the week the master adds slips from a notebook, subtracts advances and gets a total that someone disputes.

    What it costs you

    Hours of the master's time, lost trust and good karigars who leave for a shop that pays on time.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each piece done is recorded against the karigar and its rate. Weekly pay is calculated from the records with advances taken out, and the karigar can see the same statement.

    See the screen
  6. 06Work

    Trials and alterations are not recorded

    What it looks like

    A customer tries a blouse and asks for changes. The alteration goes to a karigar who is busy. At the second trial the same fault is still there.

    What it costs you

    Rework that nobody counts, delays and annoyed customers. A shop that does not record alterations cannot tell which karigar or which style causes them.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each trial is a record with what was found, who altered it and the new trial date. Verity shows alterations by karigar and by style.

    See the screen
  7. 07Stock

    Customer fabric and shop fabric get mixed up

    What it looks like

    A customer leaves three metres of silk. Another buys fabric from the shop. Leftover pieces sit on the shelf and nobody can say whose they are.

    What it costs you

    A dispute over missing fabric, a customer's silk used for the wrong garment or leftover cloth with no value attached.

    How Verity fixes it

    Fabric received from a customer is recorded with a photo and quantity against the order. Shop fabric is stock with a cost. Leftover pieces are recorded and returned or priced.

    See the screen
  8. 08Stock

    Cutting mistakes and fabric shortfalls

    What it looks like

    The master estimates yardage by eye. A blouse is cut and the fabric falls short by a hand.

    What it costs you

    Spoiled fabric, a replacement at your cost and a delayed order.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each style has a usual fabric requirement by size, and the order checks the fabric received against it. Verity records shortfalls and wastage per cutter and style.

    See the screen
  9. 09Stock

    Trims and lining run out at the wrong time

    What it looks like

    Buttons, hooks, zips and lining are in a cupboard. The one colour you need today is not there, so the garment waits.

    What it costs you

    Delays, trips to the market in the middle of the day and a rushed purchase at a higher price.

    How Verity fixes it

    Trims and lining are stock with reorder points. Verity checks the week's orders against what is on hand and drafts a purchase list.

    See the screen
  10. 10Money

    Advances are taken and balances are not chased

    What it looks like

    Customers pay an advance and the balance on pickup. Some garments are ready for weeks and not collected, with the balance unpaid.

    What it costs you

    Garments occupying space, money locked in unclaimed orders and a shop that does not know how much it is owed.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each order shows advance, balance and ready date. Ready garments not collected after a set period appear on a list with a call task, and the outcome is recorded.

    See the screen
  11. 11Money

    Prices change from person to person

    What it looks like

    A similar blouse is quoted at ₹800 by the master and ₹650 by the counter. A regular negotiates a discount that nobody writes down.

    What it costs you

    Margins that depend on who is at the counter, and customers who notice the differences.

    How Verity fixes it

    Common garments have a price list with options for lining, embroidery and urgent delivery. A price below the floor needs an approval, and discounts are recorded with a reason.

    See the screen
  12. 12Orders

    Bulk and uniform orders are tracked in lists on paper

    What it looks like

    A school or a small company orders thirty uniforms in different sizes. Sizes, delivery batches and payments sit on one sheet that is easy to lose.

    What it costs you

    Wrong sizes, partial deliveries argued over and payment delays from the institution.

    How Verity fixes it

    A bulk order is a record with a list of people, sizes and quantities. Deliveries are logged by batch, and payment milestones are tracked against it.

    See the screen
  13. 13Work

    Work sent to outside embroiderers and dyers loses track

    What it looks like

    A lehenga goes to an outside embroiderer with a promised date. A week later nobody knows whether it has come back.

    What it costs you

    A missed wedding date, a customer waiting and a vendor bill that does not match what was sent.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each outside job records what was sent, to whom, the promised date and the bill. Verity lists items past their return date and compares bills with the agreed rate.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    The master and the owner are the system

    What it looks like

    One person remembers which customers are waiting, which karigar is slow and which fabric is low. A week away means missed deliveries.

    What it costs you

    The shop cannot add a second outlet or a bigger team, and the owner cannot step back. Most tailors work alone or in very small units with little training, so knowledge stays in one head.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each morning Verity shows what needs a decision, each item with an owner. A second outlet becomes a location in the same system.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a tailoring shop has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so your accountant starts from clean data.

  • GST on tailoring and stitching

    What is expected

    Stitching a customer's fabric is generally treated as job work and is taxed at a lower rate than design plus stitch work or selling a garment made from your own fabric. Registration generally depends on turnover, and the limit differs in some states.

    How Verity helps

    Each order records whether fabric came from the customer or the shop, and labour, fabric and trims are separate lines.

    Busy.in, an aggregator, for general background

  • Customer data and consent

    What is expected

    Names, phone numbers and body measurements are personal data. India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, and most duties start to apply over the following 12 to 18 months.

    How Verity helps

    Customer data sits in one permissioned place, with who viewed or changed it recorded.

    AZB Partners

  • Workers and wage records

    What is expected

    Many tailors work with no contract and no social security. Shops with employees may have to keep wage, attendance and leave records under state shop and establishment rules.

    How Verity helps

    Pieces, advances and pay are recorded per karigar, so a wage record exists without a separate notebook.

    Zerodha Daily Brief

  • Customer property and delivery promises

    What is expected

    When a customer leaves fabric, the shop is holding their property and a promised delivery date is part of the service. A missed date or a lost fabric can lead to a complaint.

    How Verity helps

    Customer fabric is recorded with a photo and quantity, and the promised date and any change to it are kept on the order.

Rules differ by state and size of business and they change. Confirm current requirements with your accountant and the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions tailors and boutique owners ask.

I am the master and I do not type much. Can I use it?

Yes. We set it up around the slip you already write, and the people who handle it are the counter and the karigars. We train on live orders and keep it to the fewest taps we can.

Will my karigars accept being tracked?

They get something back: a clear list of their work, instructions that match the order and a pay statement they can check. Most disputes come from missing records, and this removes them.

Can it keep a customer's measurements for years?

Yes. Each customer has a record with measurements and the date they were taken, so a returning customer starts from the last one.

Can it help me plan for a festival?

Yes. Each day has a capacity and the order checks the date against it before a promise is made. You see how booked each date is weeks ahead.

Does it replace my billing book?

No. Billing stays as it is. Verity records the order, advance and balance, and we agree during setup how billing details reach Verity.

Can it message customers when the garment is ready?

Not by itself. Verity lists the garments ready and assigns the call or message, and your team sends it and records the outcome.

I have two outlets. Can I see both?

Yes. Each outlet is a location in one system. Orders, capacity and dues can be compared side by side, and a garment can move between outlets with a record.

Who can see customer information?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of outlets and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your shop runs.
