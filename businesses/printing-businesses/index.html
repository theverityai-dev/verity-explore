How it runs today

Most printers know the job. Few know what it earned.

A job begins with a quote written from memory, passes through artwork, plates, press and finishing, often with a vendor for binding or lamination, and ends with a delivery challan and an invoice. Each step is usually on a different piece of paper.

Paper is the biggest cost and it moves. In 2021 printers' associations reported kraft paper rising from ₹22.50 to ₹39.50 a kilogram in about five months. A quote written last month can already be wrong.

The owner or the foreman knows which job is where, which customer is slow to pay and which machine is acting up. When either is away, the shop cannot answer a simple question.

What most printing businesses run on

  • Job ticket book
  • Excel or a notebook for quotes
  • WhatsApp with customers for artwork
  • Paper stock register
  • Billing software or a bill book

Each piece answers one question. None of them says which jobs lose money, which paper is running low or which customer owes the most.

14 problems, one by one

Where a printing business loses margin and time.

These are the situations press owners describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Quoting

    Quotes are guessed and the real cost is found later

    What it looks like

    A customer wants 5,000 brochures by Friday. The quote comes from last year's similar job. Paper, plates, makeready, outsourced finishing and delivery are not added up.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: on a ₹40,000 job, missing ₹3,500 of finishing and ₹2,000 of extra paper cuts the margin by nearly 14 points. A few such jobs a month erase a good week.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each quote is built from a costing sheet with paper, plates, ink, makeready, labour, outsourced work and delivery. After the job, the quote and the actual cost are compared.

    See the screen
  2. 02Paper

    Paper prices move between the quote and the purchase

    What it looks like

    The quote was given on Monday at last month's rate. The mill raised prices on Wednesday. The job ran on Thursday.

    What it costs you

    In 2021 the All-India Federation of Master Printers reported kraft paper rising from ₹22.50 to ₹39.50 per kg in five months and held a Black Day protest over raw material costs. Prices eased in late 2025, but a single month in 2025 still saw rises of ₹2 to ₹6 per kg in some grades.

    How Verity fixes it

    Paper has a current purchase rate and a rate history. Quotes use the current rate and show when it was set, so a quote older than your limit is flagged before the job starts.

    See the screen
  3. 03Artwork

    Artwork approvals live in chats and the wrong version prints

    What it looks like

    The customer sends three versions on WhatsApp. They approve "the second one" by voice note. The press runs the first.

    What it costs you

    A full reprint, usually at your cost, and a customer who is angry and in a hurry.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job holds its artwork versions with the approval, who gave it and when. The press cannot start until the approved version is attached.

    See the screen
  4. 04Rework

    Reprints and who pays for them

    What it looks like

    A job comes back with a colour mismatch. Nobody is sure whether the file, the plate or the press was at fault.

    What it costs you

    Paper, ink and machine time spent twice, with no record of the cause. The same mistake repeats.

    How Verity fixes it

    A reprint is a record linked to the original job with its cause (file, plate, press, finishing, customer change) and cost. Verity shows reprints by cause and by operator.

    See the screen
  5. 05Jobs

    Nobody knows where a job is

    What it looks like

    A customer calls to ask whether the invitation cards are ready. Someone walks to the press, then to the binding table, then calls back.

    What it costs you

    Staff time on status checks and a customer who feels uninformed.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job shows its stage, machine, due date and who has it. The counter answers from the record.

    See the screen
  6. 06Scheduling

    Press time is booked by shouting

    What it looks like

    A rush job pushes ahead of the planned runs. One machine runs late into the night while another sits idle. Festive and election seasons make it worse.

    What it costs you

    Overtime, missed deliveries and uneven machine use.

    How Verity fixes it

    Jobs are scheduled by machine and day. Verity shows load per machine, clashes and which jobs a rush job will push, so the choice is made with the cost in view.

    See the screen
  7. 07Machines

    Machine breakdowns and maintenance are not recorded

    What it looks like

    The press stops mid-run. Nobody can say when it was last serviced or how often it fails.

    What it costs you

    Lost days and late jobs. One review of Indian digital printers noted that breakdowns cause long losses of productivity and damage customer relations because of late delivery. The review is old, but the problem remains.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each machine has a service history, due dates and breakdown records with downtime. Verity lists overdue servicing and shows which machine loses the most hours.

    See the screen
  8. 08Paper

    Paper stock is counted by eye

    What it looks like

    Sheets of different sizes and GSM sit in stacks. A job needs 40 reams of one size and finds 22. Offcuts pile up unrecorded.

    What it costs you

    Rush orders at a higher price, delayed jobs and capital locked in stock that does not move.

    How Verity fixes it

    Paper is stock by size, GSM and brand with a reorder point. Use is recorded against each job, and Verity checks stock against the coming week's jobs.

    See the screen
  9. 09Wastage

    Wastage and consumables are not measured

    What it looks like

    Plates, ink and chemicals are issued on request. Spoilage sheets are thrown away. Nobody knows what a typical job wastes.

    What it costs you

    Cost creeping up with no explanation, and quotes that assume less waste than the shop really has.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job records sheets issued and sheets delivered. Verity shows wastage per job, machine and operator, and the actual figure feeds back into future quotes.

    See the screen
  10. 10Vendors

    Outsourced work loses track

    What it looks like

    Binding, lamination, die-cutting and foil are done by vendors. A job waits at a vendor for six days and nobody knew it was there.

    What it costs you

    Late deliveries, vendor bills that do not match what was sent and lost items.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each outsourced step records what was sent, to whom, the promised date and the bill. Verity lists items past their return date and compares vendor bills with the agreed rate.

    See the screen
  11. 11Delivery

    Deliveries do not match invoices

    What it looks like

    A delivery goes in two parts. The challan says 5,000, the customer counts 4,800 and the invoice says 5,000.

    What it costs you

    Short-receipt claims, reduced payments and arguments with no record to settle them.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each delivery records quantities by challan and who received them. The invoice is checked against deliveries and shortfalls are flagged.

    See the screen
  12. 12Money

    Customers pay late and chasing is by memory

    What it looks like

    Some customers pay in 60 days. A few have not paid for 120. The owner remembers the biggest, and the rest drift.

    What it costs you

    Cash tied up in unpaid bills and a printing machine that is idle because the paper supplier needs payment first.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each invoice has a due date and an owner. Overdue ones are listed by amount and age, with a task to the person who knows the customer and the outcome recorded.

    See the screen
  13. 13Jobs

    Repeat jobs start from zero

    What it looks like

    A regular customer wants last year's catalogue again with small changes. The file, paper, colours and finish are not recorded anywhere you can find.

    What it costs you

    Hours spent reconstructing what the first job was, and a mismatch the customer notices.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job keeps its specification, files, approvals and costs. A repeat order starts from the old job with changes marked.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    The owner and the foreman are the system

    What it looks like

    One person knows which jobs are late, which customers owe money, which machines are tired and which paper is low.

    What it costs you

    The press cannot grow past what two people can hold in their heads, and a week away means slipping deliveries.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each morning Verity shows what needs a decision, each item with an owner. A second unit or a job-work partner becomes a location in the same system.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a printing business has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when a customer, an officer or your accountant asks.

  • Customer artwork and permissions

    What is expected

    Customers supply logos, photos and text, and a job can involve content you do not own. Printers are generally expected to take the customer's confirmation that they have the right to use it.

    How Verity helps

    Each job records who supplied the artwork and their approval, so you can show what you were told.

  • Customer data and consent

    What is expected

    Names, phone numbers and addresses on wedding cards, forms and invoices are personal data. India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, and most duties start to apply over the following 12 to 18 months.

    How Verity helps

    Customer data sits in one permissioned place, with who viewed or changed it recorded.

    AZB Partners

  • Tax on printing and job work

    What is expected

    GST treatment can differ between printing as a service and printing as a sale of goods, and job work for another business has its own paperwork.

    How Verity helps

    Paper, printing and outsourced work are separate lines on each job so your accountant starts from clean data.

  • Safety, fire and local permits

    What is expected

    Presses use solvents, inks and heavy machines, and local authorities may ask for fire, pollution and factory approvals depending on size.

    How Verity helps

    Each permit is a record with an expiry and an owner, linked to the unit it covers.

Rules differ by product, state and customer type, and they change. Confirm current requirements with your accountant and the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions printing business owners ask.

Will my foreman and operators actually use it?

It only works if recording takes seconds, so we set it up around the job ticket they already use. We train at the press on real jobs and give each role something back: a clear list of what is on their machine and what is waiting.

Does Verity replace my design software or billing?

No. Prepress tools and billing stay as they are. Verity records the job, costs and approvals behind them, and we agree during setup how invoice details reach Verity.

Can it help me quote faster?

Yes. Quotes are built from a costing sheet with current paper rates and your own wastage history. Verity does not set your price, it shows what the job will cost.

Can it handle jobs sent to outside vendors?

Yes. Each outsourced step records what was sent, to whom, the due date and the bill against the agreed rate.

I run two units. Can I see both together?

Yes. Each unit is a location in one system. Jobs, machine load, paper and dues can be compared side by side.

Does it track which machine causes the most delay?

Yes. Breakdowns and downtime are recorded per machine, and servicing due dates are listed.

Who can see my prices and margins?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How long before I see results?

The paper stock list, the open jobs board and the unpaid bills list need only your existing records, so they are the first things we aim to have working. Margin by job becomes reliable after a few weeks of costed jobs.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of units and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your press runs.
