Verity for veterinary clinics

Your patients cannot tell you what is wrong, and their owners forget when the next vaccine is due. The record has to remember.

A veterinary clinic keeps two kinds of records at once: the animal and the person who pays. The vaccine due in three weeks, the weight at the last visit, the surgery slot, the injection expiring in the fridge, the owner who left a balance and promised to return. Verity connects the animal's record, the owner, the schedule and the stock, so the clinic remembers what no one has time to.

How it runs today

Most veterinary clinics run on a register, the vet's memory and a WhatsApp group of owners.

Each animal needs a history: species, breed, age, weight, vaccines, deworming, past illnesses, surgeries, allergies. It is kept in a case card, a register or a simple app, and the owner's phone number is the key to find it.

The animal base is growing. A 2025 industry report puts India's pet population at about 42 million in 2024, up from 26 million in 2019, with over 36.8 million dogs. More animals means more records, more vaccine schedules and more owners to follow up.

The leaks are quiet: the puppy whose second vaccine was due last week, the cat's yearly booster no one remembered, the medicine that expired in the cabinet, the boarding stay that was never billed, the owner who promised to pay the balance on Saturday.

What most veterinary clinics run on

  • Paper case cards or a register
  • A small billing app or bill book
  • Excel or a notebook for stock
  • WhatsApp with owners and suppliers
  • Phone calendar for surgeries and boarding

Each tool does its own job. Nothing tells you which animals are overdue or what will run out before Saturday.

14 problems, one by one

Where a veterinary clinic loses patients and money.

These are the situations vets, clinic managers and technicians describe. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Vaccination

    Vaccines come due and nobody calls

    What it looks like

    A puppy had its first vaccine six weeks ago. The next is due this week. The owner has not thought about it. The clinic's register has the date, but nobody turns the pages looking for it.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 200 animals a month on a vaccine schedule, with one in four missing a due date, at ₹800 a vaccine is ₹40,000 of revenue not earned, plus animals left unprotected.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each animal has a schedule of vaccines and boosters that your vet sets, with due dates. Verity lists what is due and overdue each week, and creates a call task. Staff call or message the owner; Verity records the outcome.

  2. 02Patients

    The owner changes, the animal stays

    What it looks like

    A dog is brought by the owner's son, then by a domestic helper, then by a neighbour. The owner's number on the card is old. A reminder goes to a wrong number.

    What it costs you

    A pet that appears to be lost to follow-up may only be lost in the contact details.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each animal has an owner and an alternate contact. Phone numbers are confirmed at each visit, and a contact that fails is flagged for correction.

    See the screen
  3. 03Patients

    Records are hard to find and thin

    What it looks like

    An old patient returns after a year. The case card cannot be found. The vet starts again from the owner's recollection. Weight, allergies and past medicines are missing.

    What it costs you

    Repeat questions, repeat tests and a poor visit. A new vet cannot take over smoothly.

    How Verity fixes it

    One record per animal, with visits, weights, vaccines, procedures and documents in date order. Search by owner name, phone or animal name. Verity organises what is recorded; the vet's notes stay the vet's.

    See the screen
  4. 04Patients

    Same name, many animals

    What it looks like

    One household has three dogs, all named by the family as "Bruno" in different years. Reports and bills get attached to the wrong one.

    What it costs you

    A wrong record is more harmful than a missing one.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each animal has a unique ID, a photo or marking description, species, breed and birth date, and is listed under the owner. Duplicate or ambiguous names are flagged at registration.

    See the screen
  5. 05Stock

    Medicines and vaccines expire in the cabinet

    What it looks like

    A box of injectables is at the back of a shelf. A vaccine vial passes its date because the fridge is checked once a week. Controlled items are kept in a separate cupboard with a separate notebook.

    What it costs you

    Expired stock is paid-for stock thrown away, and a doctor who finds out at the point of use has to change plans.

    How Verity fixes it

    Items are tracked by batch and expiry. Verity lists what expires in 30 and 60 days, flags items that need cold storage, and deducts use when a visit or procedure is recorded.

    See the screen
  6. 06Stock

    Running out of the common things

    What it looks like

    The clinic runs out of a common antiparasitic on a Saturday, or of sutures before a scheduled surgery. Supplier deliveries are twice a week.

    What it costs you

    A procedure postponed is a patient that may go elsewhere.

    How Verity fixes it

    Reorder points are set by actual use, including surgery schedules. Verity drafts an order in time for the next delivery slot.

    See the screen
  7. 07Suppliers

    Supplier bills and short deliveries

    What it looks like

    A distributor delivers 20 vials and bills 24. A food company changes a price and the invoice carries it quietly.

    What it costs you

    Overpaying on frequently bought items compounds across months.

    How Verity fixes it

    Receipts are checked against orders and invoices, and rates are shown over time. A mismatch is flagged before the bill is paid.

    See the screen
  8. 08Wards

    Surgery, admissions and boarding on a phone calendar

    What it looks like

    Two surgeries are booked for the same theatre hour. A cat admitted overnight is not on the care list. A boarding dog's feeding and medicine times are told verbally to the night attendant.

    What it costs you

    Clashes and missed care tasks harm animals, and unbilled boarding days are lost income.

    How Verity fixes it

    Admissions, surgeries and boarding are on one schedule with rooms and cages. Each admitted animal has a task list of feeds, medicines due and checks that your vet sets, assigned to a person per shift.

    See the screen
  9. 09Money

    Charges used on a visit never reach the bill

    What it looks like

    A vet uses a bandage, an injection and a dressing, and the bill shows consultation only. A boarding stay of four days is billed as three.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹300 of unbilled items on 15 visits a week is ₹4,500 a week, over ₹19,000 a month.

    How Verity fixes it

    Items and services are recorded against the visit as they are used. Verity compares what was used with what the bill carries and lists gaps before the bill is closed.

    See the screen
  10. 10Money

    Estimates, deposits and unpaid balances

    What it looks like

    A family is given a rough estimate for surgery. The final bill is higher. A deposit is taken in cash and not written down. A balance is promised for next week.

    What it costs you

    Arguments at the counter, and balances that quietly become bad debts.

    How Verity fixes it

    Estimates, deposits and running charges sit on the admission. When the running charges pass the estimate by a set share, the vet and desk are alerted so the owner is told early. Balances show by owner and age. Verity does not take payments.

    See the screen
  11. 11Consent

    Owner consent for procedures and euthanasia

    What it looks like

    An owner consents to surgery by phone. A signed form is in a drawer. For a sensitive decision, the family has argued, and the clinic cannot show who agreed.

    What it costs you

    Without a clear record, a clinic is exposed in a dispute and cannot show what the owner understood.

    How Verity fixes it

    Consent for procedures, anaesthesia and boarding terms is a record with a date, the person who consented and the version. The vet explains; Verity files the record.

    See the screen
  12. 12Diagnostics

    Referrals, labs and imaging

    What it looks like

    Blood samples go to an outside lab. Results come by WhatsApp to the vet's phone. A scan report is on the technician's laptop. Nobody knows which results are pending.

    What it costs you

    Waiting results lose time, and lost reports mean repeated tests.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each test sent is a record with the animal, the lab and the date. Pending results are listed, and reports attach to the animal's record when they arrive.

    See the screen
  13. 13People

    Staff, on-call and equipment

    What it looks like

    A technician is on leave and nobody told the desk. The night attendant's duty is by WhatsApp. The anaesthesia machine is serviced when it fails.

    What it costs you

    Unplanned equipment failure postpones surgeries, and cover problems harm care.

    How Verity fixes it

    A roster shows who is on each day and night. Equipment records hold service dates and vendors, with a task before they are due. Verity does not run payroll.

    See the screen
  14. 14Compliance

    Registrations, waste and a second clinic

    What it looks like

    A vet's State Veterinary Council registration renewal date is in an email. The biomedical waste authorisation and trade licence dates are in a drawer. A second clinic or a mobile unit reports in a different format.

    What it costs you

    Practising without valid registration is a legal risk. Lapsed local authorisations cause notices. Without one view, the owner is the integrator.

    How Verity fixes it

    Registrations, authorisations and licences are records with dates and owners. Clinics are locations in one system, with visits, vaccines due, stock and dues side by side.

How we work with you

We set it up with you, in four weeks.

You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time at your front desk and in your theatre, build Verity around how your clinic really runs, bring your data in, then stay to make sure it keeps working.

  1. Week 1

    Discovery and mapping

    What we do
    Spend a morning at your front desk and an afternoon with the vets and technicians. Follow an animal from registration to discharge, count the medicine cabinet and fridge, and note where vaccines, charges and stock slip.
    What we need from you
    Two to three hours of yours and your manager's. Access to a month of bills, your case cards or register and the supplier invoices.
    What you have at the end
    A written map of your clinic: species and services you see, vaccine schedules your vets use, rooms and cages, suppliers and the first gaps we will close.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3

    Configuration

    What we do
    Set up animal and owner fields, vaccine schedules as your vets define them, services and charges, rooms and cages, items with batches and reorder points, consent forms, roles and the six screens in your own terms.
    What we need from you
    Check the setup with your lead vet and manager. Tell us where a schedule, a term or a rule is wrong.
    What you have at the end
    A working Verity with your real animals' fields, services and rules, ready for your data.

  3. Weeks 3 to 4

    Migration and training

    What we do
    Bring in your animal and owner list, vaccine dates, open dues and stock from your cards, registers and sheets. Train the desk, vets and technicians at their stations.
    What we need from you
    Your case cards or register in whatever form exists, and an opening stock count. An hour with each role.
    What you have at the end
    Your animals, due dates and stock inside Verity, and a team that has used it through a busy day.

  4. After go-live

    Operations partnership

    What we do
    Stay on after launch. Review the first weeks of overdue vaccines, charges and stock with you, tune the limits and handle changes when you add a service, a vet or a clinic.
    What we need from you
    A short monthly review of what the numbers say.
    What you have at the end
    A system that keeps matching how the clinic runs, with someone responsible for it.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a veterinary clinic has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • State Veterinary Council registration

    What is expected

    Practising as a veterinary practitioner generally requires registration with the State Veterinary Council under the Indian Veterinary Council Act, 1984. Registration is of the person, not the clinic, and renewal rules differ by state.

    How Verity helps

    Each vet's registration is a record with number, state and date, with a task before a renewal.

    CalcGuru

  • Biomedical waste authorisation

    What is expected

    A veterinary clinic generally needs authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board for biomedical waste, whatever its size, and should confirm how animal waste is classed.

    How Verity helps

    The authorisation, its date and the disposal vendor are records, with a task before a renewal.

    CalcGuru

  • Trade licence, drug licence and GST

    What is expected

    A municipal trade licence generally applies. A drug licence is generally needed only if you sell medicines over the counter, and GST registration generally only once taxable sales such as food, grooming or boarding reach the threshold. Confirm with your adviser.

    How Verity helps

    Each is a record with its number, scope and date, with an owner.

  • Records of medicines and consent

    What is expected

    Keep purchase and supply records for medicines as your licence conditions require, and keep records of owner consent for procedures. The exact rules for veterinary prescription medicines differ, so check yours with your veterinary council or authority.

    How Verity helps

    Batch-level receipts and use are recorded against the animal, and consent is a dated record.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your State Veterinary Council, your local authority and your accountant.

Questions

Questions veterinary clinic owners ask.

Does Verity set the vaccination schedule?

No. Your vets decide each animal's schedule. Verity records it, lists what is due and overdue and tracks the call to the owner.

Does it give veterinary advice or store treatment decisions?

No. Verity records and organises: visits, schedules, stock and tasks. Clinical notes and judgement stay with your vets.

Can owners book or get reminders through Verity?

No. Verity does not take online bookings or send messages on its own. Your staff call or message, and Verity records who owes a call and the outcome.

Our animals are dogs, cats and the occasional rabbit. Does it handle that?

Yes. Species, breed, birth date, weight and a schedule are fields on the animal. We set up the species you see.

One owner has several animals. Can it keep them apart?

Yes. Each animal has its own ID and record under one owner, with ambiguous names flagged at registration.

We board animals and run surgeries. Does it track those?

Yes. Admissions, surgeries and boarding are on one schedule with rooms and cages, care tasks per shift and charges per day.

I have a main clinic and a satellite. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each is a location in one system, with visits, vaccines due, stock and dues side by side. Owners and animals are one record across both.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so the desk, technicians and vets each see only what they need, and every change is recorded with who and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of clinics, users and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your clinic runs.
