How it runs today

A college office is a set of busy desks that rarely see each other's work.

Admissions has a list. Accounts has a fee register. The exam cell has attendance and internal marks. The departments have timetables. The principal gets a summary when someone has time to make one. The student is the same person in all four places, but there is no single view of them.

Seat pressure is real. A Careers360 report from November 2024 found tens of thousands of engineering seats vacant after counselling in several states, even as intake grew. Whatever the course, a college that cannot see its funnel in time cannot act on it.

Rules sit on top. Fee refund tiers, university attendance requirements, scholarship records, AISHE data and accreditation files all draw on the same student and faculty records, and they are rebuilt for each purpose.

What most colleges run on

  • Admission forms and Excel lists
  • Fee register and receipt book
  • Department attendance registers
  • Exam cell spreadsheets
  • WhatsApp groups for staff and students

Each one works inside its own office. The student's whole story is in none of them.

15 problems, one by one

Where a college actually loses seats, money and time.

These are the situations principals, registrars, accountants and department heads describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Admissions

    Enquiries go cold before counselling ends

    What it looks like

    In June the office gets two hundred calls and walk-ins. Names are on slips and in two phones. A parent who visited twice is never called back, and a rival college rings her on the day she decides.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 20 lost admissions at ₹90,000 a year is ₹18 lakh in a single year, and each student would normally pay for three or four years.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every enquiry is a record with course, rank or marks, source, visits and next step. Verity lists enquiries with no follow-up and assigns each to a named counsellor, with the outcome recorded after the call.

  2. 02Admissions

    Seats stay empty because nobody sees the funnel

    What it looks like

    The principal asks in July how many seats are filled in each course. The answer is a number from yesterday in one department and last week in another. A Careers360 report found 54,587 vacant engineering seats in Tamil Nadu after the third round of counselling in 2024.

    What it costs you

    A vacant seat is fee that never arrives, against faculty and lab costs that continue. In 2024-25 Tamil Nadu alone added 39,978 engineering seats while many stayed empty.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity shows seats, offers, acceptances, fees paid and admissions by course and category in one view, updated as work happens, so a gap is visible while there is still a round or a spot admission left to act on.

    See the screen
  3. 03Admissions

    Document verification is a pile of photocopies

    What it looks like

    A student arrives with a mark sheet, a migration certificate, a caste or income certificate and a transfer certificate. One is missing. The clerk writes it on a form that is lost by the time the student returns.

    What it costs you

    Students admitted on incomplete papers cause trouble with the university, with scholarships and with later certificates. Students delayed at the counter go elsewhere.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each applicant has a document checklist. Verity shows what is received, verified, pending or wrong, who verified it and when, and lists admitted students who still owe papers.

    See the screen
  4. 04Fees

    Fee instalments are chased from memory

    What it looks like

    Semester fees are due in three instalments. Scholarships and bank loans delay some. A student with a concession cannot be told at the counter what is still pending without checking three sheets.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 1,800 students with ₹60,000 a semester is ₹10.8 crore. A fortnight's delay on one student in ten is ₹1.08 crore not in the account while salaries run.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student has a fee plan with instalments, scholarship or loan status and concessions. Verity lists dues by course and age and assigns a follow-up task. Your staff speak to the student; the promise and the outcome are recorded.

    See the screen
  5. 05Fees

    Scholarship and fee-reimbursement claims slip through

    What it looks like

    Dozens of students are on state or central scholarships. The college has to confirm enrolment, attendance and fee details on the relevant portals. Claims are filed late for students whose papers were incomplete.

    What it costs you

    The student pays from their own pocket meanwhile, or the college waits for money it has already counted as income.

    How Verity fixes it

    Scholarship students are tagged with scheme, application status, documents and the amount expected. Verity lists claims not yet filed, rejected or pending, and the money the college is waiting on.

    See the screen
  6. 06Fees

    Withdrawals and refunds are handled case by case

    What it looks like

    A student gets a government seat and withdraws in August. The office has to decide how much of the fee is refundable, by when, and whether the seat can still be filled.

    What it costs you

    Colleges generally follow tiered refund schedules tied to the last date of admission. One college's policy page, citing UGC guidelines, refunds the fee less ₹1,000 at 15 or more days before that date, then 90%, 80%, 50% and nothing after 30 days. A wrong refund, or a late one, becomes a grievance.

    How Verity fixes it

    Withdrawal requests are records with their date, the rule that applies, the amount calculated and who approved it. Verity lists refunds not paid within your target number of days.

    See the screen
  7. 07Attendance and exams

    Attendance is kept by each teacher and reconciled at exam time

    What it looks like

    Three weeks before the university exams, the department head collects attendance sheets from every teacher to see who is short of the required percentage. The numbers differ from what students say.

    What it costs you

    Students barred from exams create disputes, appeals and last-minute condonation requests. A college without a record cannot defend its own list.

    How Verity fixes it

    Attendance is recorded daily by subject. Verity shows each student's percentage against the requirement set by the university, with those at risk listed early. Condonation requests, reasons and approvals are recorded on the student.

    See the screen
  8. 08Attendance and exams

    Internal marks, practicals and results sit in separate sheets

    What it looks like

    Internal assessments are in each teacher's file. Practical marks are on a form. When the university asks for them, the exam cell collects, re-types and checks them against each other.

    What it costs you

    Typing errors in marks are the kind of mistake a student sees first. Late submission of internals can hold up results.

    How Verity fixes it

    Internal and practical marks are entered once against the student and subject, with who entered them and when. Verity shows missing entries by subject and teacher before the university deadline.

    See the screen
  9. 09Attendance and exams

    Exam logistics: halls, invigilators and hall tickets

    What it looks like

    The exam cell builds the seating plan and invigilation duty list on a spreadsheet. A teacher is rostered in two halls at the same time. Hall tickets for students with unpaid dues are held by hand.

    What it costs you

    Chaos on exam day and a risk of a malpractice case that comes from a lapse in supervision.

    How Verity fixes it

    Exam sessions, halls, capacity and invigilator duties are records. Verity flags a clash, lists students whose hall tickets are held because of dues or attendance, and records who released each one.

    See the screen
  10. 10Faculty

    Faculty workload is uneven and invisible

    What it looks like

    One senior teacher takes 22 hours a week, another takes 12. Guest faculty are called when someone is on leave, and nobody knows how many guest lectures have been paid for.

    What it costs you

    Burnout for some, idle time for others, and guest-faculty costs that are only noticed at month end.

    How Verity fixes it

    The timetable is a record with teacher, subject, class, room and hours. Verity shows load by teacher against your norm, free slots for cover and the guest lectures taken, so an uneven load is seen and corrected.

    See the screen
  11. 11Faculty

    Faculty qualifications and renewals are in cupboards

    What it looks like

    An inspection committee asks for the qualifications, appointment letters and experience of every faculty member in a department. The registrar goes through a stack of files.

    What it costs you

    Hours of work and a risk of a gap being found only in front of the committee. Regulators and accreditation bodies set norms on faculty, so incomplete files hurt.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each faculty member has a record with qualifications, appointment, experience, publications, leave and contract dates. Verity lists missing documents and upcoming contract renewals.

    See the screen
  12. 12Records

    Accreditation and survey data are rebuilt from scratch

    What it looks like

    AISHE, NIRF, NAAC and university affiliation each ask for student, faculty and infrastructure data in a different format. Each year the IQAC team collects the same numbers again.

    What it costs you

    Weeks of staff time. AISHE data is also used by scholarship portals, so errors carry beyond the college. The AISHE 2024-25 round had a final submission deadline of 15 November 2025 at one university.

    How Verity fixes it

    Students, faculty, programmes and facilities are kept as one set of records. Verity helps the team assemble the figures for each survey from the same data and shows entries with gaps. Your staff submit on the official portals.

    See the screen
  13. 13Records

    Certificates, transcripts and no-dues clearances take days

    What it looks like

    A final-year student needs a bonafide, a no-dues certificate and a transcript before a placement joining date. The request goes from the office to the library, the hostel, the accounts section and the department, on paper.

    What it costs you

    Lost placements, anxious students and clerks who spend a day chasing signatures.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each request is a workflow with steps for the library, hostel, accounts and department. Verity shows where a request is stuck and who must act, and the certificate is drawn from the same student record.

    See the screen
  14. 14Students

    Student retention and drop-outs are noticed late

    What it looks like

    A first-year student stops coming in October. The department thinks the office knows. The office thinks the department knows. By the time anyone calls, he has taken a transfer elsewhere.

    What it costs you

    A lost student also loses the fee for the remaining years and affects the college's results.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity combines attendance, dues, marks and hostel presence and flags students showing several warning signs at once. A mentor gets a task, and the conversation and outcome are recorded.

    See the screen
  15. 15Students

    Placement and alumni records are not connected to the student

    What it looks like

    The placement cell keeps a sheet of company visits and offers. Nobody records which students had which interviews, who rejected an offer, or where the batch is two years later.

    What it costs you

    Placement is a major reason a student chooses a college. Claims made in the brochure can only be backed with a record.

    How Verity fixes it

    Company visits, shortlists, offers and outcomes are recorded against the student. Verity keeps alumni contact and current employer on the same record for later batches.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a college has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • UGC fee refund framework

    What is expected

    UGC guidelines generally require colleges to refund fees on a sliding scale when a student withdraws before or soon after the last admission date, and the UGC grievance regulations of 2023 treat delay in a refund as a grievance. Exact percentages and deadlines vary by notification and session.

    How Verity helps

    Each withdrawal is a record with its date, the tier applied, the amount and the approval, and refunds past your target days are listed.

    RKNEC refund policy citing UGC guidelines

  • AISHE and accreditation data

    What is expected

    Colleges are expected to submit survey data to AISHE each year, and NAAC accreditation requires institutional data. NAAC has been moving to a binary Accredited or Not Accredited outcome from 2025, according to one vendor summary, so confirm with NAAC.

    How Verity helps

    Students, faculty and facilities are kept as one set of records, with gaps flagged. Your team submits on the official portals.

    University of Kashmir circular on AISHE 2024-25

  • University attendance and exam rules

    What is expected

    Affiliating universities set minimum attendance and internal assessment rules for eligibility to sit exams. The percentage and condonation process differ by university.

    How Verity helps

    Attendance, internals and condonation are recorded against each student and subject, with at-risk students listed early.

  • Faculty and approval norms

    What is expected

    Regulators such as UGC and AICTE set faculty qualification and student-faculty norms for approval of programmes, and visits by committees ask for staff files. Norms differ by programme.

    How Verity helps

    Faculty qualifications, appointments and experience are records, and missing documents are listed.

  • Children's and students' data under the DPDP Act

    What is expected

    The DPDP Act and Rules apply to personal data of students, and verifiable parental consent applies to anyone under 18, which includes some first-year students.

    How Verity helps

    Role-based access and an audit trail show who saw or changed each record.

Rules differ by university, regulator and state, and they change. Confirm current requirements with your affiliating university, UGC or AICTE and your adviser.

Questions college principals and registrars ask.

Does Verity run our admissions portal?

No. If you take applications on your own site or the state portal, that keeps working. Verity holds the applicant record from enquiry to enrolment, with documents, offers and fees, so no one is lost between desks.

We follow our affiliating university's rules. Can they be set up?

Yes. The attendance requirement, internal marks structure and fee refund rules are set up in configuration, and we ask you to confirm each one. We do not promise the rules are right for your university without that check.

Can it handle several programmes and a second campus?

Yes. Each campus is a location in one system, with courses, fees, faculty and students side by side. The principal sees all of it and each head sees their own.

How does it help with scholarships?

Scholarship students are tagged with scheme, documents and claim status, and Verity lists claims not filed or not paid, with the amount the college is waiting for. Staff still file on the relevant portals.

Can students log in and see their own record?

We do not claim a student app. Today staff see everything and tell students what they need. Ask us on the first call if you have a specific need.

Will it prepare AISHE or NAAC submissions?

It keeps student, faculty and facility records as one set, so figures can be assembled from them. Your team still checks and submits on the official portals.

We have a lot of Excel. Will it all move?

Yes, in stages, starting with applicants, students and fees. Older years of marks can follow later so the work in front of you is not held up.

Who can see student data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and department, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of students, campuses and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your college runs.
