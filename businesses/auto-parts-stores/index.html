How it runs today

Most auto parts stores run on a few staff who remember which part fits which vehicle.

The counter is easy to see. A mechanic with a worn brake pad in his hand, a walk-in asking for a headlight bulb, a phone that does not stop ringing. What is hard to see is what sits behind it: which of the thousands of part numbers is dead stock, which fast movers are about to run out, how much each garage owes, and which supplier raised its rate last month.

That information exists in pieces. The billing screen knows the sale. Supplier catalogues are thick books or PDFs. The garage credit book is a ledger. Returns are a stack of slips. The head salesman knows what fits what, and takes that knowledge home with him.

The market is large and changing. ACMA reported the auto component aftermarket growing about 5 percent to ₹47,416 crore in a half-year period, helped by used-vehicle sales and the formalisation of repair and maintenance. From 22 September 2025, GST on all auto parts is a uniform 18 percent.

What most auto parts stores run on

  • Billing screen at the counter
  • Brand catalogues and part number books
  • Garage credit ledger
  • Supplier price lists and challans
  • The head salesman’s memory of what fits what

Each one does its own job well enough. None of them talk to each other, and nobody has time to join them.

15 problems, one by one

Where an auto parts store actually loses money.

These are the situations auto parts store owners and managers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Parts

    The wrong part sold because fitment lived in someone’s head

    What it looks like

    A customer asks for a clutch plate for a particular model. The salesman picks one by memory. It is for the earlier year. The customer finds out at the garage and brings it back, or the garage fits it and it does not seat.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ten wrong-part returns a week at an average ₹900 is ₹9,000 of stock to handle each week, plus a mechanic who stops coming.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each part has its part number, brand, cross-references and the vehicles, models and years it fits. Staff search by vehicle or by number and see the right part, the alternatives and the quantity on hand before it is billed.

    See the screen
  2. 02Parts

    Long tail of part numbers that rarely sell

    What it looks like

    A bearing for a discontinued model, a trim clip for a car sold years ago, a brand of filter nobody asks for. They are on the shelf because one customer asked once. Nobody has counted what they cost.

    What it costs you

    Stock that does not move holds cash and space, and in a business with thousands of part numbers, the tail quietly grows.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity lists parts by days since the last sale and value held, by brand and vehicle. Slow movers show with a suggested action, such as a return to the supplier where terms allow, a bundle, a markdown or no reorder, and each has an owner.

    See the screen
  3. 03Parts

    Fast movers that run out at the wrong time

    What it looks like

    Oil filters, brake pads, air filters and spark plugs for common vehicles sell daily. The supplier delivers on two days a week. A mechanic comes in on Friday for brake pads and they ran out on Wednesday.

    What it costs you

    The mechanic buys the whole job elsewhere, and a mechanic who changes shops takes his garage’s monthly spend with him.

    How Verity fixes it

    Fast movers have a reorder point based on real sale, not a guess. Verity checks stock against the days until the next delivery and flags a part that will run out before it, with the order drafted.

    See the screen
  4. 04Parts

    Batteries, tyres and lubricants that age on the shelf

    What it looks like

    A battery bought six months ago has been sitting while new ones arrive and are placed in front. Tyres carry a date code that few notice. A customer is sold an old battery and finds it weak in a month.

    What it costs you

    Old stock sold as new produces complaints and claims, and old stock never sold is a loss.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each delivery records the manufacturing date or batch where the product has one. Verity lists stock by age, flags the oldest and prompts the counter to sell oldest first.

    See the screen
  5. 05Returns

    Wrong-part returns and the slips that pile up

    What it looks like

    A mechanic brings back a part that did not fit. The salesman says he will fix the bill later. The part goes back on the shelf with the wrong bin number, and the credit is not given until the month end.

    What it costs you

    Stock that does not match the shelf, credit notes forgotten, and arguments with garages at the month end.

    How Verity fixes it

    Returns are logged with a reason in two taps, linked to the original bill. Stock goes back to the right bin, the garage’s account is credited, and Verity totals returns by reason and by salesperson so a pattern in wrong fitment shows.

    See the screen
  6. 06Returns

    Warranty claims and battery exchanges lost between customer and brand

    What it looks like

    A battery fails in month five. A shock absorber leaks. A customer brings the invoice or says it is lost. You promise to talk to the brand. The old battery goes in a corner and nobody tracks the exchange.

    What it costs you

    A claim that is not filed in time is a direct loss. Unrecorded old units cost you the credit the brand would have given.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each claim is a record linked to the sale, with the brand, the date, the photos and the status. Verity shows claims awaiting the brand’s reply, flags those near their window and tracks old batteries and parts returned for exchange.

    See the screen
  7. 07Garages

    Garage credit that nobody can total

    What it looks like

    A garage takes parts every day and pays at the month end, or when its own customers pay. The ledger shows one balance, the bills another, and the salesman who deals with him remembers a third.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹6 lakh of garage credit outstanding 45 days on average ties up cash while you pay suppliers on a shorter cycle.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each garage is an account with a credit limit, bills, payments and balance by age. Verity shows who owes what and for how long, holds a bill above the limit for approval, and gives a task to chase on the schedule you set.

    See the screen
  8. 08Orders

    Mechanics who phone in orders and are put on hold

    What it looks like

    A mechanic phones for a set of brake pads for three different vehicles, and a gasket. The phone rings in the middle of a rush and goes unanswered, or a salesman writes the order on a slip that gets lost.

    What it costs you

    A missed call from a mechanic is a sale to the nearest competitor, and orders written on slips go wrong.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each phone order is a record with the garage, the vehicles, the parts, the price and the status. The counter sees what is waiting to be packed, what is packed and what has gone, and a missed order is a task, not a lost slip.

    See the screen
  9. 09Orders

    Delivery runs to garages with no list

    What it looks like

    A boy on a bike takes parts to six garages in the morning. The list is on a slip. A garage says a part was missing and the boy says it was there. A delivery that should have been the first stop was the last.

    What it costs you

    Late or wrong deliveries cost a garage a half day of a vehicle on a lift. They go to a shop that delivers on time.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each delivery is an order with the garage, the parts, the window, the person carrying it and the status. The garage’s confirmation is recorded, and the counter sees what has gone out and what is late.

    See the screen
  10. 10Suppliers

    Fake parts and parts without a trail

    What it looks like

    A part arrives from an unfamiliar supplier at a price too good to be true. The packaging looks right. A customer later returns it as it failed in a week. Counterfeits are most reported in fast-moving items like filters, bearings and lubricants.

    What it costs you

    Spurious parts were reported in 2012 at 36 percent of the Indian aftermarket in one industry estimate, and a trade body later said the share had fallen sharply while the trade moved to unorganised sellers. A bad part sold under your name costs a customer and sometimes a garage.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each part records its supplier, batch and date received, so a failed part can be traced to where it came from. Verity shows returns and complaints by supplier and brand, and flags a supplier with repeated failures.

    See the screen
  11. 11Suppliers

    Supplier prices, schemes and the GST change

    What it looks like

    A brand revises its list price. A distributor offers a scheme if you buy a target. From 22 September 2025, GST on all auto parts became a uniform 18 percent, and parts that were at 28 percent now sit lower, with old and new stock together on the shelf.

    What it costs you

    Selling below the new cost without noticing, missing a scheme you qualified for, and confusion about the price and tax on old stock. Each is small and all of them add up.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every purchase records the rate and the tax category. Verity shows price changes by supplier and part, flags where your selling price is now below your margin, and tracks schemes as records with dates, targets and progress.

    See the screen
  12. 12Suppliers

    Short and damaged deliveries from distributors

    What it looks like

    A carton of brake pads arrives with six sets less than the challan. A shock absorber is dented. The person who signs the challan is serving a mechanic.

    What it costs you

    You pay for goods that did not arrive or arrived damaged, and the claim goes in late or not at all.

    How Verity fixes it

    Deliveries are received against the order by part number and quantity, with a note on condition. Verity compares order, receipt and invoice and flags a mismatch before payment, and tracks the credit note until it arrives.

    See the screen
  13. 13Money

    Margin that varies by brand and nobody has calculated

    What it looks like

    A branded part earns a modest margin. An unbranded alternative earns much more, and the customer asks for the cheaper one. Discounts to garages differ by salesman. The owner cannot say which brands pay.

    What it costs you

    Selling the wrong mix means working hard for thin margin, and unrecorded discounts hide how much is given away.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every part carries its purchase cost, so margin is known per part, brand and garage. Discounts above a limit need a one-tap approval with a reason, and Verity totals margin and discounts by brand, garage and salesperson.

    See the screen
  14. 14Money

    Day-end takings do not tie to the bills

    What it looks like

    At closing, cash, card, UPI and credit sales are counted against the billing report and are off by a few hundred rupees. A cash sale was not billed, a credit payment was taken without a receipt or a return was refunded at the counter.

    What it costs you

    Small daily gaps compound, and the owner stops trusting the numbers on the screen.

    How Verity fixes it

    The day’s sales and payments by mode are compared with what was collected. A mismatch is flagged with the shift and amount, and the person closing records the reason.

    See the screen
  15. 15Owner

    A godown, a second counter and the head salesman who knows everything

    What it looks like

    Parts are split between the counter, a godown and a second outlet. One senior salesman knows where everything is and what fits what. If he is away or leaves, the shop slows down.

    What it costs you

    The business cannot grow past what one person can hold in their head, and stock sits idle in one place while another runs out.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each morning Verity shows a short list of what needs a decision, each with an owner. Counters and godowns are locations in one system with stock, sales and balances side by side, and fitment and bin locations are on the record, not in one head.

Rules and paperwork

The rules an auto parts store has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so your accountant is not rebuilding them from a ledger.

  • GST on auto parts

    What is expected

    From 22 September 2025, auto parts are generally taxed at a uniform 18 percent, where earlier some were at 28 percent. Check the rate on your supplier invoice and your own bill.

    How Verity helps

    Each part carries its tax category, and each purchase and sale records the rate that applied, so the split your accountant needs is already clean.

    LKS Attorneys, 2025

  • Selling at or below MRP

    What is expected

    The Legal Metrology rules generally bar a dealer from selling a packed commodity above its printed retail price, with narrow exceptions for a valid tax revision in the month of packing or the next month. How to price old stock after a rate change is a question for your adviser.

    How Verity helps

    Each part records its MRP and the price charged, and a bill above it is flagged before it closes.

    IndiaLaw, 2025

  • Genuine and certified parts

    What is expected

    Selling counterfeit parts, or regulated items without the required mark, can bring legal action. Some products are covered by BIS Quality Control Orders that require the ISI mark, and which items are covered changes, so check the current list for the parts you sell.

    How Verity helps

    Each part records its brand, supplier, batch and date received, so you can show where a part came from and trace a failure.

  • Customer data and consent

    What is expected

    Phone numbers, vehicle numbers and credit details about garages are personal data. India’s data protection rules were notified on 13 November 2025, and most duties start in phases, with the main ones about 18 months later.

    How Verity helps

    Customer data sits in one permissioned place, with who viewed or changed it recorded, instead of in personal phones and ledgers.

    AZB Partners, 2025

  • Shops and establishments registration and staff records

    What is expected

    Most states require a shops and establishments registration and records of working hours, weekly offs and leave. Details differ by state, so check your own.

    How Verity helps

    The registration is stored with its renewal date and owner, and shifts, attendance and leave are recorded against each person.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions auto parts store owners ask.

I have ten thousand part numbers. Will I have to key them all in?

No. We bring in your list from the sheet or billing report you have, and tidy it in stages. Fast-moving parts come first, and slow ones are added as they sell or are counted.

Does Verity replace my billing system?

No. Your billing screen keeps taking bills and payments. We agree during setup how those bills reach Verity, which then does the stock, fitment, credit, supplier and delivery work around them.

Can it tell me what fits what?

It stores the fitment you give it, such as vehicle, model and year against each part number, and lets your staff search by vehicle or number. We build it up with you from your catalogues and your head salesman’s knowledge, in stages.

Can it replace my garage credit book?

Yes, for the record keeping. Each garage becomes an account with a limit, bills, payments and balances by age, and a bill over the limit waits for your approval. Your staff still collect the money; Verity makes sure the chase happens.

Can it take online orders?

No. If garages order by phone or message today, that continues. Verity makes sure every order has an owner and a next step.

I have a counter and a godown. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each is a location in one system, with stock, sales and balances side by side. Stock moves between them against a recorded transfer.

Can it help me with fake parts?

It records the supplier and batch on each part, so a failure can be traced and a supplier with repeated failures is flagged. It cannot tell by itself whether a part is genuine.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so counter staff, godown keepers, managers and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of counters, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your store runs.
