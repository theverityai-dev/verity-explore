How it runs today

Most music schools are run by a musician who also does the books.

The lesson is the product. A weekly 30 or 45 minute slot with one teacher, or a small group, on one instrument. Most of the work around it is invisible: rescheduling when a student travels, covering for a teacher who is ill, logging which student has the school's keyboard at home, and remembering who is entered for which grade exam.

Money is thinner than it looks. A vendor blog on music school operations notes that the payroll cost of a single student can consume 50% or more of what the student pays, and that qualified teachers are scarce. A missed slot or an empty half-hour is a direct loss.

The records live in a diary, a payment sheet and the teacher's phone. When the owner is away, no one else knows who is owed a make-up lesson or whose fee is late.

What most music schools run on

  • Paper diary or Google Calendar for slots
  • Excel for fees and renewals
  • WhatsApp groups for each teacher and class
  • Notebook for instruments lent out
  • Exam-board forms filled by hand

Each one works. None of them tells you that three students on Tuesday have no teacher.

13 problems, one by one

Where a music school actually loses students and money.

These are the situations owners, coordinators and teachers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Students

    Students drift away after a few months and nobody notices

    What it looks like

    A child who started guitar in June has missed four of the last eight lessons. The teacher thinks the parent will call. The parent assumes the school will. By January the fee has stopped.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: 120 students at ₹2,400 a month is ₹2.88 lakh a month. Losing one in ten without noticing is ₹28,800 a month, ₹3.5 lakh a year.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each lesson is recorded as taken, missed, cancelled or rescheduled. Verity lists students who miss more than your limit, with their teacher and their fee status, and assigns a follow-up task. What the parent said is recorded.

  2. 02Students

    Trial lessons and enquiries are not followed up

    What it looks like

    A parent asks about keyboard lessons on Instagram, comes for a trial on Saturday and is told to call on Monday. Nobody calls her. She joins the school across the road.

    What it costs you

    A trial is the best sale a music school has. An unfollowed trial is a lost year of fees.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each enquiry and trial is a record with the instrument, age, preferred slot and next step. Verity lists trials with no follow-up and assigns each to a named person. Staff make the call; the outcome is recorded.

  3. 03Teachers and slots

    The weekly slot grid is full of holes and clashes

    What it looks like

    Three students at 5 pm on Tuesday, none at 3, and two teachers booked in the same room. A new student is told no slot is free when two are open on Thursday.

    What it costs you

    An empty slot cannot be sold later. A clash costs a student's trust in the first month.

    How Verity fixes it

    Teachers, rooms and slots are records. Verity shows free and booked slots by teacher and room, flags a clash and lists the open slots to offer to new students.

  4. 04Teachers and slots

    Cancelled lessons and make-ups are managed by WhatsApp

    What it looks like

    A teacher is ill, so nine lessons are cancelled by message. Some students are offered a make-up, others forget. At the end of the term a parent says three lessons were never made up.

    What it costs you

    A parent who feels a lesson was lost is a parent who does not renew. Unrecorded make-ups also confuse the teacher's pay.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each cancelled lesson is a record with who cancelled it and the reason, and creates a make-up owed to the student. Verity lists make-ups owed and expiring and records each one when taken.

    See the screen
  5. 05Teachers and slots

    Teacher hours and pay are argued at month end

    What it looks like

    A tabla teacher is paid per lesson taken. She counts 62, the school counts 58. The diary and her phone disagree, and some lessons were group classes counted twice.

    What it costs you

    A disagreement over a few lessons damages the relationship with a teacher the school can ill afford to lose, and qualified teachers are scarce.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each lesson is recorded with the teacher, the duration and the outcome. Verity totals lessons by teacher and type for the month so the accountant works from a record. The school still runs its own payroll.

    See the screen
  6. 06Teachers and slots

    A teacher leaves and takes the students

    What it looks like

    The best vocal teacher sets up on her own. Her students follow, because the school has no record of their progress and no second teacher who knows them.

    What it costs you

    The loss is not one teacher. It is her whole timetable, plus the cost of finding and training a replacement from a small pool.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student has a progress record: pieces learned, level, notes from each lesson and goals. A second teacher is named for each student. Verity shows students without a backup so the school is not at the mercy of one person.

    See the screen
  7. 07Fees

    Fee renewals lapse in the quiet months

    What it looks like

    Fees are paid monthly or per term, and renewals come around when exams and school holidays distract families. A family's fee is a month late, then two, then the student stops coming.

    What it costs you

    Late fees are a direct cash gap. A lapsed renewal that is chased early is usually kept; one that is chased in month three often is not.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student has a plan with due dates, discounts such as sibling or term upfront, and what has been received. Verity lists renewals due in the next 30 days and fees overdue by age, with a task for a named person. Payments come through your existing channels.

    See the screen
  8. 08Instruments

    Instruments go home and never return

    What it looks like

    The school owns six keyboards, four guitars and a set of tablas. A student borrows one for the holidays. The notebook entry is illegible, and in March nobody knows where Guitar 3 is.

    What it costs you

    A lost or damaged instrument is a purchase the school did not plan. Lending without a record also leaves no proof of the condition when it went out.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each instrument is an asset record with condition, location, who holds it and the date it is due back. Verity lists overdue returns and instruments due for tuning or repair.

    See the screen
  9. 09Instruments

    Tuning, repairs and room upkeep are done when something breaks

    What it looks like

    The piano goes out of tune before the exam week. A drum skin splits during a lesson. The sound-proofing in Room 2 has not been looked at in years.

    What it costs you

    A lesson on a bad instrument is a bad lesson, and a repair done in a hurry costs more than one booked.

    How Verity fixes it

    Tuning and servicing dates are tasks on each instrument and room, with the technician and the last visit on the record. Verity raises a task before each is due.

    See the screen
  10. 10Exams

    Grade exams: entries, fees and rehearsals are tracked on paper

    What it looks like

    Graded exams such as Trinity College London's are offered in India. The school collects exam fees, enters candidates, books the accompanist and schedules practice. A late form means a missed session.

    What it costs you

    A missed exam entry means a student waits another cycle, and parents blame the school.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each student's exam is a record with the board, grade, date, fee, repertoire and readiness. Verity lists students who should be entered and those whose entry is not yet filed. Staff file entries with the board.

    See the screen
  11. 11Events

    Recitals and annual day need weeks of organisation

    What it looks like

    The annual recital has 90 students, 14 items, 3 rehearsals, a hired hall, a sound engineer and a programme to print. Parents ask what their child is playing and when. The details live in a chat group.

    What it costs you

    A poorly run recital is the main thing parents remember, and last-minute changes leave students unprepared.

    How Verity fixes it

    An event is a record with items, performers, rehearsal slots, venue, hire costs and tasks with owners. Verity shows who is not yet in the line-up, which items lack a rehearsal and what the event is costing against what is collected.

    See the screen
  12. 12Compliance

    Music licences for events and recorded tracks

    What it looks like

    A recital uses recorded backing tracks and some film songs. Nobody knows whether a licence is needed, who to ask, or whether a school event is exempt.

    What it costs you

    In India, IPRS handles licences for live performance of music and PPL for recorded music in public settings. Whether a school recital is exempt is not always clear and the school should confirm it.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each event record holds a checklist: the music to be used, whether recordings are used, the licence status and who decided. Verity raises a task for the owner before the date. The school confirms with the rights bodies or an adviser.

    See the screen
  13. 13Compliance

    Child safety: teacher records and reporting

    What it looks like

    A parent raises a concern about a teacher. The school has no record of the teacher's references, no written policy, and no clear person who knows what to do next.

    What it costs you

    Section 19 of the POCSO Act generally obliges any person who knows or has reason to believe an offence has been committed to report it. A school with children as pupils needs a clear procedure and records.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each teacher has a record with identity checks, references and training. A child-safety policy and the named contact are kept as documents. Concerns are logged with who acted and when, with access limited by role.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a music school has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Music licences for performances and recordings

    What is expected

    In India, the Indian Performing Right Society (IPRS) generally licenses live performance of music and Phonographic Performance Limited (PPL) licenses recorded music played outside a private setting. Whether a school recital qualifies for an exemption under the Copyright Act is not clear from the sources found, so confirm with the societies or a copyright adviser.

    How Verity helps

    Each event has a licence checklist and an owner, and the decision is recorded.

    S.S. Rana & Co., 2019

  • Child protection and POCSO reporting

    What is expected

    Section 19 of the POCSO Act generally places a duty on any person who has knowledge of an offence to report it. Institutions working with children are generally advised to have a written child protection policy, a reporting protocol and background checks for staff.

    How Verity helps

    Teacher identity and reference checks, the policy and the named contact are kept as records, and concerns are logged with limited access.

    Sansa Legal

  • Exam board rules

    What is expected

    Boards such as Trinity College London set their own entry dates, fees and conditions for graded music exams.

    How Verity helps

    Each board's dates and rules sit on the exam record, and a task is raised before the entry date.

  • Tax and local registrations

    What is expected

    GST treatment of music classes depends on the nature of the course and whether it leads to a recognised qualification. One guide lists hobby classes at 18%, but this is not an official source, so ask your accountant. Premises generally need local trade and shops registrations.

    How Verity helps

    Registrations are records with renewal dates, and your accountant prepares returns.

  • Children's data under the DPDP Act

    What is expected

    Students under 18 generally need verifiable parental consent for their data and for photos used in promotion.

    How Verity helps

    Consent is recorded on the student with its purpose, and access is by role.

Rules differ by state and by the kind of events you hold, and they change. Confirm current requirements with your adviser.

Questions

Questions music school owners ask.

I teach too. Will I have time to use this?

It is built so the record is a by-product of the work. A teacher marks a lesson as taken or missed in a few seconds. We set it up so you see what needs attention in the morning rather than searching for it.

Can it handle group classes as well as one-to-one lessons?

Yes. A lesson can be one-to-one or a group with several students, and hours for the teacher are counted once. Make-ups and fees follow each student.

Does it book lessons online?

No. Verity holds your slots and shows what is open. Students and parents still book through your team, and the booking is recorded.

Can it track which instrument each student has at home?

Yes. Each school-owned instrument is a record with its holder, condition and the date it is due back.

Will it help with grade exams?

It records each student's exam board, grade, date, fee and readiness, and lists those not yet entered. Your staff file the entry on the board's own system.

Can I run a recital from it?

Yes. An event holds the line-up, rehearsal slots, venue, costs and a licence checklist, each task with an owner. The programme itself is made with your design tools.

We have two branches. Can they be seen together?

Yes. Each branch is a location in one system, with students, teachers and instruments side by side. A teacher who works at both is one record.

Who can see student and child-safety data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, and sensitive notes can be limited to the owner. Every view and change is recorded.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of students, teachers, branches and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your school runs.
