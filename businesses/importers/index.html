How it runs today

Most importers run on one trusted person, a lot of email and a customs broker.

The deal starts with a proforma invoice and an advance. The goods ship. The supplier emails the documents. The broker files the Bill of Entry and sends a duty figure. The shipping line sends arrival notices. The goods reach the warehouse, and the real cost is worked out later, if at all.

The facts that matter sit in different places: the payment in the bank advice, the documents in an email thread, the duty in the broker's note, the free days in a shipping line message, the stock in a warehouse sheet.

Rules add dates. Import payments to overseas suppliers are generally expected to be completed within a set period of the shipment. Customs has its own forms and, from 2026, new options such as monthly deferred duty payment for eligible importers. Someone has to know which applies to which shipment.

What most importers run on

  • Email threads with suppliers and brokers
  • Excel for shipments, costs and dues
  • Bank advices and remittance records
  • Accounts software
  • WhatsApp with brokers, transporters and the warehouse

Each tool is used well. The shipment, its cost and its deadlines are in none of them as one record.

14 problems, one by one

Where an import business actually loses money.

These are the situations importers and their finance heads describe again and again. For each: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Shipments

    Nobody knows where each container is

    What it looks like

    Five containers are at sea, two are at port and one is on a truck. The tracking links are in five emails. A customer asks for a date and the answer takes an hour.

    What it costs you

    Customers who cannot get a date place the order elsewhere, and the team spends its day answering where is my container.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each shipment is a record with supplier, order, vessel or flight, container, expected dates and status. Verity shows all shipments by stage on one board, with the date each one needs a decision.

  2. 02Documents

    A document is missing or wrong and the container waits

    What it looks like

    The bill of lading says one consignee name, the invoice another. The packing list quantity differs by a carton. The broker holds the filing until the supplier corrects it.

    What it costs you

    Every day of correction can be a day of port charges, with a customer waiting for the stock.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each shipment has a document checklist: invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificates and others needed for the product. Verity compares key fields across them, lists mismatches and assigns the correction to the person who owns the supplier.

    See the screen
  3. 03Port charges

    Free days run out and demurrage starts

    What it looks like

    The vessel arrives early. The free time at the port is short. The Bill of Entry was not filed because a certificate was late, and charges start.

    What it costs you

    Demurrage is charged per container per day after free time. Example arithmetic: ₹5,000 a day on 4 containers for 6 days is ₹1.2 lakh, a cost that did not need to exist.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each container shows its free time end and the steps still pending to clear it. Verity lists containers by days left and creates tasks for the broker, the transporter and the warehouse. Staff chase; the dates are visible to all.

    See the screen
  4. 04Port charges

    The container is out of the port but not back

    What it looks like

    The goods were delivered to the warehouse. The empty container sits at the factory for a week because nobody was assigned to return it.

    What it costs you

    Detention is charged for holding the carrier's container past free time outside the port, separately from demurrage.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each container has a return task with a due date and an owner, raised when it leaves the port. Verity shows containers still out and the days left.

    See the screen
  5. 05Costing

    The real landed cost is known only after the goods are sold

    What it looks like

    The price list was set from the supplier's invoice and a rough duty. Freight, insurance, port charges, clearing fees, transport and exchange rate moved. Margin is checked quarterly.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: a ₹100 invoice value that becomes ₹138 landed, quoted at ₹135, loses money on every unit.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each shipment collects every cost line: goods, freight, insurance, duty, port charges, clearing and transport at the actual exchange rate. Verity spreads it across the items and shows landed cost per unit and margin at today's price list.

    See the screen
  6. 06Customs and duty

    Duty is paid without a clear view of what was assessed

    What it looks like

    The broker sends one duty amount for the Bill of Entry. Nobody checks it against the classification used last time. A later notice asks for more.

    What it costs you

    Wrong classification or valuation can lead to retrospective duty demands, interest and penalties. Customs law now calls a voluntary settlement of short-paid duty a charge for non-payment of duty, equal to the earlier reduced 15 percent levy.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each item records its classification, the rate and the duty assessed on past Bills of Entry. Verity compares a new shipment with earlier ones and flags a changed rate or value for the owner to ask the broker about. Any advance ruling is stored with its validity.

    See the screen
  7. 07Money

    Duty cash goes out earlier than it needs to

    What it looks like

    Duty is paid shipment by shipment at clearance. For eligible importers, the deferred payment facility has widened, but nobody has checked whether it fits this business.

    What it costs you

    Duty paid early is cash tied up. Example arithmetic: ₹40 lakh of duty paid a month early at 12 percent a year costs about ₹40,000.

    How Verity fixes it

    Duty due on each Bill of Entry sits on a calendar, showing the payment route and due date. Verity totals what is due by month so the finance head can plan cash and see when a monthly payment falls.

    See the screen
  8. 08Suppliers

    Supplier advances are paid and then forgotten

    What it looks like

    A 30 percent advance goes out for an order. Production at the supplier slips. Three months later the goods are late and nobody remembers the terms agreed.

    What it costs you

    An advance with no delivery is a loan to a supplier with little recourse. Recovery from an overseas supplier is hard.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each order records the advance, the date, the agreed shipping date and the refund terms. Verity lists advances by age and orders overdue to ship, and creates a follow-up task for the person who owns that supplier.

    See the screen
  9. 09Money

    Overseas payment dates and bank records do not match the shipments

    What it looks like

    The bank remits against an invoice. The Bill of Entry is filed later. Which remittance covers which entry is only worked out when the bank asks.

    What it costs you

    Import payments are generally expected to be completed within six months of shipment, with extensions only through the bank. A mismatch means bank queries and a scramble to find documents.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each remittance is linked to the order and shipment, with the shipment date and the Bill of Entry. Verity lists shipments with payments near the generally expected six month point, so the finance head acts early.

    See the screen
  10. 10Money

    Exchange rate moves between the order and the payment

    What it looks like

    An order is priced in dollars when the rupee is at one level. By payment, it has moved. The finance head learns the gain or loss from the bank statement.

    What it costs you

    A rupee move of 2 to 3 percent on a large order can be larger than the whole margin.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each order records its currency, amount, rate at order and the rate at payment. Verity shows exposure across open orders and the cost effect at today's rate. Verity does not hedge or buy currency.

    See the screen
  11. 11Quality

    Quality problems show up after the goods arrive

    What it looks like

    A batch of imported goods is found defective at the warehouse. The inspection report was a photo on WhatsApp. The supplier disputes the claim.

    What it costs you

    A claim without a record trail is a claim you lose, and the stock sits unsold.

    How Verity fixes it

    Inspection reports, photos and claims are records on the shipment and supplier. Verity shows the claim, the documents and the supplier's reply, and tracks the credit or replacement through to settlement.

    See the screen
  12. 12Stock

    Stock arrives and sales does not know what is available

    What it looks like

    A container is cleared, but the warehouse sheet is not updated for two days. Sales quotes the same item as out of stock.

    What it costs you

    A lost sale for stock that was sitting in the warehouse.

    How Verity fixes it

    Receipt at the warehouse updates stock against the shipment and landed cost. Sales sees what is cleared, what is in transit, and what is reserved for orders.

    See the screen
  13. 13Money

    Customers pay late while suppliers are paid on time

    What it looks like

    The importer pays the overseas supplier on the due date and carries customers on 60 to 90 days.

    What it costs you

    Example arithmetic: ₹1.2 crore outstanding beyond 60 days financed at 11 percent a year costs about ₹1.1 lakh a month in interest.

    How Verity fixes it

    Invoices are tracked against due date and customer's usual payment days. Verity lists overdue amounts by customer and age with the person who owns the relationship, and creates follow-up tasks. Staff make the call.

    The owner sees profit only after the goods are sold

    What it looks like

    The owner asks which shipments are still open, which are making money and what cash is tied up in transit. Each answer needs a different person.

    What it costs you

    Decisions about what to order next run on feel.

Rules and paperwork

The rules an importer has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or customs advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • Customs duty, Bill of Entry and classification

    What is expected

    Import duty depends on the item's classification, value and applicable notifications, and is assessed on the Bill of Entry. A wrong classification or valuation can lead to later demands with interest. Budget 2026 changes include a five year advance ruling validity and a 15 percent charge for non-payment on voluntary settlement. Confirm each with your broker.

    How Verity helps

    Each item records its classification, past assessed duty and any advance ruling with its validity, and a changed rate is flagged.

    ICAI

  • Deferred payment of duty

    What is expected

    From 1 March 2026, the deferred duty payment period is 30 days on a monthly cycle, for eligible importers, with duty on Bills of Entry returned in a month due by the 1st of the next month. Check whether you qualify.

    How Verity helps

    Duty due is a calendar by route and date, so cash is planned.

    ICAI

  • Import payments through your bank

    What is expected

    Remittances against imports are generally expected to be completed within six months of shipment, with extensions only through your authorised dealer bank, and banks match Bill of Entry data with remittances. Advance payments follow the bank's conditions. Confirm the current RBI direction with your bank.

    How Verity helps

    Each remittance is linked to its order, shipment and Bill of Entry, and shipments nearing the window are listed.

  • Port charges, free time and container return

    What is expected

    Demurrage applies while a container stays in the terminal past free time, and detention applies when a carrier's container is kept outside the port past free time. Free time varies by line, port and container type.

    How Verity helps

    Free days and return dates are tracked on each container with an owner.

    Cogoport

  • Import registration and product approvals

    What is expected

    An importer needs an Importer Exporter Code, and some products need further approvals or standards certification before clearance, which differ by product.

    How Verity helps

    Registration numbers, certificates and approvals are records with expiry, linked to the items that need them.

Rules change often and differ by product. Confirm current requirements with your customs broker, bank or adviser.

Questions

Questions importers ask.

My broker does the customs work. What does Verity add?

It keeps the broker's output, such as duty and Bill of Entry details, in the same record as the supplier, the payment, the free days and the stock. Your broker still files and clears.

Can it calculate my landed cost?

Yes, once cost lines are recorded on the shipment. It spreads goods, freight, insurance, duty, port charges, clearing and transport across the items at the actual exchange rate, and shows cost per unit against your price list.

Will it track shipments automatically from the shipping line?

We agree during setup how shipment status and dates reach Verity, and your team can also update them. Verity does not promise a live link to any named carrier.

Can it stop demurrage?

It shows free days left and assigns tasks so the right person acts early. Your team and broker still do the clearing.

Does it make payments or hedge currency?

No. Your bank makes payments. Verity records them against the order and shipment and shows your exchange exposure at today's rate.

Does it file my customs documents?

No. Your broker or your team files them. Verity keeps the documents, dates and costs in order.

I import for my own factory, not for trading. Does it fit?

Yes. Shipments, documents, duty and landed cost work the same way, and received goods can flow to your stock and production records.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so the import desk, finance, the warehouse and sales each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
