How it runs today

Most electronics shops can tell you what sold. Few can tell you what they are owed.

The showroom is easy to see. The wall of televisions, the phone counter, the weekend queue for air conditioners. What is hard to see is everything behind it: which serial numbers sit in the godown, which brand scheme each unit qualifies for, which finance approvals are pending, and which repaired phones are waiting for a customer to collect.

That information exists in pieces. The billing software knows the sale. Brand portals hold claims. Finance forms are in a folder. Service job sheets are on paper. Warranty cards are in a drawer. The owner, who also buys, sells and chases, holds the rest in memory.

Margins are thin. A vendor guide for new shops puts gross margin at 13 to 15% for a small electronics shop, with smartphone margins lower still. At that level a missed claim, a wrong price after a tax change or a pile of unclaimed repairs eats a large part of the profit.

What most electronics stores run on

  • Billing software at the counter
  • Brand and distributor portals for schemes and claims
  • Finance company forms and apps
  • Paper job sheets and warranty cards
  • WhatsApp groups with brand reps and customers

Each one does its own job. None of them tells you which serial number was sold under which scheme and who still owes you money for it.

14 problems, one by one

Where an electronics shop actually loses money.

These are the situations electronics retailers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Serials

    Serial numbers not recorded at sale

    What it looks like

    A customer returns a phone with a screen fault. The bill says model, not serial number. The box is gone. Nobody can prove this exact unit came from your shop or when it was sold.

    What it costs you

    Without the serial number, warranty disputes become arguments, and a brand can refuse a claim or a return. Example arithmetic: ten such disputes a month on ₹20,000 products is ₹2 lakh at risk.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each unit has a record with its serial number from the day it is received. At the sale, the serial number is linked to the bill and the customer. Anyone can look up a serial number and see the sale date, the customer and the warranty end.

    See the screen
  2. 02Serials

    Warranty dates worked out from memory

    What it looks like

    A customer says the fridge is under warranty. The salesperson looks for the bill. The brand gives a year on the product and five on the compressor. Extended warranty was sold separately and is on a different slip.

    What it costs you

    A wrong yes means a free repair you paid for. A wrong no means an angry customer and a review. Neither is a decision the shop should make by guesswork.

    How Verity fixes it

    Warranty terms are set per product, including parts and extended cover, and calculated from the sale date. A lookup shows what is covered and until when, and what was sold as extended warranty.

    See the screen
  3. 03Brand credit

    Brand schemes earned but never claimed

    What it looks like

    The brand runs a quarterly scheme: sell a set number of units of a model and get a payout or credit note. The count is kept in a notebook. The claim date passes. The rep says the claim is closed.

    What it costs you

    Scheme payouts are part of the margin. Example arithmetic: ₹400 a unit on 120 units is ₹48,000 a quarter, and one missed deadline loses all of it.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each scheme is a record with its terms, units counted from the serial numbers sold, the claim deadline and the amount due. Verity shows how close you are to each slab and lists claims to file before they lapse.

    See the screen
  4. 04Brand credit

    Brand credit notes, returns to brand and payments in different places

    What it looks like

    A defective lot goes back to the distributor. The credit note is promised in 15 days. Two months later it is still in a WhatsApp chat. Payments to the same brand are made without setting off what they owe you.

    What it costs you

    Money tied up in credit notes you are owed. Example arithmetic: ₹3.2 lakh outstanding across three brands for two months is lost use of that cash, and sometimes lost for good.

    How Verity fixes it

    Every return to a brand, every credit note due and every payment sit on the brand's record. Verity shows what the brand owes you against what you owe them, with age, before you pay.

    See the screen
  5. 05Pricing

    Prices change and the stock in hand is not repriced

    What it looks like

    A tax rate is cut. Televisions, air conditioners and dishwashers moved from 28% to 18% GST from 22 September 2025, and industry estimates put shelf prices about 8 to 9% lower. Old stock bought at the old rate sits beside new stock. A brand changes its dealer price on the 1st and nobody tells the counter.

    What it costs you

    Selling at the old price loses the sale to the shop down the road. Selling old stock below cost loses margin. Both happen when no one has a clear view of cost and price by unit.

    How Verity fixes it

    Purchase cost and selling price are on each stock line, with the date of the last change. Verity flags items whose price or cost changed, so the manager can reprice, protect margin or clear old stock on purpose.

    See the screen
  6. 06Stock

    Slow models and dead stock

    What it looks like

    Last year's phone model is still in the cabinet and the new one is out. Forty units of an older television sit in the godown. The distributor pushed a bulk deal that looked good in March.

    What it costs you

    Old electronics lose value every month. Example arithmetic: ₹15 lakh of old models losing 2% of value a month is ₹30,000 a month.

    How Verity fixes it

    Stock is shown by model with age, cost and sell-through. A slow list shows what to discount, return to the distributor under any stock return terms, or bundle with an accessory.

    See the screen
  7. 07Stock

    Out of the model the customer wants

    What it looks like

    A customer comes for a particular 1.5 ton air conditioner in the first week of April. It sold out on the 28th of March. The distributor takes four days. The customer buys elsewhere.

    What it costs you

    Peak season sales are the year's margin. A missed week in a peak season is not recovered.

    How Verity fixes it

    Reorder points follow actual sales by model and month. Verity compares stock with last season's demand and drafts the order early enough for the distributor's lead time.

    See the screen
  8. 08Finance

    EMI and finance files incomplete

    What it looks like

    A customer chooses a loan to buy a ₹70,000 phone. The finance form needs ID, a photograph, a bank mandate and a signature. One piece is missing and the approval is stuck. The customer left with the device on the promise of coming back.

    What it costs you

    A file that is not approved is a device that is out of the shop with no payment. Example arithmetic: 12 such files a month at an average of ₹40,000 is ₹4.8 lakh in limbo.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each finance file is a record with a checklist of what is needed, who is the customer, the unit's serial number and the status: documents pending, submitted, approved, disbursed. Files stuck for more than a day show on the manager's list.

    See the screen
  9. 09Finance

    Subvention and cashback that the shop is told it will receive

    What it looks like

    The brand and the bank offer a no-cost EMI. The shop is told the brand will pay the interest, and the bank will pay after a delay. The payout is short or late, or tied to a condition the counter did not know.

    What it costs you

    Offers that look free to the customer have a cost somebody carries. If the shop carries it by default, the margin is gone with no record of why.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each finance sale records the scheme, who funds the interest, the expected payout and the date. Verity lists payouts not received after the expected date and shows the amount due against each finance partner.

    See the screen
  10. 10Returns

    Customer returns with no clear rule

    What it looks like

    A customer returns a speaker after a week because it is not what he wanted. A staff member says yes, another says no. The box is opened, the accessories are missing. The unit goes back on the shelf as new.

    What it costs you

    Open box units sold as new lead to complaints, and returns accepted outside the brand's terms cannot be passed back. Example arithmetic: five returns a month at ₹6,000 not recoverable is ₹30,000.

    How Verity fixes it

    A return is a record with the reason, condition, accessories received and a decision: refund, replace, send to brand or sell as open box. The serial number follows the unit so it is not sold as new.

    See the screen
  11. 11Service

    Repairs pile up and customers keep calling

    What it looks like

    A phone goes to the service centre. The customer calls every second day. The job sheet is on a tray. The brand says it is with the technician. Some devices have been sitting uncollected for months.

    What it costs you

    Repair complaints are common. National Consumer Helpline data shows repair related complaints for smartphones and tablets rose from 19,057 in 2022 to 2023 to 22,864 in 2024 to 2025. A shop that stays on top of its jobs keeps the customer, whoever caused the fault.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each job is a record with the serial number, fault, where it was sent, expected date and owner. Late jobs show on a board, and the customer's call back is a task. Unclaimed items are listed by age.

    See the screen
  12. 12Delivery

    Installation, demonstration and delivery of big items

    What it looks like

    A 55 inch television is sold on Saturday. Delivery is promised for Sunday. The installer is not told. The customer waits all day and calls three times.

    What it costs you

    Large appliances are sold on trust. A missed delivery or an uninstalled unit turns into a complaint, a return or a bad review.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each delivery or installation is a job with a date, address, serial number and an owner. Verity lists the ones at risk and records proof of completion, so the unit is not counted as delivered until it is.

    See the screen
  13. 13Money

    Counter discounts and price matching with no trail

    What it looks like

    A customer shows a lower price from a website. The salesperson matches it on the spot, without asking. A friend of the owner gets a cash discount. The accessories bundle is given free.

    What it costs you

    With a thin margin, even a 2% unapproved discount is a large share of the profit. Card payments can reduce margin further, as one retailer notes, so the final take can be well below the shelf margin.

    How Verity fixes it

    Discounts above your limit are routed for one tap approval with a reason. Verity totals discounts by person and by model, and shows margin per sale after the discount.

    See the screen
  14. 14Owner

    Several showrooms, no common picture

    What it looks like

    One showroom has ten units of a model and another sold out. A unit moves between showrooms by hand and by memory. Each manager reports at night in a different way.

    What it costs you

    Pieces in transit are the easiest to lose, and the wrong stock in the wrong showroom is lost sales.

    How Verity fixes it

    Showrooms are locations in one system. A transfer is a record that has to be received at the other end. Sales, margin, stock age, claims due and service jobs appear side by side.

Rules and paperwork

The rules an electronics shop has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.

  • BIS registration of electronics

    What is expected

    Many electronics products must carry a BIS registration or certification mark to be sold. A Consumer Affairs Ministry statement reported in March 2025 says selling goods without BIS certification can bring a penalty of not less than ₹2 lakh, up to ten times the value of goods, and imprisonment of up to two years. Confirm which of your products are covered.

    How Verity helps

    Each model stores its registration or certificate details, and units without them are flagged at receiving.

    The Tribune

  • GST rate changes

    What is expected

    GST on televisions, monitors, projectors, air conditioners and dishwashers fell from 28% to 18% from 22 September 2025. Rates differ by product and can change, so check each category with your accountant.

    How Verity helps

    Each product category has its rate recorded with the date it applies from, and stock bought before a change is flagged for repricing. Your accountant prepares returns.

    Outlook Business

  • Warranty, service promises and consumer law

    What is expected

    Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, a seller who is not the manufacturer can generally be held liable in limited cases, including when the shop gives its own warranty beyond the manufacturer's, or when the manufacturer cannot be identified or served. A shop's own extended warranty or service promise is therefore worth putting in writing. Take advice before offering your own warranty.

    How Verity helps

    Warranty cover, extended cover and any promise made by the shop are recorded against the serial number, with who gave it and when.

    Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas blog, 2022

  • Consumer finance and EMI offers

    What is expected

    Banks and finance companies that lend to your customers are regulated by the Reserve Bank of India, which issued Digital Lending Directions in May 2025. Lenders generally have to give borrowers clear loan terms, and no cost EMI offers have drawn attention in the past. What the shop must hand over depends on the lender, so follow each lender's process.

    How Verity helps

    Each finance file keeps the lender, the scheme, the documents collected and the status, so the file can be shown when asked. Verity does not submit anything to a lender.

Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant, your brand or the relevant authority.

Questions

Questions electronics retailers ask.

Will my staff really scan or type serial numbers on every sale?

Only if it takes seconds. We agree during setup how serial numbers reach Verity, from your billing or from a quick entry at receiving, and we start with the categories where serial numbers matter most, such as phones, televisions and appliances.

Does Verity replace my billing software?

No. Your billing keeps making bills. We agree during setup how bills and payments reach Verity, which then does the serial, scheme, finance, stock and service work around them.

Can it file my scheme claims on the brand portal?

No. Your team files the claim. Verity counts the units, shows how close you are to each slab and lists the claims due before the deadline.

Does it submit finance forms to the bank or finance company?

No. Staff submit through the lender's own process. Verity keeps a checklist and status for each file, and tracks the payouts you are owed.

Can it handle returns and repairs sent to the brand?

Yes. Each job has the serial number, the fault, where it went, the expected date and an owner. Late jobs show on the board and the customer's call back is a task.

Can it work out my real margin after discounts and schemes?

Yes, once costs and schemes are entered. Margin by model, category and salesperson shows what is left after discounts, and the scheme amounts you have earned and not yet received.

I have two showrooms and a godown. Can I see them together?

Yes. Each is a location in one system, with stock, margin, claims and service jobs side by side. A transfer is a record that has to be received at the other end.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so a salesperson can see price but not your cost, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of showrooms, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your shop runs.
