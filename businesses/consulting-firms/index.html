Verity for consulting firms

A consulting firm sells expertise by the week. The margin leaks in the weeks nobody counted.

A consultancy has few products and many promises: a scope in a proposal, a team on a project, a deliverable on a date, an invoice at a milestone. Every one of these can drift. A consultant is on two projects at once, a client adds a workstream, a deliverable waits for sign-off, a milestone invoice goes out three weeks after the milestone. Verity gives each engagement one record with its scope, team, time, deliverables and invoices, so the firm sees drift while it can still be fixed.

How it runs today

Most consulting firms are strong on thinking and light on the machinery behind it.

The work is varied: strategy, operations, finance, HR, technology, sustainability, market entry. Each engagement has a scope, a team, a plan and a fee. The commercial shape is always the same: sell, staff, deliver, bill, collect.

In small and mid-sized firms, those five steps are held together by the partners. The pipeline is in their heads, the staffing is on a spreadsheet, the status of each project is a call, and milestone invoices go out when someone remembers.

The market is crowded and priced hard. One research estimate puts India's management consulting market at about USD 8.2 billion in 2025, and says IT services majors can undercut pure-play firms by 20 to 30 percent while clients increasingly accept outcome-based fee clauses. A small firm has little margin to waste.

What most consulting firms run on

  • Partners' memory and a pipeline sheet
  • Excel for staffing and timesheets
  • Word and PowerPoint for proposals and deliverables
  • Email and WhatsApp with clients
  • The accountant's sheet for invoices

Each one is fine for its job. None of them tell the partner, early, that a project has used 80 percent of its fee at 50 percent of its scope.

16 problems, one by one

Where a consulting firm actually loses margin and time.

These are the situations partners and engagement managers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

  1. 01Pipeline

    Proposals sent and then forgotten

    What it looks like

    A proposal went to a manufacturing client three weeks ago. The partner thought the associate was following up. The associate thought the partner would call.

    What it costs you

    Large engagements are won on follow-up as much as on content. A proposal with no next step is a project quietly lost to a faster competitor.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each opportunity is a record with the client, the contact, the fee, the stage and the next step with an owner and a date. Verity shows opportunities with no recent activity and creates a follow-up task. Partners still make the call.

    See the screen
  2. 02Engagements

    Scope in the proposal is not the scope in the project

    What it looks like

    The proposal said three stakeholder workshops and one report. The client now expects six workshops, a dashboard and a board presentation, and the team has agreed to each in a meeting.

    What it costs you

    Unpaid work and a team that is tired by the middle of the project. Scope creep is a common complaint in consulting, and the more the firm says yes, the harder a later yes-or-no becomes.

    How Verity fixes it

    The engagement record holds the agreed scope, the fee and the plan. Extra requests are logged as scope changes with hours estimated and a decision: accept, charge or decline. Verity shows hours against scope so the partner sees creep early.

    See the screen
  3. 03Engagements

    Project status is a phone call

    What it looks like

    Ask three engagement managers where their projects stand and you get three sentences. One project is amber, but no one has written down why.

    What it costs you

    Problems reach the partner after the client has noticed. Decisions about staffing and escalation come late.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each engagement shows a status, milestones, risks and the next client touchpoint, updated by the manager in a minute. The partner's board shows all engagements by status, with the ones that slipped first.

    See the screen
  4. 04People

    Consultants double-booked or on the bench

    What it looks like

    One consultant is promised to two projects in the same fortnight. Another has had no billable work for three weeks, but nobody noticed because the staffing sheet is a month old.

    What it costs you

    Missed commitments on one side and unpaid time on the other. A bench week is a cost that no client will ever pay.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each person's allocation across engagements is a plan with dates and percentages. Verity highlights over-allocation and open capacity for the next eight weeks, so the partner staffs new work and fixes conflicts before they hurt.

    See the screen
  5. 05People

    Utilisation known only at year end

    What it looks like

    The partners suspect that the firm's billable share is low, but the data is in timesheets that were filled in at the end of each month, if at all.

    What it costs you

    Hiring, pricing and bench decisions are made without knowing how many hours were actually sold. Example arithmetic: a team of 10 with a utilisation 8 points lower than planned loses about 320 hours a month, or about ₹8 lakh at ₹2,500 an hour.

    How Verity fixes it

    Staff log time against engagements in a few taps. Verity shows billable and non-billable hours per person and per engagement, against plan, every week.

    See the screen
  6. 06Money

    Fixed-fee projects that quietly run at a loss

    What it looks like

    A ₹12 lakh fixed-fee diagnostic was planned at 400 hours. By week 6 it has used 380 and the client wants a second phase "to wrap up".

    What it costs you

    Margin disappears one extra week at a time. Example arithmetic: 150 extra hours at an internal cost of ₹1,500 an hour is ₹2.25 lakh of unpaid work.

    How Verity fixes it

    Fee, planned hours, hours used and cost to date sit on the engagement. Verity warns the manager at the thresholds you set, such as 70 percent of hours used with 50 percent of milestones done.

    See the screen
  7. 07Money

    Milestone invoices raised weeks late

    What it looks like

    The inception report was accepted on the 3rd. The invoice went out on the 28th because the associate was waiting for a PO number and the partner was travelling.

    What it costs you

    Cash arrives a month later than it should. For a firm with 15 clients, that is a permanent working capital gap.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each milestone is linked to a deliverable and an invoice amount. When the deliverable is marked accepted, Verity creates an invoice task for the engagement manager with the amount, PO details and contact. The firm raises the invoice; Verity tracks it.

    See the screen
  8. 08Money

    Receivables chased from memory

    What it looks like

    A client is 80 days past due. The last reminder was a polite message eight weeks ago. The partner is reluctant to chase because he is also pitching for the next project.

    What it costs you

    Cash tied up, and an uncomfortable conversation delayed until it is bigger. Under Section 43B(h), a buyer who pays a micro or small enterprise late may lose the tax deduction for that year, so your client may care more than you think, if you are registered as a micro or small enterprise.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity ages invoices by client, shows the agreed payment terms and creates follow-up tasks for the right person with the history of earlier reminders. Whoever chases is not the person who has to sell.

    See the screen
  9. 09Deliverables

    Deliverables with no owner and no version

    What it looks like

    The final deck exists as "final_v6", "final_v6_partner" and "final_v7_client_changes". A reviewer edits the older one. The client receives the wrong version.

    What it costs you

    Rework, an embarrassing correction and a partner's last-hour review.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each deliverable is a record with an owner, a due date, a version history and a state: draft, in review, with client, accepted. Verity shows who is holding it and flags the ones that are late.

    See the screen
  10. 10Deliverables

    Client sign-off that drifts

    What it looks like

    A draft report sits with the client's CFO for three weeks. The project plan assumed two days. The team has nothing to do and the next milestone slips.

    What it costs you

    Delayed milestones mean delayed invoices, and a team that costs money while waiting.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each deliverable has a client reviewer and a review date. Verity shows items waiting on the client and for how long, so the engagement manager can escalate with facts. The next milestone date moves visibly.

    See the screen
  11. 11Knowledge

    Knowledge locked in individual laptops

    What it looks like

    A senior consultant built a pricing model for a retailer last year. A new project in the same sector starts from scratch because nobody knows it exists.

    What it costs you

    Hours spent redoing work, inconsistent quality and weaker proposals.

    How Verity fixes it

    Reusable material, such as templates, frameworks, benchmarks and past reports, is stored as records tagged by sector and topic with permissions for client confidentiality. People search and find it, with the engagement it came from.

    See the screen
  12. 12Compliance

    Confidentiality across clients

    What it looks like

    A team member working on two competing clients has access to both folders. A shared drive link meant for one client is forwarded to another.

    What it costs you

    A breach of an NDA is a serious loss of trust, and in a small market, a reputation problem. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, notified in November 2025, add duties for personal data when most provisions apply about 18 months later.

    How Verity fixes it

    Access to an engagement and its documents follows the team on it. A conflict flag shows when one person is on competing clients, and every document view is logged.

    See the screen
  13. 13Money

    Expenses and subcontractors that are not billed back

    What it looks like

    Flights, a research subscription and an associate consultant's invoice are paid by the firm. Some are billable under the contract and some are not. The list is on three emails.

    What it costs you

    Recoverable costs that are never recovered, and margins that look better than they are until the accounts are closed.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each expense and subcontractor invoice is recorded against an engagement with a billable flag. Verity shows unbilled recoverable costs by client and adds them to the next invoice task.

    See the screen
  14. 14People

    Hiring decisions based on a hunch

    What it looks like

    The firm is busy, so the partners decide to hire two analysts. A quarter later the pipeline is thin and the new hires are on the bench.

    What it costs you

    A salary line that does not match the work. In a firm of 12, two idle people is a visible dent.

    How Verity fixes it

    Verity shows the signed pipeline, the probability-weighted pipeline and the capacity for the next quarter on one screen, so hiring follows work that is likely to come.

    See the screen
  15. 15Clients

    Repeat clients not noticed

    What it looks like

    A client you did a diagnostic for in March is now planning a rollout. Nobody has been in touch, and another firm has been called in.

    What it costs you

    The cheapest business a consulting firm wins is its own past clients.

    How Verity fixes it

    Each client has a relationship record with engagements, contacts, outcomes and the next touchpoint. Verity lists past clients with no contact for your set number of months and creates a task for the partner who knows them.

    See the screen
  16. 16Owner

    The partner cannot see the whole firm

    What it looks like

    At the monthly partner meeting, every partner reports their own projects. Nobody has a combined view of margin, utilisation, receivables and pipeline.

    What it costs you

    Decisions about hiring, pricing and which clients to chase are argued, not read from a page.

    How Verity fixes it

    The owner's screen shows engagements by status and margin, utilisation, unbilled work, receivables and pipeline, live from the records the team already updates.

How we work with you

We set it up with you, in four weeks.

You do not get a login and a manual. We sit with your partners and managers through a real project week, build Verity around how you sell, staff and bill, bring your data in, then stay on.

  1. Week 1

    Discovery and mapping

    What we do
    Sit with a partner, two engagement managers and whoever raises invoices. Follow one engagement from proposal to final invoice, and note where scope, time, deliverables and money are held today.
    What we need from you
    Two to three hours of a partner's time and an hour from each manager. Your pipeline sheet, two recent proposals, staffing sheet and invoice tracker.
    What you have at the end
    A written map of how your firm works: service lines, engagement types, fee models, staffing rules, deliverable stages and the first gaps we will close.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3

    Configuration

    What we do
    Set up engagement templates by service line, scope and change logging, milestone and fee structures, time codes, utilisation targets, deliverable states, billable expense rules, conflict flags and permissions for partners, managers, consultants and finance.
    What we need from you
    Review the setup with one partner and two managers. Tell us where a stage, a fee model or a rule is wrong.
    What you have at the end
    A working Verity with your engagement types, fee models and staffing rules, ready for your data.

  3. Weeks 3 to 4

    Migration and training

    What we do
    Bring in your pipeline, live engagements with scope and milestones, staffing plan, open invoices and clients from the sheets and files you use. Train partners, managers and consultants on their own screens during real work.
    What we need from you
    Your pipeline and staffing sheets in whatever form they exist, and a check of each live engagement's scope and fee. An hour with each role.
    What you have at the end
    Your live engagements, people and invoices inside Verity, and a team that has logged a week of time on it.

  4. After go-live

    Operations partnership

    What we do
    Stay on after launch. Review the first months of utilisation, margin and receivables with you, tune the thresholds and stages, and handle changes when you add a service line, a partner or an office.
    What we need from you
    A short monthly review with the managing partner.
    What you have at the end
    A system that keeps matching how the firm runs, with someone responsible for it.

Rules and paperwork

The rules a consulting firm has to keep up with.

Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not searching when a client, an auditor or an authority asks.

  • Payment terms and the MSME rule

    What is expected

    Where a buyer purchases from a micro or small enterprise, payment is generally due within 15 days, or the agreed period up to 45 days under a written agreement. Late payment generally defers the buyer's tax deduction to the year of payment, from 1 April 2024. This matters if you are a registered micro or small enterprise or if you buy from one, such as an associate consultant.

    How Verity helps

    Each client and subcontractor has its terms on the record, and invoices age against them.

    ClearTax, Section 43B(h), 2026

  • Data protection

    What is expected

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with most duties due about 18 months later. If you handle personal data for clients, such as employee records in an HR project, check with your adviser how the rules apply.

    How Verity helps

    Engagement documents have permissions by team, and every view and change is logged.

    Mondaq, 2025

  • Client contracts and confidentiality

    What is expected

    Consulting work is usually governed by an engagement letter, a statement of work and a non-disclosure agreement. Terms on ownership, liability and confidentiality differ for each client.

    How Verity helps

    The signed agreements are stored on the engagement with their key dates, and team access follows the NDA.

  • Tax on professional fees

    What is expected

    Fees are generally subject to GST and clients may deduct tax at source. The details depend on your registration and your client.

    How Verity helps

    Invoice tasks carry the details your accountant needs. Your accountant prepares returns and Verity files nothing.

Rules change and depend on your firm's structure and your clients. Confirm current requirements with your accountant and legal adviser.

Questions

Questions consulting partners ask.

We are a firm of eight. Is this too much?

A small firm feels every unbilled week. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.

Will my consultants actually log time?

Only if it takes seconds, so we set it up as a few taps per day against the engagements they are on, with a short list of codes. We start with what matters and add detail later.

Does Verity write proposals or reports?

No. You keep using Word and PowerPoint. Verity holds the fee, scope, versions, reviewers and states around them.

How does it help with scope creep?

The agreed scope and hours sit on the engagement. Extra requests are logged as changes with an hours estimate, and the partner decides to accept, charge or decline. Hours against scope are visible all the time.

Can it handle fixed-fee, time-and-materials and retainer work in one place?

Yes. Each engagement has its own fee model, and the same screens compare fee, hours and cost for all of them.

Can it keep clients' work confidential from each other?

Access follows the engagement team. A conflict flag shows when one person is on competing clients, and views are logged.

Does it send invoices?

No. Verity creates the invoice task with the amount and details, and your finance person raises the invoice. It tracks the status until payment.

Who can see my data?

Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and engagement, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.

How soon will I see something useful?

The pipeline, unbilled milestones and receivables need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. Utilisation becomes reliable after a few weeks of logged time.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of people and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your firm runs.
