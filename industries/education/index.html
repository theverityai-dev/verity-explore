The shape of the operation

What these businesses have in common.

A school or institute runs several parallel operations at once: admissions and enrolment, timetabling and staffing, fee collection and follow-up, academic and administrative records, parent communication, and reporting to boards or regulators. Each tends to acquire its own system, and none of them share a person record.

The result is that the same student exists five times, the same parent is contacted from three systems, and any question spanning two of those systems is answered by a member of staff with a spreadsheet.

The short version

What owners usually say first.

  • The same person exists in several systems

    A student in admissions, in the fee ledger and in the academic record is three records that never reconcile.

  • Fee follow-up is manual and awkward

    Chasing outstanding payments depends on someone maintaining a list and working through it.

  • Staff workload is uneven and unmeasured

    Teaching and administrative load is distributed by habit rather than by what the records show.

  • Compliance reporting is an annual scramble

    Data that should be a query becomes a project because it was never held in one place.

Does Verity replace the systems we already use?

About four weeks: discovery and mapping, configuration, migration, then an ongoing operations partnership rather than a handover.
