## How it runs today

**A catalogue of thousands, a dozen engineers, and a lot of it in email.**

- Most industrial suppliers carry thousands of lines across a few brands: bearings, fasteners, hoses, valves, cutting tools, safety gear, electricals. Enquiries come by phone, email and WhatsApp from plant purchasers and maintenance engineers, often urgent, often with only a part number or a photograph.
- The knowledge is in people. One senior salesman knows which bearing replaces the discontinued one. The store keeper knows which shelf has the odd size. Another colleague knows which plant accepts what delivery document. When any of them is away, the quote is slower and the chance of a wrong part goes up.
- The money side is slower still. Plants pay on their own cycle, rebates from the brand arrive late if at all, and slow-moving parts sit in the store while a fast one runs out. Margins are thin, so each of these has a visible cost.
- runs on: Email threads with quotes; Brand price lists and portals; Accounting software or Tally; Excel of rate contracts; Store register; Engineers’ phones
- stack note: All of them useful. None of them can tell you that a plant owes ₹14 lakh, past 90 days, and just sent an urgent enquiry.

## Pains

Heading: Where an industrial supply business actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations industrial suppliers describe most often. For each: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Urgent enquiries answered late, or not at all
- area: Enquiries
- scene: A maintenance engineer emails at 9 a.m. for a bearing that has failed. It lands in one person’s inbox. He is at a customer. By the time someone replies, the plant has called your competitor.
- cost: Breakdown orders go to whoever answers first. An unanswered enquiry is a lost order and sometimes a lost vendor listing.
- fix: Every enquiry becomes a record with the plant, the contact, items and a response time. Anything unanswered past your limit goes to the next person, and the open list shows who owns what.
- screen: quotes

### 2. Quotes sent and never followed up
- area: Quotes
- scene: Forty quotes went out last week. Nobody knows which were read, which were won and which went to the other supplier. The salesman remembers the big ones only.
- cost: Example arithmetic: if 40 quotes a week average ₹60,000 and you win 25%, the 30 you do not win are ₹18 lakh of enquiries. If a follow-up call lifts even 3 of them to a win, that is ₹1.8 lakh a week.
- fix: Each quote has a status and a follow-up date. Verity lists quotes due for a call, with the plant’s history, and records the outcome and the reason you lost, so patterns show up.
- screen: quotes

### 3. The wrong part is quoted or sent
- area: Catalogue
- scene: The plant asks for “6205 bearing”. There are three variants: sealed, shielded, open. The quote says one, the stock is another, and the part that arrives does not fit.
- cost: Returns, a second dispatch, a delayed breakdown fix and a purchaser who stops trusting your word.
- fix: Each part is a record with brand, specification, size, alternatives and supersessions. A quote line pulls from the record, and a salesman sees the variants side by side before he commits.
- screen: catalogue

### 4. Brand prices change and quotes stay on the old list
- area: Pricing
- scene: The brand raises prices on the 1st. Quotes from the last two weeks were on the old list, and the plant places the order on the 12th at the old price.
- cost: You sell below your new cost and the margin on that order is gone, sometimes the whole of it.
- fix: Brand price lists are loaded with effective dates. A quote shows its validity, and an order against an expired quote waits for a decision, with the current cost and the old price beside it.
- screen: catalogue

### 5. Rate contracts kept on paper and quietly crossed
- area: Pricing
- scene: A plant has an annual rate contract on fasteners, valid until March. A new salesman quotes spot prices. Another sells at the contract rate after it has lapsed.
- cost: Contract abuse in either direction. Sell below and you lose margin, sell above and the plant complains.
- fix: Contracts are records with items, rates, validity and quantity caps. The order screen applies the live contract and shows what is left, and a lapsing contract creates a renewal task weeks before the end.
- screen: catalogue

### 6. Plants pay late and the cash sits in invoices
- area: Credit
- scene: A large plant pays at 90 days, sometimes 120. A small workshop pays at 15 but takes the credit anyway. Your own supplier wants payment in 30.
- cost: One analysis of industrial distribution says 52% of B2B payments in industrial hubs are overdue past 90 days and distributor margins rarely exceed 5 to 8%. Long credit eats the margin on every sale.
- fix: Each plant has terms, a limit and an ageing record. Verity shows dues by bill and age, logs the purchaser or accounts person to call, and puts a hold flag on new orders when a plant is over limit.
- screen: credit
- source: (UNICbiz analysis, via Devdiscourse, May 2026 (vendor): https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/business/3916401-indias-45-day-msme-payment-rule-exposes-structural-gap-in-industrial-supply-chain-finance)

### 7. Payment chasing depends on one person’s relationship
- area: Credit
- scene: Only one executive knows how to get the invoice through the plant’s vendor portal. When he leaves, a dozen bills are stuck for lack of the right PO number.
- cost: Invoices rejected for a missing PO or GRN number are re-raised, and payment slips by weeks.
- fix: The bill record holds the customer PO, delivery note, GRN number and the submission status. Pending submissions are listed with the plant’s process notes, so any colleague can pick one up.
- screen: credit

### 8. POs do not match quotes, and invoices do not match POs
- area: Orders
- scene: The plant’s PO shows a rate lower than your quote. Nobody notices until the invoice is raised and the plant refuses to pay the difference.
- cost: A debit note, a credit note and a month’s delay. The margin on that order is gone.
- fix: A customer PO is matched line by line to the quote and the contract rate on arrival. A difference is flagged for a salesman’s decision before the order is accepted, not after the bill is rejected.
- screen: orders

### 9. Test certificates and documents missing at dispatch
- area: Orders
- scene: A consignment of high-tensile fasteners reaches the plant without the mill test certificate. The inward quality team rejects it, and your van is back on the road.
- cost: A wasted trip, a delayed plant job and a quality note against your name on the vendor list.
- fix: Each order line lists the documents the plant needs: test certificate, datasheet, batch number, material code. The order cannot move to dispatched until each required document is attached.
- screen: orders

### 10. Partial deliveries and back orders lost in the follow-up
- area: Orders
- scene: You send 60 of 100 pieces and promise the rest in a week. The week passes, the supplier is late, and the plant calls.
- cost: Trust. A plant that has to chase you for a promised delivery looks at other vendors.
- fix: The order keeps the open quantity as a back order with a promised date and a supplier purchase order behind it. A date at risk goes to the salesman before the customer calls.
- screen: orders

### 11. Fast movers run out and slow movers pile up
- area: Stock
- scene: A common V-belt size is out for ten days. The odd size bought for one customer in 2022 is still on the shelf.
- cost: A missed sale for the fast item and cash locked in the slow one. Both happen when minimum levels are set once and never checked.
- fix: Each part carries a reorder level, the lead time of its supplier, and sales history. A short list shows what will run out before the next delivery, and another shows parts that have not moved in your chosen months.
- screen: stock

### 12. Brand rebates, targets and claims tracked only at year end
- area: Suppliers
- scene: The brand’s annual rebate depends on hitting a purchase target by quarter. Nobody tracks progress. Also, a batch of defective bearings was returned under warranty, but the credit note never came.
- cost: A rebate missed by a small gap, or a warranty credit written off, is real money for a business on thin margins.
- fix: Each brand has its target, purchase to date and days left. Warranty claims are records with the batch, the customer complaint and the stage, from raised to credited.
- screen: brands

### 13. Brand and supplier payments and the small supplier rule
- area: Suppliers
- scene: A local manufacturer of gaskets, a small enterprise, is owed for 70 days while the brand distributor with strict terms is paid on the day.
- cost: Under the MSME payment rule, a bill to a micro or small supplier paid after the allowed days is generally deductible only when paid. Late payments can raise your tax bill and strain supply.
- fix: Supplier bills carry due dates and a flag for micro and small suppliers. The payables list sorts by due date and the flag, so payments go out in the order that matters.
- screen: brands
- source: (ClearTax, Section 43B(h): https://cleartax.in/s/section-43bh-of-income-tax-act)

### 14. You cannot say which plants and brands really make money
- area: Owner
- scene: Sales are up, but which plant is worth keeping? A plant that buys ₹40 lakh a year but pays at 110 days and negotiates every rate may earn less than a small workshop that pays on time.
- cost: Time and capital go to accounts that look big and earn little.
- fix: For each plant and brand Verity shows margin after the cost of credit days, discount given, returns and claims. The owner’s screen shows the five accounts that need attention today.
- screen: owner

## Day, before and after

Heading: The same Wednesday at the counter, before and after.
Lede: The work is the same. What changes is that each enquiry, quote and bill leaves a record, and the record chases.

- 9:00: before: Three enquiries sit in two inboxes. Nobody is sure who is on the bearing request. | after: All three have an owner and a reply time. One is already overdue and has moved to the next person.
- 11:30: before: A PO arrives for fasteners at a rate below your quote. The order desk accepts it. | after: The PO is flagged on arrival. The salesman confirms the contract rate and a note goes back to the plant.
- 2:00: before: A van leaves without the test certificate. The plant rejects the lot on arrival. | after: The order cannot move to dispatched until the certificate is attached.
- 4:00: before: You find out a quote of last month is being honoured on the brand’s new price. | after: The quote shows as expired against the new list. The salesman revises it before the order is accepted.
- 6:00: before: A plant’s bill has been stuck for 19 days for a missing GRN number. Nobody knew. | after: The stuck bill is on the dues list with the missing item. Accounts resubmits on the same day.
- Month end: before: You learn you missed the brand’s rebate slab by a small purchase. | after: The brand screen showed the gap 10 days earlier. You placed a planned purchase.

- we: Stay on after launch. Review quote follow-up, plant ageing and brand targets with you, and tune rules as brands and contracts change.

## Rules and paperwork

Note: Rules and thresholds change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

- **Payment to micro and small suppliers**: what: Payment to a micro or small supplier is generally due within 15 days with no written agreement, or the agreed term, not above 45 days. ## FAQ

Heading: Questions industrial suppliers ask.

- **We have thousands of parts. Do we need to enter all of them?** No. We start with the parts that make most of your sales and the ones with variants that cause mistakes. The rest come in from your existing sheet during migration, and are completed as they are used.
- **Does it send quotes to customers?** No. Verity builds the quote from the part, price and contract records, and your salesman sends it as he does today. The follow-up date and the outcome are then recorded.
- **How does it handle different documents for different plants?** Each plant has a list of documents it needs, such as test certificates, batch numbers or datasheets. An order cannot move to dispatched until each required one is attached.
- **Can it work with our brand’s distributor portal?** You still place orders and raise claims on the brand’s portal. Verity records the claim, the batch and the stage, and we agree during setup how data from your side reaches Verity.
- **We have three stores. Can I see all stock together?** Yes. Each store is a location in one system. Stock rolls up, and a transfer between stores is recorded so parts do not appear or disappear.
- **Can salespeople see each other’s quotes?** Only if you allow it. Many suppliers let each salesman see his own accounts, and keep the full pipeline for owners and managers. Access is set by role.
- **Who can see my cost prices?** Only the people you allow. Salesmen can see a selling price and floor without seeing brand cost, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How quickly will it help?** The quote follow-up list and plant dues by age need only your existing quotes and ledger, so they come first. Margin by plant and brand becomes reliable after a month or two of recorded orders.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of stores, users and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your counter works.
