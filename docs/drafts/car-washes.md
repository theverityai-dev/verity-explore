## How it runs today

**The wash runs well in the bay and blind in the office.**

- The work is visible. Cars come in, foam goes on, the bay clears. What is not visible is everything around it: which monthly customer has not been in for three weeks, how much shampoo concentrate went into last week’s washes, how many litres each wash really used, and whether the cash in the box matches the vehicles that left.
- That information exists in pieces. A slip book or a phone app for jobs, a notebook of monthly passes, a register of staff days, a tanker driver’s message, and a lot of memory. The owner is often at the site as well, so the system that joins the pieces is you, standing near the bay.
- The result is not a crisis. It is a slow leak across idle hours, expired passes nobody chased, free washes nobody approved and chemicals used by habit. It shows up only when the month’s collection feels lower than the number of vehicles you saw.
- runs on: Job slip book or phone app; Cash box and UPI QR code; Notebook of monthly passes; WhatsApp groups with the team; Supervisor’s memory
- stack note: Each of these does its own job. None of them talk to each other, which is the problem.

## Pains

Heading: Where a car wash actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations car wash owners describe again and again, whether they run a fixed site, a doorstep service for apartment societies or a detailing studio. For each one: what it looks like on a normal day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Monthly plan customers pay once and then stop visiting
- area: Customers
- scene: A customer buys a pass for eight washes. Three weeks later only two have been used. Nobody on the team notices, the pass lapses, and the customer quietly goes to the wash near the office.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 150 plan customers paying ₹1,800 a month is ₹32 lakh a year. If 20 of them drift away each month without a single call, that is a steady ₹4 lakh a year of repeat income that you never see leaving.
- fix: Every pass is recorded against the customer and the vehicle with the washes used and left. Verity lists customers who are behind on visits or near expiry, and gives the supervisor a call task with the reason, so the follow-up happens while the customer is still undecided.
- screen: members

### 2. Walk-in customers are strangers every single time
- area: Customers
- scene: A man in a white Creta comes in on the first Sunday of each month. Nobody knows his name, his number or what he usually takes. He is a regular in practice and a stranger in your records.
- cost: You cannot offer him a plan, remind him when he is overdue, or tell whether he has gone elsewhere. Every walk-in who could have become a plan customer is a missed sale.
- fix: The vehicle number is the record. A phone number and the usual service are noted at the first visit, and Verity shows repeat visitors who do not have a plan yet, with a suggested offer for the front desk to make.
- screen: members

### 3. Doorstep and apartment society customers dispute whether the car was cleaned
- area: Customers
- scene: In a society with 60 subscribed cars, the cleaner skips a few on a rainy day. At month end a resident says the car was cleaned only twice a week, and the cleaner says four times. There is no record either way.
- cost: Refunds given to avoid an argument, a complaint in the society’s WhatsApp group, and the risk of losing the whole society, which may be a dozen or more subscribers at once.
- fix: Each doorstep clean is ticked on the cleaner’s list against the vehicle, with the date. The resident’s record shows what was done and what was missed, and a missed clean becomes a task for a make-up visit rather than an argument.
- screen: job

### 4. Coating and detailing enquiries go cold
- area: Detailing
- scene: A customer asks about a ceramic coating or paint protection, gets a quote in a message, and says he will think about it. Nobody calls him again. A week later he has booked with a studio that did.
- cost: Example arithmetic: one coating job at ₹18,000 is worth the same as about 60 basic washes. Five lost enquiries a month is ₹90,000 of work that was asked for and not chased.
- fix: An enquiry is a record with the vehicle, the quote, the date and an owner. Verity lists quotes with no reply after three days, and puts them on the owner’s list with the customer’s history beside them.
- screen: quotes

### 5. Bays sit idle in the afternoon and queue on Sunday morning
- area: Bays and time
- scene: On Sunday between 8 and 11 there are twelve cars waiting and customers leave. On Tuesday after lunch two bays stand empty for three hours while the full team is paid.
- cost: A staffed bay hour that earns nothing is lost for good. Because nobody writes it down as a number, nobody manages it.
- fix: Each vehicle is logged with its check-in and hand-back time and its bay. Verity shows vehicles per bay per hour and the quiet and busy patterns by weekday, so you can plan offers for slow hours and shifts for the rush.
- screen: owner

### 6. Nobody knows which vehicle is stuck at which stage
- area: Bays and time
- scene: A customer asks where his car is. The supervisor walks around the site to look. One car has been waiting for the interior vacuum for forty minutes because the person on that task was pulled to another bay.
- cost: Waiting customers get angry, tips and repeat visits suffer, and the slowest stage is never identified because it is never measured.
- fix: Every vehicle moves through states on a simple board: waiting, wash, interior, finish, ready. Whoever is free can see what is next, and the owner can see which stage holds cars the longest.
- screen: job

### 7. Scratch and damage complaints you cannot settle
- area: Customers
- scene: A customer says the bumper scratch was not there when the car came in. Your worker says it was. Without a record you either pay for a touch-up or lose a good customer.
- cost: A repair or a refund on the spot, and a customer who tells others. Even one such case a month is a recurring cost for something that a two-minute check-in could settle.
- fix: At check-in the existing marks are noted on the vehicle’s job record with photos, the customer’s valuables are listed, and both are visible at hand-back. A complaint is then a comparison, not a dispute.
- screen: job

### 8. Shampoo, wax and polish used by habit, not by measure
- area: Stock
- scene: Workers pour the foam concentrate by eye. A drum meant for four weeks is empty in three. Microfibre cloths disappear, and nobody can say whether the spend is high because volume rose or because usage is loose.
- cost: Example arithmetic: at 2,000 washes a month, an extra ₹6 of chemicals per wash is ₹12,000 a month, or ₹1.44 lakh a year, hidden inside the purchase bills.
- fix: Stock items carry a standard quantity per service. Verity compares what was used with the washes done, per bay and per shift, and shows the cost per wash against your target, so overuse appears as a number.
- screen: stock

### 9. Water cost and water rules are both tightening
- area: Water
- scene: Tanker bills climb, the borewell level drops in summer, and a neighbour complains to the authorities. You do not know litres per wash, and you cannot say whether the recycling plant is working.
- cost: In 2024 Bengaluru warned and fined potable-water use for washing vehicles, and some washes saw their daily cars fall sharply. A site that cannot show where its water goes is exposed.
- fix: Tanker deliveries and meter readings are recorded, and Verity shows litres per wash by week. Recycling plant servicing and filter changes are scheduled tasks, so the plant is serviced before the water turns dirty.
- screen: stock

### 10. Casual washers come and go, and attendance is a guess
- area: Staff
- scene: Half the team is daily-wage. Someone does not show on Monday, a friend steps in, and at month end nobody agrees on how many days each person worked or what advance they already took.
- cost: Wage disputes, overpaid or underpaid days, and a bay run short just when the queue builds.
- fix: Attendance is marked against each person each shift. Advances taken are recorded and shown against the month’s dues, and the shift list shows gaps a day ahead so cover can be arranged.
- screen: staff

### 11. Cash in the box does not match the vehicles that left
- area: Money
- scene: At closing the cash box, the UPI total and the slip book are compared. They are off by ₹600. Was it a free wash, a missed slip, or cash that never reached the box?
- cost: Small daily gaps add up, and the owner cannot trust the numbers on the day. Free and discounted washes with no approval are a large part of the gap.
- fix: Each vehicle is a record with a price and a payment mode. Verity compares the day’s vehicles with the day’s collection by mode and points to the exact vehicle that does not tie. Free or discounted washes need an approval with a reason.
- screen: staff

### 12. Fleet and company accounts pay late and nobody chases
- area: Money
- scene: A taxi fleet owner, a school bus operator and two offices send cars every week on credit. The month’s dues sit in a notebook, and the follow-up depends on you remembering.
- cost: Example arithmetic: three fleet accounts at ₹45,000 a month each, paid 30 days late, hold back about ₹1.35 lakh of cash every month.
- fix: Each account is a record with its vehicles, washes done and amount due. Verity lists overdue accounts by days late and assigns a payment call task with the last conversation noted.
- screen: members

### 13. Machines break down because service is a memory
- area: Equipment
- scene: The high-pressure pump loses power, the foam lance clogs and the vacuum motor burns out in the middle of a Saturday. The last service date was written on a wall, if at all.
- cost: A bay out of action during the busiest hours, an emergency repair bill, and customers who walked away while the machine was down.
- fix: Every machine is an asset record with its service interval and history. Verity creates a service task before it is due and records breakdowns, so you can see which machines cost the most to keep running.
- screen: stock

### 14. Licences, consents and renewals live in a drawer
- area: Owner
- scene: The trade licence, the shop registration, the pollution board consent and the groundwater permission each renew on a different date. You find out one has lapsed when an inspector visits.
- cost: Penalties and, in the worst case, a notice to stop operations, for a business whose water use draws attention.
- fix: Each licence and consent is a record with its renewal date and the document attached. Verity creates a task ahead of each date and shows what is expiring on the owner’s screen.
- screen: owner

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping

## FAQ

Heading: Questions car wash owners ask.

- **Will my washers actually use it?** It works only if a vehicle is checked in and handed back, so we make that take seconds on a phone at the bay. We train at the site with real cars. Washers also get something back: their own count and attendance, visible and unarguable.
- **I run a doorstep service for apartment societies. Does it work?** Yes. A society is an account, each car is a vehicle on it, and each clean is ticked on the cleaner’s list. A missed clean becomes a make-up visit task instead of a complaint in the society group.
- **Does Verity take bookings or payments from customers?** No. If you use a booking app or a QR code, it keeps doing that job and we map it during setup. Verity records the vehicle and the payment and gives you the picture around it.
- **Can it message my customers about their plan?** Not on its own. Verity builds the call list and assigns it to the supervisor, who calls or sends the message. The outcome is recorded.
- **I only have one bay. Is this too much?** A one-bay wash has the same leaks and less spare time to notice them. We set up only what you need, and the four-week process is the same.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so washers, supervisor, desk and accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **Can it handle detailing and coating jobs, which take days?** Yes. A job can stay open across several days with its stages, photos, deposit and delivery date, and the customer’s history stays on the vehicle.
- **How long before I see a difference?** The plan call list, fleet dues and stock warnings need only your existing customer and stock data, so they come first. Water and chemical cost per wash become reliable after a few weeks of recorded vehicles.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of sites, bays and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your wash runs.
