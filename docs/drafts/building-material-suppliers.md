## How it runs today

**Most suppliers sell plenty and still do not know who owes them, or how much stock they really have.**

- The yard is easy to see. The trucks, the stack of cement bags, the TMT bundles, the tile pallets. What is hard to see is everything behind it: which contractor has taken material on credit for five different sites, how many deliveries were actually signed for, what rate was promised on the phone last Tuesday, and whether the stock book matches the yard.
- That information exists in pieces. The billing software knows invoices. The challan book has carbon copies. The ledger of contractor dues is a notebook or a sheet kept by the accountant. Rates are in the owner's head and in WhatsApp chats. Stock counts are done when someone has time.
- Material costs also move. A 2022 report quoting Colliers said the costs of key materials such as cement and steel had risen by over 20 per cent every year as of March 2022. A supplier who quotes on Monday and buys on Friday carries that risk.
- runs on: Billing software or a bill book; Challan book with carbon copies; Ledger or Excel for contractor dues; WhatsApp and phone calls for orders and rates; Transporter and driver phone calls
- stack note: Each one works on its own. None of them can tell you that site C has not signed for the last three deliveries and the contractor's bill is now 60 days old.

## Pains

Heading: Where a building material business actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations suppliers and dealers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Orders come on the phone and the details get lost
- area: Orders
- scene: A site engineer calls at 6 pm for 200 bags of cement, 2 tonnes of TMT and 4 cubic metres of sand, to be at site by morning. The counter writes it on a slip. The slip is found at 8 am without the site address or the rate.
- cost: A wrong site, a wrong quantity or a late truck. Each one costs a return trip, an angry contractor and sometimes an order moved to a competitor.
- fix: Each order is a record with the customer, the site, items, quantities, agreed rate, delivery date and who took it. The yard and the dispatcher see the same order, and the customer's earlier orders sit beside it.
- screen: orders

### 2. Deliveries are made but nobody signs for them
- area: Deliveries
- scene: The driver drops 120 bags at a site and comes back with a challan nobody signed. A week later the contractor says only 90 arrived.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 30 bags of cement at ₹380 is ₹11,400 disputed on one delivery. Across a month of such disputes, you either give credit notes or lose the customer.
- fix: Each delivery is a record linked to the order and the challan, with the vehicle, the driver, the quantity loaded and the quantity received on site. A delivery without a recorded receipt after a set number of days appears on a list for follow-up.
- screen: deliveries

### 3. E-way bills and challans are handled in a rush
- area: Deliveries
- scene: A load of steel worth more than the e-way bill limit is loaded on a Saturday evening. The invoice is not raised yet. The driver goes with a hand-written challan, and the transporter calls from the road.
- cost: Goods moved above the e-way bill threshold without the right document can be held by officials. The threshold is generally Rs 50,000 and intrastate limits differ by state, which makes it easy to get wrong.
- fix: Each dispatch shows the value of the load against the limit you set for your state, and flags a load that needs an e-way bill before the vehicle leaves. The bill number is recorded on the delivery. Your accountant or billing system raises the bill; Verity makes sure someone did.
- screen: deliveries
- source: (Refrens, E-way bill threshold, 2023: https://refrens.com/grow/e-way-bill-threshold)

### 4. Contractors owe you for months and you cannot see it
- area: Credit
- scene: One contractor takes material for four sites on credit, pays a little each month and has a balance nobody has added up. The owner learns the number when the accountant closes the quarter.
- cost: Credit is a loan without interest. Example arithmetic: ₹18 lakh outstanding for 60 days at 18 percent a year is about ₹53,000 of lost interest, if the money is ever recovered.
- fix: Each contractor has a live balance split by site and age: current, 30, 60 and over 90 days. A limit on each account warns the counter before a new order goes out on credit over the limit.
- screen: credit

### 5. Credit limits exist only in the owner's head
- area: Credit
- scene: The owner knows that Mr. Reddy is good for ₹5 lakh and a new builder is good for ₹1 lakh. The counter boy releases a ₹3 lakh order for the new builder because he said the owner knew him.
- cost: Bad debt is the biggest single loss a dealer can take, and it arrives as a series of orders, each one reasonable at the time.
- fix: Credit limits and terms are set per customer. An order that goes over the limit, or comes from an account with an overdue balance, is held for the owner's approval with the reason on record.
- screen: credit

### 6. Contractors pay late because their own clients pay late
- area: Credit
- scene: A contractor says the developer has not released his running bill, and promises to pay you next week. Next week he says the same. Industry sources describe this chain: delayed client payments and retention money tie up the contractor's cash, and suppliers are paid late.
- cost: Your working capital becomes the contractor's working capital. If you cannot see who is stretching you, you cannot choose whom to supply.
- fix: Every follow-up is a task on the account with a promise date and the person who gave it. Verity shows promises kept and broken per customer, so credit decisions use history rather than hope.
- screen: followups

### 7. Rates change and the quote on the phone was yesterday's
- area: Pricing
- scene: Steel moved overnight. The counter quoted a contractor on the old rate at 9 am. The mill invoice at the new rate arrives at noon.
- cost: A 2022 report quoting Colliers said cement and steel costs had risen by over 20 per cent every year as of March 2022. Even a small gap on a large order is a loss on that order.
- fix: Each item has a current sell rate and a latest purchase rate. When a purchase rate moves beyond your limit, Verity flags the items and the open quotes that are now below your margin, and a rate change needs a named approver.
- screen: stock
- source: (Indian Infrastructure, Cost Concerns: Price fluctuations in the material market, 2022: https://indianinfrastructure.com/2022/11/20/cost-concerns-price-fluctuations-in-the-material-market/)

### 8. Different customers, different rates, no record of who got what
- area: Pricing
- scene: A big contractor gets cement at ₹5 less a bag. A small builder hears about it and demands the same. Nobody remembers who was promised what, or until when.
- cost: Margin leaks where the owner is not looking, and arguments cost goodwill.
- fix: Special rates are recorded per customer and item, with a start and end date and who approved them. The counter sees the right rate when the order is raised.
- screen: orders

### 9. Stock in the book and stock in the yard do not match
- area: Stock
- scene: The book says 640 bags of cement. The yard has 590. Some were loose, some were lent to another dealer, some were never entered. Nobody knows which.
- cost: A dealer who cannot trust his stock either over-orders and ties up cash, or says yes to an order he cannot supply.
- fix: Receipts from mills and dispatches to sites update stock automatically by yard. A count is a task, and any gap is shown by item with the people and shifts involved.
- screen: stock

### 10. Several yards, godowns and trucks, with no single view
- area: Stock
- scene: The main yard has plenty of TMT bars. The second godown is out of 12 mm. A truck is already carrying 8 tonnes to a customer in a third location.
- cost: Orders are delayed or turned away while stock sits elsewhere, and trucks run half empty.
- fix: Yards and godowns are locations in one system. Stock by location is visible to the counter, and a move between locations is a recorded transfer, so it does not look like a loss.
- screen: stock

### 11. Mill and manufacturer orders are placed too late or too early
- area: Purchasing
- scene: The cement company delivers in four days. The monsoon is coming and pour season picks up. The owner orders on feel and either runs out or fills a godown with bags that harden.
- cost: Lost sales when out of stock. Stock that sits for months loses quality and ties up cash.
- fix: Each item has a reorder point based on real recent dispatch. Verity drafts a purchase order with the supplier's usual lead time and shows what you are still waiting for.
- screen: stock

### 12. Short supply and damage claims on supplier deliveries
- area: Purchasing
- scene: A truck of tiles arrives with 14 boxes broken. A cement load is 20 bags short. The driver has gone before anyone counted.
- cost: You pay the mill for what never arrived, and you cannot claim without a record at the time.
- fix: Receipts record quantity received and damage with photos against the purchase order. A short or damaged load creates a claim task to the supplier and holds that part of the invoice for payment.
- screen: deliveries

### 13. Returns, breakage and credit notes are handled by memory
- area: Returns
- scene: A contractor returns 20 bags he did not use. A customer says the tiles do not match the sample. The counter promises a credit note, and the owner finds out when the ledger does not tie.
- cost: Money given away without a record, and customers who feel they were not treated fairly.
- fix: Returns are recorded against the original order with a reason and a photo. A credit note over your limit needs approval, and stock is updated when the goods are back in the yard.
- screen: orders

### 14. Payments to suppliers are late, or early for no reason
- area: Money
- scene: A micro or small manufacturer supplier has your invoice outstanding for 70 days. At the same time, you pay another supplier two weeks early because he called and asked.
- cost: From 1 April 2024, payments to micro and small suppliers beyond 45 days from acceptance can lose the buyer a tax deduction until actually paid. The rule generally applies to micro and small manufacturers and service providers, not to pure traders, so check each supplier's registration.
- fix: Each supplier invoice shows its due date and age, and suppliers marked as micro or small show the 45-day line. The accounts person sees what is due this week and what is approaching the limit.
- screen: credit
- source: (Busy, Section 43B(h) explained, 2026: https://busy.in/tds/section-43bh-msme-payment-rule-and-45-day-limit-explained/)

### 15. Rate changes in tax are not reflected on time
- area: Compliance
- scene: GST on cement fell from 28 to 18 percent from 22 September 2025. Invoices raised on or after that date had to show the new rate. Quotes written earlier still showed the old price.
- cost: Customers argue about prices, stock bought at one rate is sold at another, and invoices have to be corrected.
- fix: Tax rate changes are tasks that list the open quotes and orders affected, with the date they apply from. Items with price lists show the new landed cost and margin once the rate is updated by your accountant.
- screen: stock
- source: (Tally Solutions, GST on cement, 2025: https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-on-cement-tax-rate-applicability/)

### 16. The owner finds out about all of this at month end
- area: Owner
- scene: The owner works the counter from 8 to 8, takes the calls and checks the yard. The numbers come at month end from the accountant: sales, who owes, stock, margins. By then, it is too late to change anything.
- cost: Slow decisions. A bad customer or a bad rate is found after the third order, not the first.
- fix: One owner view each morning shows sales, who owes and for how long, stock below reorder, deliveries not signed, quotes under margin and promises due, with the items needing a decision listed under it.
- screen: owner

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks.
Lede: You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time at your counter and your yard, build Verity around how orders, trucks and credit actually move, bring your data in, then stay to make sure it keeps working.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend a day at the counter and a morning at the yard. Follow an order from phone call to challan to site, and a credit account from first delivery to payment. Note where quantities, rates and promises are lost.
- you: Two to three hours of yours and your counter lead's. A month of challans, your contractor ledger and the rate list.
- get: A written map of your business: items and units, yards, rate rules, credit terms, customers who matter and the first gaps we will close.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your items with units and conversions, yards and godowns, customer credit limits and terms, special rates, challan and delivery records, reorder points and approvals, and build the six screens around your own terms.
- you: Check items, units and limits with the counter lead and accountant. Tell us where a rule is wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your real items, customers and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in your customers, balances by site and age, current stock by yard, supplier lists and rates from your sheets, ledgers and billing exports. Train the counter, yard and dispatch teams at their own stations.
- you: Your customer list, outstanding list, opening stock count and the supplier list. One hour with each role.
- get: Your customers, balances and stock inside Verity and a team that has run a real week of orders through it.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first month with you, tune credit limits and reorder points, and handle changes when you add an item, a yard or a truck.
- you: A short monthly review of what the numbers say.
- get: A system that keeps matching how the business runs, with someone responsible for it.

- bring: Your contractor and customer ledger with sites; A month of challans and invoices; Item list with units, current rates and supplier rates; Opening stock by yard; Supplier list with usual lead times
- time: About 9 to 12 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. Your counter lead and accountant need a few more.
- after: Nobody is hired to run Verity. Orders, challans and receipts are recorded as part of work the team already does, and the reports come from those records.

## Automations

Heading: The follow-ups nobody has time to make.
Lede: Set once, then they run from your records. Your staff still talk to customers, drivers and mills; Verity makes sure the right person knows what to do.

- when A new order would take a customer over their limit: Order held; Owner sees the balance, age and last promise; Decision recorded on the order
- when A delivery has no signed receipt after three days: Added to the unsigned list; Task to the dispatcher to collect the signature; Closed when the receipt is recorded
- when A purchase rate moves beyond your limit: Items and open quotes below margin listed; Task to the counter lead to reprice; New rate and approver recorded
- when A promise to pay date passes unpaid: Account flag raised; Call task to the person who took the promise; Outcome recorded: paid, new date or refused
- when A stock item falls below its reorder point: Checked against recent dispatch; Purchase order drafted with the usual lead time; Stock updated when the load is received
- when A supplier load arrives short or damaged: Quantity and photos recorded; Claim task to the supplier; That part of the invoice held until settled

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a building material business has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.
Note: Tax rates, thresholds and state rules change. Confirm current requirements with your accountant and the official notifications.

- **GST rates and invoices**: what: GST rates on building materials change from time to time. For example, cement moved from 28 to 18 percent from 22 September 2025, and invoices issued on or after the change date have to show the new rate. | how: Rate changes are tasks that list the quotes and orders affected, and invoices raised by your billing system or accountant are matched to the orders and challans in Verity. (Tally Solutions, GST on cement: https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-on-cement-tax-rate-applicability/)
- **E-way bill for moving goods**: what: An e-way bill is generally needed when goods worth more than Rs 50,000 move, and states set their own limits for movement within the state. A delivery challan does not by itself remove the need. | how: Each dispatch shows its value against the limit you set for your state, and the e-way bill number is stored on the delivery. (Refrens, E-way bill threshold: https://refrens.com/grow/e-way-bill-threshold)
- **Payments to micro and small suppliers**: what: Under Section 43B(h), a buyer generally loses the tax deduction for amounts owed to micro and small suppliers beyond the time limit until paid, which is 45 days with a written agreement or 15 days without one. Pure traders are generally outside the rule. From 1 April 2026 the corresponding provision is Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025. | how: Each supplier is marked with its type, and invoices show age against the limit so accounts can pay on time. (Busy, Section 43B(h) explained: https://busy.in/tds/section-43bh-msme-payment-rule-and-45-day-limit-explained/)
- **Local trade licence and weights**: what: Shops and Establishments registration, trade licences and rules for weights and measures differ by city and state. | how: Each licence is a record with a renewal date and an owner, so a renewal becomes a task weeks before it lapses.

## Roles

Heading: Everyone sees the same records, through their own window.
Lede: A driver should not see contractor balances. The counter should not edit supplier rates. Each role opens on what it needs.

- Owner: Who owes me, what is my stock worth and where is the margin going? | Credit by age, sales, stock by yard, rate changes, quotes under margin and promises due.
- Counter lead: What is pending today? | Orders to raise, rates to confirm, held orders, special rates and returns.
- Dispatcher or driver: What goes where and what must be signed? | Today's loads, site addresses, quantities, challans and receipts to collect.
- Yard supervisor: What do I have and what is coming? | Stock by yard, incoming loads to count, transfers and count tasks.
- Accountant: What was sold, bought and owed? | Invoices matched to challans, supplier dues and age, credit notes and e-way bill numbers.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions building material suppliers ask.

- **Does Verity replace my billing software?** No. Your billing software keeps raising invoices. We agree during setup how invoices and receipts reach Verity, which then does the order, delivery, credit, stock and follow-up work around them.
- **My counter staff are not good with computers. Will they use it?** Orders and challans are short forms with the customer, items and quantity. We train at the counter during a real day, and the screens use your own item names and units.
- **Can it handle bags, tonnes, boxes and cubic metres together?** Yes. Each item has its own unit and the conversions you need, for example bags to tonnes, set up once.
- **Can it stop me giving more credit than I should?** It holds an order that goes over the limit or from an account with an overdue balance, and shows you the history. You decide whether to release it.
- **Do you send payment reminders to my contractors?** No. Your staff call and message. Verity creates the task, shows the balance and the last promise, and records what happened.
- **I have two yards and a godown. Can I see all stock?** Yes. Each is a location in one system, with stock side by side. A move between them is a recorded transfer.
- **Does it make e-way bills?** No. Your accountant or billing system does. Verity shows loads above your limit before the vehicle leaves and stores the bill number on the delivery.
- **How do you bring in what contractors already owe?** From your ledger or sheet. We load balances by customer, site and age, then check a sample with your accountant before go-live.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. A driver sees loads, the counter sees orders and rates, and the owner and accountant see credit. Every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of yards, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your business runs.
