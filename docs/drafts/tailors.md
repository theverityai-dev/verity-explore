## How it runs today

**Tailoring shops run on memory. A good master remembers every customer. A busy week breaks that.**

- A customer's measurements are on a slip, in a register or in the master's head. A returning customer is measured again because the old slip cannot be found.
- The shop works in bursts. In the weeks before festivals and weddings, orders pile up faster than the karigars can finish them, and promised dates are given by hope.
- Work is shared across cutting, stitching, finishing and sometimes outside embroidery. Payment to karigars is by piece, tracked in a notebook, and disputes are settled by memory.
- runs on: Order slip book; Measurement register; WhatsApp with customers and karigars; Cash book; Fabric and trim cupboard
- stack note: Each tells you one thing. Nothing tells you which orders are at risk this week or what each karigar is owed.

## Pains

Heading: Where a tailoring shop loses time, money and customers.
Lede: These are the situations tailors and boutique owners describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Measurements are lost or out of date
- area: Orders
- scene: A regular customer returns after a year. The old slip cannot be found, so she is measured again, and the fit is slightly different from last time.
- cost: Time at the counter, a garment that does not fit like her favourite one and a customer who starts looking elsewhere.
- fix: Each customer has a record with her measurements, date taken and notes on posture and preferences. A returning customer starts from the last record and changes are marked.
- screen: order

### 2. Order details are said, not written
- area: Orders
- scene: The customer asks for a deeper neck, a longer sleeve and piping on the border. The counter says "noted". The karigar gets a slip that says "blouse, red".
- cost: A garment that is wrong, a redo at the shop's cost and a customer who will not accept the explanation.
- fix: Each garment order records style, fabric, lining, trims and every instruction, with a photo of the reference if available. The karigar sees the same record the counter wrote.
- screen: order

### 3. The festival and wedding rush overbooks the shop
- area: Capacity
- scene: Three weeks before a festival, the counter accepts every order and promises "by the 28th". By the 25th, thirty garments are unfinished.
- cost: Missed deadlines, disappointed customers and overtime pay. Tailoring is seasonal, with work clustered around festivals and wedding months, so the same problem returns every year.
- fix: Each day has a capacity set by karigars and garment type. Verity shows how booked each date is, and a new order checks the date against it before a promise is made.
- screen: board
- source: (Zerodha Daily Brief, 24 Dec 2025: https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/the-stitch-that-doesnt-hold)

### 4. Nobody can say where a garment is
- area: Work
- scene: A blouse is with the cutter, then the karigar, then waiting for hooks. A customer comes at 5 pm and nobody can find it.
- cost: Hours spent searching, a late delivery and a customer who feels ignored.
- fix: Each garment moves through cutting, stitching, trial, finishing and ready. Everyone sees its stage and who has it, and the counter answers from the record.
- screen: board

### 5. Karigar pay is counted by hand and argued about
- area: People
- scene: Karigars are paid per piece. At the end of the week the master adds slips from a notebook, subtracts advances and gets a total that someone disputes.
- cost: Hours of the master's time, lost trust and good karigars who leave for a shop that pays on time.
- fix: Each piece done is recorded against the karigar and its rate. Weekly pay is calculated from the records with advances taken out, and the karigar can see the same statement.
- screen: karigars

### 6. Trials and alterations are not recorded
- area: Work
- scene: A customer tries a blouse and asks for changes. The alteration goes to a karigar who is busy. At the second trial the same fault is still there.
- cost: Rework that nobody counts, delays and annoyed customers. A shop that does not record alterations cannot tell which karigar or which style causes them.
- fix: Each trial is a record with what was found, who altered it and the new trial date. Verity shows alterations by karigar and by style.
- screen: board

### 7. Customer fabric and shop fabric get mixed up
- area: Stock
- scene: A customer leaves three metres of silk. Another buys fabric from the shop. Leftover pieces sit on the shelf and nobody can say whose they are.
- cost: A dispute over missing fabric, a customer's silk used for the wrong garment or leftover cloth with no value attached.
- fix: Fabric received from a customer is recorded with a photo and quantity against the order. Shop fabric is stock with a cost. Leftover pieces are recorded and returned or priced.
- screen: stock

### 8. Cutting mistakes and fabric shortfalls
- area: Stock
- scene: The master estimates yardage by eye. A blouse is cut and the fabric falls short by a hand.
- cost: Spoiled fabric, a replacement at your cost and a delayed order.
- fix: Each style has a usual fabric requirement by size, and the order checks the fabric received against it. Verity records shortfalls and wastage per cutter and style.
- screen: stock

### 9. Trims and lining run out at the wrong time
- area: Stock
- scene: Buttons, hooks, zips and lining are in a cupboard. The one colour you need today is not there, so the garment waits.
- cost: Delays, trips to the market in the middle of the day and a rushed purchase at a higher price.
- fix: Trims and lining are stock with reorder points. Verity checks the week's orders against what is on hand and drafts a purchase list.
- screen: stock

### 10. Advances are taken and balances are not chased
- area: Money
- scene: Customers pay an advance and the balance on pickup. Some garments are ready for weeks and not collected, with the balance unpaid.
- cost: Garments occupying space, money locked in unclaimed orders and a shop that does not know how much it is owed.
- fix: Each order shows advance, balance and ready date. Ready garments not collected after a set period appear on a list with a call task, and the outcome is recorded.
- screen: dues

### 11. Prices change from person to person
- area: Money
- scene: A similar blouse is quoted at ₹800 by the master and ₹650 by the counter. A regular negotiates a discount that nobody writes down.
- cost: Margins that depend on who is at the counter, and customers who notice the differences.
- fix: Common garments have a price list with options for lining, embroidery and urgent delivery. A price below the floor needs an approval, and discounts are recorded with a reason.
- screen: order

### 12. Bulk and uniform orders are tracked in lists on paper
- area: Orders
- scene: A school or a small company orders thirty uniforms in different sizes. Sizes, delivery batches and payments sit on one sheet that is easy to lose.
- cost: Wrong sizes, partial deliveries argued over and payment delays from the institution.
- fix: A bulk order is a record with a list of people, sizes and quantities. Deliveries are logged by batch, and payment milestones are tracked against it.
- screen: board

### 13. Work sent to outside embroiderers and dyers loses track
- area: Work
- scene: A lehenga goes to an outside embroiderer with a promised date. A week later nobody knows whether it has come back.
- cost: A missed wedding date, a customer waiting and a vendor bill that does not match what was sent.
- fix: Each outside job records what was sent, to whom, the promised date and the bill. Verity lists items past their return date and compares bills with the agreed rate.
- screen: karigars

### 14. The master and the owner are the system
- area: Owner
- scene: One person remembers which customers are waiting, which karigar is slow and which fabric is low. A week away means missed deliveries.
- cost: The shop cannot add a second outlet or a bigger team, and the owner cannot step back. Most tailors work alone or in very small units with little training, so knowledge stays in one head.
- fix: Each morning Verity shows what needs a decision, each item with an owner. A second outlet becomes a location in the same system.
- screen: owner

## Rules and paperwork

Note: Rules differ by state and size of business and they change. Confirm current requirements with your accountant and the relevant authority.

- **GST on tailoring and stitching**: what: Stitching a customer's fabric is generally treated as job work and is taxed at a lower rate than design plus stitch work or selling a garment made from your own fabric. - **Workers and wage records**: what: Many tailors work with no contract and no social security. Shops with employees may have to keep wage, attendance and leave records under state shop and establishment rules. | how: Pieces, advances and pay are recorded per karigar, so a wage record exists without a separate notebook. - **Customer property and delivery promises**: what: When a customer leaves fabric, the shop is holding their property and a promised delivery date is part of the service. A missed date or a lost fabric can lead to a complaint. | how: Customer fabric is recorded with a photo and quantity, and the promised date and any change to it are kept on the order.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions tailors and boutique owners ask.

- **I am the master and I do not type much. Can I use it?** Yes. We set it up around the slip you already write, and the people who handle it are the counter and the karigars. We train on live orders and keep it to the fewest taps we can.
- **Will my karigars accept being tracked?** They get something back: a clear list of their work, instructions that match the order and a pay statement they can check. Most disputes come from missing records, and this removes them.
- **Can it keep a customer's measurements for years?** Yes. Each customer has a record with measurements and the date they were taken, so a returning customer starts from the last one.
- **Can it help me plan for a festival?** Yes. Each day has a capacity and the order checks the date against it before a promise is made. You see how booked each date is weeks ahead.
- **Does it replace my billing book?** No. Billing stays as it is. Verity records the order, advance and balance, and we agree during setup how billing details reach Verity.
