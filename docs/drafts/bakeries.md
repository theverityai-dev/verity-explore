## How it runs today

**Most bakeries are good at baking and guess at everything around it.**

- The craft is visible: the smell at 6 am, the full display, a wedding cake delivered on time. What is not visible is the arithmetic behind it. How many tray bakes went unsold last Thursday. Whether the butter you used in September still costs what you priced it at. Which custom cake orders still owe a balance. Which bag of cream expires on Saturday.
- That knowledge sits in separate places: an order diary or WhatsApp chat, a production sheet pinned in the kitchen, the billing machine at the counter, a stock register that is updated when someone has time, and the head baker’s memory. The owner is usually in the kitchen or at the counter, so the thing connecting these is you.
- Bakery margins depend heavily on ingredient cost and on how much of what you make is sold at full price. A price rise in flour, fat or sugar, or a bad guess on Friday’s quantity, goes straight to profit, and it often shows only at month end.
- runs on: Order diary or WhatsApp chats; Bake sheet on the kitchen wall; Billing machine at the counter; Stock register or Excel; Head baker’s memory
- stack note: Each one works for its own job. Nothing joins an order to a bake to an ingredient to a payment.

## Pains

Heading: Where a bakery actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations bakery owners and head bakers describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Custom cake details live in a chat and a diary
- area: Orders
- scene: A birthday cake for Saturday is booked on a call and noted as “chocolate, 1 kg, Aarav”. The customer thought eggless. The message on top, the pickup time and the allergy were in a WhatsApp voice note the decorator never heard.
- cost: Remade cakes, an angry parent on the day, and a refund. A custom cake can take hours of decorating, so a wrong one is hours lost as well as ingredients.
- fix: Each cake order is a record: flavour, weight, eggless or not, message, photo, allergy, pickup or delivery time and balance. The kitchen sees the same sheet the counter took, and a change after booking is tracked with who made it.
- screen: orders

### 2. Advances are taken, balances are chased, cancellations hurt
- area: Orders
- scene: A ₹500 advance on a ₹3,200 cake. The customer cancels the night before, after the sponge is baked. Another collects the cake and promises to pay the balance tomorrow. Nobody writes it down.
- cost: Example arithmetic: ten unpaid balances of ₹1,500 a month is ₹15,000, and a few cancelled custom cakes each month are ingredient cost and time with no sale.
- fix: Advances, balances and cancellation terms are recorded on the order. Verity lists unpaid balances with the customer and the order, and shows cancellations late enough to waste a bake, so you can set advance rules from facts.
- screen: orders

### 3. Festival weeks overbook the oven and the vans
- area: Orders
- scene: Christmas, Diwali, Valentine’s Day and Eid bring a rush of hampers, plum cakes and box orders. Orders are accepted until the phone stops ringing, and only on the 23rd does anyone notice there is no oven time for all of them.
- cost: Orders delivered late or turned away on your best days of the year, and overtime and mistakes when the kitchen is overloaded.
- fix: Each order carries a date, a bake time and a delivery slot. Verity totals the load by day against your oven and van capacity, flags the days that are full and shows what can still be accepted.
- screen: orders

### 4. Trays baked at night, unsold by evening
- area: Bakes
- scene: The head baker bakes 120 buns because that is what Tuesday used to be. Today only 85 sold. The rest are marked down at 7 pm or end up with staff. On Friday the opposite happens and the shelf is empty by noon.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 30 unsold items a day at ₹18 ingredient and labour cost each is about ₹16,000 a month, plus the sales missed when you run out.
- fix: Every bake is recorded by item and time. Each day’s sales and leftovers are compared with the bake, and Verity shows sell-through by item, day and hour so tomorrow’s quantity follows the pattern. Bakers still decide; the sheet shows the evidence.
- screen: production

### 5. Loose items are marked down with no record of by how much
- area: Bakes
- scene: At closing, pastries that did not sell are sold at half price, packed as “day-old” boxes or given away. The register shows the sale, but not what the item would have earned.
- cost: Markdowns turn a margin into a loss on one tray at a time, and without a record you cannot tell if a new item deserves its shelf space.
- fix: Markdowns and day-old sales are recorded as separate lines per item. Verity shows what share of each product was sold at full price, at a discount or not at all.
- screen: production

### 6. Butter, flour and cocoa prices move and the price list does not
- area: Costing
- scene: The butter supplier raises the rate. Cream is dearer before festivals. Maida moves too. The price list was set two years ago, and the signature cake may now sell at a loss.
- cost: In 2020 the Trade Promotion Council of India said input costs for biscuit makers had risen 35 to 40 percent, which shows how fast wheat, fat and sugar can move together. A bakery that does not reprice absorbs it.
- fix: Each product has a recipe with ingredient quantities and a yield. Purchase rates feed in, so Verity shows each product’s current cost and margin and flags the ones that dropped below your target.
- screen: costing
- source: (The Morung Express (IANS), TPCI on biscuit input costs, 2020: https://morungexpress.com/higher-input-cost-bites-into-biscuits-manufactures-profits-tpci)

### 7. Batch yields drift and nobody weighs the dough
- area: Costing
- scene: A 25 kg flour batch should give 180 loaves. This week it gave 168. A new baker scales differently, the proofing room is warmer, and the mixer was refilled by hand.
- cost: Twelve loaves a batch, every batch, is a sale lost before it reaches the counter. It is also invisible, because the flour that went in looks the same.
- fix: Each batch records ingredients in and units out. Verity compares the actual yield with the recipe’s, per baker and per product, so a drifting batch is spotted in days rather than months.
- screen: costing

### 8. Cream, eggs and fresh fillings expire in the fridge
- area: Stock
- scene: Cream bought for a wedding order is left over. Paneer fillings, custard and fresh fruit have days, not weeks. Nobody checks dates until something smells wrong or a batch has to be thrown.
- cost: Perishable ingredients are bought, paid for and lost, and a bad batch of filling can mean a recall of a day’s cakes.
- fix: Received lots have a date. Verity lists what expires in the next few days, suggests using the oldest first and shows how much perishable stock is on hand against what the next days’ orders need.
- screen: stock

### 9. Running out of eggs or packaging in festival week
- area: Stock
- scene: Cake boxes, cake boards, gift ribbons and egg trays are counted by looking. The box supplier needs two days. You run out on the 24th of December.
- cost: Orders delayed or packed badly, and rush purchases at higher prices from a nearby shop.
- fix: Every ingredient and pack item has a reorder point. Verity totals what confirmed orders and the usual walk-in sales need for the coming days and drafts the purchase with lead time in mind.
- screen: stock

### 10. Hotel, café and shop accounts run on credit and memory
- area: Accounts
- scene: You supply buns to three cafés and a hotel on credit, delivered each morning. Who has paid what, which delivery was short and what is overdue is in a notebook the delivery boy updates.
- cost: Bulk accounts are the steadiest income a bakery has, and also the place where money gets stuck for months with no one chasing it politely.
- fix: Each account has standing orders, delivery records and a running balance. Verity lists overdue balances with the customer and last payment, and assigns the follow-up to the person who knows the account.
- screen: accounts

### 11. A central kitchen sends goods to outlets and no one reconciles
- area: Outlets
- scene: The kitchen dispatches 400 items to three outlets. Outlet A says it received 380. Outlet C ran out by noon and nobody told the kitchen. Returns are collected, but not counted.
- cost: Items lost between the oven and the shelf, and the wrong quantities baked for the wrong outlet the next day.
- fix: Each dispatch is a transfer with items and counts, received and signed at the outlet. Verity shows sent, received, sold and returned per outlet, and the gap on each.
- screen: accounts

### 12. Skills sit in two or three people’s hands
- area: People
- scene: One decorator does all fondant cakes. One baker knows the sourdough. When either is on leave or resigns, an entire line of products stops, and the replacement learns from scratch on the busiest week.
- cost: A single resignation can take away the product that earns the most, and covering a night shift at short notice costs overtime.
- fix: Recipes and procedures are kept as records, and each person’s skills and sign-offs are recorded. Verity shows who can cover which product, and a shift swap is visible before it is needed.
- screen: upkeep

### 13. Oven, mixer or chiller failure at 3 am
- area: Equipment
- scene: The deck oven runs uneven on one side for weeks. The walk-in chiller’s door seal is torn. The planetary mixer stops at the start of a batch.
- cost: A night’s production lost, orders late, and an emergency service call at a premium. A chiller failure can also spoil a week of cream and butter.
- fix: Ovens, mixers, proofers and chillers are asset records with service dates and the vendor’s details. Verity raises a task before each service falls due and keeps the history of repairs per machine.
- screen: upkeep

### 14. Labels, licence details and tax rates differ by product
- area: Compliance
- scene: Packed cakes need dates and ingredient lists on the label. A box of mixed items has bread, cake and cookies, and these have not always been taxed the same way. The FSSAI certificate is on the wall; the details are in a drawer.
- cost: A notice from an inspector, wrong tax on a bill your accountant must later correct, and customers with allergies who are not told what is in the cake.
- fix: Each product record holds its label details, allergens and tax category, and each licence is a record with its dates. The accountant gets a clean split by category, and the labels come from the same record.
- screen: upkeep

### 15. Counter takings, discounts and the day’s bakes never tie
- area: Money
- scene: At closing, the counter has sold 340 items, the kitchen made 380, 25 were marked down and 10 were given to staff. The cash is short by ₹300 and the explanation is “the rush”.
- cost: Small unexplained gaps every day, and an owner who cannot tell stock loss from a billing error.
- fix: Bakes, sales, markdowns, staff items and waste are reconciled by item at day end. A shortfall in cash or count is flagged with the shift, and the closer records the reason.
- screen: owner

### 16. You are the planner, the buyer and the closer
- area: Owner
- scene: You decide the next day’s bake, check the order diary, approve discounts, place purchases and count the till. When you are ill or travelling, quality and numbers both slip.
- cost: The bakery cannot grow past what one person can carry, and a second outlet multiplies the load.
- fix: Each morning Verity shows a short list of what needs a decision: the bake plan, low stock, orders needing action and unpaid balances, each with an owner, so a manager can run it on days you are away.
- screen: owner

## How we work

Heading: Four weeks from your first call to a working bakery system.
Lede: You do not receive a login and a manual. We spend a night shift and a counter day with you, build Verity around your products and orders, bring your data in, and stay to keep it working.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Join a night bake and a counter morning. Follow an order from booking to pickup, see how stock arrives and how the day is closed, and list the leaks that cost most.
- you: Two to three hours of yours and your head baker’s. A few weeks of bake sheets, your order diary and recent supplier bills.
- get: A written map of your bakery: products and recipes, the order flow, ingredient list, outlets and accounts, and the first problems we will close.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your products with recipes and yields, ingredient units, reorder points, order stages, advance and cancellation rules, outlets and bulk accounts, and build the seven screens in your own terms.

- get: A working Verity with your real products, outlets and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in your customer and account lists, open orders, stock and recipes from diaries and sheets. Train the kitchen at its stations and the counter during a real shift.

- get: Your orders, accounts and ingredients inside Verity, and a team that has run a day on it.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Remain involved after launch. Review the first bake plans and leftover numbers with you, adjust the rules, and add products, outlets or festival plans as they come.

- get: A system that keeps pace with your menu and calendar, with a named person responsible for it.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a bakery has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules lean on in order, so your accountant is not rebuilding them from a diary.
Note: Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

- **FSSAI registration or licence and hygiene**: what: A bakery needs FSSAI registration or a licence for its category. From 1 April 2026 basic registration generally applies up to ₹1.5 crore of turnover, and licences generally no longer need yearly renewal. Hygiene standards for bakeries continue to apply. | how: The registration or licence is stored with its category and dates, and the daily hygiene checks are tasks signed off in the kitchen. (SetIndiaBiz summary of FSSAI 2026 changes: https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/fssai-2026-reforms)
- **GST by product category**: what: From 22 September 2025, pastries, cakes and biscuits generally moved from 18% to 5%, and many Indian breads to nil. Rates for items such as rusks and some packed goods can differ, so confirm each item with your accountant. | how: Each product record carries its tax category, so the monthly split by rate is already in the sales data your accountant receives. (ClearTax, GST on cakes and pastries: https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-cakes-pastries)
- **Labels, dates and allergens on packed goods**: what: Packed food generally needs a date of manufacture or packing, a use-by or expiry date and an ingredient list, with allergens declared. FSSAI has also notified labelling amendments for 2026 and 2027, so check the current rule for your product. | how: Label details and allergens are kept on the product record, and the shelf life drives the use-by date shown.
- **Local trade and shop rules**: what: Municipal trade or health licences and Shops and Establishments registration generally apply and differ by city and state. | how: Each is stored with a renewal date and an owner, and a renewal becomes a task in good time.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions bakery owners ask.

- **My bakers start at 4 am. Will they really use a screen?** Only if it saves them effort. They see the bake plan and cake details on one screen, and recording a bake and leftovers takes a few taps. We train at their station, during a night shift.
- **Does Verity take cake orders from customers online?** No. Customers keep ordering by call, message or your usual channel. Your counter enters the order, and from then on Verity tracks it through the kitchen to pickup and payment.
- **Can it tell me how much to bake?** It shows how each item sold and was left over, by day and hour, and suggests a quantity. Your head baker decides, because they know about weather, holidays and local events that numbers miss.
- **We make some things in-house and buy others. Can both be tracked?** Yes. Made items have recipes and ingredient use. Bought items are tracked as stock with a purchase rate. Both show margin and sell-through the same way.
- **I only have one shop with a small kitchen. Is this too big?** A single-shop bakery has the same leftovers and price swings. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, with fewer screens in daily use.
- **Can I see my outlets and central kitchen together?** Yes. Each outlet and the kitchen is a location. You see what was sent, received, sold and returned, and the gap, per outlet and per item.
- **Who can see my recipes and costs?** Only the people you allow. Recipes and costs can be limited to you and your head baker, and the counter sees only what it sells. Every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How soon will it be useful?** Order tracking, unpaid balances and expiry lists need only your existing orders and stock, so we aim to have them first. Bake advice becomes reliable after a few weeks of recorded sales and leftovers.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of outlets, people and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your bakery runs.
