## How it runs today

**A skin clinic is a long relationship told in short visits.**

- Most dermatology patients are not one-visit patients. Acne is reviewed every month or two. Pigmentation, hair loss and psoriasis can take months of follow-up. Aesthetic work such as laser hair reduction, peels and microneedling is sold as a course. The income is in the second, third and sixth visit, not the first.
- The detail needed for each visit is scattered. The prescription is in the doctor’s system or on a pad. Photographs are on a clinic phone or the assistant’s own. The consent for a procedure is on paper. The session count is on a card the patient carries. Injectable vials have batch numbers on a box that was thrown away. Staff who run the laser know what happened better than anyone, and they may leave.
- The result is not a single crisis. It is patients who disappear half-way through a course, photographs nobody can find when a patient asks, stock that expires in the fridge, and a clinic owner who cannot see which room or provider is earning.
- runs on: Prescription software or pad; Photos on a phone; Paper consent forms; Session cards and a register; WhatsApp with patients
- stack note: Each one holds a piece of the patient. Nothing tells you which course is unfinished or which vial belongs to whom.

## Pains

Heading: Where a skin clinic loses patients, money and records.
Lede: These are situations dermatologists and clinic managers describe. For each: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it. Verity records what your team decides and never advises on treatment.

### 1. Follow-up patients stop coming after the first improvement
- area: Patients
- scene: A teenager’s acne improves in six weeks and the parent decides they are done. A woman with pigmentation stops after the first review. Nobody calls, because nobody has a list of who was meant to return.
- cost: Chronic skin conditions need repeat visits, and patients often stop once things look better. The clinic loses the revenue of the visits that were planned, and the patient may relapse. Example arithmetic: 57 overdue reviews at ₹800 a consult is about ₹45,000 of visits that should have happened.
- fix: The doctor records a review date with each consult. Verity lists patients past their review date by condition and doctor, and assigns a call task to the front desk. The outcome, whether booked, declined or unreachable, is recorded.
- screen: followups

### 2. Enquiries from Instagram and ads are never followed up properly
- area: Patients
- scene: A reel brings forty enquiries about hair removal packages. They go to a phone that three people check. Some get a reply the same hour, some never. Nobody knows which enquiry became a patient.
- cost: Money spent on advertising, and the cost of a lead that was never called. Without a record you cannot tell which source is worth paying for.
- fix: Each enquiry is a record with its source and an owner. Verity shows enquiries with no reply, how long they have waited and which turned into a first visit, by source.
- screen: followups

### 3. Laser and peel courses end with sessions unused
- area: Courses and sessions
- scene: A patient buys six hair-reduction sessions, comes for three, and then travels for work. The card is lost. Six months later she is back, argues about how many remain, and the register has a different number.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 40 unfinished courses with two sessions left at ₹3,500 a session is ₹2.8 lakh of work owed to patients, and a liability and a dispute waiting to happen.
- fix: A course is a record with sessions bought, sessions done, spacing and an end date. Verity reduces the count at every visit, shows balances that are about to lapse and tasks the desk to rebook while the patient is still interested.
- screen: packages

### 4. Discounts, free add-ons and extensions are granted verbally
- area: Courses and sessions
- scene: A regular is promised a free peel. A manager adds two extra laser sessions to settle a complaint. A staff member gives 20 percent off to a friend. Each one was a small decision made at the desk.
- cost: Thin margins on aesthetic work disappear in small concessions, and the owner finds out at month end.
- fix: Anything above a limit you set waits for one-tap approval with a reason. The decision is recorded against the patient’s course so it can be reviewed later.
- screen: packages

### 5. Before and after photos live on personal phones
- area: Records
- scene: The assistant photographs a scar before a procedure and keeps the picture in her gallery. She changes her phone, resigns or shares one in a group. A patient later asks for her photographs.
- cost: Clinical images are sensitive health data. Loose copies create a privacy risk and a gap in the record when a result is questioned.
- fix: Photographs are attached to the patient record and the procedure, with who added them and when. Viewing and sharing follow role permissions, and every access is logged.
- screen: patient

### 6. Consent, patch tests and allergies are on paper or in memory
- area: Records
- scene: A patient with a past reaction to a peeling agent sees a different assistant this time. The earlier reaction is in a folder. The consent for the new laser course was signed, but where?
- cost: A repeat reaction is a patient-safety problem, and a missing consent is a record problem if anything is later disputed.
- fix: Allergies, patch-test results, reactions and signed consents sit on the patient record and show on the screen whoever opens it. Staff cannot close a procedure without the consent marked as on file.
- screen: patient

### 7. Injectables and creams are tracked by memory
- area: Stock
- scene: Botulinum toxin, fillers and vitamin vials are kept in a small fridge. The count is in an assistant’s head, expiry dates are on boxes, and the batch used on a particular patient is not written anywhere.
- cost: Expired vials are thrown away at the clinic’s cost. If a batch is ever flagged, you cannot say which patients received it.
- fix: Each vial or pack is received with batch, expiry and cost. Every procedure records the product and batch used, so one search shows where a batch went and what remains in the fridge.
- screen: stock

### 8. Injectable treatments now face tighter rules and checks
- area: Stock
- scene: In 2026 the drug regulator said cosmetic products are not permitted to be administered by injection. Clinics offering glutathione drips, skin boosters and similar are unsure what they can still stock and promote.
- cost: Selling or advertising a product the wrong way can bring inspections, seizure of stock and action against the doctor. The details are still being worked through by clinics and advisers.
- fix: Each product carries a type, source and approval note that your doctor sets. Verity lists products in stock by that note so questions can be answered quickly, and keeps the batch trail for any item that must be traced. It does not judge what is lawful.
- screen: stock
- source: (Medical Dialogues on the CDSCO notice, May 2026: https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/cdsco/beauty-through-syringes-no-longer-cosmetic-glutathione-drips-injectable-aesthetic-procedures-under-cdsco-lens-171408)

### 9. Lasers and devices break down, or run past their service date
- area: Stock
- scene: The laser’s handpiece reaches the end of its shot count without anyone noticing. The cooling unit fails on a Saturday with six patients booked.
- cost: A day of cancelled procedures, and expensive emergency repair. Heavy daily use makes a missed service more likely.
- fix: Each device is a record with service dates, vendor and usage per procedure. Verity warns before service is due and shows which devices earn their cost and which sit unused.
- screen: stock

### 10. Skincare and prescription products sold at the desk do not tally
- area: Stock
- scene: Sunscreens, moisturisers and doctor-recommended products sell from a shelf. Some are billed, some are gifted to a patient. The monthly count never matches.
- cost: Retail is a useful margin line for a skin clinic and the least tracked. Shrinkage hides in it.
- fix: Product sales are recorded against the patient and deducted from stock as they happen. A monthly count shows exactly which items do not match.
- screen: stock

### 11. Procedure rooms and doctor time are not matched
- area: Chairs and time
- scene: The laser room has a gap on Tuesday and a wait on Saturday. The doctor is stuck in review consults while a procedure patient waits. Nobody can see which slot type is overbooked.
- cost: Idle devices and idle hours cost the same as busy ones. Long waits cost patients.
- fix: Visits are recorded by type, room, device and doctor. Verity shows use by room and hour, the average length of each visit type against the slot booked, and the no-shows by patient.
- screen: owner

### 12. Therapists and assistants carry the patient relationship, and can take it away
- area: People
- scene: The senior laser therapist knows every patient’s skin and history. She is also paid on procedures she performs. When she leaves, many patients follow her to the new clinic.
- cost: A therapist’s exit can take months of recurring revenue with it. Incentives worked out by hand also go wrong.
- fix: Every procedure is recorded against the provider, and the patient’s history belongs to the clinic. Verity builds an incentive statement from your slabs and prepares a handover list the day someone resigns.
- screen: staff

### 13. Cash, card and UPI do not match the procedures done
- area: Money
- scene: At closing the desk counts cash, card slips and UPI against the procedure list. The total is off by ₹1,800. Was a session billed, discounted or forgotten?
- cost: Small daily gaps add up, and the owner stops trusting the numbers on the page.
- fix: Every procedure is a record with an amount and a payment mode. Verity compares the day’s procedures with the day’s payments and shows the exact visit that does not tie.
- screen: owner

### 14. A second clinic runs on nightly messages
- area: Owner
- scene: The second clinic’s manager sends a figure each night. Patients who visit both appear in two registers. A product runs out at one site while it sits unused at the other.
- cost: No fair comparison of clinics, weeks of delay before a problem surfaces, and stock that cannot be moved with a record.
- fix: Clinics are locations in one system, with a patient shown once. Revenue, utilisation, follow-up rates and stock compare side by side, and a stock transfer is recorded when it moves.
- screen: owner

- we: Sit with you and your clinic manager for a day. Follow a patient from the enquiry to the consult room, the laser room and the desk, and see how courses, photos, stock and fees are recorded today.

- we: Set up your procedures, course types and spacing, review intervals, product list with batch and expiry fields, devices and service dates, approval limits, provider incentive slabs and your clinics.

- get: A working Verity with your real procedures, rooms and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in patient lists, running courses with sessions left, product stock with batches and dues from your registers and sheets. Train doctors, therapists and the desk at their own stations.
- you: Your patient list and any history you have, in whatever form it exists. One hour per role.
- get: Your patients, courses and stock inside Verity, and a team that has used it on real visits.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first weeks with you, tune follow-up lists and alerts, and handle changes when you add a doctor, a device or another clinic.
- you: A short monthly review of the numbers.
- get: A system that keeps matching how the clinic runs, with someone accountable for it.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions dermatologists ask.

- **Does Verity give clinical or treatment advice?** No. Your doctors decide diagnosis, prescriptions and procedures. Verity records what they decide, tracks courses and reviews, and tells your team what is waiting.
- **We already use clinical software for prescriptions. Do we replace it?** You do not have to. If your doctors prefer it, keep it. We agree during setup how the information Verity needs, such as the review date and procedures done, reaches it.
- **Where do patient photographs go?** They attach to the patient record and the procedure, visible only to the roles you allow. We agree during setup whether images are held in Verity or stay in another tool with a link on the record.
- **Can it send reminders to patients for reviews and sessions?** Not by itself. It builds the list and assigns the call or message to a named person. They send it, and the outcome is recorded.
- **Can it track courses where sessions are spaced weeks apart?** Yes. Each course type has its own number of sessions and spacing. Verity shows when the next one is due and who is overdue.
- **Is it suitable for a single-doctor clinic?** A one-doctor clinic has the same lost follow-ups and unused sessions, with less time to notice them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks.
- **Can I see two clinics in one view?** Yes. Each clinic is a location in one system and a patient seen at both is one person. Revenue, room use and stock compare side by side.
- **Who can see patient data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so the desk, doctors, therapists and your accountant each see what they need. Every view and change is logged with person and time.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of clinics, providers and what you want set up. Message us and after a short conversation about how your clinic runs, we will give you a clear number.
