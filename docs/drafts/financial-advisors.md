## How it runs today

**Most practices know their clients well and track them badly.**

- The advisor remembers the big things: who has a daughter in college, who retires in three years, who panicked in the last fall. What is not remembered is the dull list: whose annual review is overdue, whose SIP bounced, whose nominee is blank, whose risk profile is two years old.
- Those details live in the distributor or adviser back-office platform, a spreadsheet, a phone and a notebook. Client conversations happen on calls and WhatsApp. A review leaves behind a feeling that it went well and little else.
- The market behind the practice is large and moving. AMFI data reported for September 2026 shows record monthly SIP inflows of about ₹32,490 crore, while 49.68 lakh SIPs were discontinued or completed in the same month. Every client who stops is a conversation the advisor either had or missed.
- runs on: The distributor or adviser back-office platform; Excel sheet of clients and reviews; WhatsApp and phone with clients; Email for statements and documents; A paper or digital register for regulatory records
- stack note: Each one is correct for its piece. None tell you which client needs a call this week, or whether the file would stand up if the regulator asked.

## Pains

Heading: Where an advisory practice actually slips.
Lede: These are the situations advisors and their service staff describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Annual and half-yearly reviews that depend on memory
- area: Clients
- scene: The advisor promised the Mehta family a review before Diwali. It is the 16th of October and nobody has set a date. Another client's last review was in April last year.
- cost: A client who is not reviewed starts to feel like a number, and a client who feels like a number looks around. The firm also loses the chance to find new needs that lead to more business.
- fix: Each client has a review cycle based on the service you offer. Verity lists reviews due in the next 30 days, who is responsible, and those already late. The advisor holds the meeting; Verity creates the task, the agenda from the record and the follow-up.
- screen: clients

### 2. Client goals buried in old notes
- area: Clients
- scene: A client mentioned a plan to fund a child's overseas education in 2029. The note is in a notebook, and the portfolio has been reviewed twice since without mentioning it.
- cost: Advice that ignores the real goal, and a client who has to repeat themselves.
- fix: Goals are records on the client or household with an amount, a date, a priority and the investments linked to them. Review tasks show the goals due soon, so every meeting starts from what the client said last time.
- screen: clients

### 3. Families treated as separate files
- area: Clients
- scene: A husband, a wife, two parents and a daughter's first investment are five separate files. Nobody sees that together they are the practice's third largest relationship.
- cost: Service is uneven, opportunities are missed, and an adult child leaves the practice when the parent retires.
- fix: Clients are linked into households. Verity shows the combined relationship, the people in it and their goals, and the next contact is planned for the household, not for each file.
- screen: clients

### 4. SIPs that stop and nobody notices
- area: SIPs
- scene: A client's SIP bounced twice because of a bank change. Another cancelled during a market fall. Neither was flagged until the next review.
- cost: A stopped SIP is a goal that has stopped being funded. AMFI data for September 2026 shows 49.68 lakh SIPs discontinued or completed in a single month, against 63.26 lakh registered, so lapses are routine, and the advisor who calls first keeps the client.
- fix: SIP instructions are recorded on the client with amount, date, scheme and status. When a SIP bounces, stops or ends, Verity creates a task for the responsible person with the reason where known. The call is made by your team.
- screen: sip
- source: (IANS via Telangana Today, AMFI data for September 2026: https://telanganatoday.com/sip-stoppage-ratio-falls-to-78-5pc-in-september-as-inflows-hit-record-high)

### 5. Step-ups, maturities and renewals tracked by sheet
- area: SIPs
- scene: A fixed deposit matures on the 28th. A child plan completes next March. A SIP step-up was promised at the last review and never applied.
- cost: Money that sits idle in a savings account for weeks, and promises that are quietly dropped.
- fix: Maturities, step-ups and completion dates are tracked on the client with reminders at your set intervals. The advisor sees what is coming for the next 60 days and calls before the money lands.
- screen: sip

### 6. Risk profile and suitability not on file when advice is given
- area: Records
- scene: A client was advised on a portfolio switch by phone. The risk profile on file is three years old and the reason for the switch is a line in a WhatsApp chat.
- cost: Under the SEBI Investment Advisers Regulations, registered advisers generally have to assess a client's risk profile and the suitability of advice, and keep records. A weak file is a regulatory risk and a poor defence against a later complaint.
- fix: The risk profile, its date, the questionnaire answers and the rationale for each recommendation are records on the client. Verity flags profiles older than your set limit and advice without a linked rationale, so the file is complete before the meeting, not after.
- screen: suitability
- source: (SEBI IA regulations as summarised in a SEBI order; details not verified here. See Rules section)

### 7. Client agreements and consent not properly recorded
- area: Records
- scene: A client agreed to advisory terms over a call and a "yes" on WhatsApp. The signed agreement was never collected.
- cost: SEBI's recent changes to the Investment Advisers framework say consent must be obtained through a legally acceptable mode, and that a simple tick on a website is not enough. A missing agreement weakens both the fee and the defence.
- fix: Each client has an agreement record with its type, date, mode of consent, fee basis and renewal date. Verity shows clients with no agreement or an expired one.
- screen: suitability
- source: (AZB & Partners, 2025: https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/overhaul-of-the-regulatory-framework-for-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts/)

### 8. KYC, nominee and contact details gone stale
- area: Records
- scene: A widow's folio still lists her late husband's phone number. A client moved cities and the address on record is five years old. Several folios have no nominee.
- cost: Statements and alerts go to the wrong person, and claims take longer when the time comes. The advisor looks careless at the worst moment.
- fix: KYC status, nominee and contact details are fields on the client. Verity lists clients with a missing nominee or an old contact detail and creates a service task for each.
- screen: suitability

### 9. Fee billing and trail income tracked by hand
- area: Money
- scene: An advisory client pays a fixed annual fee in two instalments. Trail income from fund houses arrives monthly in different statements. The practice cannot say which clients make money.
- cost: Hours go to clients who pay little, and fee reminders are forgotten. SEBI amended the cap on fixed fees for individual advisory clients to ₹1.51 lakh a year in December 2024, so the fee basis must be recorded with care.
- fix: Fee terms and invoice dates are recorded on each client. Verity shows fees due, fees overdue and, where you record it, income per client against the time spent, so the practice can see where it earns. Your accountant handles billing and tax.
- screen: owner
- source: (AZB & Partners, 2025: https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/overhaul-of-the-regulatory-framework-for-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts/)

### 10. Prospects and referrals that cool off
- area: Growth
- scene: A client's brother asks for an introduction. The advisor says "next week". Three weeks later nobody has called.
- cost: Referrals are the cheapest and best source of new clients and they cool fast.
- fix: Each prospect is a record with a source, an owner, a next step and a date. Verity shows prospects with no contact in your set number of days and tracks the stage from first call to onboarding.
- screen: onboarding

### 11. Onboarding that takes weeks of paperwork
- area: Onboarding
- scene: A new client needs KYC, a risk profile, an agreement, bank details, nominee forms and a first investment. The documents trickle in over a month, and the first SIP starts two months late.
- cost: Momentum lost, and a new client who starts to wonder whether the practice is organised.
- fix: A standard onboarding checklist creates tasks and shows what is missing. The advisor and the service person each see their steps, with dates, until the client is fully set up.
- screen: onboarding

### 12. Team members who know the client and then leave
- area: People
- scene: The service executive who handled 80 families resigns. The advisor has to rebuild contact preferences, family details and recent conversations.
- cost: Service dips during a handover and clients ask who looks after them now.
- fix: Notes, preferences, tasks and conversation history are on the client. Reassigning clients moves their tasks and history to the new person.
- screen: clients

### 13. Regulatory registers and renewals in different places
- area: Compliance
- scene: The AMFI registration renewal, NISM certificate expiry, the annual compliance audit, the adviser registration renewal and the grievance register each have their own date and person.
- cost: A lapsed registration or certificate can stop the practice from doing business. The SEBI framework also expects an annual compliance audit within six months of year end for advisers.
- fix: Each registration, certificate and audit is a record with an expiry date and an owner. Verity raises tasks well in advance, and the grievance log shows each complaint, its date and its closure.
- screen: compliance
- source: (AZB & Partners, 2025: https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/overhaul-of-the-regulatory-framework-for-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts/)

### 14. Growth beyond the individual adviser limit
- area: Compliance
- scene: An individual adviser has grown to 280 clients and annual fees of ₹2.7 crore. Nobody has checked what happens at the next threshold.
- cost: A surprise change in registration type at the busiest time. A secondary source describing the SEBI Second Amendment of November 2025 says an individual adviser above 300 clients or ₹3 crore in annual fees must apply to become a non-individual adviser within three months.
- fix: Client count and fee income to date are shown on the owner's screen against the limits you set, with a task as you approach one. The advisor decides how to restructure; Verity makes sure the question is raised early.
- screen: owner
- source: (Advocate Gandhi, a secondary summary, 2025; verify against SEBI: https://advocategandhi.com/major-update-alert-what-the-new-sebi-investment-advisers-second-amendment-regulations-2025-mean-for-you/)

### 15. Client data on personal phones and shared folders
- area: Compliance
- scene: PAN, bank statements, account numbers and family details are on a staff phone, in email and on a shared drive with no access control.
- cost: Data leaks in a trust business are serious. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules notified in November 2025 are due to apply to most data handlers about 18 months later.
- fix: Documents and client details have permissions by role and by client, and views and downloads are logged. Closing a leaver's access is one step.
- screen: compliance
- source: (Mondaq, DPDP Rules 2025, 2025: https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified)

### 16. The principal cannot see the whole practice
- area: Owner
- scene: At the end of a quarter, the principal wants to know how many reviews were done, how many SIPs lapsed, which clients are inactive and which records are incomplete. It takes a week of sheets.
- cost: Decisions about hiring, pricing and where to spend time are made without data.
- fix: The owner's screen shows reviews due and overdue, SIPs stopped, missing records, fees due, client count against limits and each team member's load, live from the records.
- screen: owner

## FAQ

Heading: Questions advisors ask.

- **Does Verity give investment advice or recommend funds?** No. It records your clients, goals, instructions and the reasons you recorded for your advice. The advice is yours.
- **Can it place orders or start SIPs?** No. Orders and SIPs are placed on your back-office or fund house platforms. We agree during setup how their data reaches Verity, and Verity tracks what needs attention.
- **Will it connect to my back-office platform?** We agree how data reaches Verity during setup, whether by regular export or another route that suits your platform. We do not promise a specific integration before we have seen your setup.
- **I am a solo advisor with 120 clients. Is this too much?** A solo practice has the same promises and no one else to remember them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.
- **Is this useful if I am only a mutual fund distributor?** Yes. Reviews, SIP follow-ups, nominee and KYC gaps, prospects and your ARN and NISM renewals are all relevant. The advisory-specific records, such as risk profile and agreements, are set up only if you need them.
- **Does it help me meet regulator record requirements?** It keeps the records in one place with dates and gaps flagged. What the rules require of you depends on your registration, so confirm that with your compliance adviser.
- **Can clients see their own records?** Only if you choose and set it up that way. By default, only your team sees client records, by role.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and by client, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How soon will I see something useful?** The reviews due list, stopped SIPs and missing nominee list need only your existing client data, so we aim to have them first. Fee and income views become reliable once fee terms are recorded.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of advisors, clients and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your practice runs.
