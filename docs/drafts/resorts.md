## How it runs today

**Most resorts have a full weekend and no clear picture of the other five days.**

- The weekend is easy to see. The cars in the lot, the cottages booked, the lawn set up for a wedding. What is hard to see is everything behind it: how many covers the package really needs, whether the spa, safari or boat slots are over or under booked, which stock is a long drive from refill, which cottage is waiting for a part, and whether the pollution consent and the fire certificate are in date.
- That information lives in pieces. The booking system knows rooms. The wedding is in a planner's spreadsheet and the sales manager's phone. The kitchen runs on a chit book. The vehicle roster is on a whiteboard. The licence file is at the head office.
- Domestic tourism is large and growing. The RBI recorded 294.8 crore domestic tourist visits in 2024, 17.5 percent more than in 2023. But the number does not fill a given resort on a given Tuesday. A year-end hotel review for 2025 shows leisure markets with mixed results: Goa at 65.3 percent occupancy with its average rate down, and Udaipur at 54 percent market-wide.
- runs on: Booking system or register for rooms; Spreadsheets for weddings and groups; Chit books in the kitchen and bar; WhatsApp groups for staff, drivers and vendors; Head-office file for licences
- stack note: Each one works. None of them know about the others, and the manager is the only connection.

## Pains

Heading: Where a resort actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations resort owners and managers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary week, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Full weekends, empty weekdays
- area: Revenue
- scene: Friday to Sunday is sold out. Monday to Thursday, 26 of 40 cottages sit empty. The weekday rate is a guess, and nobody has tracked which midweek offer ever filled a room.
- cost: Example arithmetic: lifting midweek occupancy from 35 to 45 percent in a 40-cottage resort is four more cottages a night. At ₹6,000 for four nights a week, that is ₹96,000 a week.
- fix: Bookings are recorded by date, channel and package, so Verity shows occupancy and rate for each weekday against the weekend and which offers actually brought guests. The manager sees the thin nights ahead early enough to act. Your team sets the rates and sends the offers.
- screen: owner

### 2. Wedding and group enquiries handled by phone
- area: Sales
- scene: A family wants 60 rooms, a lawn and a three-day programme in February. The enquiry comes through a planner, an Instagram message and a phone call. Three site visits are promised and two are forgotten. The quote lives in a spreadsheet.
- cost: One wedding can be worth more than a month of weekends. Skift reported that one in four Indian weddings is now a destination wedding. Lost to slow replies, it is lost to a competitor.
- fix: Each enquiry is a record with dates, headcount, budget, the planner, site visit and quote. Verity lists enquiries with no reply or no follow-up after the visit, and tells the sales manager. You still speak to the family.
- screen: events
- source: (Skift, India's $72 Billion Wedding Season, 2025: https://skift.com/2025/12/10/indias-72-billion-wedding-season-is-reshaping-travel-again/)

### 3. A three-day wedding run from a spreadsheet
- area: Events
- scene: Mehendi on the lawn, sangeet in the banquet, wedding at dawn, a reception the next night. Each function has its own set-up, caterer, decorator, DJ and rooms. The lawn is promised to a second booking for the same morning.
- cost: A clash or a miss at a wedding is remembered. It costs the balance payment, a discount and the family's referrals.
- fix: Each wedding is an event record with functions by date and venue, headcount, menu, vendors, set-up times, rooms and payments. Verity shows venue clashes across events, and tasks for each department flow from the function sheet.
- screen: events

### 4. Meal plans and package covers that do not match the kitchen
- area: Food
- scene: The package includes breakfast, lunch and dinner. The chef is told 90 guests, 74 turn up for lunch and 120 for dinner because day visitors came. Buffet food is thrown out and a late request runs short.
- cost: Over-cooked buffets are food cost with no sale, and short ones lose guest goodwill. The two come from guessing the covers.
- fix: Expected covers by meal come from the in-house guests, packages and function headcounts. Actual covers are recorded, and Verity compares the two so the kitchen's plan follows the real pattern by day and season.
- screen: supplies

### 5. Activity, spa and safari slots that clash or sit empty
- area: Activities
- scene: A nature walk, a boat ride, a spa session and a jeep safari all need a guide, an instructor or a driver. Two groups are given the same 4 pm slot. A guide called in sick and nobody knows.
- cost: Experiences are what guests remember and what resorts charge extra for. A clash costs the sale and the review.
- fix: Each activity is a schedule with capacity, an assigned guide or therapist, equipment and a state. Verity shows clashes and unstaffed slots, and lists guests who have asked but have not been given a time.
- screen: experiences

### 6. Transfers, pickups and the vehicle whiteboard
- area: Transport
- scene: A guest lands at 3 pm and the resort is 90 minutes from the airport. The pickup is on a WhatsApp message to a driver who has not seen it. The second guest arrives and finds no one.
- cost: A bad first hour colours the whole stay. Idle vehicles are paid for in fuel and wages, and missed pickups are paid for in refunds.
- fix: Each transfer is a task linked to the booking, with flight or train, pickup time, vehicle and driver. Verity shows unassigned transfers by time and which vehicles are free, and records pickups as done.
- screen: experiences

### 7. Stock that is an hour from the shop
- area: Stock
- scene: The chef realises at 6 pm that the fish for Saturday did not come. The nearest market is 40 km away and vendors do not deliver. Linen, pool chemicals and kitchen staples are bought in bulk, so some run out and some expire.
- cost: Vendors often decline to deliver to remote sites because it does not pay them. Stock-outs cost covers or a costly emergency run, and over-buying ties up cash.
- fix: Every item has a reorder point set from real use and the vendor's lead time. Verity checks stock against expected covers and arrivals for the coming days and drafts the order before the last delivery slot.
- screen: supplies
- source: (NP Chandrashekhar, Resort Opening in a Remote Location, 2014, general lessons on remote supply: https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4067248/resort-opening-in-a-remote-location-a-challenging-effort)

### 8. Generators, borewells, pools and the machine that fails on a wedding eve
- area: Upkeep
- scene: The generator is serviced when it fails. The pool pump hums. The water treatment plant is checked by whoever remembers. A cottage's geyser is out for a week because the part is two days away.
- cost: A pool closed or a power cut on a full house costs the stay and the review. Emergency call-outs from far away cost more, and remote outside help can be slow.
- fix: Every machine and utility is an asset record with its service history, next date and spares. Verity raises tasks before the date and blocks a cottage from sale while a fault is open. Spares for vital machines have a minimum level.
- screen: upkeep

### 9. Staff in a remote place: hiring, housing and leaving
- area: People
- scene: Staff are hired for the season and housed on site. A cook leaves in the middle of peak. The new housekeeper has a day's training. Overtime is not tracked and rest days vanish.
- cost: A 2025 accommodation barometer reported about ten hires planned per property over the next year, with housekeeping and food and beverage in most demand. Constant hiring without a record means constant retraining.
- fix: Shifts, rest days, staff housing and leave are one roster. Training and sign-offs sit on each person's record, so the manager sees who is cleared for which station. Seasonal staff get a checklist from day one.
- screen: team
- source: (Booking.com and Statista, 2025 India Accommodation Barometer via HospitalityNet, 2025: https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4130834.html)

### 10. Cottages spread over acres, rooms not ready
- area: Rooms
- scene: Housekeeping covers cottages hundreds of metres apart. The cottage allotted to the first arrival is the last one cleaned. A buggy is not free. The supervisor finds out when the guest is at the gate.
- cost: A late room at a resort is a long wait in the sun with a car full of luggage. Staff then rush work that needs care.
- fix: Each cottage has a status that housekeeping updates from the floor, ordered by arrival time. Verity shows cottages at risk, the attendant and the buggy for each, and puts the first arrival's cottage first.
- screen: stays

### 11. Safety at the pool, on the trek and on the water
- area: Safety
- scene: A guest slips at the pool. A child wanders into the shallows at an hour the lifeguard was not on duty. A boat goes out with one life jacket short. None of it is written down.
- cost: An accident can mean harm to a guest, a legal case and a closed activity. Without a record you cannot show you did your checks.
- fix: Safety checks, lifeguard shifts, equipment counts and guest waivers are records with a name and a time. An incident is logged with the outcome. Verity lists checks not done before an activity starts.
- screen: experiences

### 12. Consents, licences and the environment rules for a resort
- area: Compliance
- scene: A resort needs pollution board consent, a groundwater permission, fire clearance, tourism registration, an FSSAI registration, perhaps an excise licence, and in some places forest or coastal permissions. Each has its own date and department.
- cost: The Green Tribunal has ordered action in cases where resorts operated without the needed consents. A lapse can mean a stop-work order in peak season.
- fix: Each consent, permit and certificate is a record with its authority, conditions, date and owner. A renewal opens as a checklist weeks ahead, with the conditions listed. Your team or adviser files; Verity keeps the record.
- screen: upkeep

### 13. Foreign guests and the 24-hour form
- area: Compliance
- scene: A group of foreign guests checks in during a wedding weekend. The IDs are collected, but the online forms are left for Monday. No one notices that one guest was missed.
- cost: A Goa government notice of December 2025 says an accommodation keeper who does not submit the details of a foreign guest faces a penalty of ₹50,000 in each case. The details are due within 24 hours of arrival.
- fix: Each foreign guest's ID and form status sit on the arrival record. Verity lists arrivals with a form due and the time left, and raises a task for the person on duty. Your team files; Verity records that it was done.
- screen: stays
- source: (Government of Goa, Department of Information and Publicity, 2025: https://www.goa.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ONLINE-FILLING-OF.pdf)

### 14. Commission on guests who would have booked direct
- area: Guests
- scene: A family who stayed last winter books through a portal this year. A planner brings a group and the commission is agreed on a call and forgotten. The resort does not know which guests are repeat.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 12 rooms a night booked at 18 percent on ₹6,000 is about ₹13,000 a night in commission, over ₹3.9 lakh in a month.
- fix: Every booking has a channel, rate and commission or planner fee. Verity shows what each channel leaves and lists repeat guests who came through a portal, as tasks for your team to invite them to call direct.
- screen: owner

### 15. Advances, cancellations and the balance on departure
- area: Money
- scene: A wedding pays an advance in three parts. A cancellation arrives with 20 days to go. A guest's extras at the spa and the bar do not reach the bill, and the family leaves before the final count.
- cost: Without a record of what was received and what was agreed, balances are argued about and extras are lost.
- fix: Each booking and event holds the advance schedule, payments received, cancellation terms, extras added on the stay and the balance. Verity flags balances past due and extras missing from the bill. Your billing system still makes the invoice.
- screen: events

### 16. Two resorts, a wedding wing and one view
- area: Owner
- scene: A second property opens in the hills. One does weddings and the other leisure. The numbers come by WhatsApp in different formats, and nobody can say which segment actually makes the money.
- cost: Without margin by segment, effort goes to the busiest part rather than the best one.
- fix: Each property is a location in one system, with occupancy, rate, wedding revenue, activity use, stock, faults and licence dates side by side. Each morning Verity lists what needs a decision, each with an owner.
- screen: owner

Lede: What changes is not the lawn or the view. It is that the day leaves a record, and the record does the chasing.

- 6:00 am: before: The manager walks the property with a list in his head: a clash on the lawn, a delivery that did not come. | after: The Today screen shows the lawn clash and the fish shortage, with owners and times already against both.

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks.
Lede: You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time on your property, build Verity around how your resort really runs, bring your data in, then stay to make sure it keeps working.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend a weekend and a weekday at your resort. Walk the cottages, kitchen, stores, workshop and staff housing, watch an arrival day and a function, and note which leaks matter most.
- you: Two to three hours of yours and your general manager's. Access to your room list, a month of bookings, wedding contracts and the licence file.
- get: A written map of your resort: cottage types and rates, packages, venues, activities, vehicles, stores, machines, licences and the first leaks we will close.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your cottages, packages, venues, activities, vehicles, stock and reorder points, machines, shifts, consents and licences, and build the seven screens around your own terms.
- you: Check the setup with your housekeeping head, chef, engineer and sales manager. Tell us where a rule or a name is wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your real cottages, functions, staff and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in your guest list, wedding diary, vendor details, stock counts, machine details and licence dates from the sheets, registers and phones you use. Train housekeeping, the kitchen, the front desk and the engineer on a live arrival day.
- you: Your wedding diary and guest history in whatever form they exist, and an opening stock count. One hour with each role.
- get: Your cottages, functions and suppliers inside Verity, and a team that has used it through a busy weekend.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first weekends with you, tune the stock levels and checklists, and handle changes when you add a venue, an activity, a vehicle or a property.
- you: A short monthly review of what the numbers say.
- get: A system that keeps matching how the resort runs, with someone responsible for it.

- bring: Your cottage list with types and published rates; A month of bookings and your package details; Wedding and group contracts, even if they are chats; Your vendor list and the last invoices; The licence and consent file with dates; The roster for the last month and each person's role
- time: About 10 to 13 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. Your general manager, chef and engineer need a few more.
- after: Nobody is hired to run Verity. Room status, covers and faults are recorded as part of work the team already does, and the reports come from those records.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a resort has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when an officer asks.
Note: Rules change and differ by state and by site. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

- **Foreign guest reporting**: what: Under the Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, in force from 1 September 2025, accommodation keepers generally must submit a foreign guest's details electronically within 24 hours of arrival. A Goa government notice states a penalty of ₹50,000 in each case for not doing so. | how: Each arrival shows ID and form status with the time left. A task goes to the person on duty and the filing is recorded. (Government of Goa, 2025: https://www.goa.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ONLINE-FILLING-OF.pdf)
- **Pollution control and water consents**: what: Resorts generally need a state pollution control board consent to operate and permission for the use of groundwater, each with conditions and a renewal date. Pollution board consents are often renewed some weeks before expiry. Check the conditions for your site. | how: Each consent is a record with its conditions, date and owner. A renewal opens as a checklist weeks ahead.
- **Site permissions near forests, coasts and protected areas**: what: A resort inside or near an eco-sensitive zone, a forest or a coastal regulation zone may need additional clearances, and the Green Tribunal has ordered action where these were missing. | how: Each permission is a record with the authority and conditions. Your adviser confirms what applies to your site.
- **FSSAI registration or licence**: what: A resort that serves food needs FSSAI registration or a licence. From 1 April 2026 the basic registration band generally applies up to ₹1.5 crore of turnover and a state licence above that up to ₹50 crore. Hygiene duties continue. Check your category. | how: The registration is a record with its number and category, and daily hygiene checks are signed off by the kitchen. (SetIndiaBiz, 2026: https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/fssai-2026-reforms)
- **Fire, tourism and excise licences**: what: Fire clearance, state tourism trade registration, and if a bar is run an excise licence are generally needed. Each is set by the state or city and renewed on its own date. | how: Each is a record with its date and owner. A renewal is a task weeks ahead.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions resort owners ask.

- **My staff live on site and many are not office people. Will they use it?** They do not need to be. Housekeeping updates a cottage with one tap, the kitchen enters counts at the end of a service, and the engineer ticks a work order. We train them on their own station during a live arrival day, and a supervisor can enter from a printed sheet where phones are not carried.
- **Does Verity replace my booking system?** No. Your system keeps taking bookings and making bills. We agree during setup how bookings, stays and payments reach Verity, which then does the housekeeping, event, stock, maintenance and licence work around them.
- **Can it handle multi-day weddings with several functions?** Yes. A wedding is one record with functions by date and venue, headcount, menu, vendors, set-up times, rooms and payments. Verity shows clashes between events and sends tasks to each department.
- **How would it help with empty weekdays?** Verity shows occupancy and rate by weekday and which offers actually brought guests, so you see the thin nights early. Your team sets the rates and sends the offers. Verity does not run offers or promotions.
- **Can it send guests their pickup details or reminders?** No. Verity assigns a transfer task and records the outcome. Your staff contact the guest.
- **I run a 15-cottage resort with a small team. Is this too much?** A small resort has the same leaks and less time to notice them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.
- **I have two resorts, one in the hills and one by the sea. Can I see them together?** Yes. Each is a location in one system, with occupancy, rate, weddings, stock, faults and licences side by side. Guests are the same people across both.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so housekeeping, the kitchen, the engineer and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of cottages, properties and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your resort runs.
