## How it runs today

**Most bars know their sales to the rupee and their stock to the guess.**

- The bar is easy to see. The packed Friday, the DJ, the birthday booth, the queue for the last round. What is hard to see is what sits behind it: whether the pegs poured match the bottles gone, whether the free round was approved, which cocktail makes money after the garnish and the syrup, and which licence date is next.
- That information lives in pieces. The billing system knows what was rung up. The excise stock register is a book, filled in by hand or by the manager at the end of the week. The distributor invoices sit in a file. The roster is a WhatsApp image. The licence certificate is framed behind the bar and its renewal date is in the owner's head.
- India's food services industry is put at about ₹5.69 lakh crore in FY24 by the National Restaurant Association of India, and a market research firm puts pubs, bars, cafés and lounges at about USD 3.0 billion in 2025. A growing market is not the same as a bar that keeps its margin. Rent, licence fees and pour control decide that.
- runs on: Billing system at the bar and tables; Excise stock register and distributor invoices; Excel or notebook for bottle counts; WhatsApp roster and supplier chats; Instagram and WhatsApp for table and event enquiries
- stack note: Each one does its own job well enough. None of them check each other, and the one person who could is behind the bar until 1 am.

## Pains

Heading: Where a bar actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations bar owners and managers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary night, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Pegs sold do not match bottles gone
- area: Stock
- scene: The system says 108 pegs of blended whisky were sold on Friday. The bar count says the opened bottles hold room for 121. Nobody can say if the 13 pegs were spilled, over-poured, given away or never billed, because nobody counted until the weekend.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 13 pegs a night at ₹350 a peg is ₹4,550 a night. Over a 26-night month that is about ₹1.18 lakh from one brand.
- fix: Each bottle is a stock record in pegs. Opening count, deliveries and pegs sold from the bills give the expected balance, and the closing count shows the gap by bottle and by shift, the next morning, not at month end.
- screen: stock

### 2. A heavy hand on the pour
- area: Stock
- scene: A new bartender pours 35 ml where the house peg is 30. On a busy night nobody is measuring, and the spirit well is the most expensive part of the bar.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 120 whisky pegs a night poured at 33 ml instead of 30 ml is 360 ml extra, about 12 pegs or ₹4,200 a night, roughly ₹1.09 lakh in 26 nights.
- fix: Verity compares pegs billed with bottle usage per bartender shift. A person or shift whose usage keeps running above sales shows on the stock screen with the brands involved, so the manager can retrain before it becomes a habit.
- screen: stock

### 3. Drinks served but never billed
- area: Money
- scene: A round goes to a friend of the bartender. A bill is cancelled after payment. A kitchen or bar ticket is changed and never becomes a bill. One login makes the same voids every Friday after midnight.
- cost: A POS software vendor describes unbilled drinks, cancelled bills, reprints and free drinks as the usual ways bars lose money. Every one is sale value and stock that disappears without a record.
- fix: Voids, reprints and comps are logged with a reason, a person and a time. Above your limit they wait for a manager's approval. Verity totals them by person and shift and flags a login whose voids stand out.
- screen: controls
- source: (Petpooja, Bar and brewery liquor pilferage control, 2026: https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/bar-brewery-pos-india-liquor-pilferage-control/)

### 4. A cocktail menu priced by feel
- area: Menu
- scene: The signature cocktail uses a premium gin, a house syrup, fresh herbs and a special ice cube. The menu price was set two years ago. The mocktail sells more than any cocktail and nobody knows its cost.
- cost: Example arithmetic: a cocktail costing ₹210 to make and sold at ₹550 earns ₹340. If the gin price rises and the cost becomes ₹260, the margin falls by ₹50 on every glass and no one notices.
- fix: Each drink is a recipe with its pour, garnish and mixer. Costs come from your latest purchase rates, so a gin or syrup price change shows up as a changed drink margin, ranked by what you actually sell.
- screen: menu

### 5. Comps, happy hours and staff drinks run on goodwill
- area: Money
- scene: The manager clears a round for a regular. Happy hour runs ten minutes past its end. Staff have a drink after close. The owner's friends drink free. Each decision is kind, and together they are a line on the profit and loss that nobody owns.
- cost: Margin lost, and a door left open to misuse, with no way to see a pattern by person, night or outlet.
- fix: Comps and discounts need a reason and, above a limit, a one-tap approval. Staff drinks have a recorded allowance. Verity shows the total by person and night against sales.
- screen: controls

### 6. The excise stock record is a book filled in later
- area: Compliance
- scene: The register is updated by the manager on Sunday from loose slips. Receipts, sales and breakage are written from memory. A page is missing when an officer asks for last quarter.
- cost: A mismatch between what the record says and what the shelf holds can bring a notice, a penalty or trouble at renewal. Rules differ by state, so the book you keep is the book you will be asked for.
- fix: Receipts from the distributor, daily sales in pegs and breakage all build a dated stock record by brand and size. When an officer or your accountant asks, the record already exists, with the person who entered each line.
- screen: stock

### 7. The licence renewal that arrives at the last minute
- area: Compliance
- scene: The bar licence is renewed every financial year. The fee goes up, a no-objection paper from the landlord is needed, a fire safety clearance has expired, and the application window is shorter than the owner remembered.
- cost: In Delhi, for example, hotels, clubs and restaurants were told to renew under the existing framework for 2026-27 with a 10 percent rise in fees for the L-17 family of licences, and unpaid hikes can lead to transport permit suspension. A lapse can stop you buying stock.
- fix: Every licence, permit and supporting paper is a record with a due date and an owner, and the renewal opens as a checklist weeks ahead, with the documents already attached. Verity does not file anything; your team or adviser does.
- screen: upkeep
- source: (India TV News, Delhi extends excise policy for HCRs, 2026: https://www.indiatvnews.com/delhi/delhi-government-extends-current-excise-policy-for-hcrs-by-one-year-amid-new-framework-delays-2026-02-25-1031686)

### 8. Dry days, closing hours and the night before an election
- area: Compliance
- scene: A dry day is announced two days before. Stock is on the shelf, a DJ is booked, a table of forty has paid an advance. Somebody serves anyway.
- cost: Each state sets its own dry days. Election closures can carry penalties for the licensee, and some states seal an outlet for a breach. The cost is fines, a sealed bar and a cancelled night.
- fix: The state's dry days and closing hours are a calendar on the licence record. When a closure falls on a night with an event or a booking, Verity raises a task for the manager to move or cancel it and to tell the guests.
- screen: upkeep
- source: (Petpooja, Dry day list for restaurants and bars, 2026: https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/dry-day-list-restaurants-bars/)

### 9. Deliveries, permits and rates nobody checks
- area: Suppliers
- scene: The cases arrive with a transport permit or pass. The bartender signing is already mid-rush. A rate in the state price list changed and the invoice reflects it three weeks later. A case of beer is short by two bottles.
- cost: You pay for what did not arrive, and a stock count that starts wrong stays wrong for the whole month.
- fix: Each delivery is received against the order, bottle by bottle, with the permit or pass attached. Verity compares order, receipt and invoice, flags a short or a rate change, and holds the bill from payment until it is settled.
- screen: stock

### 10. Bartenders leave and the house standard leaves with them
- area: People
- scene: The head bartender resigns. The replacement has a different idea of a pour, a stir and a garnish, and does not know the house recipes. Nobody confirmed that every person behind the bar meets the age and ID rules for serving.
- cost: Drinks taste different, pours drift and a new person may be put on the bar before anyone has checked them. Hiring and training start again.
- fix: Recipes, pour sizes and the opening and closing checklists live as records. Each person's training, age and ID check and sign-offs are on their record, and the manager sees who is cleared for which station and which shift.
- screen: team

### 11. The roster does not match the night
- area: People
- scene: Friday is packed from 9 pm and thin by 12:30. Tuesday has three people for eleven guests. A bartender leaves at 1 am with no plan to get home, and a no-show at 8 pm leaves the bar half-staffed.
- cost: Wages for empty hours, overtime for rushes, and a night that runs short when it matters. Late-night staff safety is also the owner's duty.
- fix: Covers by hour for each night of the week sit beside the roster, with the over and under hours marked. Late shifts carry a task for the travel plan, and a no-show triggers a call list of people who are free.
- screen: team

### 12. Taps, ice machines and coolers that fail on Saturday
- area: Equipment
- scene: The beer lines were due for cleaning in August. The ice machine stops at 9 pm. The bottle cooler runs warm and a day of stock is at risk. The vendor is called after the failure, not before.
- cost: Flat beer, warm drinks and lost covers on the best night, plus a call-out that costs more on a weekend.
- fix: Each machine, line, cooler and glass washer is an asset record with its service history and next due date. Verity raises the task before the date, with the vendor and past visits on it.
- screen: upkeep

### 13. Table, birthday and event enquiries lost on Instagram
- area: Sales
- scene: A booth for twelve is asked for on Instagram. A company asks for a hosted evening for sixty. A birthday group wants a cover and a minimum spend. The replies are late and the advance is noted in someone's phone.
- cost: Group nights are the biggest tickets a bar sees. They are lost to slow replies and mixed-up details more often than to price.
- fix: Each enquiry is a record with a date, a headcount, a quote, an advance and a next step. Verity lists the ones with no reply and tells the person responsible. Staff still reply to the guest, and the outcome is recorded.
- screen: nights

### 14. DJ fees, promoter payouts and music rights
- area: Events
- scene: A DJ is paid in cash by whoever is on duty. A promoter claims forty guests; the door count says twenty-eight. Nobody is sure the music being played is covered by a licence.
- cost: Payouts that cannot be checked, and music rights bodies that pursue venues playing recorded or live music without a licence.
- fix: Each event night has its budget: artist fee, promoter guest list and payout, and cover collected. Payouts are approved against the door and bill count. The music licences are records with a renewal date.
- screen: nights

### 15. The night ends with a count nobody trusts
- area: Money
- scene: At 1:30 am cash, card and UPI are counted against the bills. They are off by ₹1,150. The closing manager cannot say if a bill was reversed, a tip was entered wrongly or the float was short.
- cost: Small gaps compound, and more importantly the owner stops trusting the numbers on the screen.
- fix: The night's bills by payment mode are compared with what was collected. A mismatch is flagged with the shift and the amount, and the closer records the reason before leaving.
- screen: owner

### 16. Several bars, one picture
- area: Owner
- scene: A second outlet opens. One has a good weekday crowd and a weak weekend. The numbers arrive on WhatsApp the next afternoon in different formats.
- cost: You cannot compare pour cost, labour or event results fairly between outlets, so nothing is tested and the same leak runs in both.
- fix: Outlets are locations in one system, with sales, variance, voids, labour and licence dates side by side. Each morning Verity shows a short list of what needs a decision, each with an owner.
- screen: owner

## Day, before and after

Heading: The same Friday, before and after.
Lede: What changes is not the music or the crowd. It is that the night leaves a record, and the record does the chasing.

- 11:00 am: before: The distributor's truck arrives. Whoever is in signs the slip. The count is done later, when someone has time. | after: The delivery is received against the order, bottle by bottle. The short case of gin is marked and credit is asked before the bill is paid.
- 5:00 pm: before: The manager guesses from last Friday what to prepare, and the dry-day calendar lives in his head. | after: The screen shows tonight's covers estimate, the cocktails that sold most last Friday and a note that no closure applies.
- 9:30 pm: before: The bar is two people short and a booth for twelve is unanswered on Instagram. | after: The roster was filled on Wednesday from the covers by hour, and the booth enquiry is already quoted.
- 12:15 am: before: A friend of the manager gets a round. Nobody sees it until the report. | after: The comp needed a reason and a tap from the manager. It is on the list with his name.
- 1:30 am: before: The closing count is ₹1,150 off and nobody can say why. | after: Three bills do not tie. The closer notes all three before leaving, and it takes two minutes.
- Next morning: before: The pegs billed against bottles gone are worked out at month end, if at all. | after: The stock screen shows the 41 pegs that did not tie, by bottle and shift, and the manager has the first one open.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a bar has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when an officer asks.
Note: Liquor rules are set by each state and change often. Confirm current requirements with your state excise department, your accountant or your adviser.

- **State excise licence**: what: Selling liquor needs a licence from the state excise department, in a category set by the state (for example L-17 in Delhi, FL-III in Maharashtra, CL-9 in Karnataka). Licences are generally for one financial year and renewed each year, with fees and conditions that can change. In Delhi, hotels, clubs and restaurants were directed to renew under the existing framework for 2026-27, with a 10 percent fee rise. | how: The licence is a record with its category, fee, supporting papers and renewal date. Renewal opens as a checklist weeks ahead. Your team or adviser files it. (India TV News, 2026: https://www.indiatvnews.com/delhi/delhi-government-extends-current-excise-policy-for-hcrs-by-one-year-amid-new-framework-delays-2026-02-25-1031686)
- **Excise stock and sale records**: what: Licensees are generally required to keep records of liquor received, sold and in stock, in the form your state prescribes, and to produce them when an officer asks. The format and frequency differ by state. | how: Receipts, daily sales in pegs and breakage build a dated stock record by brand and size, with who entered each line. Confirm the format your state requires.
- **Dry days and serving hours**: what: Each state sets its own dry days and closing hours. Election closures can carry penalties for the licensee. Alcohol cannot generally be served after the hour in your licence terms (in Delhi, 1 am). | how: The calendar of closures sits on the licence record and is checked against event nights and bookings. (Petpooja, Dry day list, 2026: https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/dry-day-list-restaurants-bars/)
- **FSSAI registration or licence for food**: what: A bar that serves food needs FSSAI registration or a licence. From 1 April 2026 the basic registration band generally applies up to ₹1.5 crore of turnover, and licences generally no longer need renewal. Hygiene duties continue. Check your category. | how: The registration is a record with its number and category, and daily hygiene checks are signed off at the bar. (SetIndiaBiz, 2026: https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/fssai-2026-reforms)
- **Music licences**: what: Playing recorded music in a public place generally needs a licence from the recorded-music rights body (PPL), and live music or lyrics a licence from the performing rights society (IPRS). Rates change, so confirm with them. | how: The licences are records with a renewal date, and live nights are tasks with the cover checked before the event. (iPleaders, 2015: https://blog.ipleaders.in/take-licence-playing-music-restaurant-cafe/)

## FAQ

Heading: Questions bar owners ask.

- **My bartenders are slammed at peak. Will they actually record breakage and voids?** Only if it takes seconds, so we set it up as two taps with a reason and keep the list of reasons short. Counts happen before opening and after close, not mid-rush, and we train during a real night, not in a classroom.
- **Does Verity replace my billing system?** No. Your billing system keeps taking orders and payments. We agree during setup how bills reach Verity, which then does the stock, supplier, staff and licence work around them.
- **Does it file my excise returns or renew my licence?** No. Your team or adviser files. Verity keeps the licence, its papers, the dates and the stock records in one place and tells the right person in time.
- **Can it take table reservations or sell tickets for events?** No. If guests book or buy online today, that tool keeps working. Verity makes sure every enquiry has an owner and a next step, and your team replies to the guest.
- **How would it catch over-pouring?** It compares pegs billed with the bottle usage from your counts, per shift and per brand. A person or shift whose usage keeps running above sales is flagged. It does not watch anyone; it shows where the numbers do not tie.
- **I run one small bar with five staff. Is this too much?** A five-person bar has the same leaks and less time to notice them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.
- **I have two bars and a rooftop. Can I see them together?** Yes. Each is a location in one system, with sales, variance, voids, labour and licence dates side by side. Stock can be moved between them against a recorded transfer.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so bartenders, managers and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of outlets, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your bar runs.
