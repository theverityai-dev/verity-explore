## How it runs today

**The business runs on trust and memory. The numbers sit in four places.**

- Most wholesalers know their top ten retailers by face and their credit by instinct. Orders come on the phone, on WhatsApp or through a salesman. The ledger sits with the munim, the rate list is whatever was said this morning, and the godown count lives with the godown boy.
- That works until the business has more than a few hundred retailers and a few thousand lines. Then nobody can say, without an hour of digging, how much is outstanding past 45 days, which items have sat for four months, or whether Tuesday’s rate to a new retailer was below your floor.
- The loss is slow and spread out. A bit of udhaar that stretches. A bag of stock bought high that the market has since walked away from. A rate quoted too low by a new salesman. None of it looks like a problem on the day, and all of it shows up in the cash you do not have at month end.
- runs on: Ledger or Tally with the munim; Rate list on WhatsApp or paper; Salesman order slips; Godown register; Post-dated cheque diary; Owner’s memory
- stack note: Each one is useful. None of them can tell you that a buyer who owes ₹2.4 lakh has just placed a ₹1.5 lakh order.

## Pains

Heading: Where a wholesale business actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations wholesalers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on a normal day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Udhaar grows and nobody checks the limit
- area: Credit
- scene: A retailer who owes ₹2.4 lakh calls for another ₹1.5 lakh of goods. The salesman says yes because the retailer is old. The ledger is with the munim, who is at the bank. By the time anyone looks, the limit was crossed two orders ago.
- cost: Example arithmetic: a wholesaler with 150 retailers and an average ₹1.2 lakh outstanding is carrying ₹1.8 crore of other people’s money. At a 12% cost of funds, every extra 15 days of delay across the book costs about ₹90,000.
- fix: Each retailer has a credit limit and a credit period. When an order would take them over either, it waits for one tap from you, with the dues and age of the oldest bill shown beside it.
- screen: credit

### 2. Collections depend on who remembers to ask
- area: Credit
- scene: The collection salesman has a list in his pocket. The retailer who always says “next week” is chased every week. The quiet one who is 70 days late is not chased at all, because he never complains.
- cost: Delayed payment is a national habit. A 2025 report put delayed payments to small businesses at ₹7.34 lakh crore in March 2024. Your share of that sits in your own outstanding list.
- fix: Outstanding is aged into 0 to 30, 31 to 60 and over 60 days per retailer. Each late buyer gets a follow-up task for a named person, and the promise date and the result are written back to the retailer’s record.
- screen: credit
- source: (GAME, FISME and C2FO report, via Storyboard18, 2025: https://storyboard18.com/how-it-works/delayed-payments-to-msmes-fall-to-rs-7-34-lakh-crore-report-84822.htm)

### 3. Post-dated cheques that bounce, discovered late
- area: Credit
- scene: Seven cheques from different retailers are in the drawer, dated across the month. One is deposited late, one bounces, and a second bounce from the same buyer is found only when the bank statement is read.
- cost: A bounced cheque means a bill that was counted as received but is not, plus a week or more before you chase it. Repeat bouncers keep buying because nobody joined the dots.
- fix: Every cheque is a record against the retailer with its date, bank and the bills it settles. A cheque due this week appears in a deposit list, and a bounce reopens the bills and marks the buyer for approval on the next order.
- screen: credit

### 4. Rates quoted from memory, floors broken quietly
- area: Rates
- scene: A new salesman gives a big retailer the same rate as the wholesale slab for 50 bags, but the retailer took 8. A regular gets last month’s lower rate because it was still on the order slip.
- cost: Example arithmetic: on a line sold at ₹1,000 a unit with a 4% margin, a ₹20 slip is half the profit. Across 200 invoices a month, one quiet rate error a day adds up quickly.
- fix: Rate slabs by quantity and buyer type are kept on each line. A quote below the floor for that quantity needs an approval, and Verity shows each week which invoices went under their slab and who raised them.
- screen: rates

### 5. The market moves daily and your stock does not
- area: Rates
- scene: You bought a lot of a commodity at the old rate. A week later the mandi rate has dropped. Your stock list shows what you paid, not what you could sell it for today.
- cost: Price exposure is the whole risk of the trade. Without a daily comparison, you find out the stock is under water when you try to sell it.
- fix: You enter the day’s market rate per line, or a small team member does. Verity sets it against your holding cost, flags lines now below cost, and shows the rupee value of the gap before you decide to hold or sell.
- screen: rates

### 6. Slow stock that ties up cash in the godown
- area: Stock
- scene: A carton of an item bought in the festival rush is still on the third rack in July. Nobody sells it because nobody has seen it in a list.
- cost: Example arithmetic: ₹10 lakh sitting for four months at a 12% cost of funds is about ₹40,000 of interest, before damage, shrinkage or expiry.
- fix: Every stock lot carries its receive date. Verity lists lines by age, how many days of sales they represent at the current pace, and which retailers last bought them, so a clearance push goes to the right buyers.
- screen: stock

### 7. Buying by gut leaves fast lines empty and slow lines full
- area: Stock
- scene: The best-selling pack is out on a Friday and the retailers go to the other wholesaler. Meanwhile, the big order of a seasonal line has arrived early and fills a bay.
- cost: A missed sale goes to a competitor, who may keep the retailer. An over-buy ties up money and space. Both come from the same missing view.
- fix: Verity shows sales per week, stock in hand, goods on order and the supplier’s lead time for each line, and drafts a purchase list of what is likely to run out before the next delivery. You approve or change it.
- screen: stock

### 8. Orders on phone, WhatsApp and slips, all in different formats
- area: Orders
- scene: A retailer sends a voice note. A salesman writes six lines on a slip. The counter clerk types it in later. At picking time the godown has a version that is not what the retailer asked for.
- cost: Wrong quantities mean returns, a second trip and a retailer who trusts you a little less each time.
- fix: Every order becomes one record with lines, rates, the salesman, the retailer and a state: received, checked for credit, picked, loaded, delivered. Whoever takes it sees the same thing as the godown does.
- screen: orders

### 9. Picking mistakes and short deliveries found at the retailer’s shop
- area: Orders
- scene: The van goes out with 18 cartons where the order said 20. The retailer notices after the van has left and calls the owner, not the godown.
- cost: Credit notes, a free top-up trip, and arguments over who is right. Each one eats the margin on a whole order.
- fix: The picklist is generated from the order and ticked off by the loader. The delivery record carries the count, and a shortage or damage on arrival is noted against the order the same day.
- screen: orders

### 10. Trucks, e-way bills and freight as paper at the weighbridge
- area: Transport
- scene: A consignment goes out above the e-way bill value and the document is generated at the last minute. A transporter’s freight bill for the month does not match the lorry receipts.
- cost: A vehicle held at a check post, a penalty, or a freight bill paid twice. All avoidable with the right record at the right moment.
- fix: Each dispatch records the vehicle, driver, transporter, lorry receipt number and your e-way bill number. A dispatch without one gets flagged before the van leaves, and monthly freight is checked against the dispatches.
- screen: orders

### 11. Supplier dues paid late, or early and then short of cash
- area: Suppliers
- scene: A small manufacturer who supplies you is owed money for 70 days. Another gets paid early because he rang more often. Nobody lined payments up against the 45-day rule.
- cost: Under the MSME payment rule, amounts owed to micro and small suppliers beyond the allowed days generally become deductible only when paid. Late supplier payments can mean a higher tax bill, as well as upset suppliers.
- fix: Each supplier bill shows its due date and a flag for whether the supplier is a micro or small enterprise. A payables list sorts by due date, so payments go out in the right order, with the cash they need.
- screen: suppliers
- source: (ClearTax, Section 43B(h): https://cleartax.in/s/section-43bh-of-income-tax-act)

### 12. No memory of which supplier gave the better rate
- area: Suppliers
- scene: Three suppliers sell the same grade. The one you buy from is the one who calls first. Last quarter’s better rate lives in someone’s WhatsApp chat.
- cost: A 1% better buying rate on a ₹5 crore annual purchase is ₹5 lakh. You only get it if you can see the comparison.
- fix: Each purchase records the supplier, rate, quantity, delay in delivery and quality complaints. A comparison per line shows who was cheapest, who was late, and who sent returns.
- screen: suppliers

### 13. Salesmen, loaders and the munim each know a part
- area: People
- scene: The salesman who handles 40 retailers leaves. His order history, his promises on rates and the dues he was chasing leave with him. The godown helper who knows where things are is on leave.
- cost: Retailers drift to a competitor’s salesman. The new person starts from nothing, and the owner is back to answering every call.
- fix: Retailers belong to the business, not to a salesman. Each salesman’s route, orders, collections and targets are visible, and reassigning a route moves the history and the open dues in one step.
- screen: owner

### 14. You cannot say which retailers and lines make the money
- area: Owner
- scene: Sales are up, but cash is tight. You do not know whether the cause is slow collection, a low-margin line that is growing, or stock that is heavy.
- cost: Decisions are made on turnover, which tells you how busy you are, not how well you are doing.
- fix: Margin per line and per retailer is calculated from the buying rate, the selling rate and the credit days. The morning screen shows dues, stock under water, orders waiting and approvals, so you start from facts.
- screen: owner

## Day, before and after

Heading: The same Monday at the godown, before and after.
Lede: The work is the same. What changes is that every order, rate and payment leaves a record, and the record does the chasing.

- 8:30: before: You open the shop and ask the munim for yesterday’s dues. He needs an hour to total them. | after: Outstanding by retailer and age is on the Today screen. The three oldest are already assigned.
- 10:30: before: A retailer 70 days behind places a big order. The salesman agrees on the phone. | after: The order is held for credit. You see the dues and the oldest bill, and approve half.
- 12:00: before: Mustard oil has been in the godown since June. You only see it on the stock count. | after: It shows as aged 96 days. The list of retailers who bought it last is ready for a clearance push.
- 3:00: before: The van returns. A retailer says two cartons were short and the clerk argues. | after: The picklist, the loaded count and the delivery note are on the order. The shortage is settled in a minute.
- 5:30: before: A post-dated cheque you deposited last week turns out to have bounced. | after: The bounce is already on the retailer’s record and his next order is waiting for your approval.
- Month end: before: The supplier list is checked against the ledger and one small supplier is found 70 days overdue. | after: Payables were sorted by due date all month. Small suppliers were paid inside the limit.

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks, around your godown and your retailers.
Lede: You do not get a login and a thick manual. We learn how your trade runs, build Verity around it, move your retailers, lines and dues in, then stay on until it holds.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend time at the counter, the godown and the collection desk. Follow one order from phone call to payment, and note where rates, credit and stock are decided today.
- you: Two to three hours of your time, and the munim’s. Your ledger, price list, stock register and a sample of order slips.
- get: A written map of your trade: retailer groups and routes, rate slabs, godowns, suppliers, and the leaks we will fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your lines, units, rate slabs and floors, credit limits and periods, approval rules, godowns and salesman routes. Build the screens above in your words.
- you: Check the rules with your senior people. Tell us where a slab or a limit is wrong.
- get: A working Verity holding your rate card, limits, routes and approvals, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in retailer and supplier lists, opening dues by bill, stock by lot, and open orders from your ledger and sheets. Train the counter, salesmen and godown on live orders.
- you: Your ledger export or sheets, in whatever shape they exist. One hour with each role.
- get: Your retailers, dues and stock inside Verity, and a team that has taken real orders on it.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first month’s dues and stock ageing with you, tune slabs and limits, and add lines and routes as you grow.
- you: A short monthly review of dues, ageing and margin by line.
- get: A system that keeps matching how you trade, with someone responsible for keeping it right.

- bring: Your ledger of retailers with opening dues by bill; Your rate list and any quantity slabs; Your stock register by godown, with buying rates; Supplier list with terms and open bills; Salesman routes and the retailers on each; Your current credit rules, even if only spoken
- time: About 9 to 12 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. The munim and the godown in-charge need a few more.
- after: Nobody extra is hired. Orders and receipts are recorded as part of the work already done, and the reports are built from them.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions wholesalers ask.

- **My salesmen and munim are not used to computers. Will it work?** The screens use your words: retailer, line, bill, godown. Entry is built to be short, and we train at the counter and the godown with real orders. Salesmen also get something back, which is their own dues and collections list, so they have a reason to use it.
- **Does Verity replace Tally or my billing software?** No. Your accounting software keeps doing the books and the bills. Verity holds the orders, dues, stock ageing and rates around it, and we agree during setup how the data reaches Verity.
- **Can it chase retailers for money?** It does not message them by itself. It builds the collection list, gives each late bill to a named person, and records the promise and the result. Your people make the call or send the message.
- **I have three godowns and a shop. Can I see them together?** Yes. Each godown is a location in one system. Stock, ageing and dispatches roll up for you, and a transfer between godowns is recorded so stock does not appear or vanish.
- **Can I set different rates for different retailers?** Yes. Slabs can differ by quantity, retailer group or an agreed special rate, each with a floor. Anything under the floor waits for approval, and the approved rate is kept on the record.
- **Does it track daily market rates for me?** No. Someone on your team enters the day’s market rate for the lines you care about, which takes a few minutes. Verity then compares it with your holding cost and flags the lines that are under water.
- **Who can see my dues and my margins?** Only the people you allow. Salesmen see their own routes, the godown sees picks and stock but not buying rates, and you see everything. Each change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How long before I see anything useful?** The dues-by-age list and stock ageing need only your ledger and stock register, so we aim to have them working first. Margin by retailer and line becomes reliable after a month or two of recorded orders.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of godowns, users and what you want set up. Message us and after a short conversation about how you trade, we will give you a clear number.
