## How it runs today

**Most studios know their best class by feel and their worst one by the empty mats.**

- The studio floor is easy to read. The 6 am class is full, the 2 pm class has four people, the Saturday boxing session has a waiting list. What is hard to read is everything attached to it: which members came twice this month after buying a package for twelve, whose pack expires on Friday, which trainer's class loses people every week, and what each trainer is owed at month end.
- The information exists in pieces. A reservation app knows who booked. The front desk knows who paid. A sheet knows the trainer rates. A WhatsApp group knows who is covering whom. The owner, who is often also a trainer, carries the rest in memory.
- Boutique studios are the fastest growing part of Indian fitness, with the Deloitte and Health & Fitness Association report expecting nearly 19 percent a year to 2030. More studios nearby means members have more choice, and a lapsed member is harder to win back.
- runs on: Reservation app or class sheet; Front desk billing and receipts; Excel for packs and trainer rates; WhatsApp groups for members and trainers; Instagram for new enquiries
- stack note: Each tool works for its own job. None knows that the member who booked eight classes has attended two.

## Pains

Heading: Where a fitness studio actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations studio owners and managers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Classes run half empty while others have a waiting list
- area: Classes
- scene: The 7 pm HIIT class is full every night and four people are waitlisted. The 2 pm mobility class has had three people for six weeks. The timetable has not changed since the studio opened.
- cost: Example arithmetic: a class with 20 places that runs at 9 people instead of 15, four days a week, is about 24 empty places a week. At ₹500 a place that is ₹12,000 a week, or ₹48,000 a month, left on the table.
- fix: Every class records bookings, attendance and no-shows. Verity shows fill by class, weekday and hour, so you can move, merge or add a class based on the pattern instead of a hunch.
- screen: classes

### 2. Booked members do not show up
- area: Classes
- scene: Eighteen people book a 6:30 am class and eleven come. The trainer plans for eighteen. Three people on the waitlist were never told a place was free in time.
- cost: Empty places that someone else wanted. Members who are always on the waitlist start to feel the studio is full and stop trying.
- fix: Booking, cancellation and attendance are recorded per member. Verity lists repeat no-shows and tasks the desk to release places on the waitlist when someone cancels. Your reservation app can stay; we agree how its bookings reach Verity during setup.
- screen: classes

### 3. Class packs and memberships expire unused
- area: Packs
- scene: A member buys a twelve-class pack, uses five, and the pack lapses. Another is frozen for a month and nobody remembers to restart the clock. The front desk finds out when the member argues at the counter.
- cost: Expired packs cause arguments, goodwill refunds and quiet churn. Unused classes are also a liability on your books that nobody can see.
- fix: Each pack and membership is a record with classes bought, used, left, expiry and freeze dates. Verity shows packs that will lapse in the next two weeks with the member's usual class, so the desk can speak to them first.
- screen: members

### 4. Members who stopped coming are only noticed after the renewal date
- area: Retention
- scene: A member who came four times a week in July has come once in September. Nobody noticed. On the renewal day she does not renew, and no one asks why.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 150 members at ₹3,500 a month is ₹5.25 lakh a month. Losing one in ten a month is about ₹52,000 a month of repeat income to replace with new joins.
- fix: Attendance builds each member's usual pattern. When it drops, the member goes on a list with last visit and usual class, and a task goes to the trainer who knows them. The outcome is recorded.
- screen: members

### 5. New joiners vanish in the first month
- area: Retention
- scene: The member who joined after a New Year offer has come twice. No one has asked how it is going, and her trial week is long over.
- cost: A new member who fails to form a habit in the first weeks rarely renews, and most of the money spent winning them is lost.
- fix: Every new member gets a simple first-month plan as tasks: a welcome check, a first-week check and a first-month review, each with an owner. Verity shows which new members are on track and which are not.
- screen: renewals

### 6. Trainer payouts are worked out by hand and disputed
- area: Trainers
- scene: One trainer is paid per class, another a share per head, a third a monthly sum plus personal training sessions. At month end the owner rebuilds the count from the timetable, a WhatsApp group and memory. Someone is always sure the number is wrong.
- cost: Hours of owner time each month, arguments with good trainers, and under or over payment that nobody can prove either way.
- fix: Each class taken and each personal session is recorded against the trainer with the rate rule you agreed. Verity totals what each trainer is owed for the month from those records. You pay them, or your accountant does; Verity does not run payroll.
- screen: trainers

### 7. A trainer is absent and the cover is arranged in a chat
- area: Trainers
- scene: A trainer messages at 5:15 am that she is ill. The 6 am class is in forty-five minutes. The manager scrolls through a WhatsApp group to find who can cover and who is qualified for that format.
- cost: A cancelled class loses members' trust. A cover who is not trained for the format risks injuries and complaints.
- fix: Each trainer's formats, availability and leave are on one schedule. When a trainer is out, Verity lists who is cleared for that class and free at that time, and the cover is recorded so the payout follows.
- screen: trainers

### 8. Personal training sessions are sold in packs that nobody tracks
- area: Packs
- scene: A client buys twenty personal training sessions in March. By August nobody can say how many are left or whether the sessions were taken with the trainer who was paid for them.
- cost: A balance that is argued at the end, and revenue that cannot be reported by trainer or by client.
- fix: A personal training pack records sessions bought, taken, with whom and on what date. Both the client and the trainer see the same balance, and a session is counted once.
- screen: trainers

### 9. Enquiries from Instagram and walk-ins go unanswered
- area: Sales
- scene: A message about the studio's six-week transformation program arrives on Instagram on Sunday night. A walk-in takes a trial class and leaves with a flyer. The follow-up is "whoever remembers."
- cost: A person who has already decided to try a studio will try the next one if no one replies by the next day.
- fix: Each enquiry and trial is a record with an owner, source, date and next step. Verity lists the ones with no follow-up and tells the person responsible. Staff still talk to the person; the outcome is recorded.
- screen: renewals

### 10. Equipment breaks quietly and service is only after a failure
- area: Equipment
- scene: A reformer spring is worn, a treadmill belt slips, a kettlebell handle cracks. The cleaner mentions it on Tuesday. The class on Wednesday uses it anyway.
- cost: Injury risk, a damaged reputation and an expensive repair booked in a hurry. Equipment that is out of order also reduces the places you can sell in a class.
- fix: Each machine is an asset record with its service history and next due date. A fault is logged in two taps from the floor, and a maintenance task goes to the owner of that asset. The class capacity is adjusted while it is out.
- screen: classes

### 11. Members' health and injury details live on paper forms
- area: Compliance
- scene: A signed PAR-Q style form is in a folder. A member with a knee injury is in the squat-heavy class and the trainer was not told. A waiver is signed once and never renewed.
- cost: A hurt member, a complaint and a studio with nothing on record to show it asked the right questions.
- fix: Health notes, injuries and waivers sit on the member's record with a date and a review reminder. The trainer sees a flag on the class list for any member with a note. Verity stores the information; it does not give medical advice.
- screen: members

### 12. Refunds, freezes and transfers are decided at the counter
- area: Money
- scene: A member moves cities and asks for a refund. Another wants to transfer her membership to her sister. The front desk says yes to one and no to the other, and the owner hears about it later.
- cost: Unequal treatment, complaints and, when it goes to a consumer commission, a fight you may lose if your own terms are unclear.
- fix: Freeze, transfer and refund requests are records with the member's pack, the rule that applies and an approval step. Verity shows the decisions made, by whom and why, so the policy is applied the same way every time.
- screen: members

### 13. Month-end money does not match the classes
- area: Money
- scene: The owner opens the bank statement and the front desk report. Cash, UPI and card do not add up to the packs sold. A discount was given at the counter and not written anywhere.
- cost: A month of reconciling that someone has to do, and an owner who stops trusting the numbers.
- fix: Packs and memberships sold are records with the payment mode and any discount with its approver. Verity compares them with what was collected and flags the gaps by day and by person.
- screen: owner

### 14. Several branches or a studio plus a pop-up with no single view
- area: Owner
- scene: The second studio uses a different sheet. A member who joined in one place wants to attend a class in the other. Nobody can say if branch two is doing better than branch one.
- cost: Nothing can be compared fairly, so nothing can be fixed, and members who travel between branches are treated as strangers.
- fix: Each studio is a location in one system. Classes, members, packs and trainers sit side by side, and a member's pack shows where it can be used. The owner's morning screen shows both.
- screen: owner

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks.
Lede: You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time at the front desk and on the studio floor, build Verity around how your classes really run, bring your data in, then stay to make sure it keeps working.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Sit through a morning class block and an evening block. Watch how a booking becomes attendance, how a pack is sold and used, and how the month's trainer count is made. Note which gaps matter most.
- you: Two to three hours of yours and the studio manager's. Your timetable, a month of class sheets, pack prices and each trainer's rate rule.
- get: A written map of your studio: the class formats, the pack and membership types, the trainer rules and the first gaps we will close.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your classes and places, pack and membership types with their expiry and freeze rules, trainers with formats and rates, equipment and locations, and build the five screens around your own terms.
- you: Check the setup with your head trainer and front desk. Tell us where a pack rule or a payout rule is wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your real classes, packs, trainers and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in your member list, current packs and their balances, and trainer rates from the sheets and apps you use. Train the front desk and trainers at the studio during real classes.
- you: Your member and pack list in whatever form it exists, and a check of ten members' balances. One hour with each role.
- get: Your members, packs and trainers inside Verity, and a team that has run a full week of classes on it.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first month with you, tune the timetable views and payout rules, and handle changes when you add a format, a trainer or a second studio.
- you: A short monthly review of what the numbers say.
- get: A system that keeps matching how the studio runs, with someone responsible for it.

- bring: Your timetable and class formats; Your pack and membership price list with expiry and freeze rules; Your member list with current pack balances, even if it is a sheet; Each trainer's rate rule and last month's classes; Equipment list and last service dates
- time: About 9 to 11 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. Your front desk lead and head trainer need a few more.
- after: Nobody is hired to run Verity. Attendance, packs and sessions are recorded as part of work the team already does, and the reports come from those records.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions fitness studio owners ask.

- **I already have a booking app. Why would I need this?** A booking app knows who reserved. It rarely knows what pack they hold, whether they are drifting away, or what each trainer is owed. We keep your app and agree during setup how its bookings reach Verity, which then does the member, pack and trainer work around them.
- **Will my front desk really record all this?** Only if it takes seconds. Check-in is one tap against the class list, and a pack is used when the member is marked present. We train at the desk during real classes and start with the few records that matter, not a long form.
- **Can it work out what I owe each trainer?** Yes, once their rate rules are set up. Each class taken and personal session is recorded against the trainer, and Verity totals the month from those records. You or your accountant make the payment; Verity does not run payroll.
- **Does it handle packs that freeze, transfer or expire?** Yes. Each pack is a record with classes bought, used, left, expiry and any freeze, and the rule you apply for a transfer or refund is attached to the decision.
- **I run one small studio with four trainers. Is this too much?** A small studio has the same gaps and less time to notice them. We set up only what you need in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.
- **Can it take online reservations or payments?** No. If members reserve or pay online today, that tool keeps working. Verity records the outcome and makes sure someone follows up with members who drift.
- **I have two studios. Can I see them together?** Yes. Each is a location in one system, with classes, members and trainers side by side. A member's pack shows where it can be used.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so trainers, the front desk and your accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How quickly will I see something useful?** The lapsing packs list and the quiet members list need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. Class fill and no-show patterns become reliable after a few weeks of recorded classes.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of studios, staff and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your studio runs.
