## How it runs today

**A physiotherapy clinic sells recovery, one session at a time.**

- The first assessment is straightforward. The real work is the course after it: ten sessions over five weeks for a frozen shoulder, a long programme after a knee replacement, a fortnight of exercises after a sports injury. Patients feel better in session four and wonder if they need the rest. Some leave. Some come back three months later with the same problem.
- Information is spread thinly. Assessment notes are in a file or on a tablet. Pain scores and movement measures are on a sheet the therapist keeps. The exercise handout went out on a printout. The session pack is a card in the patient’s wallet. Referring doctors rarely hear back. Home visits are planned in a WhatsApp group. Machines are used and serviced on trust.
- Everyone is busy, and everyone is treating a patient. Nobody is watching the whole course, so the signs that a patient is slipping away, or that a therapist is overbooked, or that a referral source has gone quiet, show up late or not at all.
- runs on: Assessment forms or a basic app; Session cards and a register; WhatsApp for home visits; Printed exercise sheets; Therapists’ memory
- stack note: Each is fine for its small job. None of them can tell you who stopped coming this fortnight.

## Pains

Heading: Where a physiotherapy clinic loses patients and money.
Lede: These are the situations physiotherapists and clinic owners describe. For each: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does. Verity records what your therapists decide and never advises on treatment.

### 1. Patients stop when the pain eases, with the course unfinished
- area: Patients
- scene: A man with a lower back strain has done five of eight planned sessions. He feels fine and says he will call if it returns. Two months later he is back with the same pain and the earlier work has to be repeated.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 30 patients a month who leave with three sessions undelivered at ₹600 each is ₹54,000 of planned work a month. Published accounts of Indian physiotherapy settings describe non-adherence as a central problem, driven by cost, family duties and access.
- fix: The therapist records how many sessions the plan needs. Verity shows courses with sessions left and no visit in a set number of days, and gives the desk a call task for each. The outcome is recorded so the next call knows what was said.
- screen: followups
- source: (Sociocultural factors affecting patient adherence in physiotherapy settings in India (World Physiotherapy congress proceeding): https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/sociocultural-factors-affecting-patient-adherence-physiotherapy-settings-india)

### 2. Session packs are bought and then lost, argued over or left unused
- area: Patients
- scene: A patient buys a pack of ten. Four visits later nobody is sure how many remain. The card is in an old bag. A relative asks if the remaining sessions can be used for her mother.
- cost: Unused packs are work the clinic is owed, and a source of arguments when the count differs. Packs with no end date make revenue hard to plan.
- fix: A pack is a record with sessions bought, sessions used, the person it belongs to and an end date. Every visit reduces it. Verity lists packs about to lapse and the desk can rebook while the patient still cares.
- screen: packages

### 3. No-shows and late cancellations break the therapist’s day
- area: Time and rooms
- scene: The 6 pm patient does not turn up and the therapist waits twenty minutes. Two others cancelled an hour before. Evenings are packed and mornings are thin.
- cost: A therapist on salary or on a per-session fee with no patient in front of them. Because the same patients repeat the pattern, the loss is predictable but never measured.
- fix: Each no-show and cancellation is recorded on the patient. Verity shows repeat cases, empty slots with a waiting list for earlier dates, and the hours that stay empty week after week.
- screen: owner

### 4. Treatment rooms, tables and machines are unevenly used
- area: Time and rooms
- scene: The traction table has a queue on Monday mornings. The shockwave unit was used twice in a month. One therapist is double-booked while another has a gap.
- cost: A machine that is bought and rarely used is a poor purchase. Overbooked therapists rush, and empty ones cost wages.
- fix: Visits are recorded with therapist, room and equipment used. Verity shows utilisation by therapist, room and machine, and which ones earn their cost.
- screen: schedule

### 5. Home visits are planned over WhatsApp
- area: Time and rooms
- scene: Six home patients across the city, two therapists, one two-wheeler each. A last-minute cancellation leaves a therapist forty minutes from the next patient. Travel is not paid for, so it is not counted.
- cost: Hours of travel with no billing, missed visits when a message is not seen, and patients unsure who is coming.
- fix: Home visits are scheduled records with address area, therapist, time window and status. Verity shows each therapist’s day in order, flags gaps and overlaps and records travel time next to billable time.
- screen: schedule

### 6. Progress is argued from memory, not from measurements
- area: Clinical records
- scene: A patient says the shoulder is no better. The therapist remembers it improving. The pain scores and range-of-movement readings are on three different sheets, and the last two are missing.
- cost: Hard to show a patient or a referring doctor what has changed. Insurers and corporate clients increasingly ask for progress evidence.
- fix: Your therapist records pain score, movement and function measures at each review, using the scales you already use. Verity shows them as a trend on the patient record. It records what is entered and makes no clinical judgement.
- screen: patient

### 7. The home exercise plan is a printout that cannot be checked
- area: Clinical records
- scene: The patient is given a sheet of exercises. At the next visit nobody knows which ones were advised, which were changed, or what the patient actually did.
- cost: The follow-up becomes a second assessment, and time is lost re-establishing where the patient is.
- fix: The exercises advised, the changes made and the patient’s own report at each visit are recorded on the patient record. The next therapist opens it and sees what was set last time.
- screen: patient

### 8. Referring doctors send patients and never hear back
- area: Referrals
- scene: An orthopaedic surgeon sends post-operative knee patients. Nobody tells him how they progressed. After a few months he sends fewer.
- cost: Referrals are the cheapest patients a clinic gets, and the least tracked. A silent source dries up without anyone noticing.
- fix: Each patient has a referral source on record. Verity shows referrals by doctor over time, and creates a task to send a short progress summary at set points. Your therapist writes it; Verity makes sure it is sent.
- screen: followups

### 9. Insurance, accident and corporate claims wait in a file
- area: Money
- scene: A post-surgery patient has an insurance-backed course. A company covers a tie-up for its staff. A road accident claim needs session records. Each has its own forms and its own follow-up.
- cost: Money owed and not chased. Claims lapse when papers are missing or late.
- fix: A claim is a record with the patient, payer, amount, documents needed and state. Verity lists those unpaid past their usual period and tasks the person who sends them.
- screen: packages

### 10. Consumables and machines are used until they fail
- area: Stock
- scene: Electrode pads, gel, kinesiology tape and resistance bands run out on a busy day. The ultrasound unit’s annual check is overdue. A TENS unit has a loose lead nobody has reported.
- cost: A therapist improvising with a poor substitute, a machine out of use at a bad time, and emergency purchases at higher prices.
- fix: Each consumable has a reorder level and usual supplier. Each machine has a service date and a vendor. Verity warns ahead and drafts the order for you to approve.
- screen: stock

### 11. Part-time therapists are paid per session, and patients follow therapists
- area: People
- scene: Three therapists are on different fee arrangements: a monthly salary, a share of fees, a per-session rate. Totals are added up by hand each month. When a favourite therapist leaves, her patients go with her.
- cost: Hours of owner time, mistakes that make people feel unfairly paid, and revenue that walks out of the door.
- fix: Sessions are recorded against the therapist who gave them, and Verity builds the statement from your arrangement. The patient relationship belongs to the clinic’s record, and a handover list is ready on the day someone resigns.
- screen: staff

### 12. Consent, privacy and who may see what are matters of habit
- area: Records
- scene: Consent for dry needling or manual therapy is verbal. A female patient prefers a female therapist and this is not noted anywhere. Case notes are on a tablet that several people share.
- cost: A dispute or complaint is hard to answer when the record is thin. Health data is sensitive.
- fix: Consent, preferences and notes sit on the record, and the therapist cannot close a procedure without the consent marked on file. Access follows role, and each view is logged.
- screen: patient

### 13. Takings do not match sessions delivered
- area: Money
- scene: The desk takes cash, UPI and card. A session was billed at pack rate for one patient and full rate for another. At closing the day is off by ₹1,200.
- cost: Small gaps compound, and the owner cannot trust the figures.
- fix: Every session is a record with its rate, pack use and payment mode. Verity compares the day’s sessions with the day’s payments and shows the exact visit that does not tie.
- screen: owner

### 14. Corporate camps, a second centre and the owner’s own patients
- area: Owner
- scene: The owner treats patients all day, runs an ergonomic camp for an IT company on Saturdays, and has opened a second centre. Information comes by message at night. Nobody has the whole picture.
- cost: No fair comparison between centres or camps, and decisions made late.
- fix: Centres and camps are locations or jobs in one system, with a patient seen at both shown once. Revenue, sessions per therapist and dropout rates compare side by side.
- screen: owner

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, over four weeks.
Lede: No login and a handbook. We learn how your clinic works, build Verity around it, bring your patients across, and stay on afterwards.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend a day at the clinic. Follow a patient from the first assessment to the last session, watch how home visits are planned, and see how packs, notes and fees are kept today.
- you: A few hours of yours and your senior therapist’s. Access to the patient register, pack cards, referral list and payment book.
- get: A written map of your clinic: therapists, rooms, machines, session types and price list, plus the problems we will fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your session types and packs, plan templates, review measures, referral sources, home-visit areas, consumables, machines and service dates, therapist arrangements and centres.
- you: Review the setup with your senior therapist and correct anything that does not match how you work.
- get: A working Verity with your real session types, rooms and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in patient lists, running courses, packs with sessions left, referrals, claims and stock from your registers and sheets. Train therapists and the desk at their own stations in short sessions.
- you: Your patient list and any history you have, in whatever form. One hour per role.
- get: Your patients, packs and stock inside Verity, and a team that has used it on real sessions.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first weeks with you, tune the quiet-patient rules and alerts, and handle changes when you add a therapist, a machine or a centre.
- you: A short monthly review.
- get: A system that keeps matching how the clinic runs, with someone accountable for it.

- bring: Your session types, durations and prices, including packs; Your patient list, even if it is a register; Running courses with sessions already used; Referring doctors and companies you work with; Therapist pay or share arrangements
- time: About 8 to 10 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. Your senior therapist needs a few more.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions physiotherapists ask.

- **Does Verity advise on treatment or tell my therapists what to do?** No. Your therapists decide assessment and treatment. Verity records what they decide, tracks courses and sessions and tells your team what is waiting.
- **We use an exercise-prescription tool. Do we have to stop?** No. Keep it if your therapists like it. During setup we agree how the information Verity needs, such as exercises advised and sessions done, reaches the patient record.
- **Can it handle home visits?** Yes. A home visit is a scheduled record with an area, time window and therapist. You see each therapist’s day in order, with travel time shown next to treatment time.
- **Can it contact patients who stopped coming?** Not by itself. It builds the quiet list and gives each call to a named person on your team. They call or message, and the result is recorded.
- **How does it deal with session packs?** A pack records sessions bought and used, who it belongs to and when it ends. Each visit reduces it, and you see packs about to lapse.
- **I run one room with two therapists. Is this too much?** A small clinic has the same unfinished courses with less time to notice them. We set up only what you need in the same four weeks.
- **Can I track the doctors who refer patients to me?** Yes. Each patient has a referral source on record, and you see referrals over time by doctor along with whether a progress note was sent.
- **Who can see patient data?** Only the people you allow. Access is by role, so the desk, therapists, part-timers and your accountant see what they need. Every view and change is logged with the person and time.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of centres, therapists and what you want set up. Message us and after a short conversation about how your clinic runs we will give you a clear number.
