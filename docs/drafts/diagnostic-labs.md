## How it runs today

**A lab is a promise kept by many hands.**

- A patient gives a sample at a collection centre or at home. It travels, sometimes in a cold box, sometimes in a bag on a two-wheeler. It is received, checked, run on one of several analysers, reviewed, signed by a pathologist, and sent out. Each step has a person and a time. Any step can go wrong without anyone noticing until the patient rings.
- Behind the bench are the things that keep it running: reagent kits with lot numbers and expiry dates, controls that need to be run every day, analysers that need calibration and a service visit, an outsourced list of tests sent to a reference lab, and referring doctors who decide where their patients go. Most of this lives in registers, spreadsheets and the memory of one senior technician.
- The money side is no easier. Walk-in patients pay on the spot. Corporate tie-ups, hospitals and collection partners pay weeks later. Reference lab payables build up. Reagent suppliers raise prices and expect payment on time. The owner sees all this only when the month ends.
- runs on: Lab software for results and reports; Registers for samples and QC; Spreadsheets for stock; WhatsApp with collection staff; One senior technician’s memory
- stack note: Your lab software handles results well. Nothing holds the chain of steps, the stock and the people around it.

## Pains

Heading: Where a diagnostic lab loses time, money and trust.
Lede: These are the situations lab owners and managers describe. For each: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it. Verity tracks the status of samples and reports and never sees or interprets a result.

### 1. Nobody can say where a sample is
- area: Samples
- scene: A sample left a collection centre at 9:30. The runner says it was delivered. The lab says it did not arrive. The patient calls for the third time, and someone searches the fridge.
- cost: A repeat collection, an unhappy patient and sometimes a doctor who stops sending work. Each hour of searching is a technician not running samples.
- fix: Every sample is a record with a barcode or number, a collection time, who collected it, who handed it over and who received it. Verity shows samples in transit past their usual time and the last known hand.
- screen: samples

### 2. Samples are rejected or recollected because of avoidable errors
- area: Samples
- scene: A haemolysed tube, too little blood, a wrong label, a missing patient detail. The patient is called back, and the collection staff are blamed.
- cost: One Indian lab audit found 1.4% of 67,438 routine samples had an error, and about three quarters of the errors were before the sample reached the analyser. Each one is a recollection, a delay and a repeat cost.
- fix: Rejections are recorded with a reason and the person who collected the sample. Verity shows rejection rate by centre, by collector and by reason, so training goes to the right people.
- screen: samples
- source: (Goswami et al., Evaluation of errors in a clinical laboratory (CCLM, 2010), via Westgard QC: https://www.westgard.com/blog/error-rates-in-an-indian-clinical-laboratory.html)

### 3. Reports promised for the evening come out the next morning
- area: Reports
- scene: A batch of tests waits for the pathologist to sign. A reagent ran out mid-run and a rerun was needed. The patient was told 6 pm. The report goes at 10 am.
- cost: Doctors and patients compare labs on how fast they get results. A habit of lateness sends the work elsewhere.
- fix: Each test has a promised time. Verity shows what is due in the next two hours, what is waiting at which step and who holds it, and what is already late with the reason noted.
- screen: owner

### 4. Home collection runs on WhatsApp and luck
- area: Collection
- scene: Eleven home visits are booked for the morning, many fasting samples that must be taken early. Two phlebotomists, one traffic jam. A fasting patient waits until noon.
- cost: Repeat visits, fasting samples taken late and a patient who books with another lab next time.
- fix: Each home visit is a scheduled record with an area, time window and collector. Verity shows each collector’s route in order, flags tight gaps and moves a visit to a free collector when someone falls behind.
- screen: collection

### 5. Collection centres and franchise partners are hard to see
- area: Collection
- scene: Six collection points feed the central lab. One sends samples at the end of the day instead of in batches. Another has the highest rejection rate. No one has a clear picture of which centre is worth keeping.
- cost: Poor sample quality, long delays before analysis and no basis for deciding which partner to support.
- fix: Every collection point is a location with its own volumes, rejection rate, time to arrive at the lab and dues. They compare side by side.
- screen: collection

### 6. A reagent runs out in the middle of a run
- area: Stock
- scene: The reagent for a test is finished on a busy morning. The next kit is on order. The batch of tests waits, and some are sent to another lab at a loss.
- cost: Delayed reports, outsourcing at a margin loss and emergency purchases at higher prices.
- fix: Each reagent has a usage rate drawn from tests run, a reorder level and a lead time. Verity checks the next days’ expected volume against the shelf and drafts an order before the lab runs short.
- screen: stock

### 7. Kits expire, lots change and nobody notices until QC fails
- area: Stock
- scene: A new lot of reagent is opened without checking that it was verified against the old one. Another kit has two weeks to expiry in the back of the fridge.
- cost: Expired kits are money in the bin. An unchecked new lot can produce a run of results that need to be repeated.
- fix: Each kit is received with lot, expiry and storage. Verity lists what expires in 60 days, and a new lot cannot be marked in use until the verification step is ticked by a named person.
- screen: stock

### 8. Calibration, QC runs and service visits are tracked in separate books
- area: Quality
- scene: The analyser’s calibration is due on Thursday. The daily controls are written in a register that has one missing day. The annual service is an email from a vendor three months ago.
- cost: A missed QC day can force a whole day’s results to be reviewed. An analyser that fails unannounced stops the lab.
- fix: Each analyser is a record with calibration dates, QC checks assigned to a named person and a service schedule with the vendor. Verity shows what has been done today and what is missing, and keeps the history searchable.
- screen: quality

### 9. Critical-value calls and report corrections are not recorded
- area: Quality
- scene: A critical result is phoned to a doctor. The call is noted on paper by whoever picked up the phone. A report is amended after a transcription fix and the patient already has the first version.
- cost: If a question arises later, the lab cannot show who was told what and when. This is both a patient-safety and a reputation risk.
- fix: Each critical call and each amended report is a logged entry with who, when and to whom. Verity shows the open ones and keeps the trail attached to the sample. It records the call and does not judge the value.
- screen: quality

### 10. Referring doctors drift to another lab and nobody sees it happen
- area: Referrers
- scene: A group of physicians sent 90 samples a month last year. This quarter it is 55. There was a delayed report in June and a missed critical call in August. Nobody connected the two.
- cost: Referral volume is the lab’s lifeblood. A quiet drop can mean a lakh of rupees a month going elsewhere.
- fix: Each sample carries its referrer. Verity shows volume by referring doctor and clinic over time, with their late reports and complaints beside it, and creates a visit or call task for the owner or the relationship manager.
- screen: referrals

### 11. Samples sent to a reference lab are lost in a shared spreadsheet
- area: Referrers
- scene: Special tests are outsourced. Each batch is listed in a sheet, sent by courier and the report returns days later. Which were sent, which are back, which are overdue, and what is owed to the reference lab?
- cost: A patient waiting on an outsourced test and a payable that is paid twice or missed.
- fix: Each outsourced test is a record with the reference lab, date sent, promised date, report received and the cost. Verity lists overdue send-outs and the payable per lab.
- screen: dues

### 12. Corporate, hospital and partner payments arrive late
- area: Money
- scene: A hospital group sends 400 tests a month on credit. A corporate health-check contract invoices at month end. A collection partner’s share depends on who sent what. Chasing is done by whoever remembers.
- cost: Cash tied up for 60 days or more, while reagent suppliers expect their payment in 30.
- fix: Each account has a credit period and a running balance. Verity shows ageing by account, lists the invoices past terms, and tasks the person who chases. Your accountant issues the invoices and raises any tax invoices.
- screen: dues

### 13. Technicians, collectors and the pathologist are unevenly loaded
- area: People
- scene: One technician is covering the afternoon alone, and the evening pathologist has 80 reports waiting. Collectors in one zone are idle while another is overbooked.
- cost: Overtime, mistakes made by tired people and delayed reports.
- fix: Shifts, leave and tasks are recorded by role. Verity shows load by bench, collector and sign-off queue and flags a pile-up before it makes reports late.
- screen: samples

### 14. Audit evidence and documents are assembled in a rush
- area: Records
- scene: An accreditation assessor or a hospital client wants SOPs, training records, QC logs and complaint records. Each is in a different folder, and some are out of date.
- cost: Weeks of preparation, missing evidence and findings that could have been avoided.
- fix: SOPs, training sign-offs, QC checks and complaints are records with owners and review dates. Verity lists documents due for review and shows evidence for any date on request.
- screen: quality

### 15. Several centres, one owner and one WhatsApp group
- area: Owner
- scene: The owner sees volumes from each centre only by message at night. Patient reports are forwarded in chat groups. Who saw what, and who has a copy, is not known.
- cost: Problems at one centre surface late, and health data sits in many phones.
- fix: Centres are locations in one system with comparable volumes, rejections, turnaround and dues. Access to patient records follows role, and each view is logged.
- screen: owner

Heading: The numbers behind these problems.
Lede: From published sources, with the source named under each figure. Your lab’s numbers will differ, and showing you yours is the first thing Verity does.
Note: The error audit is from 2008 to 2009 in a single lab and its authors note limits in how analytical errors were measured. The CareEdge share is quoted from a search summary of the note and not from the full text. The survey is from 2017. They show the scale of the issue, not what your lab will see.

- **1.4%**: of 67,438 routine blood samples in one Indian clinical laboratory had an error over a year, and 736 of the 954 errors, about three in four, happened before the sample was analysed. Haemolysed samples were the biggest cause. (Goswami et al., CCLM, via Westgard QC, 2010: https://www.westgard.com/blog/error-rates-in-an-indian-clinical-laboratory.html)
- **20 to 25%**: is the estimated share of India’s diagnostics market held by organised chains, with the rest in standalone labs, according to a credit rating agency’s November 2025 note. Independent labs compete with chains on speed and trust. (CareEdge Ratings, Indian Diagnostics Industry opinion piece, 2025: https://www.careratings.com/uploads/newsfiles/1763463740_Indian%20Diagnostics%20Industry_%20Opinion%20Piece.pdf)
- **Less automation**: was reported by Indian labs than by other Asia-Pacific labs in a survey of lab quality practice. Turnaround time was monitored more often in India, but fewer labs had a laboratory information system. (Macquarie University, Diagnostic laboratories in India: investigating quality characteristics, 2017: https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/diagnostic-laboratories-in-india-investigating-quality-characteri/)
- **11 states**: and all union territories except Delhi had adopted the Clinical Establishments Act as of December 2018. It covers diagnostic labs, and the number may have grown since. (Medical Dialogues, citing the Government of India, 2018: https://medicaldialogues.in/11-states-all-uts-except-delhi-have-adopted-clinical-establishment-act-government)

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, over four weeks.
Lede: No login and a manual left on the bench. We learn how your lab runs, build Verity around it, bring your data across and stay on afterwards.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend time at the central lab and at a collection centre. Follow a sample from collection to the signed report, see how reagent orders, QC and credit are managed today and where the registers stop agreeing.
- you: A few hours of yours and the lab manager’s. Access to the sample register, stock sheet, QC logs and the credit list.
- get: A written map of your lab: centres, collectors, tests with promised times, reagents, referrers and the problems we will fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your tests and promised turnaround times, sample steps, rejection reasons, reagents with reorder levels, analysers with calibration and QC schedules, referrers, credit accounts and your centres, then build the screens in your lab’s terms.
- you: Review the setup with your lab manager and pathologist. Correct anything that does not match how the bench runs.
- get: A working Verity with your real tests, steps and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in referrers, credit accounts, reagent stock with lots, analyser records and open send-outs from your sheets and registers. We agree how sample and report status reach Verity from your lab software. Train collectors, technicians and the desk at their own stations.
- you: Your referrer and account lists, stock sheet and any QC history. One hour per role.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a diagnostic lab works under.
Lede: Verity does not give legal or clinical advice and files nothing for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not assembling them in a hurry before an inspection.
Note: Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accreditation body, state authority or adviser.

- **Clinical Establishments Act and state registration**: what: The Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 covers laboratories offering pathological and other diagnostic services, but it applies only where a state has adopted it, and states have different rules. Minimum standards for diagnostic labs exist under it. | how: Registration details, staff qualifications and facility records are held in one place with renewal reminders. (Clinical Establishments Act, 2010 (PRS India): https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2010/the-clinical-establishments-(registration-and-regulation)-act,-2010.pdf)
- **NABL accreditation under ISO 15189**: what: Accreditation by NABL against ISO 15189 is generally voluntary. Labs that seek it must keep quality records, run internal audits and join proficiency testing. The standard has been moving to a 2022 version. | how: QC checks, calibration, training sign-offs, complaints and document reviews are records with owners and dates.
- **Bio-medical waste**: what: Under the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016, a healthcare facility that produces bio-medical waste, including a lab, generally needs authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board and must follow prescribed handling and records. | how: A daily waste checklist with a named person and a searchable history. (Central Pollution Control Board, bio-medical waste: https://cpcb.nic.in/bio-medical/)
- **Patient data protection**: what: India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. The main duties on notice and consent are expected to start on 13 May 2027. Test reports are sensitive personal data. | how: Access to patient records follows role and each view is logged, so reports are not passed around in chat groups. ## FAQ

Heading: Questions lab owners ask.

- **Does Verity see patient results?** No. Verity tracks the status of samples and reports, the stock, the people and the money around the test. Result values stay in your lab software.
- **We already have a lab information system. Do we replace it?** No. Keep it for orders, results and reports. During setup we agree how sample status, promised times and the referrer reach Verity from it.
- **Does Verity handle home collection bookings from patients?** It does not take bookings from patients. Once a visit is booked in your usual way, it becomes a scheduled record with a collector and time window that you can plan and reassign.
- **Can it help with NABL preparation?** It keeps QC checks, calibration, training sign-offs, complaints and document reviews as dated records with owners, so evidence is easy to show. It does not decide whether you meet the standard.
- **Can it track reagent lots and expiry?** Yes. Each kit is received with lot, expiry and storage, and usage is drawn from the tests run. A new lot waits for the verification tick before it is marked in use.
- **I run one lab and two collection points. Is this too much?** A small lab has the same lost samples and stock-outs with less spare time to notice them. We set up only what you need in the same four weeks.
- **Can I see all my centres together?** Yes. Each centre is a location in one system. Volumes, rejections, time to reach the lab and dues compare side by side.
- **Who can see patient data?** Only the people you allow. Access is by role, so collectors, technicians, pathologists, the desk and your accountant each see what they need. Every view and change is logged with the person and time.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of centres, staff and what you want set up. Message us and after a short conversation about how your lab runs we will give you a clear number.
