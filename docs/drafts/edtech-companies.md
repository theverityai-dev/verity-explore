## How it runs today

**The product is software. The business around it is still run by people and spreadsheets.**

- A learning app can show every video watched and every quiz taken. What it usually cannot show is the human side: what the counsellor promised, which instructor cancelled a class, whether the refund was approved, which school partner has gone quiet. That side lives in a CRM, a support inbox, a content tracker and several WhatsApp groups.
- When a learner complains, the support agent has to ask three teams to find out what happened. When a cohort slips, the first the founder hears of it is a social media post. When revenue dips, it is hard to tell whether the cause is lead quality, sales conduct, delivery or price.
- This matters more for EdTech than for most businesses, because the business sells trust to parents and students, and regulators have been watching how the sector sells and refunds.
- runs on: Sales CRM or sheet; Support inbox; Content production tracker; Learner app admin screens; WhatsApp groups for each cohort
- stack note: Each is good at its own job. None of them shows one learner’s whole story.

## Pains

Heading: Where an EdTech company loses learners, money and trust.
Lede: These are the situations founders and operations heads describe. For each: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Leads from ads and webinars are called late, or by whoever is free
- area: Sales
- scene: A webinar brings in 900 sign-ups on Saturday. By Tuesday, half have been called once and a third not at all. The best-fit leads are mixed in with the rest.
- cost: Example arithmetic: ₹400 to acquire each lead and 900 leads is ₹3.6 lakh. If a third are never called, about ₹1.2 lakh of ad spend produced nothing.
- fix: Each lead is a record with source, class or goal and an owner. Those not called within your limit show on the sales lead’s list, and each call outcome is written down so the next step is clear.
- screen: leads

### 2. What the counsellor promised on the call is nowhere in writing
- area: Sales
- scene: A parent says the counsellor said classes would start in a week and a mentor would call daily. The counsellor says nothing of the kind. Support has no record of the call.
- cost: Complaints, refunds and bad reviews. Consumer authorities raised concerns in 2022 about aggressive mis-selling to parents by EdTech companies.
- fix: Every sales call ends with a note on the learner’s record: the course, the price, the start date and any promise about mentoring or outcomes. Promises outside your approved list are flagged to the sales lead.
- screen: leads
- source: (Outlook Business on the Consumer Affairs meeting, July 2022: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/news/edtech-startups-in-india-govt-expresses-concern-over-alleged-malpractices-by-byju-s-other-edtech-companies-edtech-startup-funding-news-209376)

### 3. A cohort fills slowly and then starts late
- area: Cohorts
- scene: A cohort is planned for the 1st with 120 seats. On the 25th, 74 have paid and 20 are waiting for a call. The instructor was told to expect a full room. The start slides by a week, then another.
- cost: Learners who paid and wait become the loudest complainers. A Telangana consumer commission has ordered a refund where classes did not start for weeks.
- fix: Each cohort has a start date, seats, minimum viable size and an instructor. Verity shows paid, pending and waiting learners against the date, and flags the cohort 14 days ahead if it is likely to slip.
- screen: cohorts

### 4. Instructors cancel, swap or run over, and learners hear last
- area: Cohorts
- scene: A live class is moved at 8 pm for a 9 pm session. The cohort’s WhatsApp group knows. The learners who muted it do not.
- cost: No-shows, missed sessions and a complaint that the platform is disorganised.
- fix: Every session is a record with instructor, time and link. A change creates a task listing the learners affected, and the support team sees the changed sessions that day.
- screen: cohorts

### 5. A course goes live late because the recording chain has no owner
- area: Content
- scene: Module 6 needs a script, a recording, an edit, a check and an upload. The edit has been "almost done" for nine days. Learners are already at module 5.
- cost: Learners who finish what exists and then wait, so engagement falls exactly where it was growing.
- fix: Each lesson moves through script, recording, edit, quality check and publish, with a named person and a date at each step. The content lead sees which lessons are late and what they block.
- screen: content

### 6. Freelance instructors and editors are paid per piece and tracked in a sheet
- area: Content
- scene: Twenty freelancers record videos, set questions and edit. Payments are by lesson. A few lessons are paid twice and a few are forgotten until the creator asks.
- cost: Overpayment, late payment and the loss of good creators who lose patience.
- fix: Each piece of work is a task against the creator with a rate and a status. Approved work builds a monthly statement, and your accounts team makes the payment.
- screen: content

### 7. Learners go inactive and nobody notices until renewal
- area: Learners
- scene: A learner paid for twelve months and has not opened a lesson in five weeks. The first contact the company makes is a renewal reminder.
- cost: A learner who has not used the product does not renew and may ask for a refund. In the worst case, they post about it.
- fix: Each learner’s record carries last activity, attendance and progress as they reach Verity during setup. Those silent beyond your limit go to a mentor’s list with their goal and last topic.
- screen: learners

### 8. Mentors and teaching assistants carry uneven loads
- area: Learners
- scene: One mentor has 140 learners and another has 60. The first replies in two days, the second in two hours. Learners compare notes in the group.
- cost: Unequal service, and the busiest mentors burn out first.
- fix: Learners are assigned to mentors with a load number. Doubt tickets show age and owner, and the lead can rebalance with a few moves.
- screen: learners

### 9. Refund requests wait in an inbox while the learner posts about them
- area: Refunds
- scene: A parent asks for a refund on day 12. Support forwards the mail to finance, finance asks sales, sales asks the founder. Three weeks later the parent files a consumer complaint.
- cost: A refund that would have been ₹30,000 becomes ₹30,000 plus interest, compensation and legal time. Complaints against EdTech companies have been counted in the hundreds, and surveys have found many learners reporting issues with refunds.
- fix: A refund request is a record with the course, days attended, policy applied and a decision path with a deadline. It shows where it is stuck and how many days it has waited, and a decision is written with its reason.
- screen: refunds
- source: (Outlook Business, July 2022: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/news/edtech-startups-in-india-govt-expresses-concern-over-alleged-malpractices-by-byju-s-other-edtech-companies-edtech-startup-funding-news-209376)

### 10. Complaints from many places are never counted together
- area: Refunds
- scene: A grievance comes by email, a call, a tweet and a consumer helpline notice. Each is handled by a different person. Nobody can say how many are open or what the common cause is.
- cost: Repeat causes stay unfixed. A regulator or a consumer forum sees a pattern that the company cannot see itself.
- fix: Every complaint, from any channel, becomes one record with its cause, owner, deadline and result. A weekly view shows causes by count so a repeated problem becomes visible.
- screen: refunds

### 11. Instalments and loan-linked fees fail, and no one follows up
- area: Money
- scene: A learner joined on a monthly instalment plan. The second payment fails twice. The system marks it failed. No human sees it until the third month, when access is cut and the parent complains.
- cost: Revenue stuck and a learner cut off with no warning, which is exactly how complaints start.
- fix: Each plan has instalment dates and status as they reach Verity. A failed or late instalment becomes a task for finance with the learner’s plan and last contact, and access decisions are written down with a reason.
- screen: refunds

### 12. School and college partnerships go quiet between pilot and renewal
- area: Partners
- scene: A school pilots your platform for one grade. By March nobody has checked how many students used it. The contract renews on 31 March and the principal has not heard from you since August.
- cost: A renewal that should have been won is lost, and the replacement costs a full sales cycle.
- fix: Each partner is a record with contract dates, students enrolled, usage reached and a contact. Partners with falling use or a renewal in 90 days appear on the account owner’s list.
- screen: partners

### 13. Advertised outcomes and learner data practices are not backed by records
- area: Compliance
- scene: An ad says "1,000 students got placed". Marketing cannot say how many were on the paid course or consented to their name being used. The platform also holds children’s data with no clear consent record.
- cost: The consumer authority fined an EdTech company ₹10 lakh in 2023 for misleading ads about successful candidates it could not substantiate. Education has also been named the most violative sector in advertising reviews.
- fix: Each claim links to the learners behind it, their course, their dates and their consent. Parental consent for children’s data is stored as a record with date and scope, so it can be shown on request.
- screen: partners
- source: (Storyboard18, January 2026: https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/how-misleading-coaching-ads-are-eroding-trust-in-indias-education-sector-87793.htm)

### 14. Revenue dips and nobody can say why
- area: Owner
- scene: Monthly revenue falls 12%. Marketing blames price, sales blames lead quality, product blames the sales pitch. Each has a dashboard that supports its view.
- cost: Months spent on the wrong fix, and decisions made on the loudest voice.
- fix: Leads, calls, enrolments, cohort starts, activity and refunds are joined on the same learner record. The owner’s page shows where learners are lost, stage by stage, by source and by cohort.
- screen: owner

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you in four weeks, starting with the learner’s journey.
Lede: EdTech teams work fast, so we start with the handoffs where learners get lost, and build outward.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Meet the founders, sales lead, operations head, support lead and content lead. Follow one learner from ad click to refund request, and one cohort from planning to the last class.
- you: Two to three hours of your time and an hour from each team lead. A sample of recent refunds and complaints.
- get: A written map of your learner journey, the teams that touch it, and the three or four handoffs we will fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up courses, cohorts, plans, refund policy steps, sales call note fields, content pipeline stages, partner records and permissions, and build the screens above using your language.
- you: Review the refund policy steps and the list of promises sales may make. Correct anything we have wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your courses, cohorts and rules, ready for data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in leads, learners, cohorts, refund and complaint history, and partner contracts from your CRM, sheets and support inbox. We agree how activity data reaches Verity from your app during setup. Train each team on its own screen.
- you: Exports from the tools you use now, as they are. An hour with each team.
- get: Your learners and cohorts in Verity and teams that have used it on live cases.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Review the first cohort cycle with you, tune inactivity limits and refund timings, and set up each new course and cohort as you launch.
- you: A monthly review with the founder or operations head.
- get: A system that grows with each new course, with a named person responsible for it.

- bring: Exports of leads and learners from your CRM or sheets; Your refund and cancellation policy as written, and as applied; A list of cohorts with dates, seats and instructors; Recent complaints and refund cases, even in an inbox; Partner contracts with dates and contacts
- time: About 10 to 14 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. The sales, support and content leads need a few more.
- after: No one is hired to run Verity. Teams record the work they already do, and the pages come from those records.

## FAQ
