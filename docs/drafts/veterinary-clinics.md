## How it runs today

**Most veterinary clinics run on a register, the vet's memory and a WhatsApp group of owners.**

- Each animal needs a history: species, breed, age, weight, vaccines, deworming, past illnesses, surgeries, allergies. It is kept in a case card, a register or a simple app, and the owner's phone number is the key to find it.
- The animal base is growing. A 2025 industry report puts India's pet population at about 42 million in 2024, up from 26 million in 2019, with over 36.8 million dogs. More animals means more records, more vaccine schedules and more owners to follow up.
- The leaks are quiet: the puppy whose second vaccine was due last week, the cat's yearly booster no one remembered, the medicine that expired in the cabinet, the boarding stay that was never billed, the owner who promised to pay the balance on Saturday.
- runs on: Paper case cards or a register; A small billing app or bill book; Excel or a notebook for stock; WhatsApp with owners and suppliers; Phone calendar for surgeries and boarding
- stack note: Each tool does its own job. Nothing tells you which animals are overdue or what will run out before Saturday.

## Pains

Heading: Where a veterinary clinic loses patients and money.
Lede: These are the situations vets, clinic managers and technicians describe. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Vaccines come due and nobody calls
- area: Vaccination
- scene: A puppy had its first vaccine six weeks ago. The next is due this week. The owner has not thought about it. The clinic's register has the date, but nobody turns the pages looking for it.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 200 animals a month on a vaccine schedule, with one in four missing a due date, at ₹800 a vaccine is ₹40,000 of revenue not earned, plus animals left unprotected.
- fix: Each animal has a schedule of vaccines and boosters that your vet sets, with due dates. Verity lists what is due and overdue each week, and creates a call task. Staff call or message the owner; Verity records the outcome.
- screen: vaccines

### 2. The owner changes, the animal stays
- area: Patients
- scene: A dog is brought by the owner's son, then by a domestic helper, then by a neighbour. The owner's number on the card is old. A reminder goes to a wrong number.
- cost: A pet that appears to be lost to follow-up may only be lost in the contact details.
- fix: Each animal has an owner and an alternate contact. Phone numbers are confirmed at each visit, and a contact that fails is flagged for correction.
- screen: patients

### 3. Records are hard to find and thin
- area: Patients
- scene: An old patient returns after a year. The case card cannot be found. The vet starts again from the owner's recollection. Weight, allergies and past medicines are missing.
- cost: Repeat questions, repeat tests and a poor visit. A new vet cannot take over smoothly.
- fix: One record per animal, with visits, weights, vaccines, procedures and documents in date order. Search by owner name, phone or animal name. Verity organises what is recorded; the vet's notes stay the vet's.
- screen: patients

### 4. Same name, many animals
- area: Patients
- scene: One household has three dogs, all named by the family as "Bruno" in different years. Reports and bills get attached to the wrong one.
- cost: A wrong record is more harmful than a missing one.
- fix: Each animal has a unique ID, a photo or marking description, species, breed and birth date, and is listed under the owner. Duplicate or ambiguous names are flagged at registration.
- screen: patients

### 5. Medicines and vaccines expire in the cabinet
- area: Stock
- scene: A box of injectables is at the back of a shelf. A vaccine vial passes its date because the fridge is checked once a week. Controlled items are kept in a separate cupboard with a separate notebook.
- cost: Expired stock is paid-for stock thrown away, and a doctor who finds out at the point of use has to change plans.
- fix: Items are tracked by batch and expiry. Verity lists what expires in 30 and 60 days, flags items that need cold storage, and deducts use when a visit or procedure is recorded.
- screen: stock

### 6. Running out of the common things
- area: Stock
- scene: The clinic runs out of a common antiparasitic on a Saturday, or of sutures before a scheduled surgery. Supplier deliveries are twice a week.
- cost: A procedure postponed is a patient that may go elsewhere.
- fix: Reorder points are set by actual use, including surgery schedules. Verity drafts an order in time for the next delivery slot.
- screen: stock

### 7. Supplier bills and short deliveries
- area: Suppliers
- scene: A distributor delivers 20 vials and bills 24. A food company changes a price and the invoice carries it quietly.
- cost: Overpaying on frequently bought items compounds across months.
- fix: Receipts are checked against orders and invoices, and rates are shown over time. A mismatch is flagged before the bill is paid.
- screen: stock

### 8. Surgery, admissions and boarding on a phone calendar
- area: Wards
- scene: Two surgeries are booked for the same theatre hour. A cat admitted overnight is not on the care list. A boarding dog's feeding and medicine times are told verbally to the night attendant.
- cost: Clashes and missed care tasks harm animals, and unbilled boarding days are lost income.
- fix: Admissions, surgeries and boarding are on one schedule with rooms and cages. Each admitted animal has a task list of feeds, medicines due and checks that your vet sets, assigned to a person per shift.
- screen: wards

### 9. Charges used on a visit never reach the bill
- area: Money
- scene: A vet uses a bandage, an injection and a dressing, and the bill shows consultation only. A boarding stay of four days is billed as three.
- cost: Example arithmetic: ₹300 of unbilled items on 15 visits a week is ₹4,500 a week, over ₹19,000 a month.
- fix: Items and services are recorded against the visit as they are used. Verity compares what was used with what the bill carries and lists gaps before the bill is closed.
- screen: money

### 10. Estimates, deposits and unpaid balances
- area: Money
- scene: A family is given a rough estimate for surgery. The final bill is higher. A deposit is taken in cash and not written down. A balance is promised for next week.
- cost: Arguments at the counter, and balances that quietly become bad debts.
- fix: Estimates, deposits and running charges sit on the admission. When the running charges pass the estimate by a set share, the vet and desk are alerted so the owner is told early. Balances show by owner and age. Verity does not take payments.
- screen: money

### 11. Owner consent for procedures and euthanasia
- area: Consent
- scene: An owner consents to surgery by phone. A signed form is in a drawer. For a sensitive decision, the family has argued, and the clinic cannot show who agreed.
- cost: Without a clear record, a clinic is exposed in a dispute and cannot show what the owner understood.
- fix: Consent for procedures, anaesthesia and boarding terms is a record with a date, the person who consented and the version. The vet explains; Verity files the record.
- screen: patients

### 12. Referrals, labs and imaging
- area: Diagnostics
- scene: Blood samples go to an outside lab. Results come by WhatsApp to the vet's phone. A scan report is on the technician's laptop. Nobody knows which results are pending.
- cost: Waiting results lose time, and lost reports mean repeated tests.
- fix: Each test sent is a record with the animal, the lab and the date. Pending results are listed, and reports attach to the animal's record when they arrive.
- screen: wards

### 13. Staff, on-call and equipment
- area: People
- scene: A technician is on leave and nobody told the desk. The night attendant's duty is by WhatsApp. The anaesthesia machine is serviced when it fails.
- cost: Unplanned equipment failure postpones surgeries, and cover problems harm care.
- fix: A roster shows who is on each day and night. Equipment records hold service dates and vendors, with a task before they are due. Verity does not run payroll.
- screen: owner

### 14. Registrations, waste and a second clinic
- area: Compliance
- scene: A vet's State Veterinary Council registration renewal date is in an email. The biomedical waste authorisation and trade licence dates are in a drawer. A second clinic or a mobile unit reports in a different format.
- cost: Practising without valid registration is a legal risk. Lapsed local authorisations cause notices. Without one view, the owner is the integrator.
- fix: Registrations, authorisations and licences are records with dates and owners. Clinics are locations in one system, with visits, vaccines due, stock and dues side by side.
- screen: owner

## FAQ

- **Does Verity set the vaccination schedule?** No. Your vets decide each animal's schedule. Verity records it, lists what is due and overdue and tracks the call to the owner.
- **Does it give veterinary advice or store treatment decisions?** No. Verity records and organises: visits, schedules, stock and tasks. Clinical notes and judgement stay with your vets.
- **Can owners book or get reminders through Verity?** No. Verity does not take online bookings or send messages on its own. Your staff call or message, and Verity records who owes a call and the outcome.
- **Our animals are dogs, cats and the occasional rabbit. Does it handle that?** Yes. Species, breed, birth date, weight and a schedule are fields on the animal. We set up the species you see.
- **One owner has several animals. Can it keep them apart?** Yes. Each animal has its own ID and record under one owner, with ambiguous names flagged at registration.
- **We board animals and run surgeries. Does it track those?** Yes. Admissions, surgeries and boarding are on one schedule with rooms and cages, care tasks per shift and charges per day.
- **I have a main clinic and a satellite. Can I see them together?** Yes. Each is a location in one system, with visits, vaccines due, stock and dues side by side. Owners and animals are one record across both.
