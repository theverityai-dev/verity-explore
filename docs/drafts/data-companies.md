## How it runs today

**Most data firms track volume well and quality loosely.**

- The work is counted in units: images, documents, minutes or records. Volumes are in a tool, and instructions are in a document that changes. Quality is checked by sampling, and feedback arrives by email.
- The people are many and the turnover is high. One 2026 report on annotators described pay of ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month, strict targets and software that monitors speed and accuracy, so performance data matters, and so does how it is used.
- The commercial pressure is real. A 2026 industry page says high volume commitments compress quality review cycles, and that hundreds of vendors compete with few shared quality frameworks.
- runs on: Annotation or work tools from each client; Instruction documents and chat groups; Spreadsheets for output and quality; Attendance and payroll sheets; Email for client feedback
- stack note: Each tool shows one slice. None of them shows whether a project makes money, which team is slipping on quality or which client data is still on a machine it should not be.

## Pains

Heading: Where a data company loses margin, quality and trust.
Lede: These are the situations data firm owners and delivery heads describe. For each one: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Guidelines change and the team works from the old version
- area: Projects
- scene: A client revises the labelling rules on day six. The update goes to a chat group. Half the team keeps working to the old version for a day.
- cost: A batch with inconsistent labels, rework at your cost and a quality complaint that is hard to explain.
- fix: Each project holds its guidelines as versions with the date they took effect. A change creates a task for team leads to brief their teams and confirm, and work is tagged to the version it followed.
- screen: projects

### 2. Throughput targets squeeze quality checks
- area: Quality
- scene: The client wants 40,000 items by Friday. Review is the first thing cut so the target is met.
- cost: High-volume commitments compress review cycles, and the errors appear when the client audits. The cost of rework and lost trust is higher than the review time saved.
- fix: Each batch shows items done, items reviewed and error rate. Verity warns when review coverage falls below the level you set before a batch is sent.
- screen: quality
- source: (HanDigital on the annotation industry, 6 Apr 2026: https://www.handigital.com/?p=10449)

### 3. Annotator performance is measured, but not fairly or usefully
- area: People
- scene: Speed is tracked and accuracy is sampled. A new hire is compared with a veteran and a slow week is read as a poor worker.
- cost: Good people leave. In one 2026 report annotators described strict targets and monitoring by software, with pay of ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month.
- fix: Output and accuracy are shown per person by task type and by week, with experience and project difficulty alongside. Leads see trends and coaching needs, not only a red cell.
- screen: workforce
- source: (ThePrint, 1 Sep 2026: https://theprint.in/ground-reports/ai-is-creating-lakhs-of-low-paying-jobs-in-gurugram-noida-it-is-indias-new-bpo/3030288/)

### 4. Work queues and deadlines are juggled by hand
- area: Projects
- scene: Five projects share one floor. A team lead moves people between queues by feel. A deadline is missed because a queue was drained too slowly.
- cost: Late deliveries, overtime and idle people on a different project.
- fix: Each project has a queue of work, a deadline and an allocation of people. Verity shows queue size, daily output and the date the batch will finish at the current pace.
- screen: projects

### 5. Error types are not categorised, so the same mistakes repeat
- area: Quality
- scene: Reviewers mark items wrong. The reasons are in comments and nobody counts them.
- cost: The same error is made again by many people, and coaching is generic.
- fix: Reviewers choose an error type when they mark an item. Verity shows common errors by project, person and guideline section, so fixes aim at the cause.
- screen: quality

### 6. Projects are priced per unit and nobody knows the real unit cost
- area: Money
- scene: A project is sold at ₹4 per item. Rework, review, tool time and idle hours are not added up.
- cost: Projects that look busy and lose money. Throughput pressure and many competing vendors make price cuts tempting.
- fix: Each project tracks units delivered, hours spent by role, rework and tool cost. Verity shows cost per accepted unit against the price.
- screen: owner

### 7. Client acceptance is slow and disputed
- area: Delivery
- scene: A batch is delivered. The client takes three weeks to review and rejects 6 percent with a spreadsheet of comments. The invoice waits.
- cost: Cash held up by acceptance and disputes with no record of what was delivered and when.
- fix: Each delivery records items, the version, the date and the client's acceptance or rejection with reasons. Rejected items become rework tasks and invoices follow accepted units.
- screen: delivery

### 8. Client data spreads onto machines and chats
- area: Security
- scene: A client shares sensitive documents. Annotators view them on shared machines and a team lead forwards a sample to ask a question.
- cost: A leak ends a client relationship and may breach the contract. Data privacy concerns in sectors like banking and healthcare can require on-shore delivery.
- fix: Each project records what data it holds, who has access, where it may be processed and when it must be deleted. Access is removed when a person leaves a project, and a log shows who opened what.
- screen: security

### 9. Domain experts and language reviewers are scarce
- area: People
- scene: A medical imaging project needs reviewers with a clinical background. Only two are available and both are booked on another project.
- cost: Delays or reviews done by people who lack the skill, and quality that suffers for the sensitive part of the work.
- fix: People have skills, languages and certifications recorded. Verity shows who can review a type of work and their load, so scarce reviewers are planned.
- screen: workforce

### 10. Tools and licences are tracked per client
- area: Projects
- scene: Each client asks for a different annotation tool. Licences and seats are bought, and some stay unused after a project ends.
- cost: Money spent on seats nobody uses and delays when a person has no access on day one.
- fix: Each tool and its seats are records linked to projects and people. Verity lists unused seats and projects that need access.
- screen: owner

### 11. Recurring data deliveries and refresh cycles are scheduled in calendars
- area: Delivery
- scene: A client buys a dataset refreshed monthly. A delivery date slips because the source update was late and nobody told the client.
- cost: A client who counts missed refreshes at renewal and credits that are argued.
- fix: Each recurring delivery has a schedule, an owner and checks before it ships. A late source creates a task, and a renewal list shows what each client has received.
- screen: delivery

### 12. Invoices follow memory, not accepted work
- area: Money
- scene: Invoices are raised from a spreadsheet of units. Some include items later rejected, some leave out accepted items, and a foreign client pays late.
- cost: Credit notes, delayed cash and an accounts team that reconciles by hand.
- fix: Billable units come from accepted deliveries. Verity lists work accepted and not invoiced, and invoices overdue by client, with a task for the account owner. Billing stays in your accounts software.
- screen: delivery

### 13. Personal data duties are written, not tracked
- area: Compliance
- scene: Contracts say the firm will delete data at the end and report any breach. Nobody can show that it did.
- cost: A client audit that fails, or a dispute after an incident. India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025, with most duties applying over the next 12 to 18 months.
- fix: Each project has deletion dates, breach contacts and the steps to follow. Verity lists projects whose data is due for deletion and records that it was done.
- screen: security
- source: (AZB Partners on the DPDP framework: https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)

### 14. Hiring and training come in waves
- area: People
- scene: A large new project needs 80 people in two weeks. Hiring, training and quality ramp-up are tracked in separate sheets.
- cost: New people on live work before they are ready, and early errors that hurt a new client's first impression.
- fix: Each batch of new people has a training plan, assessments and a probation period. A person starts live work only after the assessment, and early quality is tracked separately.
- screen: workforce
