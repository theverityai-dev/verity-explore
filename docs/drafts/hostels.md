## How it runs today

**Most hostels know their residents by name and their dues by rough memory.**

- The corridor is easy to see. Who is on the second floor, which room has the loud roommate, which bed became free last week. What is hard to see is what sits behind it: how many beds are empty and for how long, who has not paid this month and how many days late, which deposits are held and against what, how many plates the mess cooked against how many ate, and whether every resident, including foreign students and working guests, has the verification the local police asked for.
- That information lives in pieces. A register or sheet holds names and rent. Payments come as cash, UPI and bank transfers. Meals are a headcount shouted at the cook. The gate book is a ruled notebook. Police verification forms are in a file or on the warden's phone. The owner, who is often also the warden, holds the rest in memory.
- A 2020 study of Bengaluru PGs found operators on three to five year leases, with occupancy averaging about 80 percent before the lockdown and about 10 percent after. That was an extreme year, but it shows the shape of the business: rent to the landlord is fixed, and every empty bed is a loss from day one. In Delhi alone, the municipal corporation has identified 1,355 buildings running PG facilities, housing about 27,000 people, in a sector with no PG-specific licence.
- runs on: Resident register or Excel sheet; Cash, UPI and bank transfers; Gate book and visitor notebook; WhatsApp groups for residents and parents; Mess headcount on a notice board
- stack note: Each one does its job. None knows about the others, and the owner finds out who has not paid only when the landlord's rent falls due.

## Pains

Heading: Where a hostel actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations hostel owners and wardens describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Beds, not rooms, are the product and nobody counts them
- area: Beds
- scene: A triple-sharing room has two residents and one bed free. A double room became single when a roommate left. The sheet shows "room 12 full" because the first name was never updated. A caller asks for one bed and is told there is none.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 6 beds idle for 3 months at ₹7,500 is ₹1.35 lakh. A bed that is free but not known to be free is the same as an empty bed.
- fix: Every bed is a record with its room, type of sharing, rent and state: occupied, notice given, reserved or free. The warden and the owner see one board, and each bed shows how many days it has been empty.
- screen: beds

### 2. Notice periods and the semester gap
- area: Beds
- scene: Five final-year residents will leave when exams end. Three said so verbally, two did not. Nobody knows in June how many beds will open in July, and the new batch of students starts looking for a PG in the same month.
- cost: A bed that opens without notice is a bed without a plan. Beds that open at the wrong time of year can stay empty until the next intake.
- fix: Notice is a date on the resident record. Verity shows beds expected to open by month, with the number already reserved, so the owner starts selling them weeks ahead. Your team talks to the families; Verity shows who needs a call.
- screen: beds

### 3. Enquiries, visits and token advances on WhatsApp
- area: Leads
- scene: A parent calls from another city and asks for photos. A student visits on Saturday and says "I will confirm". A token of ₹2,000 is paid by UPI and the bed is promised. A second student is promised the same bed on Monday.
- cost: A bed sold twice means one family is let down, and a token with no record can mean an argument and a refund. A slow reply loses a student to the next PG on the road.
- fix: Each enquiry is a record with the beds asked for, dates, visit time, token paid and next step. A token reserves a specific bed on the board. Verity lists enquiries with no reply, and tells the person responsible. Staff still reply to the family.
- screen: leads

### 4. Rent due on the fifth, collected by the fifteenth
- area: Money
- scene: Thirty-five residents pay by UPI, ten in cash, a few in instalments "after the stipend". The sheet shows who paid on one date, not who paid for which month. By the tenth the warden is making calls, and some families are called three times.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 12 residents paying 10 days late on ₹8,000 a month means ₹96,000 sitting outside your account for 10 days, while the landlord's rent and the cook's wages are due on time.
- fix: Each resident has a monthly due with an amount and date. Payments are recorded against the month they cover. Verity lists who is late and by how many days, and creates a follow-up task for the warden. The receipt is made the way you make it today; Verity compares it to what was recorded.
- screen: dues

### 5. Security deposits and refunds that turn into arguments
- area: Money
- scene: A resident leaves and asks for the full ₹10,000 deposit. The owner remembers a broken window and two months of electricity unpaid. The resident says no one told him. The notebook has no line for either.
- cost: A refund argued by memory costs goodwill, and an under-refund can turn into a complaint to parents or police. An over-refund is lost cash.
- fix: The deposit is held on the resident record. Damages, unpaid charges and notice shortfalls are logged during the stay with a date and photo reference. At exit, Verity shows the deposit, the deductions and the refund due, so the conversation starts from the same page.
- screen: dues

### 6. Electricity, water and common charges split by guess
- area: Money
- scene: Electricity is shared among the residents in a room, and the room's air-conditioner use is higher in summer. Some PGs split charges by unit, some add a fixed amount. A resident questions the ₹600 line and no one can show how it was calculated.
- cost: Newslaundry reports Delhi PGs billing tenants ₹12 to ₹14 a unit for power, split among residents. When the split is unclear, small charges become the largest source of complaints.
- fix: Meter readings or the fixed rule are recorded per room and month. The share for each bed is calculated by the rule you set, and the resident sees the line item. The owner sees the month's electricity bill against what was collected.
- screen: dues

### 7. Plates cooked against plates eaten
- area: Mess
- scene: The cook prepares for sixty. On Sunday, thirty are out; on Thursday, the dal is thin because more guests ate than expected. Residents who are travelling still pay for meals they do not eat, and complain about the menu in the group.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 12 plates a day cooked and not eaten at ₹60 of cost each is ₹720 a day, about ₹21,600 a month. Too few plates is a complaint, and a complaint is a lost renewal.
- fix: Each resident's meal plan is on their record, with skipped meals marked ahead. The cook sees the headcount by meal. Verity compares plates cooked with plates eaten and total food bought, and lists the meals where the gap is widest.
- screen: mess

### 8. Mess purchases, vendors and the licence
- area: Mess
- scene: Rice, vegetables, milk and gas come from five vendors, paid in cash by the cook or the warden. Prices change on the bill. The FSSAI registration is in a drawer and nobody can say when the kitchen was last cleaned.
- cost: Unrecorded purchases hide waste and theft. A hostel that serves meals generally needs FSSAI registration or a licence, and an inspection with no papers is costly.
- fix: Purchases are recorded with rate, quantity and vendor, and compared with plates cooked. Verity shows what is owed to each vendor and flags a rate that moved. The FSSAI registration is a record with a number and date, and kitchen cleaning is a daily checklist.
- screen: mess

### 9. Resident ID and police verification kept on the warden's phone
- area: Compliance
- scene: A new resident hands over an Aadhaar copy and a college ID. The warden photographs them. The police verification form goes in a file, or does not go at all because the resident is "joining next week". Six months later nobody can say who was verified.
- cost: In Pune, police booked four PG operators in July 2026 under Section 223 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for not giving tenant details, after a 3 July order from the Special Branch. Delhi landlords must also inform police under Delhi Police rules, per press reports. Rules differ by city.
- fix: Each resident has a verification record: documents seen, who checked them, date submitted to the police station and the acknowledgement. Verity lists residents whose verification is pending, with the days since joining. Your team submits to the police; Verity records it.
- screen: entry

### 10. Foreign students and the 24-hour form
- area: Compliance
- scene: An exchange student joins for a semester. Her passport copy is taken. The online report to the authorities waits until the warden has time, and the first week passes.
- cost: Under the Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, accommodation providers including hostels and PGs generally must report a foreign resident within 24 hours of arrival. A Goa notice states a penalty of ₹50,000 in each case; other states may differ.
- fix: A foreign resident's record shows the form status and the time left from arrival. Verity raises a task for the person on duty and keeps the acknowledgement. Your team files on the government portal.
- screen: entry

### 11. Curfew, late entry and visitors in a notebook
- area: Residents
- scene: The gate closes at 10:30 pm. A resident returns at 11:20 and the guard writes it, or does not. A parent visits on Sunday, a friend is "just dropping a book". A resident who has not come back at midnight is a worry that nobody knows how to escalate.
- cost: A hostel that cannot say who is in the building cannot answer a parent, a police officer or a fire marshal. Curfew rules are also a common source of friction, so a fair record protects both sides.
- fix: Entry and exit are logged against the resident, with the curfew rule that applies to them. Visitors are logged with whom they met, time in and out. Verity lists residents not back by the curfew and tells the warden, who calls. Rules are set by you and agreed with residents; Verity applies them evenly.
- screen: entry

### 12. Complaints about water, wi-fi and roommates
- area: Residents
- scene: The hot water stopped three days ago. The wi-fi drops each evening. A resident asks to change rooms because of a noisy roommate. Each complaint is a message to the warden, and answers vary with who was on duty.
- cost: Repeat complaints are the usual reason a resident does not renew, and the usual reason a parent calls the owner.
- fix: Each complaint is a task on the resident and room with an owner, a time and a state. Verity lists complaints past their time and room-change requests with the available beds beside them. The outcome is recorded, so a pattern shows by floor or by month.
- screen: upkeep

### 13. Repairs that wait because nobody owns them
- area: Maintenance
- scene: A leaking tap, a broken lock, a fan that does not turn. The plumber comes when the owner remembers. At move-out, a damaged item is found and argued about.
- cost: Small faults make a bed hard to sell, and unrecorded damage cannot be charged to the person who caused it.
- fix: A fault is a work order with the room, who reported it, priority and the person assigned. A room with a serious fault is blocked until the order is closed. A walk-through at check-in and check-out records the state of the room.
- screen: upkeep

### 14. Fire safety, registration and local licences
- area: Compliance
- scene: After a PG fire in Chandigarh killed three students in 2020, the administration said every PG must be registered, hold a fire NOC and face annual checks. In Delhi, the MCD does not register PGs at all, and one landlord said NOCs are hard to get because two staircases are required. In your city, the position may be different again.
- cost: A notice, a closure order or, far worse, a fire in a building that failed its checks. A lapsed certificate or an unmarked exit is not worth the risk.
- fix: Each registration, fire certificate, extinguisher refill and wiring check is a record with a date and an owner, and a renewal is a task weeks ahead. Weekly walks check exits, extinguishers and corridors. Verity does not give legal advice; it makes sure nothing slips.
- screen: upkeep
- source: (Tribune India, 2020: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/crackdown-on-illegal-pgs-7-shut-operations-47242/amp)

### 15. Fixed rent to the landlord, variable income from the beds
- area: Owner
- scene: You lease the building for five years and pay the landlord on the first. In a good month, the hostel is full. After exams, twenty beds are empty for six weeks. A rent revision is due.
- cost: The Bengaluru study found operators on three to five year leases with occupancy that fell from about 80 to 10 percent in the pandemic. In a normal year the swings are smaller, but the rent is just as fixed.
- fix: Verity shows income by month against the fixed rent, the occupied-bed trend, notices expected and beds reserved for the next intake. The owner sees whether the next three months cover the lease before signing a renewal.
- screen: owner

### 16. Several hostels, or a hostel and a mess, in one WhatsApp group
- area: Owner
- scene: A second block opens near the college gate. The warden of each sends a nightly message in a different style. The owner cannot compare occupancy, dues or complaints.
- cost: You cannot see which block leaks, so the same leak runs in both.
- fix: Each block is a location in one system, with beds, dues, complaints, mess use and licence dates side by side. Each morning Verity lists what needs a decision, each with an owner.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a hostel has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when an officer asks.
Note: Rules for hostels and PGs differ widely by city and state, and several are set by local police orders. Confirm current requirements with your accountant, your local police and the relevant authority.

- **Police verification of residents**: what: Many cities require PG and hostel operators to give police the details of residents. In Pune, a 3 July 2026 order from the Special Branch required PG owners to give tenant details to police, and four operators were booked under Section 223 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for not doing so. Delhi landlords are also required to inform police of tenants, per press reports. Ask your local police station for the current form and timeline. | how: Each resident has a verification record with documents seen, who checked them, date submitted and the acknowledgement. Pending ones are listed with days since joining. (Free Press Journal, 2026: https://freepressjournal.in/pune/pune-police-book-4-pg-operators-in-chandan-nagar-for-failing-to-verify-tenants)
- **Foreign resident reporting**: what: Under the Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, in force from 1 September 2025, accommodation providers including hostels and PGs generally must report a foreign resident online within 24 hours of arrival, through the FRRO portal. A Goa notice of December 2025 states a penalty of ₹50,000 in each case; other states may differ. | how: The resident's record shows form status and time left, and a task goes to the person on duty. The acknowledgement is stored. (Right to Information wiki, 2026: https://righttoinformation.wiki/immigration-foreigners-act-2025-hotel-hospital-reporting-india)
- **Tax on accommodation rent**: what: Residential accommodation rent of ₹20,000 or less per person per month for stays of 90 days or longer is generally exempt from GST. Shorter stays, higher rent or bundled services may be taxed at 12 percent, so confirm your own position with your accountant. | how: Rent, stay length and any bundled meals are recorded per resident, so your accountant can see which stays fall on either side. Verity does not file returns. (ClearTax, 2026: https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-hostel-and-pg)
- **FSSAI registration for the mess**: what: A hostel or PG that serves meals to residents generally needs FSSAI registration or a licence, with the band set by turnover. Check your own band and category on the FoSCoS portal. | how: The registration is a record with its number, category and renewal date, and the daily kitchen checklist is signed off by the cook.
- **Fire safety, registration and local licences**: what: Many cities expect PGs and hostels to be registered locally and to hold fire safety clearance. In Chandigarh, after a PG fire in 2020, the administration said every PG must be registered, hold a fire NOC and face annual checks. In Delhi, the municipal corporation does not register PGs, per a 2026 report. Rules differ by place. | how: Each certificate and check is a record with a date and owner, and a renewal becomes a task weeks ahead. (Tribune India, 2020: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/crackdown-on-illegal-pgs-7-shut-operations-47242/amp)

## FAQ

Heading: Questions hostel owners ask.

- **I run one PG with 30 beds and a part-time warden. Is this too much?** A small hostel has the same leaks and less time to notice them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.
- **Does Verity collect rent from residents?** No. Residents pay the way they do today: UPI, bank or cash. Verity records each payment against the resident and the month it covers, and shows who is late.
- **Can it show me bed-level occupancy, not just room-level?** Yes. Every bed is a record with its room, sharing type and rent, so a triple room with two residents shows one free bed and how many days it has been empty.
- **How does it help with deposits and refunds?** The deposit sits on the resident record. Damage and unpaid charges are logged during the stay, and at exit Verity shows the deposit, the deductions and the refund, so both sides see the same numbers.
- **Will my warden and guard actually record entries and visitors?** Only if it takes seconds, so we set it up as one tap or one line per entry, kept at the gate, and train during a real evening. If the gate has no phone or tablet, the warden can enter the book at the end of each shift.
- **Can it submit the police verification or foreign resident form?** No. Your team submits them to the police or the government portal. Verity shows who is pending, how many days or hours are left, and records the acknowledgement.
- **Can it help with the mess?** Yes. It records meal plans and skipped meals so the cook gets a headcount, compares plates cooked with plates eaten and food bought, and tracks what vendors charge. It does not run a kitchen display or take food orders.
- **I run a boys block and a girls block. Can I see them together?** Yes. Each is a location in one system with its own warden, curfew rules and mess, and you see beds, dues and complaints side by side.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so the warden, cook, guard and accountant each see what they need, and every change is recorded with who made it and when. Residents' ID and verification records are visible only to the roles you choose.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of beds, blocks and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your hostel runs.
