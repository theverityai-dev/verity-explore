## How it runs today

**A college office is a set of busy desks that rarely see each other's work.**

- Admissions has a list. Accounts has a fee register. The exam cell has attendance and internal marks. The departments have timetables. The principal gets a summary when someone has time to make one. The student is the same person in all four places, but there is no single view of them.
- Seat pressure is real. A Careers360 report from November 2024 found tens of thousands of engineering seats vacant after counselling in several states, even as intake grew. Whatever the course, a college that cannot see its funnel in time cannot act on it.
- Rules sit on top. Fee refund tiers, university attendance requirements, scholarship records, AISHE data and accreditation files all draw on the same student and faculty records, and they are rebuilt for each purpose.
- runs on: Admission forms and Excel lists; Fee register and receipt book; Department attendance registers; Exam cell spreadsheets; WhatsApp groups for staff and students
- stack note: Each one works inside its own office. The student's whole story is in none of them.

## Pains

Heading: Where a college actually loses seats, money and time.
Lede: These are the situations principals, registrars, accountants and department heads describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Enquiries go cold before counselling ends
- area: Admissions
- scene: In June the office gets two hundred calls and walk-ins. Names are on slips and in two phones. A parent who visited twice is never called back, and a rival college rings her on the day she decides.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 20 lost admissions at ₹90,000 a year is ₹18 lakh in a single year, and each student would normally pay for three or four years.
- fix: Every enquiry is a record with course, rank or marks, source, visits and next step. Verity lists enquiries with no follow-up and assigns each to a named counsellor, with the outcome recorded after the call.
- screen: admissions

### 2. Seats stay empty because nobody sees the funnel
- area: Admissions
- scene: The principal asks in July how many seats are filled in each course. The answer is a number from yesterday in one department and last week in another. A Careers360 report found 54,587 vacant engineering seats in Tamil Nadu after the third round of counselling in 2024.
- cost: A vacant seat is fee that never arrives, against faculty and lab costs that continue. In 2024-25 Tamil Nadu alone added 39,978 engineering seats while many stayed empty.
- fix: Verity shows seats, offers, acceptances, fees paid and admissions by course and category in one view, updated as work happens, so a gap is visible while there is still a round or a spot admission left to act on.
- screen: principal
- source: (Careers360 on empty engineering seats, 2024: https://news.careers360.com/btech-admissions-2024-2025-lakhs-seats-empty-cse-private-engineering-colleges-tamil-nadu-gujarat-karnataka-andhra-pradesh-aicte)

### 3. Document verification is a pile of photocopies
- area: Admissions
- scene: A student arrives with a mark sheet, a migration certificate, a caste or income certificate and a transfer certificate. One is missing. The clerk writes it on a form that is lost by the time the student returns.
- cost: Students admitted on incomplete papers cause trouble with the university, with scholarships and with later certificates. Students delayed at the counter go elsewhere.
- fix: Each applicant has a document checklist. Verity shows what is received, verified, pending or wrong, who verified it and when, and lists admitted students who still owe papers.
- screen: admissions

### 4. Fee instalments are chased from memory
- area: Fees
- scene: Semester fees are due in three instalments. Scholarships and bank loans delay some. A student with a concession cannot be told at the counter what is still pending without checking three sheets.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 1,800 students with ₹60,000 a semester is ₹10.8 crore. A fortnight's delay on one student in ten is ₹1.08 crore not in the account while salaries run.
- fix: Each student has a fee plan with instalments, scholarship or loan status and concessions. Verity lists dues by course and age and assigns a follow-up task. Your staff speak to the student; the promise and the outcome are recorded.
- screen: fees

### 5. Scholarship and fee-reimbursement claims slip through
- area: Fees
- scene: Dozens of students are on state or central scholarships. The college has to confirm enrolment, attendance and fee details on the relevant portals. Claims are filed late for students whose papers were incomplete.
- cost: The student pays from their own pocket meanwhile, or the college waits for money it has already counted as income.
- fix: Scholarship students are tagged with scheme, application status, documents and the amount expected. Verity lists claims not yet filed, rejected or pending, and the money the college is waiting on.
- screen: fees

### 6. Withdrawals and refunds are handled case by case
- area: Fees
- scene: A student gets a government seat and withdraws in August. The office has to decide how much of the fee is refundable, by when, and whether the seat can still be filled.
- cost: Colleges generally follow tiered refund schedules tied to the last date of admission. One college's policy page, citing UGC guidelines, refunds the fee less ₹1,000 at 15 or more days before that date, then 90%, 80%, 50% and nothing after 30 days. A wrong refund, or a late one, becomes a grievance.
- fix: Withdrawal requests are records with their date, the rule that applies, the amount calculated and who approved it. Verity lists refunds not paid within your target number of days.
- screen: fees
- source: (RKNEC, refund policy as per UGC guidelines 2025: https://www.rknec.edu/?p=153478)

### 7. Attendance is kept by each teacher and reconciled at exam time
- area: Attendance and exams
- scene: Three weeks before the university exams, the department head collects attendance sheets from every teacher to see who is short of the required percentage. The numbers differ from what students say.
- cost: Students barred from exams create disputes, appeals and last-minute condonation requests. A college without a record cannot defend its own list.
- fix: Attendance is recorded daily by subject. Verity shows each student's percentage against the requirement set by the university, with those at risk listed early. Condonation requests, reasons and approvals are recorded on the student.
- screen: attendance

### 8. Internal marks, practicals and results sit in separate sheets
- area: Attendance and exams
- scene: Internal assessments are in each teacher's file. Practical marks are on a form. When the university asks for them, the exam cell collects, re-types and checks them against each other.
- cost: Typing errors in marks are the kind of mistake a student sees first. Late submission of internals can hold up results.
- fix: Internal and practical marks are entered once against the student and subject, with who entered them and when. Verity shows missing entries by subject and teacher before the university deadline.
- screen: attendance

### 9. Exam logistics: halls, invigilators and hall tickets
- area: Attendance and exams
- scene: The exam cell builds the seating plan and invigilation duty list on a spreadsheet. A teacher is rostered in two halls at the same time. Hall tickets for students with unpaid dues are held by hand.
- cost: Chaos on exam day and a risk of a malpractice case that comes from a lapse in supervision.
- fix: Exam sessions, halls, capacity and invigilator duties are records. Verity flags a clash, lists students whose hall tickets are held because of dues or attendance, and records who released each one.
- screen: faculty

### 10. Faculty workload is uneven and invisible
- area: Faculty
- scene: One senior teacher takes 22 hours a week, another takes 12. Guest faculty are called when someone is on leave, and nobody knows how many guest lectures have been paid for.
- cost: Burnout for some, idle time for others, and guest-faculty costs that are only noticed at month end.
- fix: The timetable is a record with teacher, subject, class, room and hours. Verity shows load by teacher against your norm, free slots for cover and the guest lectures taken, so an uneven load is seen and corrected.
- screen: faculty

### 11. Faculty qualifications and renewals are in cupboards
- area: Faculty
- scene: An inspection committee asks for the qualifications, appointment letters and experience of every faculty member in a department. The registrar goes through a stack of files.
- cost: Hours of work and a risk of a gap being found only in front of the committee. Regulators and accreditation bodies set norms on faculty, so incomplete files hurt.
- fix: Each faculty member has a record with qualifications, appointment, experience, publications, leave and contract dates. Verity lists missing documents and upcoming contract renewals.
- screen: faculty

### 12. Accreditation and survey data are rebuilt from scratch
- area: Records
- scene: AISHE, NIRF, NAAC and university affiliation each ask for student, faculty and infrastructure data in a different format. Each year the IQAC team collects the same numbers again.
- cost: Weeks of staff time. AISHE data is also used by scholarship portals, so errors carry beyond the college. The AISHE 2024-25 round had a final submission deadline of 15 November 2025 at one university.
- fix: Students, faculty, programmes and facilities are kept as one set of records. Verity helps the team assemble the figures for each survey from the same data and shows entries with gaps. Your staff submit on the official portals.
- screen: records
- source: (University of Kashmir circular on AISHE 2024-25: https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/events/10374.pdf)

### 13. Certificates, transcripts and no-dues clearances take days
- area: Records
- scene: A final-year student needs a bonafide, a no-dues certificate and a transcript before a placement joining date. The request goes from the office to the library, the hostel, the accounts section and the department, on paper.
- cost: Lost placements, anxious students and clerks who spend a day chasing signatures.
- fix: Each request is a workflow with steps for the library, hostel, accounts and department. Verity shows where a request is stuck and who must act, and the certificate is drawn from the same student record.
- screen: records

### 14. Student retention and drop-outs are noticed late
- area: Students
- scene: A first-year student stops coming in October. The department thinks the office knows. The office thinks the department knows. By the time anyone calls, he has taken a transfer elsewhere.
- cost: A lost student also loses the fee for the remaining years and affects the college's results.
- fix: Verity combines attendance, dues, marks and hostel presence and flags students showing several warning signs at once. A mentor gets a task, and the conversation and outcome are recorded.
- screen: attendance

### 15. Placement and alumni records are not connected to the student
- area: Students
- scene: The placement cell keeps a sheet of company visits and offers. Nobody records which students had which interviews, who rejected an offer, or where the batch is two years later.
- cost: Placement is a major reason a student chooses a college. Claims made in the brochure can only be backed with a record.
- fix: Company visits, shortlists, offers and outcomes are recorded against the student. Verity keeps alumni contact and current employer on the same record for later batches.
- screen: records

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks.
Lede: You do not get a login and a manual. We spend time in your admissions office, accounts section and exam cell, build Verity around how your college really runs, bring your records in, then stay to make sure it keeps working.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend time at the admissions desk, the accounts counter and in the exam cell. Follow an applicant from enquiry to enrolment and a student from admission to a final certificate, and list the first problems to close.
- you: Two to three hours of the principal's and registrar's time. Last year's admission list, fee structure by course, timetable, faculty list and the university's attendance rule.
- get: A written map of your college: courses, seat matrix, fee plans, faculty, steps for certificates and the first problems we will close.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your courses and seat matrix, fee plans and refund rules, attendance requirement, timetable, faculty records, exam sessions and certificate workflows. Build the six screens in your college's terms.
- you: Check the setup with the admissions head, the accountant and the exam cell. Tell us where a rule or a step is wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your real courses, fees and staff, ready for your records.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in your student list, applicants, fee ledgers, faculty files and marks from the sheets and registers you use. Train admissions, accounts, departments and the exam cell at their own desks.
- you: The data in whatever form it exists, and the pending dues as of today. One hour with each role.
- get: Your students, fees, faculty and attendance inside Verity, and a team that has used it through a working week.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first weeks with you, adjust limits, and handle changes at the start of a new session, new courses and exam periods.
- you: A short monthly review with the principal and the registrar.
- get: A system that keeps matching how the college runs, with someone responsible for it.

- bring: The seat matrix and fee structure by course; Last year's admission and applicant lists; The faculty list with qualifications and contract dates; The timetable and attendance rule; Scholarship, refund and concession policies
- time: About 9 to 13 hours of the principal's or registrar's own time across the four weeks, mostly in weeks 1 and 3. The admissions head, accountant and exam cell need a few more.
- after: Nobody is hired to run Verity. Admissions, fees and attendance are recorded as part of work the office already does, and the lists come from those records.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a college has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so you are not hunting for them when someone asks.
Note: Rules differ by university, regulator and state, and they change. Confirm current requirements with your affiliating university, UGC or AICTE and your adviser.

- **UGC fee refund framework**: what: UGC guidelines generally require colleges to refund fees on a sliding scale when a student withdraws before or soon after the last admission date, and the UGC grievance regulations of 2023 treat delay in a refund as a grievance. Exact percentages and deadlines vary by notification and session. | how: Each withdrawal is a record with its date, the tier applied, the amount and the approval, and refunds past your target days are listed. (RKNEC refund policy citing UGC guidelines: https://www.rknec.edu/?p=153478)
- **AISHE and accreditation data**: what: Colleges are expected to submit survey data to AISHE each year, and NAAC accreditation requires institutional data. NAAC has been moving to a binary Accredited or Not Accredited outcome from 2025, according to one vendor summary, so confirm with NAAC. | how: Students, faculty and facilities are kept as one set of records, with gaps flagged. Your team submits on the official portals. (University of Kashmir circular on AISHE 2024-25: https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/events/10374.pdf)
- **University attendance and exam rules**: what: Affiliating universities set minimum attendance and internal assessment rules for eligibility to sit exams. The percentage and condonation process differ by university. | how: Attendance, internals and condonation are recorded against each student and subject, with at-risk students listed early.
- **Faculty and approval norms**: what: Regulators such as UGC and AICTE set faculty qualification and student-faculty norms for approval of programmes, and visits by committees ask for staff files. Norms differ by programme. | how: Faculty qualifications, appointments and experience are records, and missing documents are listed.
- **Children's and students' data under the DPDP Act**: what: The DPDP Act and Rules apply to personal data of students, and verifiable parental consent applies to anyone under 18, which includes some first-year students. | how: Role-based access and an audit trail show who saw or changed each record.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions college principals and registrars ask.

- **Does Verity run our admissions portal?** No. If you take applications on your own site or the state portal, that keeps working. Verity holds the applicant record from enquiry to enrolment, with documents, offers and fees, so no one is lost between desks.
- **We follow our affiliating university's rules. Can they be set up?** Yes. The attendance requirement, internal marks structure and fee refund rules are set up in configuration, and we ask you to confirm each one. We do not promise the rules are right for your university without that check.
- **Can it handle several programmes and a second campus?** Yes. Each campus is a location in one system, with courses, fees, faculty and students side by side. The principal sees all of it and each head sees their own.
- **How does it help with scholarships?** Scholarship students are tagged with scheme, documents and claim status, and Verity lists claims not filed or not paid, with the amount the college is waiting for. Staff still file on the relevant portals.
- **Can students log in and see their own record?** We do not claim a student app. Today staff see everything and tell students what they need. Ask us on the first call if you have a specific need.

- **We have a lot of Excel. Will it all move?** Yes, in stages, starting with applicants, students and fees. Older years of marks can follow later so the work in front of you is not held up.
- **Who can see student data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and department, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of students, campuses and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your college runs.
