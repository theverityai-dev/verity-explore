## How it runs today

**Most language institutes teach well and run the front desk on a notebook.**

- A typical institute runs German, French, Japanese, English or IELTS batches at different levels, at different times, often in the evening and at weekends. Each batch has a teacher, a room or an online link, a start date and a minimum number of learners to make it viable. One public German A1 course in Kerala, for example, ran 80 hours with a 30-seat cap and a minimum of 10 candidates.
- Demand is real. The Goethe-Institut reported about 25,000 course participations and 192,000 exam participations in India in its January 2026 release. A small institute sees that demand as a hundred calls a month and a front desk that can only answer so many.
- The information that decides the business is scattered: who enquired, who took a trial, which level each learner is on, who has attended four of six sessions, who has paid for next term, and who has booked the exam. It lives in WhatsApp, a notebook and the teacher's memory.
- runs on: Enquiry notebook and phone contacts; Excel for batches and fees; WhatsApp groups per batch; Teacher attendance sheets; Exam registrations on the exam body's own site
- stack note: Each one works for one task. None of them tells you that a batch is about to fall below its minimum.

## Pains

Heading: Where a language institute actually loses learners and money.
Lede: These are the situations owners, coordinators and teachers describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Enquiries are not followed up
- area: Enquiries
- scene: Forty people ask about German A1 in the month before a batch starts. Names are in a notebook and on two phones. A learner who asked about timings on the 3rd is called on the 19th, after another institute has already enrolled him.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 40 enquiries, 10 enrolments at ₹14,000 is ₹1.4 lakh. If better follow-up converts four more, that is ₹56,000 on one batch alone.
- fix: Every enquiry is a record with the language, level wanted, timing, source and next step. Verity lists enquiries with no follow-up and assigns each to a named person. Staff make the call or send the message; the outcome is recorded.
- screen: enquiries

### 2. Trial classes are booked, forgotten and never converted
- area: Enquiries
- scene: A learner is told to come for a free demo on Saturday. The teacher is not told, the room is double-booked, and nobody calls her on Monday to ask whether she wants to join.
- cost: A trial is the best sales moment an institute has, and without a follow-up it is only a free class.
- fix: A trial is a record with date, teacher, level and the learner. Verity reminds the teacher, lists trials attended and not followed up, and records the decision: joined, thinking or declined, with the reason.
- screen: enquiries

### 3. Batches start below the minimum or run too full
- area: Batches
- scene: A batch of nine is run because it was announced. Another has 26 learners in a room built for 18. The coordinator finds out when the teacher complains.
- cost: A batch below the minimum loses money every session. One that is too full loses learners who feel unseen. A public example batch needed at least 10 learners and capped at 30.
- fix: Each batch has a minimum, a cap and a break-even number you set. Verity shows batches by learners enrolled and flags those under the minimum or over the cap, early enough to merge, move or close the batch.
- screen: batches

### 4. Levels and placement are decided by feel
- area: Batches
- scene: A learner who says she knows some French is put into A2. After two weeks she cannot follow the class, and a learner at the right level in the same batch is slowed down.
- cost: A learner in the wrong level is a learner who drops out, and her story is told to friends.
- fix: A placement result is recorded on the learner, with the level, the evaluator and the date. Verity shows learners whose attendance or marks suggest the wrong level so a coordinator can move them in the first two weeks.
- screen: learners

### 5. Attendance gaps are noticed after the learner has left
- area: Learners
- scene: A working learner misses four of the first six German classes. The teacher assumes he is busy. Nobody calls. At week eight he does not come back.
- cost: Dropping out part way through a level is the largest silent leak in a language institute, and the next-level fee goes with it.
- fix: Attendance is recorded per session. Verity lists learners who miss more than your limit, with their pattern, and gives the teacher or coordinator a task to speak to them. What the learner said is recorded.
- screen: learners

### 6. Make-up classes are arranged on WhatsApp
- area: Learners
- scene: A learner missed two sessions for travel. The teacher said he could attend another batch's class. Nobody told the coordinator, and the other batch is now one over its cap.
- cost: Unrecorded make-ups distort attendance and batch size, and cause arguments about what was promised.
- fix: A make-up is a record: which learner, which session, which batch, approved by whom. It shows on both batches and counts toward attendance.
- screen: learners

### 7. Fees by level, instalment and discount are tracked in a sheet
- area: Fees
- scene: Fees depend on language, level, online or classroom, and whether the learner came by referral. Some pay in two instalments. The accountant keeps a sheet, and the coordinator has a different one.
- cost: Dues are missed and discounts are given twice. Learners who are chased in the wrong way go quiet.
- fix: Each learner has a fee plan with instalments, discounts and what has been received. Verity lists dues by batch and age and assigns follow-up to a named person. Payments themselves go through your existing channels.
- screen: fees

### 8. Learners who finish a level are not offered the next one
- area: Fees
- scene: A batch of A1 completes in December. The institute starts A2 in January, but only half the learners hear about it, and half of those do not know the fee or the start date.
- cost: Moving a learner to the next level costs almost nothing compared with finding a new one. Missing a conversion is a batch that does not start.
- fix: Learners nearing the end of a level appear on a next-level list with their attendance and marks. Verity assigns a task to a person to speak to each, and the answer is recorded: enrolled, later or declined.
- screen: fees

### 9. Exam registrations and dates are in the learner's head
- area: Exams
- scene: An institute prepares learners for German or IELTS exams that are booked on the exam body's own site. Learners miss registration dates, forget what ID is needed or book a date that clashes with the course.
- cost: Exam fees are large; IDP lists IELTS at ₹19,000 and charges 25% for cancelling within 9 days of the test. A missed date or a missing ID is an expensive lesson for the learner and a reputational problem for the institute.
- fix: Each learner's exam is a record with the exam body, date, fee, ID needed and status. Verity lists learners who should have booked by now and those whose date clashes with classes. Staff help the learner; the learner books on the exam site.
- screen: exams
- source: (IDP IELTS India, test fee page, 2026: https://ieltsidpindia.com/information/test-fee)

### 10. Exam centre rules change and the front desk learns late
- area: Exams
- scene: From 1 September 2025 the Goethe-Institut Bangalore centre accepted only a valid passport as ID for A1 to C2 exams. A learner turns up with an Aadhaar card and is turned away.
- cost: A turned-away learner pays again, and blames the institute that prepared them.
- fix: ID rules and exam conditions are kept as notes on each exam body. Verity attaches the current note to the learner's exam record and creates a checklist task for the learner's coordinator a week before the date.
- screen: exams
- source: (Goethe-Institut Bangalore exam notice, 2025: https://www.goethe.de/bangalore)

### 11. Teachers are part-time and their time is hard to track
- area: Teachers
- scene: A French teacher takes two evening batches and a weekend one. She is paid per hour. The hours taken are counted from the batch sheet at month end and she disagrees with the count.
- cost: Pay disputes sour a relationship the institute depends on, and a teacher who leaves takes her learners.
- fix: Each session is recorded with the teacher, hours and whether it was taken, replaced or cancelled. Verity totals hours by teacher and batch for the month, so the accountant works from a record instead of memory. The institute still runs its own payroll.
- screen: batches

### 12. A teacher leaves and the batch goes with her
- area: Teachers
- scene: The Japanese teacher takes a job abroad with two weeks' notice. Her batches have no lesson record, no notes on where each learner is and no second teacher who knows them.
- cost: Learners lose momentum, and some leave with the teacher or the institute loses a batch mid-way.
- fix: Each batch has a syllabus position, lesson notes and learner progress on its record. A second teacher is named for each batch, and Verity shows batches without a backup.
- screen: batches

### 13. Certificates, results and learner records are loose
- area: Records
- scene: A learner asks for a certificate of completion for a visa file. The coordinator checks attendance, finds the level and writes it by hand. Nobody has a list of who finished what.
- cost: Delayed certificates, errors and no way to prove a learner's history when asked years later.
- fix: Each learner's record holds batches, levels, attendance, results and certificates issued. Verity lists learners eligible for a certificate and records each one issued.
- screen: learners

### 14. Online and classroom batches are managed separately
- area: Owner
- scene: Online batches run on a video link, classroom batches in two rooms. A teacher is booked for both at the same hour. The owner cannot say which mode earns more.
- cost: Double bookings, wasted room hours and no clear view of what to grow.
- fix: Batches carry their mode, room or link, teacher and hours. Verity flags a teacher or room clash and shows enrolment and collections by language, level and mode.
- screen: owner

## FAQ

Heading: Questions language institute owners ask.

- **Do teachers have to log in and enter a lot?** No. Teachers mark attendance and add a one-line note per learner. We train them at the end of a live batch, not in a classroom of their own.
- **Can it handle online and classroom batches together?** Yes. Each batch carries its mode, room or link, teacher and hours. Verity flags a clash for a teacher or a room.
- **Does Verity book learners for exams?** No. Learners book on the exam body's own site. Verity records the exam, the date, the ID required and the status, and reminds your coordinator to check.
- **Can it send reminders to learners?** Verity does not send messages on its own. It assigns the task to the right person and records what was said and the outcome.
- **We run German, French and IELTS. Is the setup different for each?** Each language has its own levels, batch rules and exams, which we set up in the first weeks. The records and screens are the same.
- **Can it issue certificates?** It lists learners who qualify and records each certificate issued. The format and the signature stay yours.
- **How is it different from a spreadsheet?** A spreadsheet holds what you type. Verity connects the enquiry to the batch, the batch to attendance and the learner to the next level, and flags what has not been done.
- **Who can see learner data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of learners, batches and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your institute runs.
