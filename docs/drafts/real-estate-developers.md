## How it runs today

**Most developers build well and chase badly.**

- The building goes up. What slips is everything that has to happen in step with it. The plinth is done, but the demand letter for that stage goes out two weeks late. A buyer’s loan is sanctioned, but nobody told the bank the stage was complete. The quarterly RERA update is due and the site engineer has the photographs on his phone.
- Each of these is handled by a good person. The trouble is that the facts sit in different places: the sales team’s sheet for bookings, the accounts team’s ledger for receipts, the site team’s reports for progress, the lawyer’s folder for agreements and approvals. The owner or the project head is the only one who connects them, usually on a call.
- So money comes in later than it could, buyers hear about delays from rumour, and compliance work is done in a rush near the deadline. None of it is a disaster in one week. Together it is a slow drag on cash, on trust and on the next launch.
- runs on: Sales sheet in Excel; Accounts software or Tally; Site progress in WhatsApp; Lawyer’s folder of agreements; Channel partner call lists
- stack note: Each tool does its own job well enough. The gap is that none of them knows what the others know.

## Pains

Heading: Where a developer actually loses time and money.
Lede: These are the situations project heads, sales heads and finance heads describe again and again. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Site visits and enquiries go cold with no follow-up
- area: Sales
- scene: A family visits the sample flat on Sunday. The sales executive notes it in his own diary. Two weeks later nobody remembers that they liked the east-facing unit on the ninth floor and wanted to see it with a loan estimate.
- cost: Example arithmetic: if a project gets 400 site visits in a quarter and 3 in every 100 book at ₹90 lakh, that is 12 units, about ₹10.8 crore. One missed follow-up in ten costs more than one unit.
- fix: Every visit becomes a record with the unit they liked, their budget and a next step. Verity lists visits with no follow-up in a set number of days and assigns each to the executive who met them.
- screen: units

### 2. Channel partners claim the same buyer, and brokerage is argued over
- area: Sales
- scene: Two brokers say they brought the same family. The buyer came once with one broker and again alone. The brokerage is held up for weeks while sales, accounts and both brokers argue from memory.
- cost: Hours of senior time per dispute, and a channel partner network that gets quieter after being treated unfairly.
- fix: The first registered visit is recorded against the partner with the date. Brokerage is then linked to the booking, so the claim can be settled from the record instead of from memory.
- screen: units

### 3. Units are held verbally and the availability chart is wrong
- area: Inventory
- scene: A senior sales person says “keep that unit for my client”. It stays blocked for six weeks. A walk-in buyer is told it is sold, and goes to the next project down the road.
- cost: A good unit sitting idle while a ready buyer walks away. In a project with 200 units, even ten stale holds change what you can say yes to.
- fix: Each unit has a state: available, on hold, booked, agreement signed, possession given. A hold carries a name, a reason and an expiry date, and lapses back to available unless someone renews it.
- screen: units

### 4. Booking is made, but the agreement stalls on paperwork
- area: Sales
- scene: The token is paid. KYC is incomplete, the co-applicant’s documents are missing and the draft agreement is waiting on a change request. Weeks pass with the unit shown as booked and the buyer not yet committed.
- cost: A booking that is not yet an agreement can still be cancelled. The unit and the cash both stay uncertain.
- fix: The path from booking to agreement is a checklist on each buyer’s record: KYC, loan documents, draft sent, stamp duty paid, registration date. Verity shows which buyers are stuck and on which step.
- screen: demands

### 5. Demand letters go out late because someone has to notice the stage
- area: Collections
- scene: The payment plan says a demand is due on completion of the fourth slab. The site team finishes it on the 3rd. Accounts hears on the 17th. The letters go out on the 20th and the payments arrive next month.
- cost: Example arithmetic: a stage worth 10% on 200 units of ₹90 lakh is about ₹18 crore. Each week of delay in raising it is a week of that money not available for contractor bills.
- fix: Each stage in the payment plan is linked to a site milestone. When the site team marks the milestone complete and the engineer’s certificate is attached, Verity creates the demand for every affected buyer, ready for accounts to review and send.
- screen: demands

### 6. Overdue buyer payments are chased by different people in different ways
- area: Collections
- scene: Some buyers pay late every time. Some are waiting on a bank. One is quietly in trouble. The collections list is a sheet sorted by flat number and it does not say who has been spoken to, or what they promised.
- cost: Cash that is owed, not received. Interest terms in the agreement are only useful if they are applied evenly and on time.
- fix: Each buyer’s overdue amount shows the days late, the last contact and the promised date. Follow-ups are tasks with an owner. Verity highlights those who have broken a promise twice.
- screen: demands

### 7. Bank loan disbursements are not tracked against construction stage
- area: Collections
- scene: A buyer’s loan is sanctioned, but the next tranche needs a stage certificate and a demand letter in the bank’s format. Nobody sent them. The bank pays two weeks later than it could have.
- cost: Buyer cash stuck in a queue at the bank. The buyer is shown as defaulting when the delay is in paperwork.
- fix: For each buyer with a loan, the record shows bank, sanctioned amount, tranches released and the document the next tranche needs. Verity creates a task to send it when the stage completes.
- screen: demands

### 8. The RERA quarterly update is built in a hurry from scattered numbers
- area: Compliance
- scene: The deadline is near. The site engineer sends photographs. Accounts sends sales figures from a sheet. Someone has to match unit counts, receipts and the percentage of completion, and the RERA portal wants it in its own format.
- cost: Late or inconsistent filings can invite penalties from the state authority and make buyers doubt you. Even without a penalty, it is days of senior time every quarter, per project.
- fix: The unit list, receipts, stage progress and photographs already sit on the project record. Verity assembles the figures a quarterly update needs, flags what is missing and assigns the gaps, so your team enters the update in the portal from one checked sheet.
- screen: rera
- source: (TaxGuru, Importance of RERA compliances, July 2024: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/importance-rera-compliances-real-estate-project-registration.html)

### 9. The 70 percent account and its withdrawal certificates
- area: Compliance
- scene: Seventy percent of buyer money must go to a separate project account. Withdrawals need a certificate from an engineer, an architect and a chartered accountant, in proportion to completion. Accounts is asked to withdraw for a bill before anyone has lined up the three signatures.
- cost: Delayed contractor payments, or a withdrawal that does not match the percentage of completion and has to be explained later to an auditor or the authority.
- fix: Receipts are split into the project account and the free account as they arrive. A withdrawal request is a workflow: the bill, the completion percentage and the three certificates must be attached before it is released for approval.
- screen: rera
- source: (TaxGuru, Importance of RERA compliances, July 2024: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/importance-rera-compliances-real-estate-project-registration.html)

### 10. Progress is reported in photographs, not against the plan
- area: Site
- scene: The site head sends twenty photos and a message saying “third floor slab done”. The programme in the project office says it should have been done twelve days ago. Nobody puts the two side by side until the sales team asks why the demand is late.
- cost: Delay interest exposure that nobody has added up, and buyers who learn of a slip from a rumour.
- fix: Every tower has stages with planned and actual dates. Progress is recorded against them with photographs, so the gap to plan is a number on the project head’s screen and the sales team can see it too.
- screen: site

### 11. Contractor running bills are paid on trust, not on measured work
- area: Site
- scene: The structure contractor submits a bill for 70 percent of the work. The site engineer says it is nearer 55. Accounts has no measurement sheet. The bill is settled by a phone call between the contractor and the director.
- cost: Example arithmetic: a 15 percent over-claim on a ₹6 crore work order is ₹90 lakh. It does not need to be fraud, only unmeasured work billed early.
- fix: Each work order has a bill schedule. A running bill is compared with the measured quantity and the progress recorded on site, and needs the engineer’s sign-off, then the project head’s, before accounts pays. Retention and advances are tracked on the same record.
- screen: bills

### 12. Sanctions, NOCs and the occupancy certificate run on a lawyer’s folder
- area: Approvals
- scene: The commencement certificate, fire NOC, environment clearance and lift licence each have dates and conditions. One expires in a month and only the liaison person knows. The occupancy certificate application cannot start until half of them are in order.
- cost: A project that cannot be handed over because one paper lapsed. Buyers who have paid in full wait outside the building.
- fix: Every approval is a record with its authority, validity date, conditions and owner. Verity lists those expiring soon and those that gate the next step, and assigns the follow-up to the liaison person or the lawyer.
- screen: rera

### 13. Buyers ask the sales team about progress and nobody has a clear answer
- area: Buyers
- scene: A buyer calls to ask when the tower will be ready, why the lobby finish is different from the brochure and whether the car park has been allotted. Sales calls site, site calls accounts, and the buyer waits two days.
- cost: Loss of trust in the one period when trust matters most. Unhappy buyers also tend to be slower to pay.
- fix: Each buyer’s record carries their unit, payment history, change requests and tickets. Questions become tickets with an owner and a due date, and the answer is recorded for the next person who gets the same call.
- screen: demands

### 14. Snagging and possession are managed on paper checklists
- area: Handover
- scene: Two hundred flats are ready for possession in the same month. Each buyer walks through with a printed snag list. Fixes are promised, done, not signed off and re-reported. The handover date slips because the last 20 snags per flat are not tracked in one place.
- cost: Possession delayed past the agreed date, and a quiet source of delay interest for flats that were “nearly ready”.
- fix: Each flat gets a snag list with items, photographs, an owner and a status. Verity shows how many flats are clean, how many have open snags and which contractor is holding up the most.
- screen: handover

### 15. Cash across several projects is judged by instinct
- area: Owner
- scene: Project A is collecting well. Project B has a bridge loan due. Project C needs a large advance for a contractor. Which can fund which is worked out in a meeting with three sheets on the table.
- cost: Borrowing at a higher rate than needed, or delaying a stage payment that would have kept a site moving.
- fix: Receipts due, receipts overdue, payables by contractor and committed amounts are shown for each project side by side. The owner sees which project is short and which has room, from the same live records.
- screen: owner

## Day, before and after

Heading: The same Thursday, before and after.
Lede: What changes is not the building. It is that every step leaves a record, and the record tells the next person it is their turn.

- 9:30: before: You ask the project head how Tower A is doing and wait for him to ring the site. | after: You open the Today screen. Tower A is four days behind plan, with the reason against the stage.
- 11:00: before: The fourth slab is done. The sales team hears about it a week later. | after: The engineer marks it complete. Forty-one demand letters are ready for accounts to review.
- 1:00: before: A walk-in wants unit A-904. The chart says booked, but the hold ended last month. | after: The hold lapsed on its date. The unit shows available and the executive takes the booking.
- 3:30: before: The structure contractor’s bill arrives. Accounts calls you to ask whether to pay. | after: The bill shows billed against measured. It waits for the engineer’s revised measurement before it reaches you.
- 5:00: before: A buyer calls about the car park allotment. Nobody has the file open. | after: The buyer’s record shows the allotment, the payments and the open ticket. It is answered on the call.
- Quarter end: before: The RERA update is assembled from four sheets in the last three days. | after: Verity lists the three gaps a week before. The update is entered from one checked record.

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks.
Lede: You do not get a login and a manual. We learn how your projects are actually sold, built and billed, set Verity up around that, bring your data in, then stay on so it keeps working.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Sit with the sales head, the finance head and the project head. Walk a site, follow one unit from enquiry to possession, and map where bookings, receipts, progress and approvals are recorded today.
- you: Two to three hours of your time, plus an hour each from sales, accounts and site. Access to your sales sheet, ledger and a recent RERA update.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a developer has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give legal advice and does not file anything on the RERA portal. It keeps the project records these rules depend on in order, so your team is not rebuilding them each quarter.
Note: Rules differ by state and change often. Confirm current requirements with your lawyer, your chartered accountant and the state RERA authority.

- **RERA project registration and quarterly updates**: what: A registered project is generally expected to be updated on the state authority’s portal each quarter. The format and deadlines are set by each state. | how: Unit counts, receipts, stage progress and photographs are on one project record, so the figures for the update are checked before they are entered. (TaxGuru, Importance of RERA compliances: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/importance-rera-compliances-real-estate-project-registration.html)
- **The separate project account for buyer money**: what: Under Section 4(2)(l)(D), generally 70 percent of what is collected from buyers goes into a separate account and is withdrawn in proportion to completion, with an engineer, an architect and a chartered accountant certifying. Accounts are audited every year. | how: Receipts are split as they arrive, and a withdrawal needs the bill, the completion percentage and the three certificates attached before it moves forward. (TaxGuru, Importance of RERA compliances: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/importance-rera-compliances-real-estate-project-registration.html)
- **Interest for delayed possession**: what: Under Section 18, a buyer who stays in the project can be owed interest for every month of delay past the agreed date. The rate is set by state rules. | how: Each flat carries its agreed possession date and the current expected date, so the exposure is a number you can see long before it is claimed. (Free Press Journal, Consumer Connect, March 2025: https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/consumer-connect-maharera-extension-no-excuse-to-deny-interest-payment-for-delay-says-expert)
- **Approvals and the occupancy certificate**: what: A project depends on sanctions, NOCs and licences with their own validity dates and conditions, and handover generally needs the occupancy certificate from the local authority. | how: Each approval is a record with its authority, expiry and owner, and the ones that gate handover are shown first.
- **Buyer personal data**: what: Buyer identity documents, income papers and loan details are personal data. India’s Digital Personal Data Protection law and its rules apply in stages. | how: Buyer papers sit in one permissioned place, with a log of who opened or changed them, rather than in sales staff’s phones and email.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions developers ask.

- **Will my sales and site teams actually use it?** It only works if updates are made, so we set it up around what each team already does. Site marks a stage complete, sales updates a hold, accounts approves a demand. Each person gets something back, such as a ready list or a clear sign-off, so using it saves them time.
- **Does Verity file my RERA quarterly update?** No. Your team enters the update on the state portal. Verity keeps unit counts, receipts, stage progress and photographs on the project record and lists what is missing, so the update is entered from checked information.
- **Can it send demand letters to buyers?** Not by itself. When a stage is certified complete, Verity prepares the demand for each buyer on the plan and asks accounts to review it. Your team sends it and the payment is then recorded against the buyer.
- **We run three projects at once. Is that supported?** Yes. Each project, tower and phase is set up with its own units, payment plans, stages and approvals. The owner sees them side by side, and a buyer who has bought in two projects is one person.
- **Does it replace our accounting software?** No. Your accountant keeps the books. Verity holds the buyer, unit, stage and bill records that the ledger needs, and we agree how data reaches Verity during setup.
- **How does it help with the 70 percent account rule?** Receipts are split as they come in, and a withdrawal request cannot move forward without the bill, the completion percentage and the three certificates. It does not move money or advise on your bank arrangements.
- **Who can see buyer and financial data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role. Site staff cannot see buyer receipts, sales cannot approve contractor payments, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **Can we migrate a project that is already half built?** Yes, most are. We bring in the units, bookings, receipts to date, stage history and open work orders so the record starts from where you are, and we check it against your ledger before go-live.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of projects, towers and users, and on what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your projects run.
