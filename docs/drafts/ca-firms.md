## How it runs today

**Most CA firms manage a large and strict calendar with a whiteboard and a good memory.**

- The work has a shape. Every month, every quarter, every year, the same filings return, each with a due date set by the government and not by you. What changes is the client mix, the staff and the notices that arrive without warning.
- The calendar is usually a sheet kept by a manager. Documents come through WhatsApp, email and couriers. Working papers sit on staff laptops. Fees are billed after the busy season, when someone has time. The partner signs, and holds the risk for all of it.
- The profession is large and competitive. As of February 2025, there were about 4.08 lakh ICAI members, of whom about 1.6 lakh held a certificate of practice, and nearly 99,000 registered firms. Clients can compare, and expect fast, accurate, responsive service.
- runs on: A manager's due date sheet or wall calendar; Tally, GST and income tax software; WhatsApp groups per client; Shared drives and staff laptops for working papers; Excel for fees and outstanding
- stack note: Each tool does its own job. None of them tell the partner, before the last week, that a filing is about to be late.

### 1. A compliance calendar that depends on one person
- area: Compliance
- scene: The manager who keeps the due date sheet is on leave in the week of the quarterly TDS returns. The sheet is on her laptop, and the dates for new clients were never added.
- cost: Late fees and interest fall on the client and the blame falls on the firm. For monthly GST returns, the late fee is generally ₹50 a day for returns with tax, subject to caps.
- fix: Each client has a record of its registrations and filing frequency, and every due date follows from it. Verity shows what is due this week across all clients, who is preparing and who is reviewing, and it does not depend on any one laptop. Your team files on the government portals; Verity keeps the plan and the status.

### 2. Rule changes that nobody applies to each client
- area: Compliance
- scene: The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026, while FY 2025-26 income is generally still assessed under the 1961 Act. A new return form, a changed threshold or a new portal advisory arrives, and the team asks which clients it touches.
- cost: Work done under the wrong assumption, rework on filing day and clients who ask the firm questions it cannot quickly answer.
- fix: A change is logged once with the clients it touches, such as by registration type, turnover band or entity type. Verity creates a review task for each affected client's lead, and the decision is written on the client's record.
- screen: calendar
- source: (Patron Accounting, Income Tax Act 1961 vs 2025, 2026: https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/income-tax-act-1961-vs-2025)

### 3. Clients who send documents on the last day
- area: Clients
- scene: The ITR or audit deadline is eight days away and a client's bank statements, loan certificates and TDS certificates are still missing. The client says "I thought it was ready."

- fix: Every assignment has a document checklist built from the client's type. Verity shows what has arrived and what is missing for every client, orders them by due date, and creates a chase task for the owner. Your team sends the request; Verity tracks it.
- screen: documents

- fix: Requests and receipts are recorded against the client and the assignment, with the date, the document and the person. Anyone in the firm can see what was asked and what came back.

### 5. Working papers and audit files on staff laptops
- area: Assignments
- scene: An audit assistant keeps the sampling, the confirmation letters and the ledger scrutiny in a folder on his laptop. He is moved to another client in the middle of the audit.
- cost: Lost evidence, repeated work and a review that cannot be done without the person who prepared the files.
- fix: Each assignment is a record with stages: planning, fieldwork, review, report. Working papers attach to the stage, with who prepared and who reviewed them and when. A new person can pick the file up from the record.
- screen: assignments

### 6. Review bottleneck at the partner
- area: Assignments
- scene: Forty returns and three audit reports are waiting for partner review in the last week of September. The partner reviews in the order they were handed over and not in the order of urgency.
- cost: Rushed reviews are where errors slip through, and every error carries the partner's signature.
- fix: Each item waiting for review shows its due date, the reviewer and any open points. Verity sorts the review queue by deadline and risk, and shows the partner what must be reviewed first.
- screen: assignments

### 7. Certificates and reports signed without a clean trail
- area: Compliance
- scene: A bank asks for a net worth certificate, a turnover certificate and a stock statement review on the same day. Each has to be checked, signed and logged.
- cost: ICAI made UDIN mandatory for practising CAs from 1 January 2019, so the partner has to generate a number for each certified document. A missed or mistaken UDIN is a professional conduct risk and a client embarrassment.
- fix: Each certificate or attest assignment is a record with the document, the reviewer, the sign-off state and a field for the UDIN generated on ICAI's portal. Verity shows signed items without a UDIN so the gap is caught before the document leaves.
- screen: assignments
- source: (TaxGuru, UDIN mandatory from 1 January 2019: https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/udin-mandatory-effect-1st-january2019-practising-cas.html)

### 8. Notices arrive and the reply date is missed
- area: Compliance
- scene: A client forwards a photo of a departmental notice a week after it arrived. The reply window has shortened. The notice was addressed to a registered mobile no one checks.
- cost: Ex parte orders, extra penalties and an urgent reply drafted in a day.
- fix: A notice is a record against the client with the section, the authority, the date received and the reply date. Verity assigns it to a senior, lists notices by due date and shows the reply status. Your team replies on the portal.
- screen: calendar

### 9. Article assistants and juniors overloaded or idle
- area: People
- scene: One article assistant is on four audits and a tax matter, while another is learning slowly on a single client. Training depends on who the senior happens to be.
- cost: Burnout, mistakes and slow learning. The firm also loses the chance to see who is ready for more.
- fix: Verity shows each person's assignments, due dates and logged hours side by side, and a training log shows who has worked on what. Partners assign new work from this view and rebalance before the crunch.
- screen: team

### 10. Fees billed late and work done on assurances
- area: Money
- scene: A client's annual retainer and tax return work are billed after the return is filed. An audit client gets the report before the fee is settled.
- cost: Cash tied up in receivables and little pull after the work is done. Example arithmetic: ₹22 lakh outstanding over 90 days is cash the firm has lent without interest.
- fix: Each assignment has its fee terms and invoice dates. Verity raises invoice tasks at the agreed stage, ages unpaid invoices by client and shows the partner the follow-up list with reminders noted. The firm bills and collects; Verity tracks it.
- screen: fees

### 11. Out-of-scope work that is never billed
- area: Money
- scene: A client under a GST retainer asks for a loan proposal, a CMA report and advice on a restructure. The partner agrees in a phone call.
- cost: Unbilled hours, usually the most valuable ones.
- fix: Each client has an engagement scope. Extra requests are logged as assignments with hours, and Verity flags work that falls outside what was agreed so a quote can be sent first.
- screen: fees

### 12. Which clients earn and which clients drain
- area: Owner
- scene: The partner suspects that some of the smallest clients take the most hours, but time is never logged.
- cost: Pricing is guesswork, and bad clients are kept for sentimental reasons.
- fix: Staff log time against clients and assignments. Verity compares fee, hours and internal cost per client, partner and service line, showing where the practice earns and where it loses.
- screen: fees

### 13. Client data on personal phones and in chats
- area: Compliance
- scene: PAN, Aadhaar, bank statements and Form 26AS sit on staff phones and in unmanaged shared folders. A staff member who has left still has copies.
- cost: A data leak is a trust problem for a profession built on confidentiality, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified in November 2025, with most duties applying about 18 months later.
- fix: Documents are records with permissions by role and client, and every view and download is logged. Closing a leaver's access is one step.
- screen: documents
- source: (Mondaq, DPDP Rules 2025, 2025: https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified)

### 14. Thresholds nobody watches: e-invoicing, audit limits, registration
- area: Compliance
- scene: A client's turnover crosses a limit during the year. The limit changes what applies: e-invoicing, GST registration category or audit. Nobody revisits the client until the audit starts.
- cost: Rework and non-compliance that the client discovers after the fact.
- fix: Turnover to date and the relevant thresholds are shown on each client's record, with a review task as a client approaches one. The partner decides what applies; Verity makes sure the question is raised in time.

### 15. New clients onboarded in a rush
- area: Clients
- scene: A referral arrives on the 25th of the month. KYC, previous returns, engagement letter and access to portals are collected over three weeks and the first filing is already due.
- cost: A shaky start and an engagement letter that does not match what was agreed.

### 16. The partner has no single picture of the practice
- area: Owner
- scene: At the end of a season the partner asks how many filings were late, how many clients are unpaid and who worked the most. Nobody can answer from one place.
- cost: Hiring, pricing and the next season are planned from memory.

## Day, before and after

- 11:00: before: A client forwards a photo of a notice that arrived a week ago. | after: The notice is logged in a minute, assigned to a senior and shows on the calendar with a reply date.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions chartered accountants ask.

- **Does Verity file returns or generate UDINs?** No. Your team files on the government portals and the signing partner generates the UDIN on ICAI's portal. Verity records the status and the number, and makes sure nothing leaves without it.
- **Will it replace Tally or my tax software?** No. Your team keeps using them. Verity runs the calendar, documents, assignments, hours and fees around that work.
- **How does it keep up when rules change?** You or your manager log the change and the clients it touches by registration, entity type or turnover band. Verity creates review tasks for each lead. It does not interpret the law, and your team decides what applies.
- **I run a five-person practice. Is this too much?** A small practice has the same calendar with fewer people to hold it. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.
- **Can my clients see anything?** Only if you choose. A client can have a view of their own checklist, notices and invoices, and nothing about other clients. We agree this with you during setup.
- **Can it track audit working papers?** It holds them as records on the assignment stage, with who prepared and who reviewed. It does not replace audit software your firm may use. We agree what sits where during setup.
- **How does it help with article assistants?** It shows each person's load and keeps a training log, so work and learning are spread fairly and a partner sees who is ready for more.
- **Who can see my data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and by client, and every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How soon will I see something useful?** The compliance calendar and the missing documents list need only your existing client data, so we aim to have them first. Profitability becomes reliable after a few weeks of logged time.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of staff and clients and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your practice runs.
