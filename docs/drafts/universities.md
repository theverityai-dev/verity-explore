## How it runs today

**The teaching works. The paperwork around it is held together by individuals.**

- Lectures happen, labs run and results are declared. What is harder to see is the administration behind them. Which research grants are behind on spending. Which PhD scholars have not met their doctoral committee in a year. Which statutory committee has not met this term. Which department has not sent its numbers for the ranking submission.
- This information exists, but in the finance office, in each department, in a research cell spreadsheet and in the head of the one section officer who knows where the file is. When that person is on leave or moves, the thread is lost. Every department also keeps its own format, so a university-wide number means weeks of collection.
- The cost is rarely one big failure. It is unspent grant money handed back, a scholar who drifts for two years, an accreditation file assembled in a panic, and a Vice-Chancellor who asks for a number and gets it a fortnight later.
- runs on: Finance office ledgers; Research cell spreadsheet; Departmental Excel files; Email and WhatsApp circulars; Paper files with section officers
- stack note: Each of these holds a true piece of the picture. None of them can answer a question that crosses two offices.

## Pains

Heading: Where a university loses money, time and standing.
Lede: These are the situations registrars, deans and research office heads describe. For each: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Grant conditions live in a sanction letter nobody rereads
- area: Research
- scene: A funder sanctions a project with a spending plan by year, a bar on certain heads of expense and a date for the final report. The principal investigator has the letter. The finance office has the money. Nobody has both in front of them.
- cost: Example arithmetic: a ₹60 lakh project that spends ₹9 lakh in its first year against a plan of ₹20 lakh is the kind that gets questioned at review. Unspent money may be reclaimed, and the investigator’s next application suffers.
- fix: Each grant is a record holding its sanction letter terms, yearly spending plan, permitted heads and report dates. Spend is shown against the plan, and the investigator and the research office both see the same page.
- screen: grants

### 2. Funder reports and utilisation certificates arrive late
- area: Research
- scene: The progress report is due on the 30th. The investigator is at a conference, the finance office needs a signed statement of expenditure, and the head of department has not seen a draft.
- cost: A late report can hold back the next instalment of a grant. Staff on the project, such as research fellows, keep working while the money waits.
- fix: Every report date becomes a task chain: investigator draft, finance statement, department head sign-off, research office submission. Verity shows which step each one is stuck on, 60 days before the date.
- screen: grants

### 3. Workload is uneven and supervision hours are invisible
- area: Faculty
- scene: One professor teaches 14 hours, supervises 9 scholars and sits on five committees. A colleague in the same department teaches 8 hours and supervises two. Nobody has the numbers side by side, so the allocation was done by who says yes.
- cost: Burnout in the willing few, resentment, and research output that suffers exactly where the load is heaviest.
- fix: Teaching hours, supervision count, committee seats and project roles are recorded per faculty member. Deans see the spread by department and can move load before the next timetable, instead of after a complaint.
- screen: faculty

### 4. Vacancies, ad hoc staff and visiting faculty are tracked in separate lists
- area: Faculty
- scene: Sanctioned posts, filled posts, guest lecturers on contract and extension of tenure are all handled by different sections. A department head cannot say which courses will have no teacher next semester.
- cost: Courses taught by whoever is free, students without a named teacher, and appointments that lapse because nobody saw the end date. Central universities have reported large teaching vacancies for years.
- fix: Each post is a record with its status: sanctioned, filled, contract, vacant. Contract end dates raise a task 90 days ahead, and each course lists its teacher and its backup.
- screen: faculty
- source: (ISignal (formerly IndiaSpend), citing UGC data, December 2014: https://www.isignal.in/indias-central-universities-crisis38-faculty-positions-vacant-75201)

### 5. Doctoral scholars drift without anyone noticing
- area: PhD scholars
- scene: A scholar finished coursework in year one. Since then there has been no doctoral committee meeting, no progress report, and a supervisor who changed departments. The scholar is now in year five.
- cost: Years of fellowship money for a thesis that may not finish, and a university that carries a scholar on its rolls without knowing whether they are active.
- fix: Every scholar has a record with admission date, supervisor, coursework status, committee meetings, progress reports and thesis stage. Anyone more than six months past a milestone shows up on a list for the research dean.
- screen: scholars

### 6. Fellowship and stipend payments depend on paperwork that goes missing
- area: PhD scholars
- scene: A scholar’s stipend is held because the attendance certificate or the supervisor’s approval did not reach accounts. They are told to come back next week, for the third week.
- cost: Scholars take on outside work, research slows, and some leave. Reports of stipend delays of several months are common in the sector.
- fix: Each monthly stipend is a request with its required papers attached as steps. Verity shows which scholars are missing which paper, to whom it is assigned, and how many days it has waited.
- screen: scholars

### 7. Accreditation evidence is rebuilt from scratch every cycle
- area: Accreditation
- scene: The self-study needs minutes, faculty publications, student feedback, outreach events and infrastructure details across years. Each department sends a different format, some as photos of registers.
- cost: Months of staff time in the run-up, with gaps found late. The national accreditation body has been moving towards more online, document-based checks, so the quality of your records matters more.
- fix: Evidence is kept all year as records tagged to the criterion they support: a meeting, a workshop, a publication, a feedback round. At submission the file for each criterion is already there, with the gaps listed.
- screen: evidence
- source: (Careers360, February 2025: https://news.careers360.com/naac-revamps-accreditation-process-will-implement-maturity-based-grading-current-grades-until-april-may-2025)

### 8. Ranking data is collected department by department, once a year, in a rush
- area: Accreditation
- scene: The ranking submission asks for student numbers, faculty details, placement, publications and spending over several years. The data cell mails every department and waits. Three send late and one sends numbers that do not match the admission office.
- cost: Wrong figures can be queried, and a rushed submission may understate real strengths. The weeks spent each year are staff time not spent on the work itself.
- fix: The numbers a ranking asks for come from the live records of students, faculty, projects and spending, with the department that owns each figure named. A mismatch between two sources is flagged before submission.
- screen: evidence

### 9. Statutory committees exist on paper and meet when reminded
- area: Governance
- scene: Anti-ragging, internal complaints, grievance, equal opportunity and IQAC committees all need regular meetings and a record of what was decided. The Registrar chases chairs by phone before a visit.
- cost: A missing meeting or an unclosed complaint becomes a serious matter if something goes wrong or an inspection arrives.
- fix: Each committee has a meeting schedule, a member list and an action register. Decisions become tasks with owners, and a committee with no meeting this term shows up on the Registrar’s list.
- screen: services

### 10. Certificates, transcripts and no-objection requests move by hand
- area: Students
- scene: A student needs a transcript for a foreign application. The request goes to the department, the examination section and the accounts office for a no-dues check, each with a paper note. The student comes to the office twice a week to ask.
- cost: Missed application deadlines for students, and a counter staff spending hours on status questions rather than on processing.
- fix: Each request is a record with the steps it must pass through. The student can be told which desk holds it and for how many days, and a request stuck beyond your limit goes to the section head.
- screen: services

### 11. Fee dues, scholarships and refunds are matched by hand
- area: Students
- scene: Hostel fee, exam fee, scholarship credited by the state, and a refund for a student who withdrew. Accounts matches them from three registers at the end of the term.
- cost: Students held back from exams for dues they have already cleared, and government scholarship money that sits unclaimed for lack of a follow-up.
- fix: Student dues, scholarship claims and refunds are records against the student. Verity lists unclaimed scholarships and refunds waiting for approval, and shows a student’s full position before a hold is placed.
- screen: services

### 12. Affiliated colleges send inspection and compliance papers that nobody tracks end to end
- area: Affiliation
- scene: A university with 120 affiliated colleges receives affiliation renewals, inspection reports, fee payments and staff approval requests all through the year. The affiliation section has a register and a lot of drawers.
- cost: Colleges operate with lapsed affiliation, approvals are delayed, and the university cannot say which colleges have open deficiencies.
- fix: Each affiliated college is a record with its renewal date, inspection findings, deficiencies and payments. Deficiencies carry an owner and a due date, and the list is visible to the Registrar by region.
- screen: owner

### 13. Lab purchases and equipment repairs get stuck between departments
- area: Purchase
- scene: A lab needs a replacement part for an instrument bought on a grant. The request needs three quotations, a committee approval and a budget check. It spends two months on desks.
- cost: Research stops while an instrument is down, and grant spending falls behind its plan for the reason above.
- fix: Purchase requests carry quotations, the grant head they charge to, and the approvals needed. The investigator sees where it is. Equipment is a record with its service history and the date its maintenance contract ends.
- screen: grants

### 14. MoUs with industry and other universities lapse without follow-up
- area: Partnerships
- scene: The university signs agreements for internships, joint research and student exchange. A year later nobody can say which are active, who owns each, or what came out of them.
- cost: Signing events that produce nothing, and renewal windows missed for the agreements that were working.
- fix: Each agreement is a record with its partner, owner, end date and activities done under it. Dormant ones and ones about to expire are listed, and the owner gets a task before the end date.
- screen: evidence

### 15. The Vice-Chancellor asks one question and waits two weeks
- area: Owner
- scene: How many scholars are overdue? How much grant money is unspent? Which departments have the most vacancies? Each answer needs a different office and a manual collection.
- cost: Decisions are made on old or partial numbers, and senior staff spend their time collating rather than managing.
- fix: A single page for the Registrar and Vice-Chancellor shows grants, scholars, vacancies, committees and affiliations from the live records, filtered by department or campus.
- screen: owner

## Day, before and after

Heading: The same Monday in the Registrar’s office, before and after.
Lede: The work is the same. What changes is that each obligation is a record with an owner, so the office stops chasing by phone.

- Morning: before: The Registrar starts with a stack of files and a list of calls to make about overdue items. | after: The Today page lists 12 items needing a decision, each with an owner and how long it has waited.
- Mid-morning: before: A professor asks if the final report on his project is due this month. Nobody is sure. | after: The report is a task chain on the grant, with the finance statement already assigned and due on Friday.
- Noon: before: A scholar comes to the office about a held stipend. Staff look through three files. | after: The scholar’s page shows the attendance paper is missing, who is meant to send it, and since when.
- Afternoon: before: The Vice-Chancellor asks how many posts are vacant. The answer takes a week. | after: The answer is on the Today page, by department, with the contract end dates behind it.
- Late afternoon: before: A student’s transcript sits at accounts. Nobody at the counter knows where it is. | after: The request shows it has waited six days at accounts and has already gone to the section head.
- Before an inspection: before: Departments are asked for papers, and the file is assembled over a month. | after: The evidence for each criterion is already filed, with the five gaps listed and assigned.

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you in four weeks, one office at a time.
Lede: A university is too large to switch over at once, so we start where the leaks are largest, usually research grants and doctoral scholars, and add other offices once those are working.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Meet the Registrar, the research dean, the finance officer and two department heads. Follow a grant from sanction to closure, and a scholar from admission to thesis, to see where papers stop.
- you: Two to three hours from you, and an hour each from the office heads. Sample sanction letters and one department’s files.
- get: A written map of your obligations: who owns each, what the steps are, and which three or four we will fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up departments, faculty roles, grant templates, scholar milestones, committee calendars and approval steps, and build the screens above around your own regulations and ordinances.
- you: Review the milestones and approval steps with the research dean and Registrar. Correct any rule we have wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your departments, rules and approval paths, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in the grant list, scholar register, faculty and post lists, committee members and affiliated college register from your sheets and registers. Train each office on its own work.
- you: The spreadsheets and registers as they stand, even if inconsistent. An hour with each office.
- get: Your grants, scholars, posts and committees inside Verity, and staff who have used it on real cases.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Review the first terms with you, add offices in order, tune the lists and reminders, and handle changes when regulations or reporting formats change.
- you: A monthly review with the Registrar and the research dean.
- get: A system that follows the university’s actual rules, with a named person responsible for it.

- bring: The list of active research projects with sanction letters; The PhD scholar register with supervisors and admission dates; Faculty and sanctioned post lists by department; Committee lists and last year’s meeting dates; The affiliated college register, if you have affiliated colleges
- time: About 10 to 14 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. The research dean, finance officer and Registrar’s staff need a few hours each.
- after: No new team is hired. Staff record what they already handle, and the status pages come from those records.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions registrars and deans ask.

- **Do we have to switch the whole university at once?** No. Most universities start with research grants and doctoral scholars, which are self-contained, and add faculty workload, committees and affiliations once those are running. The four-week setup covers the first set.
- **Will departments actually enter the data?** Only if it is easier than what they do now. We set each office up so that the work they already do, such as approving a report or logging a meeting, is the record. We train each office on its own tasks.
- **Does Verity replace our examination or student information system?** No. Examinations, results and the teaching side stay in the systems you use. Verity holds the administrative obligations around them, and we agree how data reaches it during setup.
- **Can it help with our accreditation and ranking submissions?** It keeps the evidence and figures through the year, tagged to the criterion they support, with owners and gaps visible. It does not submit anything for you, and your cell still reviews every figure.
- **How does it handle research grants from different funders?** Each grant is a record with that funder’s own terms: yearly plan, permitted heads and report dates. Different funders have different rules, so we set the templates with your research office.
- **We have affiliated colleges. Can Verity cover them?** Yes. Each affiliated college is a record with its renewal date, inspection findings, deficiencies and payments, and the Registrar can see open items by region. Colleges themselves can use their own page if you wish.
- **Who can see scholar and staff data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role and department, and every view or change is recorded with who made it and when. A dean sees their department, the Registrar sees across all.
- **How soon will we see something useful?** The grants-behind-plan list and the scholars-past-milestone list need only your existing registers, so they are the first things we aim to have working. Workload and vacancy views improve as posts and timetables are loaded.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of departments, campuses and offices you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your university runs.
