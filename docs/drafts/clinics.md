## How it runs today

**Most clinics are run by the doctor, one receptionist and a lot of memory.**

- The doctor sees patients. The receptionist books slots on a diary or a phone, writes up bills, finds old files and answers the phone. Medicines and consumables are counted when someone notices. Follow-ups are told to the patient in the room and remembered by nobody.
- That works at ten patients a day. At forty, with a second doctor or a second branch, things slip: files are not found, a patient due for review in two weeks never comes, a dressing pack runs out mid-clinic, and the owner learns the month's numbers when the accountant asks.
- Digital records are spreading. As of July 2026, official figures say over 93 crore ABHA numbers have been created and 2.72 lakh health facilities have adopted ABDM-enabled software, which means patients increasingly expect their records to be orderly.
- runs on: Paper files or a simple EMR; Appointment diary or phone calendar; Billing slips or a small billing app; Excel or a notebook for stock; WhatsApp with patients and suppliers
- stack note: Each works on its own. None tells you who has not come back or what will run out before Saturday.

## Pains

Heading: Where a clinic loses patients, time and money.
Lede: These are the situations clinic doctors, managers and receptionists describe. For each one: what it looks like on an ordinary day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Follow-ups are told but never tracked
- area: Patients
- scene: The doctor says, come back in two weeks. The patient nods. Nobody writes a date anywhere but the case sheet, and the case sheet is in a cabinet. Three weeks later the patient has not returned and nobody knows.
- cost: Example arithmetic: if 400 patients a month are asked to return and one in four does not, at ₹600 a visit that is about ₹60,000 of revisit income a month, plus patients whose treatment was never completed.
- fix: A follow-up is a record with a date, a patient and a reason, created at the visit. Verity lists follow-ups due and overdue each day, and the desk calls. The outcome is recorded: booked, declined or unreachable.
- screen: patients

### 2. No-shows leave empty slots
- area: Schedule
- scene: Six of twenty-eight booked patients do not turn up on Thursday. The doctor sits idle at 11 and rushes at 5. Nobody called to confirm the day before.
- cost: Published studies of outpatient clinics report no-show rates from about 12 percent to 42 percent, mostly outside India. At even 15 percent, an empty slot is a fee you do not earn.
- fix: Each slot has a status. Verity lists tomorrow's appointments for confirmation calls, marks no-shows with a reason, and shows no-show share by day, doctor and booking source, so the pattern can be addressed.
- screen: schedule
- source: (Health Science Reports review via PMC, 2024: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11231932/)

### 3. The walk-in queue and the booked queue clash
- area: Schedule
- scene: Booked patients wait behind walk-ins, and walk-ins leave when they see the crowd. The receptionist cannot say who is next, or how long the wait is.
- cost: Patients who wait too long often do not come back, and a doctor with uneven rush hours earns less per hour.
- fix: Walk-ins and bookings sit on the same day sheet with a time, a status and a waiting time. The desk sees the real queue, and the doctor's day is balanced across the hours.
- screen: schedule

### 4. Old records cannot be found
- area: Records
- scene: A patient returns after eight months and the file cannot be found. Or the file is found and the last prescription is unreadable. The doctor starts again from the patient's memory.
- cost: Time lost in the room, repeated tests, and a poorer visit. Records also matter when a patient or a regulator asks for them.
- fix: Each patient has one record with visits, reports, prescriptions and attachments in date order. The desk searches by name or number. Verity organises what is recorded; the doctor's notes remain the doctor's.
- screen: patients

### 5. Duplicate patients and wrong numbers
- area: Records
- scene: The same patient is registered twice with different spellings. A phone number is wrong, so the reminder goes to a stranger.
- cost: History splits, bills go to the wrong account, and a follow-up call reaches nobody.
- fix: Registration flags a possible duplicate for a person to merge or keep, and contact details are confirmed at each visit. One record, one history.
- screen: patients

### 6. Privacy: anyone at the desk can open anything
- area: Compliance
- scene: The receptionist, the billing clerk and a part-time intern all open the same file. A relative asks the desk about a patient's results, and the desk is not sure what it may say.
- cost: Health data is high risk. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 14 November 2025, and most duties apply 18 months later. Loose access is a legal and trust risk.
- fix: Access is set by role. The desk sees appointments and dues; only the doctor sees clinical notes. Every view and change leaves a trail of who and when, and consent given by the patient is recorded on the record.
- screen: patients
- source: (SCC Online on DPDP Rules 2025: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/)

### 7. Teleconsultation records are on WhatsApp
- area: Records
- scene: A follow-up is done over a call. The prescription goes by WhatsApp. The consent, the call time and the advice are in no file.
- cost: The 2020 Telemedicine Practice Guidelines expect consent to be recorded in the patient's record and logs, prescriptions and records to be kept. Scattered chats make that hard to show.
- fix: A teleconsultation is a visit on the same patient record, with the mode, the time, the consent and the attached prescription. The doctor writes the note; Verity files it.
- screen: patients
- source: (PSA Legal on the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020: https://psalegal.com/telemedicine-guidelines-2020-faq/)

### 8. Consumables run out in the middle of clinic
- area: Stock
- scene: Dressing packs, syringes, gloves, vaccines and test strips are counted by eye. The clinic runs out of a vaccine on a Saturday. Another vial expires in the fridge unnoticed.
- cost: A procedure postponed is a patient who may go elsewhere. Expired stock is bought and thrown away.
- fix: Each consumable has a reorder point and a batch with expiry. Use is deducted when a procedure is recorded, and Verity lists what runs short before the supplier's next delivery and what expires in 30 and 60 days.
- screen: stock

### 9. Supplier bills and rates are not checked
- area: Suppliers
- scene: A pharmacy supplier delivers 20 boxes and bills 24. A vaccine distributor changes a rate. The invoice is paid because the doctor is busy.
- cost: Overpayment is invisible and compounds across months.
- fix: Receipts are recorded against the order, compared with the invoice and rates are shown over time. A mismatch is flagged before the bill is paid.
- screen: stock

### 10. Bills, dues and package balances slip
- area: Money
- scene: A patient on a physiotherapy or dental package of six visits pays for four. A consultation fee is waived at the door. At the end of the day the till does not match the register.
- cost: Example arithmetic: ten unrecorded waivers of ₹400 in a month is ₹4,000, and unpaid package balances add up quietly.
- fix: Packages show visits used and paid. Waivers need a reason and a name. The day's collections by mode are compared with the visits recorded, and a gap is flagged with the shift.
- screen: money

### 11. Doctor shares and visiting doctors
- area: Money
- scene: A visiting specialist is paid a share of each consultation. At month end the share is calculated by hand from slips, and the doctor disputes it.
- cost: Disputes strain relationships with the doctors who bring patients.
- fix: Each visit is tagged to a doctor and a fee, so the share comes from the same records. The statement is a view, not a spreadsheet to build. Verity does not run payroll.
- screen: money

### 12. Room and equipment time sits idle
- area: Equipment
- scene: A procedure room is used two hours a day. An ECG machine or autoclave is serviced when it fails. The vendor's number is on a sticker.
- cost: Idle rooms are rent paid for nothing, and an unplanned failure cancels a day's procedures.
- fix: Rooms are on the schedule, so use shows by hour. Each device has a service date, vendor and history, with a task before it is due.
- screen: schedule

### 13. Staff cover and leave
- area: People
- scene: The nurse is on leave and nobody told the desk. The receptionist is sick on a Monday. Training dates for fire or hygiene are not recorded.
- cost: A clinic with the wrong people on the wrong day runs short or pays extra.
- fix: A roster shows who is on each day and who covers. Training and certificate dates are records with owners. Verity does not run payroll.
- screen: owner

### 14. A second branch, and no comparison
- area: Owner
- scene: The owner opens a second branch. One has long waits and the other has empty rooms. Numbers come in on WhatsApp at night in different formats.
- cost: Without a fair comparison nothing can be improved, and the owner is the integrator.
- fix: Branches are locations in one system. Visits, no-shows, follow-ups returned, stock and dues sit side by side, with the items that need a decision listed under them.
- screen: owner

## Rules and paperwork

Note: Rules change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your legal adviser, your medical council and the relevant authority.

- **Doctor registration and clinic registration**: what: Doctors must hold a valid registration with a medical council. Clinics generally need registration under the Clinical Establishments Act where a state has adopted it, or the state's own law, plus local trade permits. Rules differ by state. | how: Each registration is a record with a number, dates and an owner, with a task before a renewal.
- **Patient records**: what: Doctors are expected to keep patient records, and retention periods are set by medical council rules and state law. A June 2022 NMC draft proposed three years from the last contact for both outpatients and inpatients; check the current final rule. | how: Records stay in date order on the patient record, with the retention period set for your type of record. (PSA Legal: https://psalegal.com/telemedicine-guidelines-2020-faq/)
- **Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025**: what: Clinics handle health data, which is high risk. The Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 and most duties apply 18 months later. They include security safeguards and keeping logs for at least a year. | how: Role-based access and an audit trail on every view and change. ## FAQ

Heading: Questions clinic owners ask.

- **I have one doctor and one receptionist. Is this too much?** A small clinic has the same leaks and less time to see them. We set up only what you need, in the same four weeks, and leave out what you do not.
- **Does Verity give medical advice or store diagnoses?** No. Verity records and organises: visits, documents, follow-ups, stock and tasks. The doctor's clinical notes and judgement stay with the doctor.
- **Who can see patient data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so the desk, the nurse and the doctor each see only what they need, and every view and change is recorded with who and when.
- **Does it replace my billing software?** No. Your billing keeps taking payments. We agree during setup how visits and fees reach Verity, which then connects follow-ups, stock and the schedule around them.
- **Can patients book online through Verity?** No. Verity does not take online bookings. If you use another booking tool, it keeps working. Verity makes sure every appointment and follow-up has an owner.
- **Can it send reminders to patients?** Not on its own. Verity lists who must be called and records the outcome. Your staff call or message the patient.
- **I have two branches. Can I see them together?** Yes. Each branch is a location in one system, with visits, no-shows, stock and dues side by side. A patient can be one record across both.
- **How quickly will I see something useful?** The follow-up list, stock warnings and dues need only your existing data, so we aim to have them first. No-show and room-use patterns become reliable after a few weeks.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of doctors, branches and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your clinic runs.
