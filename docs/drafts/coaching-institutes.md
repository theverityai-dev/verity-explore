## How it runs today

**The teaching is strong. The office behind it runs on registers and memory.**

- A good institute has sharp teachers, a tested syllabus and a results record it is proud of. What it often lacks is a clear picture of the students it has right now: who is attending, who is falling behind, whose second instalment is due on Friday, which batch has 12 empty seats while another has a waiting list.
- That picture is spread across an admission register, a fee ledger, a WhatsApp group for each batch, a test results sheet kept by one faculty member, and a counsellor’s notebook. The centre head becomes the link between them, which works until the second centre opens or the head is travelling.
- The losses are not dramatic. A student who left in month three and was never asked why. A fee that was agreed verbally and never recorded. A strong teacher who leaves in April with a batch that has no handover. Each is small. Across a session they add up to a large number.
- runs on: Admission register; Fee ledger or Excel; WhatsApp groups per batch; Test results sheet; Counsellor’s notebook
- stack note: Each is useful on its own. None can tell you which student is about to leave.

## Pains

Heading: Where a coaching institute loses students and money.
Lede: These are the situations directors and centre heads describe. For each: what it looks like on an ordinary week, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Enquiries come in from five places and are chased by whoever remembers
- area: Enquiries
- scene: A parent calls after seeing a hoarding. Another fills a form online. A third walks in on Sunday. The counsellor notes two in a diary and loses the third. A week later the parent has joined the institute down the road.
- cost: Example arithmetic: 400 enquiries a season, a third never followed up properly, at a conversion of 1 in 6 on the rest, is about 22 admissions lost. At ₹80,000 a course, that is ₹17.6 lakh.
- fix: Every enquiry is a record with its source, the student’s class and target exam, and an owner. If no one has called within your limit, it shows on the head’s list, and each follow-up outcome is written against the record.
- screen: leads

### 2. Demo classes and scholarship tests do not turn into admissions, and no one knows why
- area: Enquiries
- scene: Two hundred students sit the scholarship test. Thirty join. The institute never looks at the 170 who did not, their scores or what they said when called.
- cost: The test cost money to run and the families who showed up were warm. Without a record, the next batch’s marketing starts from nothing.
- fix: Test participants are recorded with scores and school. Those who did well but did not join are listed with the follow-up outcome and reason, and a pattern such as "fee too high" or "wanted a closer centre" becomes a number.
- screen: leads

### 3. Instalments slide past their date and nobody asks until the course is half over
- area: Fees
- scene: The second instalment of ₹35,000 was due 10 October. The parent said "next week" to the front desk, which did not write it down. By December three more are due and the family has stopped answering.
- cost: Money already earned and not collected, and a conversation that becomes harder every week. Dues often turn into a quiet dropout.
- fix: Each student has a fee plan with instalment dates. Overdue ones appear on a daily list with the promise given and the date promised, assigned to a named person, and a missed promise moves the case up.
- screen: fees

### 4. Refunds, waivers and part payments are handled one by one and argued later
- area: Fees
- scene: A student leaves after two months. The parent asks for money back. The office remembers a discount given at admission but not the reason. The director is called to decide.
- cost: Disputes, ill will in a small town where word travels, and a legal risk if the refund rules are not followed. The Ministry of Education’s 2024 guidelines say a pro-rata refund should be made within 10 days when a student who paid in full leaves midway.
- fix: A refund is a request that shows fees paid, classes attended, discount history and the amount that would be due on a pro-rata basis. It goes for approval with a reason, and the decision stays on the student’s record.
- screen: fees
- source: (Careers360 on the Ministry of Education guidelines, January 2024: https://news.careers360.com/no-student-below-16-rank-guarantee-performance-based-batches-public-results-norms-for-coaching-centres-moe)

### 5. Students drop out quietly and the first sign is a missed instalment
- area: Students
- scene: A student comes four days a week, then two, then once. The faculty notice, but each assumes someone else will tell the centre head. In the fourth month the parent says their child has stopped.
- cost: The remaining fee, the seat in the batch, and a result the institute could have helped with if it had noticed early.
- fix: Attendance and test participation are recorded per student. A fall below your limit, such as three absences in a row, raises a task for the mentor, and the outcome of that call is written on the student’s page.
- screen: students

### 6. Test scores are collected and then filed away
- area: Students
- scene: The weekly test is marked, the toppers are announced, and the sheet goes to a folder. A student who has dropped from 78 to 54 over six tests is not noticed.
- cost: The main reason parents pay is to improve a result. If the institute does not act on its own tests, the value is lost.
- fix: Marks are recorded by topic. Verity shows each student’s trend and the topics they are weakest in, lists those who have fallen over several tests, and assigns a doubt session or mentor call.
- screen: students

### 7. Doubts pile up, and the best students get the most attention
- area: Students
- scene: Students put doubts in a WhatsApp group or a register. Quick, confident students get answers. The quiet ones wait, and stop asking.
- cost: The quiet students are often the ones who drop out, and they tell other parents why.
- fix: Each doubt is a task with a subject, student and faculty owner. Verity shows how long each has waited and which students have asked least, so the quiet ones are not missed.
- screen: students

### 8. One batch is overfull while another sits half empty
- area: Batches
- scene: The 7 am Class 12 physics batch has 74 students in a room built for 60. The 5 pm batch has 31. Moving students needs a call to each parent, so nobody does it.
- cost: Overcrowded rooms hurt teaching quality, and the empty seats are fixed costs for a teacher and room that are already paid for.
- fix: Each batch has a capacity, a roster and a room. The batches page shows fill levels together, and moving a student is one step that updates the roster, the fee plan and the timetable.
- screen: batches

### 9. A few star teachers carry the load, and when one leaves, the batch goes with them
- area: Faculty
- scene: The chemistry head takes six batches and half the doubt sessions. In April he gets an offer and leaves. His batches are given to whoever is free, in the middle of the session.
- cost: Students leave with the teacher, parents ask for refunds, and the institute cannot say which batches depend on one person.
- fix: Teaching hours and batches are recorded per faculty member, showing who is overloaded and which batches depend on one name. Each batch has a named backup, and the handover list is ready the day someone resigns.
- screen: faculty

### 10. Teacher pay by the lecture is counted by hand at month end
- area: Faculty
- scene: Visiting faculty are paid per class. The accounts person sums them from a diary. A class that was swapped is counted twice, another not at all, and two teachers dispute.
- cost: Hours of accounts time every month, and the risk that a good visiting teacher feels short-changed.
- fix: Each class held is recorded with the teacher and batch, including swaps and substitutions. A monthly statement is built from those records, and the teacher sees the same list. Your accountant still runs the payment.
- screen: faculty

### 11. Printed notes and test papers run out at the worst time
- area: Material
- scene: The new module is ready on Monday. Printing was ordered on Saturday and the press is closed. Half the batch gets photocopies, the rest wait.
- cost: Reprints at higher rates, students who feel they paid for less, and a teacher who loses ten minutes of every class.
- fix: Study material is stock with a count per batch and a reorder point. Verity checks the next fortnight’s syllabus plan against copies on the shelf and flags a shortfall while the printer still has time.
- screen: material

### 12. Advertised results need proof, consent and the right wording
- area: Compliance
- scene: The hoarding says "42 selections". Nobody can say, quickly, how many of those studied the full course, how many took only a test series, or whether each topper gave written consent.
- cost: Government guidelines issued in 2024 bar false claims about selections and rankings, and say a candidate’s name or photo needs written consent after selection. Penalties and loss of trust follow a mistake.
- fix: Every result is a record linked to the student, the course taken, the dates enrolled and a consent document. A claim can be checked against records before it goes on a banner.
- screen: students
- source: (DT Next on the CCPA guidelines, November 2024: https://www.dtnext.in/news/national/centre-issues-guidelines-to-crack-down-on-misleading-ads-by-coaching-centres-811047)

### 13. Registration, age limits and teacher records are held in different files
- area: Compliance
- scene: The state registration, the date of birth of each student, and the qualifications and police verification of each teacher all sit in separate places. A visit from the authority means a scramble.
- cost: Under the Ministry’s 2024 guidelines, centres generally must register, are not meant to enrol students under 16, and are expected to hire graduate tutors with no criminal record. State rules can add more.
- fix: Student date of birth is checked at admission. Teacher qualification and verification documents, and registration renewal dates, are records with an owner and a reminder.
- screen: faculty

### 14. Counselling is promised but nobody records it
- area: Wellbeing
- scene: A student seems stressed after three poor tests. A teacher mentions it to the counsellor in the corridor. No note is made and no one follows up.
- cost: Pressure is a known issue in coaching. The guidelines expect a counselling system, and an institute that cannot show one has little to say when asked.
- fix: Mentor and counsellor sessions are recorded per student with a next-step date. Access is limited to named staff. Verity assigns and tracks the follow-up. It does not give medical or psychological advice.
- screen: students

### 15. A second centre and the director becomes the bottleneck
- area: Owner
- scene: Two centres, two fee ledgers, two batches of WhatsApp numbers. The director rings both heads on Sunday night to get the week’s admissions and dues.
- cost: No fair comparison, no early warning, and the director’s week lost to collecting numbers.
- fix: Each centre is a location in one system. Admissions, dues, fill levels, dropouts and faculty load can be compared side by side, and the director’s Monday page is the same for both.
- screen: owner

- 8:30 am: before: The centre head scans the attendance sheets and guesses which students are slipping. | after: The head opens the at-risk list: 9 students below the attendance limit, each with a mentor and a call due today.
- 11:00 am: before: A parent walks in to enquire and the counsellor writes it in a diary. | after: The enquiry is a record with the source and class, and the next call is already on the counsellor’s list.
- 1:00 pm: before: The accounts person goes through the fee ledger to see who owes this week. | after: Overdue instalments are listed with promises and dates. Three are due for a call today.
- 3:30 pm: before: The weekly test is marked and posted. No one looks at who dropped. | after: Verity lists the 11 students whose scores have fallen for three tests, each assigned a doubt session.
- 6:00 pm: before: A teacher says he may leave next month. The head learns this from a colleague. | after: His batches and their named backups are on one page, so the handover starts that evening.

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you in four weeks, starting before the next batch.
Lede: We learn how your institute runs, build Verity around it, move your students and fee plans in, and stay on after.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend a day with the director and the centre head. Follow an enquiry from the first call to the first instalment and follow a student who left, to see where the record stopped.
- you: Two to three hours of your time, plus an hour each from the head counsellor and the accounts person. Your admission register, fee ledger and a sample test sheet.
- get: A written map of your batches, fee plans, teachers and tests, and the three or four leaks we will fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up batches and rooms, fee plans with instalment dates, faculty and backups, test structure, attendance limits and refund steps, and build the screens above around your terms.
- you: Check the fee plans and refund steps with your accounts person. Correct any rule we have wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your centres, batches, fee plans and rules, ready for your data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in current students, fee records, enquiry lists, attendance and test history from the sheets and registers you use. Train counsellors, accounts and mentors on their own screens.
- you: The registers and sheets as they exist, however untidy. An hour for each role.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions coaching directors ask.

- **Will my counsellors and teachers actually enter the data?** It only works if recording is quicker than not recording. We set it up so attendance, call outcomes and test marks are entered where staff already work, and we train on a live day. Staff also get something back: their own list for the day.
- **Does Verity run online classes or host tests?** No. Your classes and your test platform stay as they are. Verity records marks and attendance after the fact and shows what they say about each student.
- **Can it chase parents for fees?** Not by itself. Verity builds the overdue list and assigns each case to a person, who calls or messages. The promise and the outcome are then recorded on the plan.
- **We run two centres and a hostel tie-up. Can that work?** Yes. Each centre is a location in one system, and a student who moves between them stays one person. Hostel or mess fees can sit on the fee plan so a refund calculation includes them.
- **How does it help with the new coaching rules?** It keeps the records the rules depend on: student age, tutor papers, fee receipts, refund steps and consent for advertised results. It does not decide what applies to you, and your adviser should confirm that.
- **Can I see how well teachers are doing?** You can see hours, batch fill, attendance and test trends for each teacher’s batches, and compare them. These are numbers to start a conversation with, not a verdict.
- **Who can see student and parent data?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, and counselling notes are limited to named staff. Every view and change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How soon will we see something useful?** The overdue instalments list and the uncalled enquiries list need only your existing registers, so those are first. Attendance and test trends become reliable after a few weeks of recorded data.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of centres, students and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your institute runs.
