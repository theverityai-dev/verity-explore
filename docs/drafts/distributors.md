## How it runs today

**The margin is small, so the gaps between sheets matter.**

- A distributor often earns a single-digit or low double-digit margin on what passes through. That leaves little room for a retailer who pays in 50 days instead of 20, a scheme given to the shop but never claimed, or a pallet of damaged stock that the company has not yet credited.
- The work is spread across many hands. The salesman takes orders on his beat. The van loader packs. The collection boy brings cash. The accountant records invoices. The company sends targets, schemes and claim formats on its own cycle. Each has a sheet or a book, and the distributor is the person who reads all of them.
- The problem is not any one mistake. It is that the truth of the business only appears at month end, when a claim has aged past the window, the cash does not match the settlement, and the target is missed by a few lakh.
- runs on: Company portal and claim formats; Accounting software or Tally; Salesman order book; Van load and return sheets; Excel of schemes and outstanding; Cash register
- stack note: Each is used for its own job. The money leaks where they meet.

## Pains

Heading: Where a distribution business actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations distributors describe most often. For each: what it looks like, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Retailer credit grows beat by beat
- area: Credit
- scene: On Route 4, nine outlets are 40 to 70 days behind. The salesman keeps taking orders because the shop is a good account. The total for the beat is only seen when the accountant adds it up.
- cost: Example arithmetic: on a business doing ₹1 crore a month with an average 35 days of credit, about ₹1.2 crore is always out in the market. Each extra 10 days adds roughly ₹33 lakh to what you must fund.
- fix: Outstanding is held per outlet, per beat and per salesman, aged in bands. An order from an outlet over its limit waits for your tap, with its oldest bill and last payment beside it.
- screen: credit

### 2. Cash with the salesman does not reach the counter the same day
- area: Credit
- scene: A salesman collects ₹38,000 across a beat. He deposits ₹31,000 and says the rest is “in the bike”. The evening settlement is done by memory and the difference is carried to tomorrow.
- cost: Cash held outside the books for days is a standing risk. Small gaps repeated over a month become a large figure that nobody can place.
- fix: Each collection is recorded on the outlet against the bill it settles, with mode: cash, cheque or UPI. At the end of the day the settlement screen compares what the salesman recorded with what was handed in, per person.
- screen: settlement

### 3. A scheme given to the retailer is never claimed from the company
- area: Schemes
- scene: The company runs “buy 12 cartons, get 1 free” for ten days. Retailers get the free cartons from your stock. When the scheme ends, nobody lists which outlets got what, and the claim form is half filled.
- cost: Every free carton that is not claimed is paid for by you. Distributors say scheme recovery is often disputed and delayed, which is the same as a loan to the company.
- fix: A scheme is a record with a start, end, slab and product. Each invoice that earns it is tagged, so when it closes, the claim is already totalled by outlet and bill, ready for the claim form.
- screen: schemes
- source: (Kirana Club, FMCG distribution guide, 2026: https://kirana.club/resources/fmcg-distribution-india-guide)

### 4. Schemes change mid-month and the billing desk is the last to know
- area: Schemes
- scene: A new slab starts on the 12th. The salesmen are told on a WhatsApp group. Three invoices are billed on the old slab and the retailers want it corrected.
- cost: Credit notes issued later, retailer irritation, and a claim that does not match the invoice that supported it.
- fix: Scheme rules sit in Verity with the dates they apply. The invoice record picks the live rule for that outlet and date, and a bill that did not earn a scheme it should have shows up in a weekly exception list.
- screen: schemes

### 5. Damage, expiry and return claims pending for months
- area: Claims
- scene: Twelve cartons arrive broken. A claim is raised, photos are sent, and then nothing. Six months later the same batch is still open with the company, along with two expiry returns.
- cost: Distributors’ federations have described damage claims pending for over 15 months at one large company, and the stock value stays on your books until it is credited.
- fix: A claim is a record with the batch, quantity, value, photos and dates. Each has an owner and a state: raised, acknowledged, approved, credited. Anything older than your window appears on the Today screen.
- screen: schemes
- source: (Storyboard18, 8 October 2025, on AICPDF and Tata Consumer Products distributors: https://storyboard18.com/how-it-works/tata-consumer-products-distributors-allege-mismanagement-call-for-non-cooperation-movement-82179.htm)

### 6. Credit notes from the company that do not match your books
- area: Claims
- scene: The company sends a credit note for ₹68,000. Your records show claims worth ₹91,000 for that period. Nobody can say, without a full day of matching, which ones were paid.
- cost: Money you are owed sits unmatched, and disputes start from a pile of papers instead of a list.
- fix: Credit notes from the principal are matched to the claims they settle. Anything unmatched, short-paid or older than your window is listed with amounts, so the reply to the company is a table.
- screen: principals

### 7. Targets and stock pushed to you that the market does not need
- area: Targets
- scene: The company wants a bigger primary order before month end to hit its numbers. You take it because the margin and incentive depend on it. The stock sits for ten weeks.
- cost: Cash locked in a godown, a higher chance of expiry, and no one to blame because the order was yours.
- fix: Before you accept a primary order, Verity shows weekly secondary sales, stock in hand, goods in transit and days of cover for each item. You see what the order does to your cash and expiry risk before you say yes.
- screen: stock

### 8. Beats are visited by habit, not by plan
- area: Routes
- scene: A salesman skips a farther lane because it is a long walk. Twelve outlets there have not been visited for five weeks. Meanwhile, three shops near the depot are visited every day.
- cost: Missed outlets do not order, and the next distributor’s man does. A route that is never reviewed stays inefficient for years.
- fix: Each outlet belongs to a beat with a visit day. Verity shows outlets not visited within their cycle, orders per call, and lines sold per outlet, so beats can be rebalanced on facts.
- screen: routes

### 9. Van stock does not tally with van sales
- area: Routes
- scene: A van leaves with 140 cartons. It returns with 22 unsold and ₹1.9 lakh in sales. The numbers should tie. Nobody checks until the monthly stock count shows a shortage.
- cost: Shortages are written off with a guess. If the cause is theft or poor billing, you never find out which.
- fix: Each van load is an issue from the godown. Sales, returns and the closing load are recorded against the trip, and the difference is shown by van and by driver the same evening.
- screen: routes

### 10. Short-dated stock found too late
- area: Stock
- scene: A batch of biscuits with six weeks left sits behind newer cartons. The older stock is not billed first, and ends up as a return claim with a cost to you.
- cost: Expired goods are often only partly recovered from the company, and the rest comes out of your margin.
- fix: Stock is held by batch and expiry date. Picking suggests the oldest batch first, and a weekly list shows lots expiring within your window with the outlets that last bought them.
- screen: stock

### 11. A salesman leaves and takes the outlet knowledge with him
- area: People
- scene: The man who handled 120 outlets resigns on the 30th. His order pattern, shop owners’ phone numbers and unpaid bills were in his book and his phone.
- cost: A beat that goes cold for a month. Distributors’ bodies cite high sales team attrition as an industry problem.
- fix: Outlets, their history and dues belong to the business. A route is reassigned in one step, and the handover list shows each outlet with last order, usual items and money owed.
- screen: routes

### 12. Incentives and expenses argued from memory
- area: People
- scene: At month end, the salesmen’s target, achievement, daily allowance and fuel claims are totalled from notes. Two disagree. The accountant redoes one.
- cost: Hours of the accountant’s time and a salesman’s trust that goes down a notch.
- fix: Visits, orders, collections and trips are on each salesman’s record. Verity builds the month’s statement from your slabs. He sees the same breakdown, so there is nothing to argue about.
- screen: owner

### 13. No one knows which company actually makes you money
- area: Principals
- scene: You distribute for six companies. Sales are highest for one, but its claims are slow and its margin thin. Another is smaller but pays on time. You decide on feel.
- cost: Capital and godown space go to the principals that look big, not the ones that earn.
- fix: For every principal Verity shows margin, credit given, claims pending and stock held. You see return on money employed for each company before you renew or push a line.
- screen: principals

### 14. The depot, the vans and the books are three different views
- area: Owner
- scene: You open one file for outstanding, another for stock and a third for claims. By the time they agree, the day is over.
- cost: Decisions are delayed until the month closes, when it is too late to chase a claim or pause a credit.
- fix: The Today screen shows the day’s sales by route, outstanding, claims waiting, stock cover and approvals. Every item has an owner and opens into the record.
- screen: owner

## Day, before and after

Heading: The same Thursday at the depot, before and after.
Lede: The work stays the same. The difference is that every call, carton and claim leaves a record, and the record does the chasing.

- 7:30: before: Vans are loaded from a handwritten sheet. The loader says the count is right. | after: The load is an issue from stock against each trip. The van and the driver are accountable for it.
- 10:00: before: A shop on Beat 6, 70 days behind, orders more. The salesman agrees. | after: The order is held. You see the dues and the oldest bill and approve a smaller order.
- 1:00: before: A scheme ends and the claim is a stack of invoices that someone must add by hand. | after: The claim is already totalled by outlet and bill. The accounts clerk checks and sends it.
- 4:00: before: You find out a damage claim from June is still open and the window may be gone. | after: It appeared on the Today screen at week eight with an owner and a next step.
- 7:00: before: The cash settlement is done by memory. A gap of a few thousand is carried over. | after: Each salesman’s recorded collection is matched to what he handed in, before he leaves.
- Month end: before: You accept a bigger primary order to hit the target without seeing days of cover. | after: You see cover, expiry risk and cash impact first, and decide with the numbers.

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you, in four weeks, around your beats and your principals.
Lede: You do not get a login and a manual. We learn how your depot runs, build Verity around it, bring in your outlets and claims, then stay on until it settles.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Ride one beat with a salesman, sit through a van loading, and follow a scheme from the company circular to the claim. Map how credit, cash and claims are tracked today.
- you: Two to three hours of your time, plus your accountant’s. Principal agreements, the outlet master, outstanding and open claims.
- get: A written map of your depot: principals and their terms, beats and vans, credit rules, and the leaks to fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up principals, items and batches, beats and visit days, outlet credit limits, scheme rules, claim stages, salesman incentive slabs and van trips.
- you: Check each principal’s scheme and claim rules with your accountant. Correct any rule we got wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your principals, beats and rules loaded, ready for your outlet data.

### Weeks 3 to 4: Migration and training
- we: Bring in outlets by beat, opening outstanding by bill, stock by batch and expiry, and open claims. Train salesmen, loaders, the counter and accounts on live routes.
- you: Your outlet list and ledgers, however they exist. One hour with each role, and one beat to pilot.
- get: Your outlets, dues, stock and claims inside Verity, and a team that has run a week of real routes on it.

### After go-live: Operations partnership
- we: Stay on after launch. Review the first scheme cycle and claim round with you, tune credit limits and beats, and handle new principals.
- you: A monthly review of dues, claims and principal-wise margin.
- get: A setup that keeps up as you add companies, vans and routes, with someone responsible for it.

- bring: Principal agreements and margin terms; Outlet list with beat and visit day; Opening outstanding by bill and outlet; Stock by batch with expiry dates; Open claims and the company’s claim formats; Salesman incentive slabs and van list
- time: About 10 to 13 hours of your own time across the four weeks, mostly in week 1. The accountant and the depot in-charge need a few more.
- after: Nobody extra is hired. Orders, collections and van trips are recorded as part of the day’s work, and the reports come from those records.

## FAQ

Heading: Questions distributors ask.

- **My salesmen enter orders on the company’s app already. Why another system?** The company’s app is built for its own targets. It does not hold your credit by outlet, your van trips or your claims across six principals. Verity holds those, and we agree during setup how data from the company’s side reaches you.
- **Can it handle several principals with different schemes?** Yes. Each principal has its own items, margin, schemes, claim stages and credit note matching, and the owner sees them side by side.
- **Does it replace my billing software?** No. Billing and returns stay where they are. Verity holds the routes, dues, schemes, claims and stock around them.
- **Can it track our vans?** It records each van trip, the load issued, the sales and the return, and shows the gap. It does not track vehicle location.
- **What if we have more than one depot?** Each depot is a location in one system. Outstanding, claims and stock roll up for you, and a transfer between depots is recorded so nothing goes missing between them.
- **Can retailers pay or reorder through Verity?** No. Retailers order through your salesmen as they do now. Verity records the order, the dues and the payment once your team enters them.
- **Who can see margins and claims?** Only the people you allow. Salesmen see their own beats, the loader sees picks, and you and the accountant see the rest. Every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **How quickly will I see value?** Outstanding by beat and the open claims list need only your ledger and claim sheet, so they come first. Beat coverage and principal-wise margin become reliable after a few weeks of recorded routes.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of depots, salesmen and principals. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short chat about how your depot runs.
