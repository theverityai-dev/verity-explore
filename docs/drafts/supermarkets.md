## How it runs today

**The store is open, busy and stocked. Whether it is profitable is another question.**

- The sales number arrives every evening. What is hidden is the cost around it: how many packs expired this month, how much of the fresh section was written off, what was lost between the receiving dock and the shelf, and whether the promotional deal a supplier promised was ever credited.
- The knowledge is spread across people. The category manager knows which supplier is late. The receiving clerk knows the dock count was short. The floor supervisor knows the end-cap has been empty since morning. None of it is written anywhere the store head can see at once.
- Listed chains show how thin the margin is. Avenue Supermarts, which runs DMart, reported an EBITDA margin of 7.9 percent on ₹57,790 crore of standalone revenue in FY25, and independent supermarkets generally earn less. At that scale, a point of waste is a point of profit.
- runs on: Billing system at the counters; Purchase and GRN registers; Excel stock and expiry sheets; WhatsApp groups with suppliers and staff; Supplier schemes in emails and files
- stack note: Each system answers its own question. Nobody joins them, so nobody sees the whole cost of a product.

## Pains

Heading: Where a supermarket actually loses money.
Lede: These are the situations store heads and category managers describe. For each: what it looks like on a normal day, what it costs, and what Verity does about it.

### 1. Packs expire on the shelf and in the back room
- area: Stock
- scene: A carton of biscuits with six weeks to expiry sits behind newer stock in the back room. By the time someone finds it, it has to be written off. Nobody can say who last checked that aisle.
- cost: Example arithmetic: ₹25,000 of near-expiry packs written off each week in a store is ₹13 lakh a year, usually without a claim on the supplier.
- fix: Receiving records the batch and expiry date for each lot. Verity lists lots by days to expiry, tells the aisle owner which items to pull forward, mark down or return, and records the outcome for each.
- screen: expiry

### 2. Fresh produce, dairy and bakery are written off with no reason recorded
- area: Stock
- scene: At closing the fresh section is cleared. Some of it spoiled, some was over-ordered, some was damaged in handling. The write-off is one number and the reason is lost.
- cost: Fresh is the section where waste is highest. One Indian accounting firm puts shrinkage for organised retail at 1 to 3 percent of net sales, with grocery at the higher end.
- fix: Every write-off is entered with a reason: expired, damaged, spoiled, theft suspected or sampling. Verity totals by reason, section and shift so the fix goes to the right cause.
- screen: expiry
- source: (Patron Accounting, stock audit for retail and FMCG: https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/stock-audit-retail-fmcg-perishables-expiry-shrinkage)

### 3. Shelves are empty while the back room is full
- area: Stock
- scene: A customer finds the shelf bare for a fast-moving brand. The stock is in the back room, and no one was assigned to refill. The customer picks a rival brand or leaves.
- cost: A survey of 100 large organised chains found that 91 percent leak revenue at the shelf. A gap on the shelf for a top item is a sale lost every few minutes.
- fix: The shelf round is a task with a list per aisle: items to refill, with stock in the back room shown beside each. A refill is recorded, and gaps that repeat are flagged to the category owner.
- screen: shelf
- source: (Vector Consulting Group, The Ticking Shelf, reported by Indian Retailer, 2026: https://indianretailer.com/article/retail-business/retail/91-pc-organised-retail-stores-india-are-losing-revenue-shelf)

### 4. The shelf does not match the planogram
- area: Stock
- scene: The head office wants the new range at eye level. The floor has put it low, because that is where there was space. A supplier pays for an end-cap and finds it covered by something else.
- cost: Space is the most valuable thing you sell. Wrong placement costs sales and risks the display fees a supplier paid.
- fix: Each aisle has its intended layout recorded. A supervisor’s round checks the shelf against it with a tick and a photo note, and the exceptions go to the category owner with a due date.
- screen: shelf

### 5. Stock disappears between the dock, the shelf and the till
- area: Stock
- scene: Cases are signed for, but a few cartons never reach the shelf. Some items leave through the back door. Some are scanned wrong at the counter. The count shows a gap and no one can place it.
- cost: Trent reported shrinkage at 0.41 percent of sales in FY24, and the Retailers Association of India has said up to 0.5 percent is manageable. In a supermarket with a 5 percent margin, a point of shrinkage is a fifth of the profit.
- fix: Receiving, transfers, write-offs and sales are all recorded movements. Cycle counts by aisle are compared with the book, and each gap is listed with item, quantity, value, aisle and the shift when it fell.
- screen: owner
- source: (Economic Times piece reprinted by Third Eyesight, June 2024: https://www.thirdeyesight.in/?p=6499)

### 6. Supplier schemes, rebates and listing fees are not tracked
- area: Suppliers
- scene: A supplier agrees a 3 percent scheme if you hit a volume target. The target is hit in October. The credit note never arrives because no one remembers the deal. Listing fees are paid for items that were then delisted.
- cost: Example arithmetic: a 3 percent scheme on ₹40 lakh of quarterly purchases is ₹1.2 lakh. Missed once a quarter across ten suppliers, it is real money.
- fix: Each deal is recorded with its supplier, product, target and period. Verity compares purchases with the target as the quarter runs and lists the credit notes due, so the claim is raised on time.
- screen: suppliers

### 7. Deliveries arrive short, late or at the wrong price
- area: Suppliers
- scene: Twenty cartons are ordered and eighteen arrive. The invoice carries last month’s rate. The dock is busy, so the receiving clerk signs and moves on.
- cost: You pay for goods you did not get, or at a price you did not agree, and the correction takes weeks of calls.
- fix: The purchase order is checked at the dock against counted quantity and agreed rate. Verity flags any difference before the invoice is cleared for payment and opens a debit note task.
- screen: suppliers

### 8. Slow-moving items hold space and cash
- area: Suppliers
- scene: A product launched with a big listing push now sells two packs a week. It still has a full facing, and the supplier has stopped talking about returns.
- cost: Space and cash used by a slow item are taken from a fast one. Distributor returns policies are often time-bound, so waiting costs the right to return.
- fix: Each product shows sales speed, margin and space used. Verity ranks the weakest lines per category, with the supplier’s return terms beside them, so the decision is made while it can be returned.
- screen: suppliers

### 9. Prices differ between the shelf, the system and the supplier’s latest MRP
- area: Pricing
- scene: A new lot has a higher MRP. The shelf tag shows the old one, the billing system has the old one, and a customer notices a mismatch at the counter. Or the reverse: a lot with a lower MRP is billed at the old price.
- cost: Packaged goods must not be sold above the printed MRP. A mismatch is a complaint, a possible Legal Metrology penalty and a loss of trust at the counter.
- fix: Each lot’s MRP is recorded at receiving. Verity flags every product where the MRP changed and lists the shelf tags and billing prices to update, with a tick when each is done.
- screen: expiry
- source: (Lok Sabha reply on Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2025: https://fcainfoweb.nic.in/PMS/writereaddata/2025_LS_B_4362.pdf)

### 10. Shifts, absences and tasks are decided on the day
- area: Staff
- scene: Two cashiers call in sick on a Saturday. The aisle refill round is skipped. Floor staff do not know who is covering the fresh counter, so it is covered by whoever is nearby.
- cost: Long queues, empty shelves and overtime paid at short notice. Frontline retail in India sees heavy staff turnover, so new people are always learning.
- fix: The roster, attendance and availability sit in one place. Verity shows gaps by section and shift in advance, and assigns tasks like the refill round, the expiry check and the clean-up to a named person.
- screen: staff

### 11. Loss prevention depends on who happens to be watching
- area: Staff
- scene: A staff purchase goes through without a bill. A damaged item is “taken home”. A cashier gives a friend discounts. Nothing can be proved after the fact.
- cost: Internal loss is hard to price, but it is part of every shrinkage figure and it damages team trust when handled badly.
- fix: Voids, returns, price overrides and staff purchases are recorded with the person who made them. Verity highlights unusual patterns by cashier and shift and routes them to the store head with the facts.
- screen: staff

### 12. Cash, card and UPI collections do not tie with the day’s sales
- area: Money
- scene: Six counters, three shifts. At end of day, collections differ from sales by a few thousand rupees. Was it a wrong tender, a refund, or a missed bill? The cash room is working late again.
- cost: A few thousand rupees of unexplained gap every week, and hours of the cash-room team’s time.
- fix: Each counter’s sales and collections are compared by mode and by shift. Verity points to the counter and the bills that do not tie, and the close is recorded with who counted.
- screen: owner

### 13. Food safety licences, temperature logs and hygiene checks are on paper
- area: Compliance
- scene: The FSSAI licence is renewed on time, but the cold chain log for the dairy chiller is filled in once a day by memory. An inspection arrives and the records do not hold up.
- cost: Notices, penalties and, in the worst case, stock seized or a licence suspended. Revised FSSAI turnover bands apply from 1 April 2026, so the licence category may have changed.
- fix: The licence, expiry date and hygiene checks are recorded with a due date. Chiller and freezer readings, cleaning rounds and pest control visits are tasks with a person, a time and a tick.
- screen: expiry
- source: (Mondaq, FSSAI raises turnover limits for registration and licensing, 2026: https://www.mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/1801924/fssai-raises-turnover-limits-for-food-business-registration-and-licensing)

### 14. The store head works from instinct, because the numbers arrive late
- area: Owner
- scene: Margin by category arrives in the second week of the next month. Weekly decisions on promotions, space and ordering are made from sales alone.
- cost: A promotion that sells well but earns nothing goes on for weeks. A profitable aisle is starved of space.
- fix: Sales, cost, write-offs and scheme credits are combined into margin by category and aisle each day. Verity AI answers questions like “which category lost margin this week” from your own records.
- screen: owner

### 15. More stores, more variation, and no fair comparison
- area: Owner
- scene: Store two has more write-offs than store one. Is it the staff, the catchment, the ordering or the layout? The two stores use different sheets, so nobody can answer.
- cost: Good practice stays in one store and mistakes repeat in another. Head office hears about problems when they have grown.
- fix: Each store is a location in one system, with the same measures for waste, shrinkage, shelf gaps and margin. Transfers between stores are recorded movements.
- screen: locations

## How we work

Heading: We set it up with you in four weeks.
Lede: No login and a manual. We learn how your supermarket works from the dock to the counter, set Verity up around it, move your data in, and stay on while it settles.

### Week 1: Discovery and mapping
- we: Spend time at the dock, in the fresh section, on the floor and in the cash room. Map how a pack moves from the supplier to the shelf to the customer, and where each number is kept.
- you: Two to three hours for you and the store head. Access to purchase registers, billing reports and expiry sheets.
- get: A written map of your supermarket: sections, aisles, suppliers and deals, roles, and the problems we will fix first.

### Weeks 2 to 3: Configuration
- we: Set up your sections, aisles, intended layouts, suppliers and deal terms, expiry alerts, write-off reasons, rosters and store locations.
- you: Check the setup with the store head and category owners. Tell us where a rule is wrong.
- get: A working Verity with your real aisles, suppliers and rules, ready for your data.

## Rules and paperwork

Heading: The rules a supermarket has to keep up with.
Lede: Verity does not give legal or tax advice and does not file anything for you. It keeps the records these rules depend on in order, so your team is not rebuilding them when an inspector asks.
Note: Rules, thresholds and rates change and differ by state. Confirm current requirements with your accountant or the relevant authority.

- **FSSAI registration or licence**: what: A food business needs registration or a licence based on turnover and activity. From 1 April 2026 the bands are generally registration up to ₹1.5 crore, a state licence from ₹1.5 to ₹50 crore, and a central licence above ₹50 crore. Some activities need a central licence whatever the turnover. | how: The licence number, category and expiry are recorded with a reminder. Hygiene rounds and cold chain readings are tasks with a person and a tick. - **MRP and the Legal Metrology rules**: what: Packaged goods generally must not be sold above the printed MRP, and the MRP includes all taxes. State Legal Metrology departments inspect and penalise. - **Customer and staff data**: what: Loyalty phone numbers and CCTV or access data are personal data. ## FAQ

Heading: Questions supermarket owners ask.

- **We have thousands of products. Is that too much to load?** No. We start from your product master, usually an export from your billing system. We load it by section, check a sample against the shelf, and add batch and expiry information where the product needs it.
- **Does Verity replace our billing system?** No. Your billing system keeps ringing up sales. We agree during setup how its sales reach Verity, and Verity gives you the picture around them: expiry, write-offs, suppliers, shelves and shifts.
- **Will the floor staff really record all this?** Only what happens at points they already touch: the dock count, the shelf round and the write-off. We train on real shifts and keep each entry to a few taps. The lists they receive replace the checks they remember today.
- **Can Verity track expiry for every item?** It tracks the batch and expiry date you record at receiving, so it works as well as the receiving habit. We start with the categories where expiry hurts most, such as dairy, bakery and packaged foods, and widen from there.
- **Can you show me theft or staff loss?** Verity shows where stock does not match the book, and where voids, overrides and staff purchases stand out by cashier and shift. It gives you facts. It does not accuse anyone.
- **We have four stores. Can I compare them?** Yes. Each store is a location in one system with the same measures, so waste, shrinkage, shelf gaps and margin can be compared, with transfers between stores recorded as movements.
- **Who can see supplier rates and margins?** Only the people you allow. Access is set by role, so floor staff can see tasks and stock but not costs, and every change is recorded with who made it and when.
- **Will it message our suppliers or customers?** Not by itself. Verity drafts the order or credit note task and assigns it to a person, who sends it. The outcome is then recorded.
- **What does it cost?** It depends on the number of stores, users and what you want set up. Message us and we will give you a clear number after a short conversation about how your supermarket runs.
